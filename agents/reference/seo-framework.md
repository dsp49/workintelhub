Rule-1: Use the Proper Word Sequence
In SEO, the order of words within a sentence can significantly impact both user comprehension
and search engine understanding. Koray Tuğberk Gübür emphasizes the importance of using
the proper word sequence to optimize content for relevance and clarity.
Correct sentence structure plays a pivotal role in ensuring the content is easily understandable
by both human readers and search engines. By prioritizing attributes and context, content
writers can craft sentences that are not only informative but also strategically aligned with
search intent.
Consider the following examples to understand the impact of word sequence:
Example 1: Incorrect Word Sequence
- Penguin is a flightless seabird with flippers instead of wings that live almost exclusively below
the equator.
Example 2: Correct Word Sequence
- Penguin is a flightless seabird that lives almost exclusively below the equator and they have
flippers instead of wings.
In the correct sentence, the information is structured in a way that prioritizes the key attributes of
penguins (being flightless seabirds living below the equator) before mentioning additional
characteristics (having flippers instead of wings). This sequence not only provides a clearer
understanding for the reader but also helps search engines accurately interpret and index the
content.
Example 3: Context Sensitivity
When addressing a question like "Where does a Penguin live?", the correct structure would be:
- Penguin is a flightless seabird that live almost exclusively below the equator, and they have
flippers instead of wings.

Rule-2: Be Certain
In SEO content writing, certainty and factual accuracy are paramount. Koray Tuğberk Gübür
underscores the importance of conveying information with precision and confidence to build
trust and authority. This rule is particularly crucial for content writers and SEO practitioners
aiming to produce high-quality, reliable content.
Avoid Opinion-Based Statements
When writing SEO content, it’s essential to distinguish between opinions and facts. Statements
that reflect personal opinions, such as “will,” “should,” “have to,” and “need to,” can undermine
the perceived reliability of your content. Instead, focus on providing definitive, fact-based
information.
Example of Opinion-Based Statements:
- Incorrect: The sun will rise tomorrow.
- Incorrect: You should drink more water.
- Incorrect: We need to exercise regularly.
Example of Fact-Based Statements:
- Correct: The sun rises every day.
- Correct: Drinking water is essential for hydration.
- Correct: Regular exercise improves cardiovascular health.
