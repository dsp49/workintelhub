Two Weeks Notice Letter

WH The WorkIntel Hub editorial team
October 5, 2026 · 8 min read

A two weeks notice letter is a short written statement that you are resigning and when your last day will be. It exists to put the date on record, to protect the reference you will want later, and to start the handover. It does not need a reason, an apology or a paragraph about what you are doing next, and the best ones are under 150 words.

The generator below writes it in four versions and calculates the last day from the date you give notice. The rest of the page covers what the law actually requires in the United States (less than people think), what happens to your final paycheck and unused PTO, what to do if your employer ends your employment early, and how to hand the letter over.

What the law requires

Nothing. Employment in every US state except Montana is at will, which means either side can end it at any time without notice. Two weeks is a professional convention, not a legal requirement, and no state penalises an employee for leaving without it. Three things can change that:

  • An employment contract or offer letter with a notice clause. Common for executives, sales roles with commission plans and some professional positions. Breaking it rarely leads to a lawsuit, but it can affect what the contract pays out on departure, including bonuses, commissions and sometimes relocation or signing-bonus clawbacks.
  • A handbook policy that ties notice to a benefit. Many employers pay out unused PTO, or keep an employee eligible for rehire, only if they give two weeks. That condition is enforceable in most states, with the exception that states which treat accrued vacation as earned wages require it to be paid regardless; the PTO payout guide lists them.
  • Union agreements and public-sector rules, which usually set their own notice periods and procedures.

Your final paycheck is governed by state law, and the deadlines differ for quitting and for being fired. The final paycheck laws by state page has every state's rule; in California, for example, an employee who gives at least 72 hours' notice must be paid in full on the last day.

If your employer ends it early

Some employers accept a resignation and ask the employee to leave the same day, most often in sales, finance and any role with client relationships or system access. That is lawful under at-will employment, but it changes the character of the separation. If the employer ends the employment before the date you gave, you did not quit on that day; you were separated by the employer, and in many states that makes you eligible for unemployment benefits for the gap between the two dates, and sometimes beyond. Some employers pay out the notice period to avoid exactly that argument.

Ask, in writing, whether the employer is paying the notice period. If the answer is no, file for unemployment for the gap and let the state agency decide. Keep a copy of the notice letter with its date; it is the document that proves which day you chose.

How to hand it over

  1. Tell your manager in person, or on a call, first. The letter follows the conversation; it does not replace it. A manager who reads the resignation in an email before hearing it is a reference you have weakened.
  2. Send the letter the same day, by email with the letter attached or in the body, so there is a timestamped record. Copy HR if the handbook asks for it.
  3. Keep it short and positive. No reasons, no complaints, no feedback. The exit interview is where feedback belongs, and even there, measured.
  4. Offer a handover plan. The version in the generator with handover points is the one managers remember. Document the processes only you know, introduce your replacement or a colleague to the relationships, and return equipment on the schedule in the offboarding checklist.
  5. Do not stop working. The last two weeks are the reference. The resignation letter guide covers the longer, more formal version of the letter for senior roles and for contracts that specify what the letter must contain.

Three examples

Simple. "Dear Sam, please accept this letter as formal notice of my resignation from my position as Operations Coordinator at Northgate Logistics. My last day will be Friday, October 23, 2026. I appreciate the opportunities I have had here and the support of the team. Please let me know how you would like me to handle the handover. Sincerely, Dana Whitfield."

With a handover plan. "...My last day will be Friday, October 23. Between now and then I will document the month-end close process, walk Priya through the two key accounts, and move the vendor logins to the shared vault. Let me know if there is anything else you would like covered."

Brief. "Dear Sam, I am writing to let you know that I am resigning from my position as Operations Coordinator, effective Friday, October 23, 2026. Thank you for the opportunity. Sincerely, Dana Whitfield." This version is right when the relationship is strained; it is polite, complete and gives nothing to argue with.

Key takeaways

  • A two weeks notice letter states that you are resigning and names the last day. Under 150 words, no reasons.
  • No US state requires notice; contracts, handbook conditions on PTO payout and union agreements can.
  • Final paycheck timing is set by state law and differs for quitting and being fired.
  • If the employer ends your employment before your stated date, you were separated by the employer, which can make you eligible for unemployment for the gap.
  • Tell your manager in person first, send the letter the same day, offer a handover plan, and keep working.

Frequently asked questions

Is two weeks notice required by law?

No. Employment is at will in every state except Montana, so neither side owes notice. Two weeks is a professional convention. A contract with a notice clause, a handbook policy that conditions PTO payout or rehire eligibility on notice, or a union agreement can create an obligation, and those are enforceable.

What should a two weeks notice letter include?

Your name, the date, a statement that you are resigning from your position, your last day, a sentence of thanks, and an offer to help with the handover. Reasons, complaints and future plans belong elsewhere. The generator on this page produces four versions.

Can my employer make me leave immediately after I give notice?

Yes, under at-will employment. But if the employer ends your employment before the date you gave, the separation becomes the employer's decision, and in many states that makes you eligible for unemployment benefits for the gap. Ask in writing whether the notice period will be paid, and file if it is not.

Do I get paid for unused PTO when I quit?

It depends on the state and the policy. Around twenty states, including California, Colorado, Illinois, Massachusetts and Nebraska, treat accrued vacation as earned wages that must be paid out. Elsewhere the handbook controls, and many policies condition payout on giving notice. The PTO payout guide on this site has the state list.

Should I give more than two weeks?

For senior roles, roles with long handovers, or where a contract specifies it, three or four weeks is appropriate and the generator supports it. For most roles two weeks is expected and more is not required. Give the longer period only if you intend to work it fully.

Should I send the letter by email?

Tell your manager in person or on a call first, then send the letter by email the same day so there is a dated record. Attach it or put it in the body; copy HR if the handbook asks. A printed, signed copy is rarely needed unless the employer requests one.
