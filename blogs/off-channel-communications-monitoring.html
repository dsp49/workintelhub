Off-Channel Communications: Rules for Financial Firms

P Pavan
November 20, 2026 · 11 min read

Off-channel communications are business messages sent through channels a firm does not capture and retain: WhatsApp, iMessage, personal texts, Signal, WeChat, personal email. For most employers they are a policy question. For broker-dealers, investment advisers, swap dealers and other regulated financial firms they are a recordkeeping violation, because securities and derivatives law requires business communications to be preserved and supervised. Between December 2021 and January 2025, US regulators turned that requirement into the largest employee-communications enforcement campaign ever: the SEC brought about 95 off-channel actions with roughly $2.3 billion in penalties, and the CFTC imposed more than $1 billion on 18 institutions.

This guide covers the rules, the enforcement sweep and how it ended, the UK position, what firms now do to capture and supervise messages, and where monitoring stops on employees' personal phones. For monitoring rules outside financial services, see employee monitoring laws by state.

The recordkeeping and supervision rules

RuleWhoRequirement
SEC Rule 17a-4(b)(4)Broker-dealersKeep originals of business communications for three years, the first two in an easily accessible place; electronic records in WORM format or with an audit trail (2022 amendments)
Advisers Act Rule 204-2(a)(7)Investment advisersKeep written communications relating to advice, recommendations and transactions
FINRA Rule 4511FINRA member firmsMake and preserve books and records; six years where no other period is specified
FINRA Rule 3110(b)(4)FINRA member firmsWritten procedures for risk-based review of incoming, outgoing and internal communications
FINRA Rule 2210FINRA member firmsContent and approval standards for communications with the public
CFTC Regulations 1.31, 23.201 and 23.202Futures commission merchants, swap dealersKeep records of communications leading to trades

FINRA Regulatory Notice 17-18 (2017) made the practical point that still governs: a firm may let representatives use texting or messaging apps for business only if it can retain and supervise those communications. A message about a client trade sent on a personal WhatsApp account is a business record the firm has failed to keep, whatever device it was sent from.

The SEC and CFTC sweep

  • December 17, 2021: JPMorgan paid $125 million to the SEC and $75 million to the CFTC, after the SEC found widespread use of personal devices for business by employees including senior managers.
  • September 27, 2022: 16 firms paid the SEC $1.1 billion and 11 paid the CFTC $710 million, with individual penalties of up to $125 million.
  • 2023 to 2024: repeated waves against banks, broker-dealers and, increasingly, investment advisers, with penalties scaled to firm size and cooperation.
  • January 13 and 14, 2025: a final wave against 12 firms totaling about $63.1 million, with lighter undertakings and no compliance consultant requirement.

In total, the SEC reports about 95 off-channel actions and about $2.3 billion in penalties from fiscal 2022 to early fiscal 2025, and the CFTC more than $1 billion against 18 institutions. Settlements also required undertakings: reviews of policies and surveillance, employee training and certifications, and in many cases a demonstrated program of discipline and compensation clawbacks for violators.

Since 2025: what changed and what did not

  • Enforcement priority: under Chairman Paul Atkins, the SEC criticized the sweep as an example of how regulators should not act, noting the cases involved no investor harm, and its fiscal 2025 results described them as a poor use of enforcement resources. No new SEC off-channel cases were brought after the leadership change, and staff closed some pending investigations.
  • Settlements stand: in April 2025 the SEC denied motions by 16 firms to modify their undertakings to match the lighter January 2025 terms, and denied five more in June 2025. In August 2026 the Fifth Circuit rejected a firm's challenge to its 2024 settlement. FINRA said it would work to reduce collateral consequences for firms that settled earlier.
  • The rules have not changed: 17a-4, 3110 and 4511 are unchanged, and FINRA has continued individual enforcement, including a bar for off-channel use. Its 2026 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report flags off-channel supervision gaps and recommends watching for drops in activity on approved channels and refreshing surveillance lexicons.

The practical result is that the penalties have stopped but the compliance programs built in response remain, because examinations, FINRA enforcement, litigation and client contracts still depend on complete records.

The UK and Europe

The FCA's SYSC 10A requires in-scope firms, mainly those doing MiFID business, to record relevant telephone conversations and electronic communications and keep them for five years (up to seven if the FCA asks), and to take reasonable steps to prevent staff using private devices that cannot be recorded. The FCA's August 2025 multi-firm review of 11 wholesale banks found 8 had reported 178 breaches of their policies, 131 of them at three firms, and that 41% involved directors or senior managers; it has not fined any firm for off-channel use. Ofgem fined Morgan Stanley £5.41 million in 2023 for unrecorded WhatsApp messages by energy traders. EU MiFID II article 16(7) sets equivalent recording duties, implemented nationally, alongside GDPR limits on what may be monitored; see employee monitoring laws in the EU and in the UK.

What firms do now

  1. Approved channels with capture: archiving connectors from specialist vendors that record WhatsApp, WeChat, SMS and Teams messages on approved numbers or apps into a compliant archive.
  2. Device separation: firm-issued phones or mobile management containers on personal devices, so business messaging happens only inside captured apps; see the BYOD policy template.
  3. Written policies that ban business on unapproved channels and require any business message received off-channel to be forwarded into the archive promptly.
  4. Attestations: periodic certifications by employees, and sometimes reviews of a sample of personal devices with consent.
  5. Communications surveillance: lexicon and AI-based review of captured messages for misconduct, plus analytics that flag employees whose approved-channel activity drops suspiciously.
  6. Discipline and compensation: documented consequences, including clawbacks, as the SEC undertakings required.
  7. Training with real examples, including senior staff, who appeared in many cases.

Where monitoring stops on personal phones

Recordkeeping rules require firms to capture business communications, not to monitor employees' private lives. Well-designed programs capture only approved business channels or the work container on a personal phone, do not read personal messages, and obtain specific consent for any device review. In the US, employees' personal accounts are protected by the Stored Communications Act, and state laws in Connecticut, Delaware and New York require notice of electronic monitoring; in the UK and EU, the GDPR and the Bărbulescu test limit monitoring to what is necessary and disclosed. A firm that demands unrestricted access to employees' personal phones to prove compliance creates a privacy problem of its own.

If you work at a regulated firm

Treat any message about clients, trades, deals or firm business as a record, and send it only through approved channels. If a client texts or WhatsApps you, reply on an approved channel and forward the message into the archive as your policy describes. Do not delete business messages from any device. Expect your approved channels to be recorded and reviewed, and read the firm's policy on what it can access on a personal phone with a work container installed. Using personal channels for business has cost individuals their bonuses and, in FINRA cases, their registrations.

Key takeaways

  • Off-channel communications are business messages sent through channels a firm does not capture, such as WhatsApp, iMessage and personal texts; for regulated financial firms they violate recordkeeping and supervision rules.
  • The key US rules are SEC Rule 17a-4 (three years, two easily accessible), Advisers Act Rule 204-2, FINRA Rules 4511, 3110 and 2210, and CFTC recordkeeping rules.
  • From December 2021 to January 2025 the SEC brought about 95 off-channel actions with about $2.3 billion in penalties, and the CFTC more than $1 billion against 18 institutions, starting with JPMorgan's $200 million.
  • Since 2025 the SEC has stopped bringing these cases and criticized the sweep, but existing settlements stand, the rules are unchanged and FINRA still enforces against individuals.
  • The UK FCA requires recording under SYSC 10A and found 178 policy breaches at 11 banks in 2025, without fines so far.
  • Firms now use captured approved channels, device separation, attestations, surveillance and clawbacks, while limiting monitoring to business channels rather than employees' personal messages.

Frequently asked questions

What are off-channel communications?

Business communications sent through channels an organization does not approve, capture or retain, such as personal WhatsApp, iMessage, text messages, Signal or personal email. For regulated financial firms, failing to preserve such messages breaches recordkeeping and supervision rules.

Why did banks get fined for WhatsApp messages?

Because SEC and CFTC rules require broker-dealers, advisers and swap dealers to preserve business communications. Employees, including senior managers, discussed business on personal apps that their firms did not archive, so the records were not kept. The SEC and CFTC imposed over $3 billion in combined penalties from 2021 to 2025.

How much were the off-channel communication fines?

The SEC reports about $2.3 billion across roughly 95 actions from fiscal 2022 to early fiscal 2025, and the CFTC more than $1 billion against 18 institutions. JPMorgan paid $200 million in the first case in December 2021, and 16 firms paid $1.1 billion to the SEC in September 2022.

Is the SEC still fining firms for off-channel communications?

Not since the 2025 leadership change. The SEC under Chairman Atkins criticized the sweep and brought no new cases, though it refused to reopen earlier settlements. The recordkeeping rules themselves are unchanged, and FINRA continues to discipline individuals.

Can my firm read my personal WhatsApp?

A well-run compliance program captures only approved business channels or a work container on your phone, not your personal chats. Firms may ask you to certify that you do not use personal channels for business, and may review devices only with consent. US privacy statutes and, in Europe, the GDPR limit access to personal communications.

What should I do if a client texts me on my personal phone?

Reply on an approved channel and forward or capture the message into the firm's archive as your policy requires, and do not delete it. Most firms require this within a short time and treat repeated off-channel business as a disciplinary matter.
