10+ Resignation Acceptance Letter Templates for Every Situation

WH The WorkIntel Hub editorial team
October 9, 2026 · 8 min read

A resignation acceptance letter does four things in a page: confirms the resignation was received and accepted, states the last day, sets out what happens to pay, benefits and equipment, and thanks the person. It closes the loop so that nobody later argues about when notice was given, whether it was accepted, or what the final pay was supposed to include. It is also the last formal thing the employer says to someone who will talk about the company for years.

Ten templates cover the situations that differ in their terms: a standard acceptance, one with regret, immediate release with pay in lieu, a declined request to leave early, acceptance after a counteroffer was turned down, retirement, resignation during probation, a resignation given verbally, a remote employee, and the response to a request to withdraw. The terms the letter must get right

  • Last day. Received date plus the notice period in the contract, or the date agreed. Write the date, not "two weeks".
  • Final pay. The date and method, which several states fix by law for resignations; the final paycheck laws by state page has the deadlines, and the tracker carries a summary.
  • Accrued vacation. Payable on separation by law in California, Colorado, Illinois, Massachusetts and others; by policy elsewhere. The PTO payout guide covers it; say what will be paid.
  • Benefits. When coverage ends and that continuation information follows.
  • Equipment and access. What, by when, how; for remote staff, a prepaid label and an access end time.
  • Handover. What is expected before the last day.

Early release, counteroffers and withdrawals

An employee who resigns and asks to leave early can be released with pay in lieu of notice, which is often the better outcome for both sides, or required to work the notice for a stated business reason; the letter should say which and why. A counteroffer accepted is a retention; a counteroffer declined deserves a gracious letter, because most people who turn one down leave on good terms and some come back. A request to withdraw a resignation may be accepted (the letter should say employment continues on existing terms) or declined where a replacement has been recruited or the role reorganised; a resignation once accepted does not have to be un-accepted, but declining a withdrawal needs a reason. The two weeks notice page covers the employee's side, and the offboarding checklist the steps after the letter.

Key takeaways

  • An acceptance letter confirms receipt, the last day as a date, final pay and PTO terms, benefits, equipment, handover and thanks.
  • Final pay deadlines and vacation payout are fixed by law in several states; check before writing the date.
  • Early release, counteroffers and withdrawals each need their own letter with the reason stated.
  • Send it promptly and file it; it is the record of when notice was given and accepted.

Frequently asked questions

Do employers have to accept a resignation in writing?

No law requires it, but a written acceptance fixes the dates, the final pay terms and the handover expectations, and prevents later disputes about whether notice was given or accepted. Send it within a day or two of the resignation.

What should a resignation acceptance letter include?

Confirmation that the resignation dated X was received and accepted, the last day as a date, final pay date and what it includes (accrued PTO where payable), benefits continuation information, equipment return and system access arrangements, handover expectations, and thanks.

Can an employer refuse a resignation?

No. Employment is at will or terminable on notice; an employee may always resign. An employer can decline a request to leave before the end of the contractual notice period, but cannot prevent the resignation itself.

Can an employee withdraw a resignation?

Only with the employer's agreement once it has been accepted. Employers may accept a withdrawal, in which case the letter should confirm employment continues on existing terms, or decline it where a replacement has been recruited or the role changed, giving the reason.

When is final pay due after a resignation?

It depends on the state. California requires payment within 72 hours, or on the last day if 72 hours' notice was given; many states require the next regular payday; Oregon within five days or the next payday. Accrued vacation must be paid in several states regardless of policy. Check the final paycheck laws page.
