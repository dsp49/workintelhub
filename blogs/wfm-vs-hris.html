WFM vs HRIS: What Each System Owns

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

An HRIS owns the employee record: who works here, in what job, at what pay rate, reporting to whom, with what time-off policy. Workforce management (WFM) owns the hours: the demand forecast, the schedule, the clock punches, leave on the calendar, and the labor rules that turn worked time into payable time. The HRIS answers "who is this person?" and WFM answers "who is working at 2 p.m. on Saturday, and is that legal?"

They meet at time off, pay rates and terminations, which is why HCM vendors sell them together and why buying them separately creates integration work. This page sets out which system should own each piece of data, how the two connect, and when one suite is better than two products. For the HR labels themselves, see HRIS vs HRMS vs HCM.

The short answer

HRIS is the system of record for the person. Identity, job, pay rate, manager, location, employment status and documents. It is usually owned by HR.

WFM is the system of record for time. Forecasts, schedules, punches, approved hours and labor-law rules. It is usually owned by operations.

Payroll sits downstream of both. It takes the pay rate from the HRIS and approved hours from WFM.

An hourly, shift-based workforce needs both. A salaried office team often needs only an HRIS with time off.

Who owns which data

Most integration problems come from two systems both believing they own the same field. Decide the system of record for each field before you connect anything.

The exempt or non-exempt flag is a good test of the arrangement. It sits on the HR record, but the WFM rules engine needs it to apply overtime correctly; our exempt vs non-exempt entry covers the distinction. If the two systems disagree, someone is paid wrongly.

Where the two systems meet, and where it breaks

  1. New hires and terminations. An employee added to the HRIS must appear in WFM before their first shift; a terminated employee must lose clock access the same day. Check whether the sync is real time, nightly or manual.
  2. Pay rate changes. A raise entered in the HRIS mid-period has to reach WFM's labor cost and the payroll export with the right effective date.
  3. Time off. Balances usually live in the HRIS or payroll, and requests are often approved where the schedule is. Pick one place for approval and one for the balance, or employees will see two different numbers.
  4. Org changes. A new manager or a transfer to another location changes approval chains and scheduling groups in WFM.
  5. Hours to payroll. Approved hours, overtime and premiums leave WFM as pay codes. Map every code before the first live payroll and run one cycle in parallel.

Our HR software review lists the order in which migrated data tends to go wrong: pay data, then time-off balances, then the org chart. The same three fields fail first in a WFM integration.

When one suite covers both

HCM suites sell the HR record and workforce management together, which removes the integration but not the configuration. In our review:

  • UKG sells UKG Ready, an all-in-one HR, payroll, time and scheduling suite, and UKG Pro Workforce Management for enterprise time, scheduling, forecasting and labor compliance.
  • Dayforce runs HR, payroll, benefits, time and scheduling on a single engine, so a schedule change shows its payroll cost at once.
  • Rippling puts HR, payroll, time, scheduling and IT on one employee record; workforce management is one of its modules rather than its center.
  • ADP Workforce Now adds time and attendance with scheduling on its Premium tier; its scheduling is adequate rather than specialist.
  • Workday HCM is the enterprise system of record, with time tracking, absence and frontline scheduling as modules.
  • Paycor and Paylocity are HR and payroll suites with time and scheduling modules for hourly teams.

Specialist WFM tools take the other route. Deputy integrates with the payroll and HR systems most mid-sized firms already run, and adds payroll and HR as add-ons. Connecteam has no payroll. Homebase includes hiring, HR tools and payroll for a small number of hourly locations. On the HR side, BambooHR sells time tracking as an add-on to its HRIS. The workforce management pricing guide sets out what each route costs.

Which do you need?

  • Salaried or desk-based team, fixed hours. An HRIS with time off is usually enough. If you need attendance and workload without clocking in, a desk-based tool such as Time Champ can sit beside it.
  • Hourly team, one or a few sites, under 50 people. A specialist WFM tool with a free or low tier (Connecteam, Homebase, When I Work) plus your payroll. A separate HRIS can wait until records and onboarding become the problem.
  • Hourly or mixed, 50 to 500 people. Either a specialist such as Deputy integrated with your HRIS and payroll, or a suite such as Rippling, Paycor or UKG Ready. The choice turns on whether you want one record or the best scheduler.
  • Above about 500, or complex compliance. A suite with a strong rules engine (UKG, Dayforce) or an enterprise HCM (Workday) with workforce management modules.
  • Contact center. Interval forecasting and adherence are the job; see call center workforce management for the metrics the WFM system has to produce.

Our review places the move from specialist tools to a suite at around 300 to 500 employees, or earlier when payroll, benefits and HR records live in three systems that disagree.

WFM, WFO, HCM and workforce analytics

Four neighboring terms are worth separating. HCM is the vendor label for a suite covering HR, payroll and often WFM. Workforce optimization (WFO) goes a step beyond scheduling: it builds the schedule that meets a service or labor target at the lowest cost within the rules and feeds the results back into the forecast, a discipline that came from contact centers; see WFO vs WFM and what is workforce optimization. Workforce analytics analyzes aggregated data about how work gets done and often draws on WFM data. Key takeaways

  • An HRIS is the system of record for the person: identity, job, pay rate, manager, status and time-off policy.
  • WFM is the system of record for time: forecasts, schedules, punches, approved hours and labor-law rules.
  • Payroll takes the pay rate from the HRIS and approved hours and premiums from WFM.
  • Decide the system of record for every shared field, especially time off, pay rates and terminations, before integrating.
  • HCM suites (UKG, Dayforce, Rippling, ADP, Workday, Paycor, Paylocity) cover both; specialists such as Deputy integrate with your HRIS and payroll.
  • Salaried teams often need only an HRIS; hourly and shift-based teams need WFM too.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between WFM and HRIS?

An HRIS holds the employee record: who works here, in what job, at what pay, reporting to whom. WFM plans and records hours: forecasting, scheduling, time and attendance, absence on the calendar and labor-law compliance. The HRIS is usually owned by HR and WFM by operations.

Does an HRIS include workforce management?

Some do, as a module or add-on. BambooHR sells time tracking as an add-on, and HCM suites such as UKG Ready, Dayforce, Rippling and Workday include time and scheduling. A core HRIS on its own usually covers time off but not forecasting or scheduling.

Do I need both WFM and an HRIS?

If your workforce is hourly or shift-based, usually yes, either as two integrated products or as one suite. A salaried office team with fixed hours often needs only an HRIS with time off.

Which system should own time-off balances?

Usually the HRIS or payroll, where accrual policy lives, with WFM reading the balance and showing approved leave on the schedule. What matters is choosing one system of record for the balance and one place for approval.

Is WFM the same as HCM?

No. HCM is a vendor term for a suite covering HR, payroll, benefits, talent and often WFM. WFM is the part that plans and records hours. HCM suites include a WFM module, and specialist WFM tools connect to an HCM or payroll system.
