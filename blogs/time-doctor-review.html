Time Doctor Review (2026): Strengths, Limits and Who It Fits

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Time Doctor is a time tracker built around proof of work. People track time against projects and tasks, and while they do, Time Doctor takes screenshots, records web and application use, and reports attendance, breaks and idle time. Above that sit payroll, 60 or more integrations and, on Premium, video screen recording and anomaly detection. It is strongest for outsourcing firms and remote teams whose clients want evidence, and weakest for teams that only wanted timesheets and ended up with surveillance.

This review is built from Time Doctor's published pricing and plan features, read in October 2026, from the way other tools describe and compare it in our own research, and from recurring themes in public reviews. We have not run Time Doctor through a pay cycle ourselves, so the verdicts below are about what the product does and costs, not a scored hands-on test.

Verdict

Buy it if you run an outsourcing or remote team whose clients require screen evidence, you want payroll next to the hours, and you will read the overwork reports as closely as the idle ones.

Skip it if you want hours and attendance without screenshots, need more than six months of history without paying for Premium, or cannot tolerate a vendor with a poor cancellation record.

Expect to pay $11.70 per user a month on annual billing for Standard, the plan most employers need, plus $200 a month for each HRIS or payroll integration.

What Time Doctor is

The desktop agent runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and Chromebook, with an Android app and a Chrome agent. Screenshots are on every plan, including Basic. Standard adds attendance and schedules, time approvals, web and app usage reports, productivity ratings, leave and break tracking, payroll, real-time notifications and the integrations. Premium adds video screen recording, mouse-jiggler and irregular-keyboard detection, an unusual-activity report, an executive dashboard, SSO, an open API and a client login. In 2026 it added an AI benchmarks report and an MCP connector.

Data history is three months on Basic, six on Standard and two years on Premium. For a full list of what it records, from the employee's side, see what Time Doctor can see.

Where Time Doctor is strong

  • Overwork reporting. The work-life balance report flags late hours and long days, not just idle time. Our employee monitoring software review calls it the most employee-protective feature in the category.
  • Office versus Remote. A report that compares the two groups directly, which no other tool in our research offers.
  • The deepest evidence layer. Video recording, jiggler detection and an unusual-activity report on Premium are the deepest evidence layer among the twelve tools in our alternatives research.
  • Built for remote teams. It tracks offline and syncs later, handles time zones in attendance, and gives employees their own dashboard.
  • Platform coverage. Linux and Chromebook agents, which several competitors lack.

Where it falls short

  • Screenshots on every plan. There is no Time Doctor plan without them. Teams that only needed timesheets get screen capture they did not ask for, and employees who resent it.
  • Billing and cancellation. The Trustpilot score stood at 1.2 of 5 across 235 reviews in October 2026, and the largest cluster of one-star reviews describes accounts that kept charging after cancellation. Time Doctor replied to 42% of negative reviews there.
  • The realistic price. The features most teams need start on Standard at $11.70, and integrations are $200 each per month.
  • Short data history. Three months on Basic and six on Standard means last quarter's comparison disappears unless you pay for Premium.
  • Reliability and tracking complaints. Reviews mention silent crashes, a dated interface, and the desktop app recording activity without the timer being started on some configurations.

The review sites and the consumer review site split sharply. The quote that sums up the consumer side: I've asked to cancel my membership multiple times and am still being charged. (Trustpilot, one star, December 16, 2025).

How employees experience it

For staff, Time Doctor is a timer, a set of screenshots of their own screen, and a dashboard showing their hours, activity and the apps and sites they used. Teams told up front what is recorded and why tend to accept it; teams that discover screenshots, or a silent agent, after installation do not. Two decisions shape most of the reaction: who can see the screenshots, and whether managers read productivity ratings as a performance grade. The work-life balance report is the feature most likely to win staff over, if managers act on it. Our rollout guide covers the conversation.

Alternatives worth comparing

If mandatory screenshots are the issue, Hubstaff makes them optional and blurrable, and Toggl Track and Everhour never take them. If the price is the issue, Clockify Standard at $5.49, Apploye Elite at $4.50 and Time Champ Starter at $3.90 cover attendance and approvals for less. If data history is the issue, Insightful keeps 24 months and WorkTime keeps everything on paid plans. If you want analytics without images, read our ActivTrak review. Key takeaways

  • Time Doctor is a time tracker built around proof of work, with screenshots on every plan and video recording on Premium.
  • Its strengths are the work-life balance and Office versus Remote reports, Premium's evidence layer, offline tracking, and Linux and Chromebook agents.
  • Its weaknesses are mandatory screenshots, a 1.2 of 5 Trustpilot score across 235 reviews centered on billing after cancellation, and short data history on the lower plans.
  • Most employers need Standard at $11.70 per user per month on annual billing, plus $200 a month per HRIS or payroll integration.
  • It fits outsourcing firms with client evidence requirements; it is poor value for teams that only want timesheets.

Frequently asked questions

Is Time Doctor worth it?

For outsourcing firms and remote teams whose clients require screen evidence, and for employers who want overwork reporting, yes. For teams that only need timesheets and attendance, a tool without mandatory screenshots such as Clockify or Hubstaff costs less and is easier on trust.

What are the main complaints about Time Doctor?

Billing that continued after cancellation and refused refunds, which is the largest cluster of its Trustpilot reviews; screenshots on every plan; the realistic $11.70 price; short data history on Basic and Standard; and crashes, a dated interface and tracking that started without the timer on some configurations.

Is Time Doctor legal to use?

It is a mainstream tool, and monitoring company devices is legal in the US with notice where states require it, as in Connecticut, Delaware and New York. Tell staff what is recorded before installing it.

Does Time Doctor work offline?

Yes. Time Doctor tracks offline and syncs when the connection returns, which matters for remote staff on home internet.

What is the best alternative to Time Doctor?

It depends on why you are leaving: Hubstaff if screenshots are the problem, Clockify if the price is, Toggl Track if monitoring hurt the team, and Insightful if data history is.
