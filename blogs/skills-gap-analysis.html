Skills Gap Analysis: Steps and a Free Template

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

A skills gap analysis compares the skills your plan will need, at the depth it will need them, with the skills your people have today, and turns each shortfall into a decision: develop, hire, redeploy, contract or change the plan. You run it by listing six to ten critical skills, defining the required level for each role, rating each person on a short scale, and counting where coverage falls short.

The analysis is worth doing because a full headcount plan can still fail if the skills sit in the wrong people. The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 found 63 percent of employers named skills gaps the biggest barrier to transforming their business. This page sets out six steps, the rating scale, a template written out as a table you can copy into a spreadsheet, and how to read the result.

The short answer

Gap = required level minus current level, per skill, per person, then summed into coverage per skill.

Keep the list short. Six to ten skills that the plan depends on, not a competency library of two hundred.

The most important output is coverage, not averages. A skill only one person can perform is a single point of failure, whatever the team's mean score.

What a skills gap analysis measures

It measures two things at once. The depth gap is how far each person's current level sits below the level their role needs. The coverage gap is how many people can work independently on a skill against how many the plan needs. Depth gaps are mostly training decisions. Coverage gaps are mostly staffing decisions, and they are the ones that hurt when someone resigns.

It sits between two other pieces of planning. The headcount plan says how many people in which roles; the skills analysis says whether those people can do what the plan needs. Over a three-to-five-year horizon it becomes part of strategic workforce planning, where future skill demand is set against supply after attrition.

How to run a skills gap analysis in six steps

  1. Start from the plan, not the job descriptions. Write down what the business must be able to do in the next 12 to 24 months: launch a product, enter a market, adopt a system, meet a regulation. Each commitment points to skills.
  2. Pick six to ten critical skills. Name them specifically enough to rate: "SQL for reporting", not "data". If a skill would not change a staffing or training decision, drop it.
  3. Set the required level by role. For each role, write the level the plan needs on the 0 to 4 scale below, and how many independent practitioners (level 3 or 4) the team needs per skill.
  4. Rate current levels. The person and their manager each rate, then agree a number in a short conversation. Ask for one piece of evidence for any 3 or 4: a project, a certification, work someone else relies on.
  5. Calculate gaps and coverage. Gap per cell is required minus current, floored at zero. Coverage per skill is the count of people at 3 or 4. Flag any skill where coverage is one or zero.
  6. Assign an action and an owner to every gap. Develop, hire, redeploy, contract or change the plan, with a date. A gap without an owner is a finding, not a plan.

The rating scale

Use one scale across every skill so the numbers can be added. The 0 to 4 scale below is the same one the skills inventory in our workforce planning template uses, so the two can share data.

LevelLabelWhat it means
0NoneNo experience with the skill
1AwareUnderstands the concepts; cannot yet do the work without help
2WorkingDoes routine work with occasional review
3IndependentDoes the work unsupervised, including non-routine cases
4ExpertTeaches others, sets the standard, handles the hardest cases

Rate honestly. A sheet full of fours is a sheet nobody believes, and inflated ratings hide exactly the single points of failure the exercise exists to find.

In a spreadsheet, =SUMPRODUCT((B$2:F$2-B3:F3)*(B$2:F$2>B3:F3)) in G3, filled down, does it; coverage is =COUNTIF(B3:B8,">=3"). If roles differ within the team, add a required-level row per role and point each person's formula at their role's row.

How to read the result

In the example, three findings matter and the team average hides all of them.

  • Data modeling and forecasting each depend on one person. Analyst A is the only independent modeler and Analyst D the only independent forecaster. Either resignation leaves a skill with no coverage. These are the first entries for the succession plan.
  • Forecasting is a hiring or contracting decision. Five of six people are at 0 or 1. Training five people from near zero to independent takes longer than most plans can wait, so the faster route is one experienced hire or contractor who can also teach.
  • Stakeholder briefing is a development decision. Several people are at 2, one step short. Pairing them with the two independent practitioners on real briefings closes the gap without a course.

Notice also what the analysis does not say. SQL and dashboard building show no coverage gap, so training money spent there would improve scores without changing what the team can do. Fund the gaps that block the plan first.

Analyst E's total gap of 7 is a conversation for a one-to-one, not a team chart. Individual ratings are development data; publish coverage by skill, not scores by person.

Spreadsheet or software

For one team or a company of a few hundred, the spreadsheet above is enough. It strains when skills must be tracked across thousands of people, linked to the HR system's job architecture, and projected against future demand by location. That is the strategic workforce planning layer, and in our comparison of workforce planning software, SAP SuccessFactors Workforce Analytics and Planning is the tool positioned for planning gaps by skill and location, with Visier, Anaplan and One Model also covering the strategic horizon. Key takeaways

  • A skills gap analysis compares the skills the plan needs with the skills people have, and assigns an action and owner to each gap.
  • Measure both depth (required minus current level) and coverage (people at independent level against people needed).
  • Keep to six to ten critical skills on a single 0 to 4 scale, rated by the person and manager together with evidence for high ratings.
  • A skill with coverage of one or zero is a single point of failure and the first entry for the succession plan.
  • Small depth gaps are development decisions; wide coverage gaps on a deadline are hiring or contracting decisions.
  • Publish coverage by skill; keep individual ratings for development conversations.

Frequently asked questions

What is a skills gap analysis?

A comparison of the skills an organization's plan requires, at the level it requires them, with the skills its people currently have. It produces a gap per person and a coverage count per skill, and each gap is assigned an action: develop, hire, redeploy, contract or change the plan.

How do you calculate a skills gap?

For each person and skill, subtract the current level from the required level and ignore negative results. Sum across skills for a person's total gap. For coverage, count the people rated independent (3 or 4 on a 0 to 4 scale) on each skill and compare with the number the plan needs.

How many skills should a skills gap analysis include?

Six to ten that the plan genuinely depends on. Longer lists produce ratings nobody maintains and bury the few gaps that change staffing or training decisions.

Who should rate employees' skills?

The employee and their manager, separately, then agreed in a short conversation. Ask for one piece of evidence, such as a project or work others rely on, for any rating of independent or expert.

How often should a skills gap analysis be done?

Annually alongside the workforce and headcount plan, with a refresh when the business plan changes or a key person leaves. Critical single-coverage skills are worth checking every quarter.

Is there a free skills gap analysis template?

Yes. The table on this page can be copied into Excel or Google Sheets, with the formulas given for total gap and coverage. The workforce planning template download on this site also includes a skills inventory using the same 0 to 4 scale.
