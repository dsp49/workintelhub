QuickBooks Time vs Homebase (2026): Price, Payroll and Who Each Fits

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

QuickBooks Time and Homebase are both time clocks with scheduling for hourly staff, and they are priced in opposite ways. QuickBooks Time charges a monthly base fee plus a price for every user, and sends hours into QuickBooks payroll. Homebase charges per location with unlimited employees, is free for one location of up to ten, and sells its own payroll as an add-on.

The rest follows from that. QuickBooks Time fits crews already on QuickBooks payroll, especially field crews that need geofencing and project tracking on Elite. Homebase fits single-site shops, cafes and restaurants that run shifts rather than jobs. This comparison uses October 2026 pricing read from both vendors' sites and the features each publishes.

The short answer

Choose QuickBooks Time if payroll runs in QuickBooks Online or QuickBooks Payroll, your staff work across job sites, or you need geofencing, timesheet signatures, mileage or project tracking (Elite). Expect $20 plus $10 per user, or $40 plus $12.

Choose Homebase if you run one or two sites with shift staff and want scheduling, a clock on a tablet or POS, messaging and hiring in one app. One site of ten is free; above that, $30 to $120 per location.

Choose neither if you have many small sites, which makes Homebase expensive, and want to leave QuickBooks, which makes QuickBooks Time pointless. OnTheClock, Deputy and Jibble are the usual next looks.

Per user against per location

This is the difference that decides most comparisons. QuickBooks Time's bill rises with every person who clocks in, plus a fixed base fee. Homebase's bill rises with every site and ignores headcount. A single shop with fifteen staff pays Homebase $30 a month on Essentials and QuickBooks Time $170 on Premium.

Payroll: QuickBooks or Homebase's own

QuickBooks Time exists to feed QuickBooks. Intuit's product page says the integration needs an active QuickBooks Online, Payroll or Desktop account, and approved hours arrive without an export or mapping step each run. If QuickBooks is where you pay people, that is a real saving in admin time, and it is the reason customers stay despite the price.

Homebase runs its own payroll as an add-on at $49 a month plus $6 per employee after a free period, and also lists payroll integrations and a QuickBooks export. A single site of fifteen on Essentials with Homebase payroll comes to $169 a month ($30 plus $49 plus $90), with payroll included. The QuickBooks Time figure of $170 for the same crew excludes the QuickBooks subscription it depends on, which is priced separately; check Intuit's live page for that.

Field crews, geofencing and projects

QuickBooks Time Elite is built for crews that move: GPS geofencing restricts clock-ins to the job site, mileage is tracked, timesheet signatures close the week, and project tracking compares estimates against actuals. Homebase names location-based clock-in but not GPS tracking or geofencing on its pricing page, and has no job costing or project tracking.

If you run construction or service crews, that settles it in QuickBooks Time's favor, or in favor of a field tool such as Workyard or Timeero. Before switching GPS on for anyone, read our employee GPS tracking guide for the notice duties, and the construction timesheet guide for what the record needs to hold.

Shifts, breaks and the shop floor

Homebase is scheduling first. Shifts, a clock on the POS or a tablet, messaging and hiring are on every plan, an AI scheduling assistant arrives on Plus, and break and overtime preferences, PTO controls and labor cost tools sit on the paid tiers. For a restaurant or shop with one or two sites, that is most of the back office in one app.

QuickBooks Time also schedules on both plans and has a kiosk with optional facial recognition, but it sends overtime alerts rather than enforcing break rules by state, and California, Washington and Oregon employers need meal-break enforcement. A face-recognition kiosk collects biometric data, which in Illinois requires written consent before collection; see the biometric time clock entry.

Key takeaways

  • QuickBooks Time charges $20 plus $10 per user (Premium) or $40 plus $12 (Elite) a month; Homebase charges $0 to $120 per location with unlimited employees.
  • A single site of fifteen pays $170 a month on QuickBooks Time Premium and $30 on Homebase Essentials; twenty sites of two reverse the gap.
  • QuickBooks Time feeds QuickBooks payroll natively and needs a QuickBooks account; Homebase sells its own payroll at $49 plus $6 per employee.
  • Geofencing, mileage, signatures and project tracking are on QuickBooks Time Elite; Homebase has location-based clock-in and no job costing.
  • Choose QuickBooks Time for QuickBooks payroll and field crews, Homebase for single-site shift businesses.

Frequently asked questions

Is Homebase cheaper than QuickBooks Time?

For one or two sites, yes. Homebase is free for one location with up to ten employees and $30 to $120 per location above that, with unlimited employees, while QuickBooks Time costs $20 plus $10 per user or $40 plus $12, as read in October 2026. Businesses with many small sites can pay more on Homebase.

Does Homebase work with QuickBooks?

Homebase lists payroll integrations and a QuickBooks export alongside its own payroll add-on. QuickBooks Time is native to QuickBooks and needs an active QuickBooks Online, Payroll or Desktop account. Confirm the integration on Homebase's live page before switching.

Does Homebase have GPS and geofencing?

Homebase offers location-based clock-in, but its pricing page does not name GPS tracking or geofencing as such. QuickBooks Time records GPS on punches and offers geofencing on Elite.

Which is better for restaurants, QuickBooks Time or Homebase?

Homebase, for most single-site restaurants: scheduling, a POS or tablet clock, messaging and hiring on every plan, free for one location of ten. QuickBooks Time suits restaurants mainly when payroll already runs in QuickBooks.

Which is better for construction crews?

QuickBooks Time Elite, because it adds geofencing, mileage, timesheet signatures and project tracking with estimates against actuals. Homebase has no job costing or project tracking.
