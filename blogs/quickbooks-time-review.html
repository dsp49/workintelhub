QuickBooks Time Review (2026): Strengths, Limits and Who It Fits

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

QuickBooks Time, formerly TSheets, is a time clock and scheduler for hourly staff that sends approved hours straight into QuickBooks Online, Payroll and Desktop. Both plans include mobile and kiosk clock-in, scheduling, time off, approvals and overtime alerts; the higher Elite plan adds geofencing, signatures, mileage and project tracking. It is strongest for crews already running QuickBooks payroll, and weakest for small teams, because a monthly base fee comes before the per-user price.

This review is built from Intuit's published pricing and documentation, read in October 2026, and from recurring themes in public reviews. We have not run QuickBooks Time through a pay cycle ourselves, so the verdicts below are about what the product does and costs, not a scored hands-on test. Where Intuit documents its own behavior, that is what we cite.

Verdict

Buy it if your payroll runs in QuickBooks Online or QuickBooks Payroll, you have more than a handful of hourly staff, and you value hours arriving in payroll without mapping each run.

Skip it if you are a crew of three to five watching cost, you need geofencing without paying for Elite, you are moving payroll off QuickBooks, or you need state break rules enforced rather than flagged.

Expect to pay $20 a month plus $10 per user on Premium or $40 plus $12 on Elite, billed monthly, as read in October 2026.

What QuickBooks Time is

Intuit announced the TSheets acquisition on December 5, 2017, closed it in January 2018 and renamed the product QuickBooks Time. The mobile app is now called QuickBooks Workforce and runs on iOS and Android; there is also a web app and a tablet or desktop kiosk. Employees punch in from their phone or the kiosk, and QuickBooks Time records GPS location and photos on punches. It does no desktop monitoring: no screenshots and no activity scores.

Both plans include timesheets with approval workflows, scheduling, time off, overtime alerts, a who's-working dashboard, a kiosk with optional facial recognition and photo attachments, and phone and chat support. Elite adds GPS geofencing, timesheet signatures, project tracking with estimates against actuals, an activity feed and mileage. Intuit's product page says the integration requires an active QuickBooks Online, Payroll or Desktop account.

Where QuickBooks Time is strong

  • Native QuickBooks payroll sync. Approved hours flow into QuickBooks payroll with no export or mapping each run. That is the reason most customers buy it, and no alternative matches it exactly.
  • A complete clock on the entry plan. Scheduling, approvals, time off and a kiosk with facial recognition are on Premium, not held back for Elite.
  • Field features on Elite. Geofencing, mileage and timesheet signatures cover crews that move between job sites, and project tracking compares estimates against actuals.
  • Support and scale. Phone and chat support on both plans and a large installed base, and its review-site scores are good: people leave over price, not because the app fails.
  • No surveillance layer. It records punches, location on punches and photos, not screens or keystrokes, which keeps the conversation with staff about time and place.

Where it falls short

  • The base fee. $20 or $40 a month before anyone clocks in makes small crews pay the most per head: three people on Premium cost $50 a month, $16.67 each.
  • The July 2026 increase. Per-user fees rose $2 on July 1, 2026, from $8 to $10 on Premium and $10 to $12 on Elite. A 25-person Elite account went from $290 to $340 a month.
  • Geofencing costs the Elite tier. Restricting clock-ins to the job site, the feature field employers want most, moves every user up a price band.
  • Tied to QuickBooks. A business that moves payroll to Gusto, ADP or Paychex loses the main reason it bought the product.
  • Alerts, not enforcement. QuickBooks Time warns managers about approaching overtime; it does not enforce break or overtime rules by state. Employers in California, Washington and Oregon need meal-break enforcement.
  • Battery and GPS complaints. Reviews describe background GPS draining phones on long shifts and occasional unexpected clock-outs, a problem shared by most GPS clocks.

How employees and managers experience it

For staff, QuickBooks Time is a clock-in button in the QuickBooks Workforce app or a face at a shared tablet. The two settings that shape the reaction are GPS and facial recognition. GPS tracking is lawful in most of the country and carries notice duties that vary by state, personal phones need a written policy, and tracking outside working hours is unlawful in several states; our employee GPS tracking guide covers the notice rules. A face-recognition kiosk collects a biometric identifier, and Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act requires a written retention policy and informed written consent before collection; see the biometric time clock entry.

For managers, the who's-working dashboard and approval workflow are the daily screens, and overtime alerts arrive as suggestions they can decline. If your state requires meal breaks, a break time policy and a timestamped break punch are the defense, not the alert.

Alternatives worth comparing

If the base fee is the problem, OnTheClock charges $5 base plus $4 per employee with GPS, geofencing and a kiosk, and Timeero has no base fee with GPS and mileage from $5. If you run shifts at one site, Homebase is free for ten employees at one location. If you want break rules enforced, Workyard applies state, federal or custom rules on its entry plan. If free matters most, Jibble covers unlimited users. Our guide to the best QuickBooks Time alternatives sorts ten replacements by the reason you are leaving, with a cost table at 5, 15 and 50 employees, and the attendance tracking software review places QuickBooks Time among its peers.

If you decide to leave

  1. Export before you cancel. Intuit's cancellation article says to export first; reports stop when the subscription ends, though timesheets are preserved and reachable by renewing.
  2. Overlap one pay period so approved hours in the new tool match before it feeds payroll.
  3. Cancel in the right place. If QuickBooks Time was added through QuickBooks Online or Payroll, cancel the add-on there; standalone accounts cancel under Billing and Subscription.

Key takeaways

  • QuickBooks Time is a time clock and scheduler that sends approved hours straight into QuickBooks Online, Payroll and Desktop.
  • Its strengths are the native payroll sync, scheduling, approvals and a facial-recognition kiosk on both plans, and phone and chat support.
  • Its weaknesses are a $20 or $40 monthly base fee, the $2 per-user increase of July 2026, geofencing only on Elite, and dependence on a QuickBooks account.
  • It sends overtime alerts rather than enforcing state break rules.
  • It fits hourly crews already on QuickBooks payroll; it is poor value for crews of three to five and for businesses leaving QuickBooks.

Frequently asked questions

Is QuickBooks Time worth it?

For hourly crews already on QuickBooks Online Payroll, often yes, because hours reach payroll without mapping each run. For crews under about five people, or businesses leaving QuickBooks, the base fee and the QuickBooks dependency make alternatives such as OnTheClock, Timeero or Jibble better value.

Is QuickBooks Time the same as TSheets?

Yes. Intuit announced the TSheets acquisition on December 5, 2017, closed it in January 2018 and renamed the product QuickBooks Time. The mobile app is now called QuickBooks Workforce.

Does QuickBooks Time track GPS?

It records GPS location and photos on punches. Geofencing, which restricts clock-ins to a job site, is on the Elite plan only.

What are the main complaints about QuickBooks Time?

The base fee plus per-user pricing, the July 2026 per-user increase, geofencing locked to Elite, dependence on a QuickBooks account, overtime alerts that do not enforce break rules, and background GPS draining phone batteries.

Does QuickBooks Time monitor computer activity?

No. It is a time clock and scheduler; it records punches, GPS on punches and photos, not screenshots, applications or keystrokes.
