Hubstaff Review (2026): Strengths, Limits and Who It Fits

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Hubstaff is a time tracker that adds proof of work to the timer: optional screenshots, an activity percentage, app and URL tracking, and on the mobile app GPS location and job sites. Above that sit time off, overtime, scheduling, payroll and invoicing. It is strongest for distributed and field teams that pay by the hour, and weakest where managers mistake its activity percentage for a productivity grade.

This review is built from Hubstaff's published pricing and documentation, read in October 2026, and from recurring themes in public reviews and forums. We have not run Hubstaff through a pay cycle ourselves, so the verdicts below are about what the product does and costs, not a scored hands-on test. Where Hubstaff documents its own behavior, that is what we cite.

Verdict

Buy it if you run a remote or field team paid by the hour, need proof of work for clients or payroll, and have told staff what is recorded.

Skip it if you want hours for payroll without monitoring, a single user on a budget, or a team of meeting-heavy roles that the activity score will misread.

Expect to pay $7.50 to $10 per seat a month on annual billing for the plans most employers need, with a 2-seat minimum.

What Hubstaff is

The desktop app runs on Windows, macOS, Linux and Chromebook, with mobile apps for iOS and Android and extensions for Chrome and Firefox. People start a timer against a project, and while it runs Hubstaff records hours, an activity level, optional screenshots, applications and URLs, and idle time. The mobile app adds GPS location and job sites that clock people in and out automatically.

On the higher plans those hours feed time off, overtime rules, scheduling, payroll and invoicing, with payments through PayPal, Wise, Gusto and Deel. There are 35 or more integrations, including Jira, Asana, QuickBooks and Slack. Every seat that logs in is billed except a free Project Viewer role. For a full list of what it records, from the employee's side, see what Hubstaff can see.

Where Hubstaff is strong

  • Monitoring is a choice. Screenshots are optional, can be blurred, and can be disabled per project. Activity recording can be switched off for meetings and calls. You can run Hubstaff as a plain timer.
  • Field work. Mobile GPS and job sites with automatic clock-in cover crews, drivers and home-care staff, which most office-first trackers do not.
  • Paying people from hours. Payroll through PayPal, Wise, Gusto and Deel suits agencies and companies paying contractors across borders.
  • Platform coverage. A Linux agent and both mobile platforms, which several competitors lack.
  • Low-risk trial. A 14-day trial without a card and a 30-day money-back guarantee on paid plans.

Where it falls short

  • The activity score is read as a grade. It is the share of seconds with keyboard or mouse input. Hubstaff's own support says it misreads people on calls, but managers still rank staff by it, and it is the commonest reason teams leave.
  • Seat billing adds up. A 2-seat minimum, a charge for every manager who logs in, and add-ons for GPS, extra screenshots and Insights on lower tiers make the $4.99 headline rarely the invoice.
  • Contractor disputes. Platforms that pay workers by Hubstaff-tracked hours have generated many forum complaints about pay lost to idle detection or low activity. If you pay this way, the dispute lands on you.
  • False idle time. Reviews describe presentations and video calls marked as idle, and the agent using noticeable CPU on older machines.
  • Screenshots carry a trust cost. Even optional, they are why Hubstaff gets called bossware in employee forums when switched on by default.

How employees experience it

For staff, Hubstaff is a tray timer they start and stop, a percentage beside each ten minutes, and, if enabled, a grid of their own screenshots. Teams told up front what is recorded and why tend to accept it; teams that discover screenshots after installation do not. Two settings decide most of the reaction: whether screenshots are on, and whether managers are trained not to read the activity percentage as performance. Our rollout guide covers the conversation.

Alternatives worth comparing

If the activity score is the issue, Toggl Track and Everhour record time without scoring input. If screenshots are the issue, ActivTrak and DeskTime report applications without images on their standard plans. If the bill is the issue, Clockify Standard at $5.49 and Time Champ Starter at $3.90 cover attendance and overtime for less, and Jibble is free for unlimited users. Key takeaways

  • Hubstaff is a time tracker with optional proof of work: screenshots, an activity percentage, apps and URLs, idle time and mobile GPS.
  • Its strengths are optional monitoring, GPS job sites, payroll from hours through PayPal, Wise, Gusto and Deel, and wide platform support including Linux.
  • Its weaknesses are an activity score that managers read as a grade, seat billing with a 2-seat minimum and add-ons, and false idle time on calls.
  • Most employers need Grow ($7.50) or Team ($10) per seat per month on annual billing.
  • It fits field, agency and contractor teams; it is poor value for office teams that want hours without monitoring.

Frequently asked questions

Is Hubstaff worth it?

For remote or field teams paid by the hour that need proof of work, GPS or payroll from tracked hours, yes. For office teams that only need hours for payroll, a tool without monitoring such as Clockify or Toggl Track costs less and is easier on trust.

Is Hubstaff safe and legal to use?

Hubstaff is a mainstream tool and monitoring employees on company devices is legal in the US with notice where states require it, as in Connecticut, Delaware and New York. Tell staff what is recorded before installing it.

What are the main complaints about Hubstaff?

The activity percentage being read as a productivity grade, screenshots, pay disputes on platforms that pay contractors by tracked hours, false idle time during calls, and seat billing that grows with managers and add-ons.

Does Hubstaff work offline?

Hubstaff's desktop app is designed to keep tracking without a connection and sync when it reconnects; check the current documentation for limits on your plan.

What is the best alternative to Hubstaff?

It depends on why you are leaving: Toggl Track if the activity score is the problem, ActivTrak if screenshots are, Clockify or Time Champ if the price is, and Jibble if you only need a time clock.
