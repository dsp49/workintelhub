ActivTrak Review (2026): Strengths, Limits and Who It Fits

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

ActivTrak is productivity analytics first and monitoring second. Its agent records which applications and websites are active, for how long, and when a person is idle, then turns that into utilization, focus time, schedule adherence and workload reports, and on the higher plans burnout signals, capacity planning and AI coaching prompts. It logs no keystrokes and takes no screenshots on its standard plans. It is strongest for managers who want to see where a team's time goes, and weakest for anyone who needs billing hours, Linux coverage or a monthly contract.

This review is built from ActivTrak's published pricing and documentation, read in October 2026, and from recurring themes in public reviews. We have not run ActivTrak across a team ourselves, so the verdicts below are about what the product does and costs, not a scored hands-on test. Where ActivTrak documents its own behavior, that is what we cite.

Verdict

Buy it if you manage an office or hybrid team on Windows, Mac or Chromebook, want workload and focus data without images or keystrokes, and can commit for a year.

Skip it if you need hours against projects for billing or payroll, run Linux, need screen evidence for investigations, or want to pay month to month.

Expect to pay $15 to $17 per user a month on an annual contract for the plans with the reports most buyers want, after testing on the free plan for three users.

What ActivTrak is

The agent runs on Windows, macOS and Chromebook. There is no native Linux agent; ActivTrak's support material suggests its Chrome-based agent for other operating systems. It records the active application and website, active and idle time, and classifies each application and site as productive, unproductive or undefined for the person's role. Reports are built on that classification, and ActivTrak's offline meeting time and calendar integrations try to stop meetings being counted as idle, which most activity tools do not attempt.

It is cloud only. It is not a time tracker: there are no hours against projects, no payroll and no GPS. Exports run through the ActivConnect add-on into Google BigQuery, with pre-built tables for Tableau and Power BI, and through report downloads. Where ActivTrak is strong

  • Workload analytics. Utilization, focus time, workload balance and, on the higher plans, burnout signals and capacity planning. Our productivity monitoring review ranks its coaching and capacity views the most developed in the category.
  • Less collection. No keystroke logging on any plan, and no screenshots unless you buy an add-on. That is a design choice ActivTrak is open about, and it is far easier to explain to a team.
  • A visible agent. ActivTrak has no stealth mode, unlike several competitors.
  • A real free plan. Three users with 30 days of history, which lets a manager see the data before spending anything, plus a 14-day trial of the fuller reports.
  • Solid review scores. Around 4.3 to 4.5 on the main review sites, with complaints that are about contracts and setup rather than the product failing.

Where it falls short

  • Annual contracts only. ActivTrak's pricing page states that it currently offers only annual plans. Reviewers describe restrictive contracts, a hard cancellation process and refunds that took weeks, and "expensive" is the most cited negative.
  • Short history. Six months on the two lower paid plans and a year on Productivity Optimization, with $1 per user per month for each extra year. Year-on-year comparison costs extra.
  • The classification burden. The value depends on every application and site being classified for each team. Reviewers describe hours spent doing that, large volumes of data to sort, and an interface that is complex to navigate.
  • No Linux and no time tracking. Engineering teams on Linux need the Chrome-based workaround, and billing or payroll needs a second tool.
  • Weak for investigations. Screenshots are an add-on on the lower plans and absent on the higher ones, and periodic images are no substitute for recording when an event must be reconstructed.
  • Cost at scale. Paid support becomes mandatory above 50 licenses, on top of the plan price.

How employees experience it

For staff, ActivTrak is mostly invisible in daily work: there is no timer to start and, on the standard plans, no grid of screenshots. What they feel is the classification. If the browser tab where someone does their real work is marked unproductive, or a whole afternoon of reading shows as idle, the numbers will feel unfair, and they will be. Teams told up front what is recorded, how it is classified and what the reports will and will not be used for tend to accept it. What to settle before you roll it out

  1. Write down the question. Workload balance, focus time and schedule adherence are what ActivTrak answers well. Ranking individuals is not, and the reports will be read that way unless you say otherwise.
  2. Classify per role before the first report. An application that is productive for an engineer is undefined for a salesperson. Budget the hours reviewers describe for this, and revisit it after the first month.
  3. Decide on screenshots once. On the lower plans the Screen Details add-on is a separate purchase. Decide whether you will ever buy it before you tell staff what is recorded, so the answer does not change later.
  4. Give notice. Connecticut, Delaware and New York require written notice of electronic monitoring; our monitoring policy template has the wording.
  5. Plan your history. With six months on the lower plans, decide now whether to buy extra years or export reports through ActivConnect or as files before they age out.

Key takeaways

  • ActivTrak is productivity analytics: app and website activity, idle time and role-based classification, turned into utilization, focus, workload and, on higher plans, burnout and capacity reports.
  • It logs no keystrokes, takes no screenshots on standard plans and has no stealth mode.
  • Its weaknesses are annual-only contracts, six months to a year of history, the classification workload, no Linux agent and no time tracking.
  • Most buyers need Workforce Management ($15) or Productivity Optimization ($17) per user per month, on an annual contract.
  • It fits office and hybrid teams that want workload data without images; it is a poor fit for billing hours, Linux teams and investigations.

Frequently asked questions

Is ActivTrak worth it?

For office and hybrid teams that want to see where time goes, who is overloaded and how focus time is spent, without screenshots or keystrokes, yes, provided you can commit to an annual contract. If you need project hours, Linux coverage or monthly billing, another tool fits better.

Is ActivTrak safe and legal to use?

ActivTrak is a mainstream tool and monitoring company devices is legal in the US, but Connecticut, Delaware and New York require written notice to employees before monitoring. Tell staff what is recorded and how it will be used before installing the agent.

What are the main complaints about ActivTrak?

Annual-only contracts with a hard cancellation process, the price, short data history on the lower plans, the time needed to classify applications and websites, a complex interface, and the lack of Linux and time tracking.

Does ActivTrak record keystrokes?

No. ActivTrak does not log keystrokes on any plan. It records which applications and websites are active and whether the person is active or idle.

What is the best alternative to ActivTrak?

Insightful for the same analytics with screenshots on plan, 24 months of history and monthly billing on select plans; WorkTime for no images and unlimited history; Hubstaff or Time Doctor for time tracking and Linux; Teramind or Veriato for security-grade recording.
