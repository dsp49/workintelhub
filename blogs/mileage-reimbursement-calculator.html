Mileage Reimbursement Calculator: 2026 IRS Rates, State Law

P Pavan
November 12, 2026 · 11 min read

The IRS standard mileage rate for business use of a personal vehicle is 72.5 cents a mile for January 1 to June 30, 2026 and 76 cents a mile from July 1 to December 31, 2026, after a mid-year increase announced in July, only the second mid-year change in roughly a decade. The rate is the maximum an employer can reimburse tax-free per mile without substantiating actual costs; it is not a legal minimum, because federal law does not require mileage reimbursement at all. Three states do require it, several more require reimbursement of necessary business expenses generally, and the Fair Labor Standards Act steps in everywhere when unreimbursed driving pushes an hourly worker's pay below minimum wage.

The calculator below handles the 2026 split year, compares a company rate with the IRS rate to show the taxable excess, and runs the minimum-wage check. Below it: the 2026 rates with their IRS notices, the 2027 outlook, which states require reimbursement and what each says, the tax rules for accountable plans and fixed-and-variable-rate programs, what a compliant mileage log contains, and policy language. Mileage reimbursement calculator

IRS business rates: 72.5 cents (Notice 2026-10) for miles driven January 1 to June 30, 2026 and 76 cents (Announcement 2026-11) from July 1. Reimbursement up to the IRS rate under an accountable plan is tax-free; the excess is wages. The minimum-wage check applies 29 CFR 531.35: unreimbursed vehicle costs may not reduce pay below the minimum wage in any workweek. Not tax or legal advice.

For payroll, the July 1 split means a single 2026 expense report can carry two rates. Reimburse by trip date, not report date: a trip on June 28 submitted July 10 is reimbursed at 72.5 cents. Employers that paid a flat 72.5 cents all year have under-reimbursed second-half trips against the IRS benchmark, which is lawful federally but may matter in the states below.

Federal law: no mandate, but a minimum-wage floor

No federal statute requires an employer to reimburse employees for using their own cars. The Internal Revenue Code sets what can be reimbursed tax-free; it does not set what must be reimbursed. The floor comes from the Fair Labor Standards Act. Under 29 CFR 531.35, wages must be paid "free and clear," and where the employer requires the employee to bear the cost of tools of the trade, which the Department of Labor applies to personal vehicles used for the employer's benefit, the cost is treated as a kickback to the employer. A violation occurs in any workweek in which the unreimbursed cost brings the employee's pay below the minimum wage or cuts into overtime due.

This is the basis of the delivery-driver litigation of the past decade: pizza and restaurant drivers paid at or near minimum wage who were reimbursed at well below the IRS rate. Courts have split on whether the IRS rate is the measure of the vehicle cost or merely evidence of it (the Sixth Circuit in 2024 rejected both the IRS rate and a "reasonable approximation" standard and asked district courts to determine actual costs), but every court agrees that an employer paying minimum wage must cover the vehicle cost somehow. The calculator uses the IRS rate as a proxy, which is the conservative assumption. Salaried exempt employees are outside this rule; their protection, if any, is state law.

Only California, Illinois and Massachusetts name transportation or have case law squarely on mileage; the Iowa, Montana, New Hampshire, North Dakota and South Dakota statutes are general necessary-expense laws that reach mileage when driving is required. In the other states the FLSA floor is the only legal requirement, and reimbursement is a matter of policy and competition for drivers. New York, Pennsylvania and Washington have no general reimbursement statute, despite appearing on some vendor lists.

Tax treatment: accountable plans, FAVR and the excess

Mileage reimbursement is excluded from wages when paid under an accountable plan: the expense has a business connection, the employee substantiates it (date, destination, purpose, miles) within a reasonable time, and any excess advance is returned. Reimbursement at or below the IRS standard rate satisfies the substantiation requirement for the amount; the employee need not prove actual costs. Three situations create taxable wages:

  • Reimbursement above the IRS rate: the excess per mile is wages, subject to withholding, Social Security and Medicare, and reported in Box 1 of the W-2.
  • Flat allowances without substantiation: a $400 monthly car allowance with no mileage log is a non-accountable plan and is fully taxable, which is why allowances cost the employer 7.65% in payroll tax and the employee 20 to 30% in tax before they buy any gas.
  • Commuting: miles between home and the regular workplace are personal and never reimbursable tax-free; miles from home to a temporary work site, or between work sites, are business miles.

A fixed and variable rate (FAVR) plan pays a fixed monthly amount for depreciation, insurance and registration and a cents-per-mile variable amount for fuel and maintenance, both based on a standard automobile in the employee's area. It is tax-free under Revenue Procedure 2019-46 if the standard automobile's cost does not exceed the annual cap ($61,700 for 2026), the employee drives at least 5,000 business miles a year, and at least five employees are covered (not more than a majority of them managers). FAVR is more accurate than a flat per-mile rate for high-mileage field staff, and more administrative work. Employees have not been able to deduct unreimbursed employee business expenses since 2018, so whatever the employer does not reimburse is simply the employee's loss.

What a mileage log must contain

The IRS requires adequate records for each business use: the date, the destination, the business purpose, and the miles (odometer start and end, or a GPS-derived distance). A contemporaneous log, kept at or near the time of the trip, carries far more weight in an audit than a reconstruction. Acceptable forms range from a paper log in the glove box to a phone app that detects drives automatically; the automatic apps reduce under-reporting (employees forget short trips) and over-reporting (employees round up) at the same time. The log should also record the vehicle, the total miles for the year (for the business-use percentage if actual costs are ever claimed), and whether the trip began at home or the office. Keep logs with the payroll records for the retention period; see payroll record retention by state. The expense report template includes a mileage section with these fields.

Policy decisions and template language

  1. The rate. "Business mileage is reimbursed at the IRS standard mileage rate in effect on the date of travel." Pegging to the IRS rate avoids the taxable-excess problem and most state-law disputes; a lower rate is lawful federally but invites claims in California and Illinois.
  2. What counts. "Reimbursable miles are those driven for Company business beyond the employee's normal commute, measured from the office or, if the trip begins at home, the shorter of the distance from home or from the office." Commuting is excluded.
  3. Substantiation. "Employees must submit mileage within 30 days of travel, with the date, destination, purpose and miles for each trip, using [the expense system]." Illinois and Iowa both reference 30 days.
  4. Payment timing. "Approved mileage is paid in the next regular payroll" or within 30 days of submission.
  5. Insurance and eligibility. Employees who drive for work must hold a valid license and the state minimum insurance; the Company may require proof annually.
  6. Allowances. If a car allowance is paid, state that it is taxable and whether mileage is reimbursed in addition.
  7. Minimum-wage safeguard. "For non-exempt employees, the Company will ensure that unreimbursed vehicle costs never reduce pay below the applicable minimum wage in any workweek."

Worked examples

Example 1: the 2026 split year. An account manager drives 1,240 business miles from January to June and 1,180 from July to December. Reimbursement at IRS rates: 1,240 × $0.725 = $899.00 plus 1,180 × $0.76 = $896.80, total $1,795.80, tax-free under an accountable plan. Had the company paid 72.5 cents all year, the second-half shortfall against the IRS rate would be $41.30, immaterial federally but a Labor Code 2802 question in California if the employee's actual costs exceeded the reimbursement.

Example 2: a rate above the IRS rate. The same 2,420 miles reimbursed at a flat 80 cents: $1,936.00. The excess over the IRS benchmark, $140.20, is taxable wages: about $10.73 in employee FICA, the same for the employer, plus income tax withholding.

Example 3: the minimum-wage floor. A delivery driver earns $9.00 an hour for 30 hours ($270.00) and drives 100 unreimbursed miles in the week. At the IRS proxy of 72.5 cents the vehicle cost is $72.50, leaving $197.50, or $6.58 an hour, below the $7.25 federal minimum. The employer owes at least $20.00 more for the week (30 × $7.25 = $217.50 less $197.50). At 60 miles the net is $226.50, or $7.55 an hour, and the week is compliant under federal law, though not under a higher state or city minimum.

Example 4: the allowance trap. A sales representative receives a $500 monthly car allowance with no log. The full $6,000 a year is wages: the employer pays about $459 in FICA, the employee loses perhaps $1,800 to income and payroll tax. The same employee driving 9,000 business miles a year would receive about $6,700 tax-free under a logged IRS-rate plan. The allowance is simpler and costs both parties more.

Key takeaways

  • The 2026 IRS business mileage rate is 72.5 cents for January 1 to June 30 (Notice 2026-10) and 76 cents for July 1 to December 31 (Announcement 2026-11); medical and moving 20.5 then 23.5 cents; charitable 14 cents. The 2027 rate is expected in late December 2026.
  • Federal law does not require mileage reimbursement; the IRS rate is the tax-free ceiling, not a minimum. The FLSA floor (29 CFR 531.35) requires that unreimbursed vehicle costs never push a non-exempt employee below minimum wage in any workweek.
  • California (Labor Code 2802), Illinois (820 ILCS 115/9.5) and Massachusetts (454 CMR 27.04) require reimbursement of transportation costs; Iowa, Montana, New Hampshire, North Dakota and South Dakota have general necessary-expense statutes that reach mileage; Minnesota and DC cover tools and uniforms only.
  • Reimbursement at or below the IRS rate under an accountable plan is tax-free with a log showing date, destination, purpose and miles; amounts above the rate and unsubstantiated flat allowances are taxable wages.
  • FAVR plans pay a fixed monthly amount plus a variable per-mile amount based on a standard vehicle costing no more than $61,700 in 2026, for employees driving at least 5,000 business miles a year.
  • Reimburse by trip date in the 2026 split year, exclude commuting, require submission within 30 days, and write a minimum-wage safeguard into the policy for hourly drivers.
  • Employees cannot deduct unreimbursed business mileage, so whatever the employer does not pay is the employee's loss.

Frequently asked questions

What is the IRS mileage rate for 2026?

72.5 cents a mile for business driving from January 1 to June 30, 2026 (Notice 2026-10) and 76 cents from July 1 to December 31, 2026 (Announcement 2026-11). Medical and Armed Forces moving mileage is 20.5 cents then 23.5 cents; charitable mileage is 14 cents all year. The 2025 business rate was 70 cents.

Are employers required to reimburse mileage?

Not under federal law. California, Illinois and Massachusetts require reimbursement of work-related transportation costs, and Iowa, Montana, New Hampshire, North Dakota and South Dakota require reimbursement of necessary business expenses generally. Everywhere, the FLSA requires that unreimbursed vehicle costs not reduce an hourly employee's pay below minimum wage in any workweek.

How do you calculate mileage reimbursement?

Multiply business miles by the applicable rate. In 2026 use 72.5 cents for trips through June 30 and 76 cents for trips from July 1: 1,240 miles in the first half and 1,180 in the second is $899.00 + $896.80 = $1,795.80. Commuting miles between home and the regular workplace are excluded.

Is mileage reimbursement taxable?

Not if paid under an accountable plan at or below the IRS rate with a log showing date, destination, purpose and miles. Reimbursement above the IRS rate is taxable wages for the excess, and a flat car allowance with no substantiation is fully taxable, subject to withholding and payroll taxes.

Can an employer pay less than the IRS mileage rate?

Yes under federal law, as long as an hourly employee's pay net of vehicle costs stays above minimum wage in every workweek. In California, Illinois and Massachusetts the employer must cover the employee's actual necessary costs, and paying well below the IRS rate invites claims. Employees cannot deduct the shortfall on their own returns.

What is the 2027 IRS mileage rate?

Not yet announced as of November 2026. The IRS normally publishes the following year's rate in the second half of December. Given the mid-year increase to 76 cents in July 2026, the 2027 rate is likely to start at or above that level; check the IRS standard mileage rates page before January payroll.

What records are needed for mileage reimbursement?

For each trip: the date, the destination, the business purpose and the miles driven (odometer readings or a GPS distance), recorded at or near the time of the trip. Keep the vehicle identity and annual total miles as well. A contemporaneous log, on paper or in an app, is what the IRS and courts expect; reconstructions carry less weight.

What is a FAVR mileage plan?

A fixed and variable rate plan pays a fixed monthly amount covering depreciation, insurance and registration plus a cents-per-mile variable amount covering fuel and maintenance, both derived from a standard vehicle in the employee's area. It is tax-free under Revenue Procedure 2019-46 if the standard vehicle costs no more than $61,700 (2026), the employee drives at least 5,000 business miles a year and at least five employees participate.
