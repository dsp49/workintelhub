Visible vs Stealth Employee Monitoring: What Employers Should Choose

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

Visible employee monitoring runs an agent that employees know about and can usually see: an icon, a dashboard of their own data, a written policy they acknowledged. Stealth monitoring hides the agent from the person using the device. Employers should choose visible monitoring. Covert monitoring cannot satisfy the written notice laws in Connecticut, Delaware, Maine and New York, it is usually discovered, and once it is, every number it produced is treated as suspect.

Several vendors still sell a stealth mode, so the choice is real. This page explains what each mode does, which tools offer which, what the notice laws require, and the narrow situation in which hidden monitoring is even arguable. It is not legal advice; confirm your obligations with counsel in every state where you employ people.

The short answer

Choose visible. Tell people in writing what is collected, collect an acknowledgment, and let them see their own data.

Stealth does not remove the notice duty. In Connecticut, Delaware, Maine and New York the law requires employees to be told, and a hidden agent converts a policy decision into a penalty.

Hidden monitoring belongs only to defined investigations run with counsel, never to everyday management.

Vendors use different words for the same idea: stealth, silent, hidden. What matters legally is not whether an icon shows but whether employees were told, in the form the law in their state requires. A hidden agent with no notice is covert monitoring. In practice the two go together, which is why the safest design is visible on screen and in writing.

Which tools offer stealth, and which run visibly

From the October 2026 product research in our employee monitoring software reviews and Insightful alternatives guide:

  • Visible by design: ActivTrak (no stealth), Hubstaff (visible agent, no stealth mode), DeskTime (visible, with a private time switch).
  • Stealth or silent mode available: Insightful (on company-owned devices; personal devices default to visible), Teramind (revealed, standard and stealth agents), Controlio, Monitask (stealth option on Pro), Veriato, Apploye (Power plan or a $2 add-on), Time Doctor (silent mode), and Time Champ (silent or visible, configurable).

A tool that offers stealth can still be run visibly, and most should be. When you evaluate one, check whether the visible mode keeps the employee dashboard, and whether employees can confirm which mode is on. Our what Insightful can see and what Hubstaff can see guides show the difference from the employee's side.

What the notice laws require

There is no federal statute requiring an employer to tell staff they are being monitored. Four states have a dedicated electronic monitoring notice law, and notice duties follow the employee's location, not the head office.

StateWhat notice is requiredPenalty
ConnecticutPrior written notice of the types of monitoring, plus a conspicuously posted notice$500 first, $1,000 second, $3,000 third and each after; Labor Commissioner
DelawareA daily electronic notice, or a one-time notice the employee acknowledges$100 per violation; civil claim in court
New YorkWritten notice on hiring, acknowledged by the employee, plus a posted notice$500 first, $1,000 second, $3,000 third and each after; Attorney General
MaineNotice to applicants during hiring, plus written notice to affected employees at least once a year$100 to $500 per violation; Department of Labor

Connecticut's law, General Statutes section 31-48d, was expanded on October 1, 2026 by Public Act 26-73: the posted notice must now name the specific locations where monitoring may occur, and new hires get a plain language statement of what may be monitored without prior notice. New York's Civil Rights Law section 52-c covers telephone, email and internet monitoring, with an exemption only for processes run solely for system maintenance or protection. Maine's law, in force since July 14, 2026, reaches cameras, GPS, keystroke logging and biometrics, and lets an employee decline to install surveillance software on a personal device. The full rules are on our employee monitoring laws by state page.

Elsewhere, hidden monitoring of company equipment is not usually unlawful in itself, but it remains a poor idea. Federal wiretap law makes consent far safer ground than a business-use argument made after the fact, about a dozen states require all-party consent to record audio, and the National Labor Relations Act limits monitoring that chills discussion of working conditions.

The narrow case for covert monitoring

Connecticut allows monitoring without prior notice where the employer has reasonable grounds to believe employees are engaged in unlawful conduct, conduct that violates the legal rights of the employer or other employees, or conduct creating a hostile workplace, and the monitoring may produce evidence of it. That is a specific suspicion about identified conduct, not a standing justification for a hidden agent on every laptop.

Outside that kind of case, covert monitoring sits in a different legal category and belongs only to defined investigations run with counsel: a named concern, a named person or system, a start and an end date, and legal sign-off before anything is installed. It is not a setting to choose when buying software for everyday management.

Why stealth is usually discovered

Employees can nearly always tell. Some stealth products hide from the taskbar and the process list, but agents still leave traces: installed programs, a device management profile in system settings, and on macOS anything that captures the screen or reads keystrokes must appear in the Screen Recording or Input Monitoring permission list. Our guide to detecting employee monitoring walks through the checks employees run.

The discovery costs more than the data was worth. Pew Research Center found in April 2023 that 81% of US adults expect workers to feel inappropriately watched by AI monitoring of how they do their jobs, even when it is disclosed. Undisclosed monitoring discovered later damages a team far more than disclosed monitoring does.

How to run monitoring visibly

  1. Write the notice before choosing software. One page: what is collected, what is not, who can see it, how long it is kept. Our guide to writing a monitoring policy employees will sign has the structure.
  2. Collect an acknowledgment. Delaware and New York require it; everywhere else it turns a disputed fact into a dated document. The consent form has the wording.
  3. Post it where the work happens, and in Connecticut name the monitored locations.
  4. Put Maine's annual notice on the calendar if anyone works there.
  5. Run the agent in visible mode with self-access on. If you are moving from stealth to visible, tell staff what changed and why.
  6. Collect less. Our employee monitoring features checklist shows which features you can leave off.

Key takeaways

  • Visible monitoring runs an agent employees know about; stealth hides it. Employers should choose visible.
  • Connecticut, Delaware, Maine and New York require notice of electronic monitoring, and hidden monitoring without notice cannot satisfy it.
  • Notice duties follow the employee's location, so a distributed team inherits the strictest rule that applies to anyone on it.
  • Connecticut's no-notice exception covers a specific suspicion of unlawful or rights-violating conduct, not general monitoring.
  • ActivTrak, Hubstaff and DeskTime run visibly; Insightful, Teramind, Controlio, Monitask, Veriato and others offer stealth or silent modes.
  • Stealth agents are usually discovered through process lists, management profiles and macOS permissions, and discovery costs more than the data was worth.

Frequently asked questions

Is stealth employee monitoring legal?

Connecticut, Delaware, Maine and New York require notice of electronic monitoring, so a hidden agent with no notice breaks those laws, subject to Connecticut's narrow exception for specific suspected misconduct. Elsewhere, covert monitoring of company equipment is not usually unlawful in itself, but wiretap, audio consent and labor laws still apply, and it is a poor idea.

What is the difference between visible and stealth monitoring?

A visible agent shows on the device and employees know it runs, usually with access to their own data. A stealth agent hides from the taskbar and sometimes the process list, and often removes the employee's dashboard.

Which employee monitoring software has a stealth mode?

In October 2026, Insightful (company-owned devices), Teramind, Controlio, Monitask, Veriato and Apploye offered stealth modes, and Time Doctor and Time Champ had silent options. ActivTrak, Hubstaff and DeskTime run visibly.

Do I have to tell employees I am monitoring them?

In Connecticut, Delaware, Maine and New York, yes, in writing, and Delaware and New York require the employee to acknowledge it. Elsewhere there is no general notice statute, but written notice with an acknowledgment is the safer and fairer practice.

Can employees tell if stealth monitoring is installed?

Usually. Agents appear in process lists and installed programs, device management profiles appear in system settings, and on macOS screen capture and keystroke reading require visible permission entries.

When is covert monitoring justified?

Only in a defined investigation run with counsel. Connecticut allows monitoring without prior notice where there are reasonable grounds to believe specific unlawful or rights-violating conduct is occurring and monitoring may produce evidence of it.
