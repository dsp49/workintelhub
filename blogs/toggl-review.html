Toggl Track Review (2026): Strengths, Limits and Who It Fits

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Toggl Track is a time tracker built around one idea: tracking fails when people stop doing it, so make it the easiest thing in the working day. It is a one-click timer with projects, clients and reports, a private desktop timeline, calendar integration and a Pomodoro mode, and a written policy against monitoring employees. It is strongest for individuals and teams that bill or budget by the hour and want honest data, and weakest for anyone who needs invoices, attendance or payroll from the same tool.

This review is built from Toggl's published pricing and documentation, read in October 2026, its community forum, and recurring themes in public reviews. We have not run Toggl through a billing cycle ourselves, so the verdicts below are about what the product does and costs, not a scored hands-on test. Where Toggl documents its own behavior, that is what we cite.

Verdict

Buy it if you want a tracker your team will actually use, bill clients or budget projects by the hour, and want no screenshots or activity scores anywhere near your staff.

Skip it if you need invoices, a time clock, overtime rules, GPS or payroll, or if approvals are a must and $16 per user is more than the job is worth.

Expect to pay $9 per user a month on annual billing for billable rates, or $16 for approvals and profitability; small teams can start free.

What Toggl Track is

Toggl Track runs on the web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS and Android, with browser extensions. People start a timer against a project and client, or fill in time afterward, and reports total the hours. A desktop timeline records which applications were open, visible only to the user, so gaps can be filled in later without anyone else seeing the raw record.

Starter adds billable rates, team reports and project estimates. Premium adds timesheet approvals, labor cost and profitability, fixed-fee projects, Jira and Asana integrations and SSO. On September 15, 2026 Toggl relaunched as Toggl 2.0 with an AI assistant, an MCP connector and new plans; existing Track workspaces were grandfathered, which is why free seat counts and Premium prices now differ between accounts.

Where Toggl Track is strong

  • Adoption. One click to start, a timeline that fills the gaps privately, and nothing to resent. Our time tracking software review places it first for exactly this reason: the tool people keep using produces the data you can trust.
  • No monitoring, in writing. Toggl's FAQ states that it does not monitor employees, take screenshots or record keystrokes, browsing or location. That makes the rollout conversation short.
  • Platform coverage. Every desktop platform including Linux, both mobile platforms and browser extensions.
  • Profitability on Premium. Labor cost, profitability and fixed-fee projects answer whether a client or project earned money.
  • Leaving is easy. Toggl imports and exports CSV cleanly, and Clockify has an official importer for Toggl's Detailed Report.
  • Low-risk start. A free plan for small teams and a 30-day trial on paid plans.

Where it falls short

  • Approvals cost $16. Timesheet approvals, labor cost and profitability are on Premium. For a 20-person team that is $3,840 a year, against about $1,318 for Clockify Standard, which also invoices.
  • No invoicing. Toggl reports billable amounts but does not issue invoices or take payments, so agencies re-key hours into an accounting tool.
  • No attendance or payroll. There is no time clock, overtime rules, schedules, GPS, kiosk or payroll export. Hourly teams discover this after rollout.
  • A smaller free plan. New accounts get 3 free seats with limited report views since the relaunch; older workspaces keep 5.
  • Timers and sync. Reviews describe timers that will not stop and mobile and desktop entries that fail to sync, which leave inflated or duplicated entries to clean up.
  • Budgets out of sight. Project estimates live in Toggl's dashboards, which the people doing the work rarely open, so overruns tend to surface at month end.

How employees experience it

For staff, Toggl is a timer they start and stop and a timeline only they can see. There are no screenshots, no activity percentage and no location record, so the usual objection to time tracking, that it is surveillance, does not apply. The friction that remains is the one every timer has: remembering to start it and stop it. The private timeline helps people reconstruct a forgotten block; teams whose data still decays may be better served by automatic capture, which our guide to manual versus automatic time tracking compares.

Alternatives worth comparing

If the price is the issue, Clockify does what Toggl Starter does for $3.99 and adds approvals and invoicing at $5.49. If invoicing is the issue, Harvest turns time into invoices and payments on every plan, including the free one. If the free plan shrank on you, TimeCamp has no user cap. If you need a time clock, Jibble is free for unlimited users. If nobody remembers the timer, Timely drafts the timesheet privately. Our guide to alternatives to Toggl Track sorts eleven tools by the reason you are leaving and explains how to export your data, and the Toggl vs Clockify comparison covers the most common switch.

Key takeaways

  • Toggl Track is a one-click timer with projects, clients, reports and a private desktop timeline, and a written policy against monitoring employees.
  • Its strengths are adoption, no screenshots or activity scoring, coverage of every platform including Linux, and profitability reports on Premium.
  • Its weaknesses are approvals at $16 per user, no invoicing, no attendance or payroll, and a free plan cut to 3 seats for new accounts.
  • Starter costs $9 and Premium $16 per user per month on annual billing, as read in October 2026.
  • It fits freelancers, consultants and small teams that bill or budget by the hour; it is a poor fit for hourly crews and agencies that need invoices.

Frequently asked questions

Is Toggl Track worth it?

For individuals and teams that need tracking people will keep doing and bill or budget elsewhere, yes. If you need approvals on a budget, invoicing, attendance or payroll, Clockify, Harvest or Jibble cover those for less.

Does Toggl Track monitor employees?

No. Toggl's pricing FAQ states that it does not monitor employees, take screenshots or record keystrokes, browsing history or location. The desktop timeline is visible only to the user.

What are the main complaints about Toggl Track?

That approvals and profitability need Premium at $16, that there is no invoicing or payroll, that the free plan shrank to 3 seats for new accounts, and that timers sometimes keep running or fail to sync between devices.

Is Toggl Track good for freelancers?

Yes for tracking and billable amounts: the free plan or Starter at $9 covers a single freelancer. Toggl does not issue invoices, so freelancers export the hours to an invoicing or accounting tool.

What is the best alternative to Toggl Track?

Clockify for most teams, with approvals and invoicing at $5.49 and an official Toggl importer. Harvest if you need invoices, TimeCamp if you need a free plan for more people, and Timely if nobody remembers to start timers.
