HR Software Requirements Checklist (Free RFP Template)

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 8 min read

An HR software requirements checklist lists what the system must do, module by module, and pairs each requirement with a question the vendor answers in writing. Use the tables below as your RFP: mark each line must-have, should-have or not needed, delete what does not apply, and send the rest to two or three vendors with the pricing and contract questions at the end.

The checklist follows the evaluation in our HR software buyer's guide, which puts the parts most likely to fail first: payroll accuracy, data export, contract terms and whether an employee outside HR can use the product without help. Feature lists converge across vendors. The answers to these questions do not.

How to use this checklist

  1. Write down the ten things you do most. Hire, onboard, change pay, approve time off, run payroll, handle a leave, run a review, offboard, answer an employee's question, produce a headcount report. Every requirement below should trace back to one of them.
  2. Mark each line. Must-have (a no rules the vendor out), should-have (scored), or not needed (deleted). Keep must-haves to the few that are truly disqualifying.
  3. Decide what kind of product you are buying first. Payroll service, HRIS, HCM suite, PEO or employer of record change which sections apply. HRIS vs HRMS vs HCM explains the difference.
  4. Send the questions in writing and keep the replies. A written answer can be attached to the contract; a demo answer cannot.
  5. Prove the must-haves on your own data. The demo tests at the end of this page are where most of the shortlist falls away.

Core records and self-service

RequirementQuestion to ask the vendorPriority
Employee record with job, pay and historyWhich fields are standard, which can we add, and is every change to pay or job dated and logged with who made it?
Org chart and reporting linesDoes the org chart update from the record, and can it show more than one entity or location?
Document storage and e-signatureAre e-signature and document templates included in the plan you are quoting, or an add-on?
Employee self-serviceCan an employee update an address, bank details and emergency contact without HR, and which changes need approval?
Roles and permissionsCan a manager see pay for direct reports only? Can we restrict sensitive fields by role?
Mobile accessWhich tasks can employees and managers complete in the mobile app, and which only on the web?

Hiring, onboarding and offboarding

RequirementQuestion to ask the vendorPriority
Applicant trackingIs a hiring pipeline included, and does a hired candidate become an employee record without re-keying?
Onboarding checklistsCan we build task lists by role and location with owners and due dates, and send forms before day one?
New hire formsWhich federal and state forms are built in, and who keeps them current when a form changes?
Form I-9 trackingDoes the system track Section 2 against the three-business-day deadline, and store documents separately from the personnel file?
New hire reportingDoes payroll file the state new hire report, and for which states is that switched on by default?
OffboardingCan termination trigger final pay, access removal, equipment return and benefits notices as one workflow?

The deadlines behind those rows are the employer's, whichever product runs them: Form I-9 Section 2 within three business days of the start date under USCIS rules, and the state new hire report within 20 days under 42 U.S.C. 653a, with some states requiring it sooner. Our new hire forms checklist, onboarding checklist and offboarding checklist show what the system needs to hold at each stage.

Time off, leave and attendance

RequirementQuestion to ask the vendorPriority
PTO accrual policiesCan we set accrual by hours worked, by pay period and by tenure, with caps and carryover, and run several policies at once?
Requests and approvalsDo requests route to the manager and a backup, and does the balance update before and after approval?
Leave of absenceDoes the system track FMLA eligibility and the five-business-day notice deadlines, or only record the leave?
Time clock and timesheetsIs time tracking included or an add-on, and does it send approved hours to payroll without an export?
SchedulingIf we schedule shifts, are availability, swaps and break rules handled here or in a separate tool?

The FMLA eligibility notice is due within five business days of a leave request, and the designation notice within five business days of having enough information to decide, under 29 CFR 825.300; FMLA forms covers them. To test accrual setup, build one of your real policies in the PTO accrual calculator first and check the vendor's balance against it.

Payroll and benefits

RequirementQuestion to ask the vendorPriority
Payroll modelIs payroll native, run by a partner through a connector, or not offered? Who do we call when a paycheck is wrong?
Tax filing and liabilityDo you file under our tax accounts or yours? If you make a filing error, what does your accuracy guarantee cover?
Multi-state and internationalWhich states and countries can you pay employees in today, and through your own entity or a partner?
Overtime, garnishments and deductionsHow are daily overtime, the regular rate with bonuses, and garnishments configured, and who sets them up?
Final payCan the system produce a final paycheck off-cycle to meet each state's deadline?
Benefits administrationIs benefits enrollment included, and do you broker plans or connect to our existing broker and carriers?

Final pay deadlines vary by state; final paycheck laws by state lists them. If the vendor is a PEO, add one more question: what leaving involves, since exiting a PEO means new tax accounts and new benefits plans.

Performance, reporting and integrations

RequirementQuestion to ask the vendorPriority
Performance reviewsWhich plan includes reviews, goals and one-on-ones, and can we use our own review form?
Compensation planningIs compensation planning a separate module, and does it include salary benchmarks?
Standard reportsShow us headcount, turnover, time-off liability and payroll cost reports built from our data, not a sample account.
Custom reportingCan HR build a report without support, and can it be scheduled and exported?
IntegrationsWhich integrations with our accounting, identity, scheduling and benefits systems are native, and which need middleware or an API project?

Security, data and exit

RequirementQuestion to ask the vendorPriority
Hosting and subprocessorsWhere is our data hosted, and which subprocessors can access employee records?
Vendor staff accessCan your staff view our employee records, under what process, and is that access logged?
Single sign-on and access controlIs SSO included in the quoted plan, and can we enforce multi-factor sign-in?
Data exportIn what format can we export records, documents, payroll history and time-off balances ourselves, without a support ticket?
Retention after cancellationWhat happens to our data 30 and 90 days after we cancel, and can we get written confirmation of deletion?

Pricing and contract questions

Most RFPs stop at features. These are the questions our HR platform comparison found decide more of the outcome than any feature, and the ones vendors least often volunteer.

Ask forWhy it matters
Full price at our headcount with the modules we will useAdd-ons for payroll, time and benefits change the total more than the tier
How employees are countedEvery employee on payroll, or only people who log in
Implementation fee and what it includesRarely on the pricing page; who does data migration and configuration
Contract term and renewal capMulti-year terms with renewal increases of 5% to 15% are commonly reported
Price as headcount crosses band boundariesPer-employee rates and minimums can change at set sizes
Introductory discounts and when they expireThe first-year price may not be the renewal price
Notice period and exit termsAuto-renewal dates are easy to miss

Scoring the replies

Score in two passes. First, any vendor that answers no to a must-have is out, whatever else it offers. Second, score each should-have from 0 (not offered) to 3 (included in the quoted plan and shown on our data), and multiply by a weight you set before the replies arrive: payroll and compliance usually weigh most, then core records and time off, then reporting and talent. Setting the weights first stops a strong demo from rewriting the priorities.

Score an answer of "on the roadmap" as 0, and a feature that needs an add-on at the add-on's price, not at the base plan's.

Five tests before you sign

  1. Load a real month. Your own employees, anonymized if needed, with real pay changes, time-off requests and a new hire. A demo on sample data proves that sample data works.
  2. Run one payroll in parallel. Calculate a real pay period in both systems and reconcile to the cent. Differences usually turn up in overtime, garnishments and state tax setup.
  3. Export everything. Records, documents, payroll history and balances, during the trial. If export needs a support ticket, plan the exit before the entry.
  4. Have an employee use it. Someone outside HR requests time off, updates an address and finds a pay stub without help.
  5. Call two references at your size. Not only the vendor's list. Ask what surprised them, what the renewal did, and what they would put in the contract now.

If you have not yet narrowed the field, start with the ten HR systems compared by the job each is built for, then send this checklist to the two or three that fit.

Key takeaways

  • Pair every requirement with a written question to the vendor, and mark each must-have, should-have or not needed.
  • Decide whether you are buying payroll, an HRIS, an HCM suite, a PEO or an employer of record before writing the RFP.
  • Ask who files payroll tax and under whose accounts; a software vendor files under yours, a PEO under its own.
  • Contract questions (full price at your headcount, implementation fee, renewal cap, how employees are counted) decide more than features.
  • Test data export, run one payroll in parallel and have a non-HR employee use the product before signing.
  • Compliance deadlines such as Form I-9 within three business days and new hire reports within 20 days stay with the employer.

Frequently asked questions

What should be in an HR software RFP?

A short company overview and scope, a requirements table by module with a question for each line, a pricing form asking for the full price at your headcount with the modules you will use, implementation and contract questions, security and data export questions, a request for two references at your size, and a timeline.

What are the key requirements for HR software?

Most companies need a reliable employee record with self-service, onboarding, time off and approvals, and either native payroll or a clean integration with their payroll provider. Beyond that, requirements depend on size: reporting and performance for mid-size teams, multi-country payroll and planning for larger ones.

How many vendors should get the RFP?

Two or three that fit your size and type of product. Sending it to ten produces more replies than anyone can test properly. Shortlist first by what each product is built for, then send the checklist.

What questions should I ask an HR software vendor about pricing?

Ask for the full price at your headcount with the modules you will use, how employees are counted, the implementation fee and what it includes, the contract term and renewal cap, how the price changes as headcount grows, and when any introductory discount expires.

Does HR software handle compliance for us?

It surfaces deadlines; it does not meet them. Form I-9, new hire reporting, FMLA notices and final pay deadlines remain the employer's obligation with any product. A PEO takes on payroll tax filing under its own accounts, which shifts that part of the liability.

Is there a downloadable HR software requirements template?

The tables on this page are the template. Copy them into a document or spreadsheet, add a priority to each line, and delete what does not apply before sending them to vendors.
