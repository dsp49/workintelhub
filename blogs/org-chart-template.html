Org Chart Template: Structures, Examples and How to Build

P Pavan
November 13, 2026 · 10 min read

An organizational chart shows who reports to whom. It is the first document a new hire looks for, the one auditors ask for when they test who approves what, and the one that goes out of date fastest. A good org chart shows roles as well as names, makes dotted-line relationships explicit, has an owner and a revision date, and is built from the HR system rather than drawn by hand, so it can be regenerated after every reorganization.

This page has a builder that draws a chart from a simple list of names and managers, the five common structures with when each fits, guidance on span of control and layers, how to build a chart in Excel, Google Sheets, PowerPoint and Visio, the legal and compliance uses that make accuracy matter, and the mistakes to avoid. Five structures and when each fits

StructureHow it is organizedFitsWatch out for
FunctionalBy specialty: operations, finance, HR, sales, engineeringSingle-product companies and most firms under a few hundred peopleSilos; slow cross-functional decisions
DivisionalBy product, region or customer, each with its own functionsMulti-product or multi-region businessesDuplicated functions; inconsistent policies between divisions
MatrixDual reporting: to a functional manager and a project or product managerConsulting, engineering, project-based workConflicting priorities; needs clear decision rights (a RACI)
FlatFew layers, wide spans, small or no middle managementStartups and small teams with routine or self-directed workOverloaded leaders; unclear promotion paths
Network or team-basedTeams form around goals or customers and dissolve; the hierarchy is thinAgile product organizations; some professional firmsAccountability gaps; hard to show on a static chart

Most real organizations are hybrids: functional at the top, divisional in the field, matrixed for projects. Draw what actually exists; aspirational charts mislead new hires and auditors alike.

Span of control and layers

Span of control is the number of direct reports a manager has. There is no universal right number. Narrow spans (three to five) suit complex, non-routine work and new managers; wide spans (10 to 15 or more) work for routine, well-defined work with experienced staff and good systems, such as call centers, retail floors and warehouses. A span of one or two usually signals a layer that adds cost and delay without adding management.

The pressure is toward wider spans. Gartner predicted in October 2024 that through 2026, one in five organizations would use AI to flatten their structure and eliminate more than half of current middle-management positions, and many large employers removed manager layers in 2024 and 2025. Flattening works when the remaining managers have time to coach and the work is well defined; it fails when managers end up with 20 reports and no time for any of them, which shows up quickly in engagement and turnover data. The team capacity planning guide covers how to size teams, and the builder above reports average span and flags spans above 10.

Dotted lines, vacancies and contractors

  • Dotted lines show secondary reporting: a regional HR partner who reports to the CHRO but supports a division head, or a project manager in a matrix. Draw them, label them, and write down what the dotted-line manager decides (usually input on priorities and performance, not hiring or pay).
  • Vacancies should appear as open boxes with the title; they are part of the plan and the budget. Link to the requisition.
  • Contractors and agency staff can be shown in a different style, but be cautious: an org chart that shows a contractor reporting to a manager like an employee is evidence of control in a misclassification dispute.
  • Assistants and chiefs of staff are usually drawn to the side of the executive they support.

How to build one in Excel, Google Sheets, PowerPoint or Visio

  • Google Sheets: put names in column A and each person's manager in column B (optionally a tooltip in column C). Select the data, choose Insert, Chart, and pick Organizational chart. It redraws when the data changes, which makes it the quickest free option.
  • Excel, PowerPoint and Word: Insert, SmartArt, Hierarchy, then type names in the text pane; Tab demotes a box, Shift+Tab promotes it. Good for small charts; tedious above about 30 boxes.
  • Visio: the Organization Chart template and Data Visualizer can build a chart from an Excel table of employee and manager and refresh it when the table changes.
  • Lucidchart, Canva and similar: drag-and-drop templates with free tiers that limit the number of documents or objects; check the current limits.
  • HRIS: most HR systems generate a live chart from the reporting relationships already in the system. For anything over 50 people this is the only approach that stays accurate; the chart is a report, not a drawing. See best HR software.

Legal and compliance uses

  • Internal controls: auditors testing Sarbanes-Oxley section 404 controls use the org chart to confirm segregation of duties and who approves what.
  • Emergency action plans: OSHA's rule at 29 CFR 1910.38 requires the plan to name or list the job titles of the people employees should contact for information; the chart is often the source.
  • Harassment and complaint procedures: the Faragher and Ellerth defenses depend on a reporting channel employees can use, including one that bypasses the harasser's chain of command; the chart shows that alternative route.
  • EEO-1 reporting: employers with 100 or more employees report headcount by ten job categories; mapping titles on the chart to categories makes the filing consistent.
  • Litigation: reporting lines are evidence in discrimination, retaliation and joint-employer cases. Keep dated versions.

Org charts and AI agents

Microsoft's 2025 Work Trend Index (31,000 workers in 31 countries) argued that the org chart will give way to a "work chart" in which teams form around outcomes and include AI agents; 36% of leaders and 21% of employees expected to manage AI agents within five years. Whatever happens to the drawing, accountability still needs a human owner, so if agents perform work, the chart or the RACI should show the person accountable for each one.

Common mistakes

  • Out of date. Build it from the HR system and regenerate it monthly or after any change.
  • No owner or date. Put both in the footer.
  • Names only. Show titles, and ideally the team or function, so the chart survives turnover.
  • Hiding dotted lines. Matrix relationships that are not drawn are relationships nobody understands.
  • Showing compensation or performance data on a widely shared chart.
  • One giant page. For large organizations, publish a top-level chart and one per department.
  • Very wide spans with no team-lead layer, or layers of single-report managers.

Key takeaways

  • An org chart shows reporting lines; a good one shows titles, dotted-line relationships and vacancies, has an owner and a revision date, and is generated from the HR system rather than drawn by hand.
  • The five common structures are functional, divisional, matrix, flat and network; most organizations are hybrids, and the chart should show the real structure, not the intended one.
  • There is no ideal span of control: three to five suits complex work, 10 to 15 or more suits routine work; spans of one or two signal an unnecessary layer.
  • Gartner predicted that through 2026 one in five organizations would use AI to remove more than half of middle-management roles; flattening works only if remaining managers have time to manage.
  • Google Sheets builds a free org chart from two columns (name, manager); Excel and PowerPoint use SmartArt; Visio and HR systems generate charts from data and refresh them.
  • Org charts support SOX segregation-of-duties testing, OSHA emergency plans, harassment reporting channels and EEO-1 mapping, and are evidence in litigation, so keep dated versions.

Frequently asked questions

What is an org chart?

An organizational chart is a diagram of an organization's structure showing who reports to whom, usually as boxes for roles or people connected by lines from the top leader down. Good charts also show titles, dotted-line relationships and open positions.

How do I make an org chart in Excel?

Use Insert, SmartArt, Hierarchy, then type names in the text pane, pressing Tab to make someone a direct report and Shift+Tab to move them up. For larger charts, keep a table of employee and manager in Excel and use Visio's Data Visualizer or Google Sheets' organizational chart, which redraw from the data.

How do I make an org chart in Google Sheets?

Put each person's name in column A and their manager's name in column B, with the top person's manager left blank. Select the two columns, choose Insert, then Chart, and set the chart type to Organizational chart. The chart updates when you edit the data.

What are the different types of organizational structure?

Functional (by specialty), divisional (by product, region or customer), matrix (dual reporting to functional and project managers), flat (few layers and wide spans) and network or team-based (teams formed around goals). Most organizations combine several.

What is a good span of control?

It depends on the work. Three to five direct reports suits complex, non-routine work; 10 to 15 or more can work for routine, well-defined work with experienced staff. Very narrow spans of one or two usually add cost and delay without adding management.

What does a dotted line mean on an org chart?

A secondary or indirect reporting relationship, such as a regional HR partner who supports a division head but reports to the CHRO, or a project manager in a matrix. The solid line usually governs hiring, pay and performance; the dotted line usually governs day-to-day priorities. Write down which decisions each manager makes.

Should an org chart show names or job titles?

Both. Titles make the chart useful after people change roles, and names make it useful for finding people. For large organizations, show titles and team names on the top-level chart and names on department charts.

How often should an org chart be updated?

Whenever reporting lines change, and in any case monthly for a growing organization. The reliable approach is to generate the chart from the HR system, so it is accurate by default, and keep dated copies for audits and records.
