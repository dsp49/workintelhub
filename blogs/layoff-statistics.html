Layoff Statistics 2026: Job Cuts by Industry, AI and Trends

P Pavan
November 13, 2026 · 11 min read

US employers announced 617,189 job cuts in the first ten months of 2026, according to Challenger, Gray and Christmas, 44% fewer than the 1,099,500 announced in the same period of 2025 and the lowest year-to-date total since 2022. The 2025 spike was driven by federal government reductions; with those largely over, 2026 cuts have come mostly from technology, transportation and food companies, and for the first time artificial intelligence has been one of the two most cited reasons, behind only market and economic conditions. The broad labor market tells a calmer story: the Bureau of Labor Statistics layoff rate has held at about 1.0% a month, and unemployment was 4.1% in October 2026.

This page gathers the current figures from Challenger, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, Layoffs.fyi, the Office of Personnel Management, WARN notice trackers and worker surveys, with dates and definitions, and explains why they differ. For the legal side of a reduction in force, see WARN Act notice requirements by state and severance pay.

Headline figures

  • 617,189 announced job cuts, January to October 2026, down 44% from 2025 (Challenger, November 5, 2026).
  • 43,994 cuts announced in October 2026, down 71% from October 2025's 153,074.
  • 121,401 2026 cuts attributed to artificial intelligence, about 20% of the total; 193,226 since Challenger began tracking the reason in 2023.
  • 171,501 technology job cuts in 2026, 28% of the total and up 21% on 2025.
  • 1,206,374 announced cuts in all of 2025, up 58% on 2024 and the highest since 2020.
  • 1.0%: BLS layoffs and discharges rate, September 2026 (1.6 million people).
  • 4.1%: unemployment rate, October 2026.
  • 10.3%: decline in the civilian federal workforce during 2025.

Announced job cuts in 2026

Challenger, Gray and Christmas counts job cuts announced by US employers each month, from company statements, filings and media reports. Its October 2026 report, published November 5, 2026:

  • October cuts: 43,994, up 2% from September's 43,281 and down 71% from October 2025, which had been the highest October since 2003.
  • Year to date: 617,189, against 1,099,500 in January to October 2025; 22 of 30 industries were below their 2025 pace.
  • Hiring plans: 655,304 year to date, up 34%; seasonal hiring plans of 470,400, up 26%, including 300,000 at Amazon and 100,000 at UPS; October hiring announcements of 444,692 were the highest for an October since at least 2009.
  • Not counted: Microsoft's offer of voluntary exits to about 60,000 employees, because voluntary programs are not announced cuts.

For comparison, 2025 ended at 1,206,374 cuts, up 58% from 761,358 in 2024 and the highest since 2020's 2,304,755, with 259,948 in the fourth quarter; 2025 hiring plans of 507,647 were down 34%.

By reason, including AI

  • Market and economic conditions: 130,531 cuts in 2026, the top reason (16,407 in October, 37% of the month).
  • Artificial intelligence: 121,401 in 2026, about 20% of all cuts, against 54,836 in all of 2025 and a further 20,219 in 2025 attributed to "technological update" that Challenger said may also be AI. October's 1,265 was the lowest monthly AI figure of 2026.
  • Closings: 109,342. Restructuring: 85,391. Demand downturn: 21,347.
  • In 2025, the top reasons were Department of Government Efficiency actions (293,753, plus 20,976 in downstream cuts), market and economic conditions (253,206), closings (191,480) and restructuring (133,611); tariffs were cited for 7,908.

The AI figure counts cuts where the employer named AI as a reason; it is a measure of what companies say, not an estimate of jobs displaced by automation, and it undercounts roles eliminated quietly through attrition. New York and, from October 1, 2026, Connecticut now require employers to state in WARN filings whether AI played a role, which should improve the data; see the jobs most exposed to AI page for the exposure research.

Tech layoffs

  • Layoffs.fyi counted 122,606 tech employees laid off at 278 companies in 2025.
  • By September 10, 2026 it had counted 128,536 at 299 companies, already above the 2025 total, with projections near 210,000 for the year; check the live tracker for the current count.
  • Challenger's technology category (171,501 year to date) is larger because it includes telecommunications and electronics and counts announced plans rather than confirmed layoffs.

What BLS data shows

The Bureau of Labor Statistics' Job Openings and Labor Turnover Survey measures actual separations, not announcements.

  • September 2026 (released November 3, 2026): layoffs and discharges 1.6 million, a rate of 1.0%; job openings 7.5 million (4.5%); hires 5.4 million (3.4%); quits 3.4 million (2.1%).
  • Earlier 2026: 1.7 million (1.1%) in May, 1.8 million (1.1%) in June, 1.7 million (1.0%) in July.
  • 2025 annual: 21.2 million layoffs and discharges, an average monthly rate of 1.1%.
  • Employment Situation, October 2026 (released November 6, 2026): unemployment rate 4.1%, 7.1 million unemployed, payrolls up 165,000; the rate has been between 4.1% and 4.3% since March 2026.

Announced cuts of 600,000 sit beside 16 to 18 million actual layoffs and discharges a year because JOLTS counts every involuntary separation, including routine dismissals at small businesses that never make an announcement. The turnover rate calculator uses the JOLTS rates as benchmarks.

Federal workforce reductions

  • The civilian federal workforce fell 10.3% during 2025, from 2,312,301 in December 2024 to 2,074,649 in December 2025, a loss of 237,652; USAID shrank 92.4% and the Department of Education 42.6% (Pew Research Center, March 13, 2026, from OPM data).
  • OPM reported a headcount of 2,035,344 on January 20, 2026, down 264,228 (12%) since September 2024.
  • More than 150,000 employees took the deferred resignation offer in fiscal 2025.
  • A GAO review (GAO-26-108916, July 2026) of OPM's own staff found a 35% reduction from December 2024 to March 2026, 59% of it through deferred resignation and 10% through reductions in force.

WARN notices

LayoffAlert counted 3,191 state WARN notices covering 284,035 workers in 44 states through August 25, 2026, against 3,356 notices and 301,352 workers at the same point in 2025. WARN notices cover only events large enough to trigger federal or state notice laws, so they are a leading indicator of large layoffs rather than a total.

Worker sentiment

  • 32% of workers feared losing their job in 2026, 42% expected layoffs at their own company, and 65% were not job hunting (MyPerfectResume, February 2026), the pattern described as the "Great Stay."
  • 51% worried about losing their job to AI in 2026 (Resume Now, December 2025, 1,006 workers).
  • 23% were anxious about being laid off in 2026 (iHire, early 2026, 589 respondents).
  • The New York Fed's Survey of Consumer Expectations put the mean perceived probability of losing one's job in the next 12 months at 15.1% in May 2026 (secondary citation).

What happens after layoffs

  • In Leadership IQ's study of 4,172 layoff survivors at 318 companies (2008, still the most cited), 74% said their own productivity declined, 69% said quality declined and 77% saw more errors.
  • Visier found boomerang employees, rehired former staff, made up 28% of new hires in early 2022, typically returning within about a year, which is a reason to manage exits well.
  • For the cost of the departures themselves, see the turnover statistics; for retention after a reduction, see retention strategies.

How to read layoff numbers

  1. Announced is not executed. Challenger counts plans, which may be spread over years, reversed or met by attrition.
  2. Announced is not total. BLS layoffs and discharges are 25 to 30 times larger because they include every involuntary separation.
  3. Trackers differ by scope. Layoffs.fyi counts tech companies from public reports; Challenger's technology category is broader.
  4. Reasons are self-reported. "AI" and "restructuring" are what employers say.
  5. Federal data lags. OPM headcounts are published with a delay of several months.

Key takeaways

  • Challenger counted 617,189 announced US job cuts in January to October 2026, down 44% from 1,099,500 in the same period of 2025 and the lowest since 2022; 2025 ended at 1,206,374, the most since 2020.
  • Technology led 2026 cuts with 171,501 (28%, up 21%); transportation (+188%) and food (+43%) rose; government (-93%), retail (-80%) and warehousing (-77%) fell sharply after 2025's spike.
  • Artificial intelligence was cited for 121,401 cuts in 2026, about a fifth of the total and more than double 2025's 54,836; 193,226 AI-attributed cuts since 2023.
  • Actual layoffs per BLS stayed steady: 1.6 million (1.0%) in September 2026; unemployment was 4.1% in October 2026, in a 4.1% to 4.3% band since March.
  • The federal civilian workforce fell 10.3% in 2025 (237,652 positions), with more than 150,000 deferred resignations.
  • Hiring plans rose 34% year to date and seasonal hiring 26%, so 2026 is a year of targeted cuts rather than broad contraction.
  • About a third of workers fear job loss and half worry about AI, while most are staying put; survivor productivity falls after layoffs, and boomerang hires are common.

Frequently asked questions

How many layoffs have there been in 2026?

Employers announced 617,189 job cuts from January to October 2026, according to Challenger, Gray and Christmas, down 44% from the same period of 2025. BLS data, which counts all involuntary separations rather than announcements, showed about 1.6 to 1.8 million layoffs and discharges a month in 2026, a rate of about 1.0%.

Which industries have the most layoffs in 2026?

Technology, with 171,501 announced cuts through October 2026 (28% of the total), followed by transportation (44,838), food (39,370), health care (37,737), services (35,542) and consumer products (34,324). Government, retail and warehousing cuts fell 77% to 93% from their 2025 levels.

How many layoffs are caused by AI?

Employers named artificial intelligence as a reason for 121,401 announced job cuts in the first ten months of 2026, about 20% of all cuts, and 193,226 since Challenger started tracking it in 2023. The figure reflects what employers say, not an estimate of jobs displaced by automation.

How many tech layoffs were there in 2025 and 2026?

Layoffs.fyi counted 122,606 tech employees laid off at 278 companies in 2025 and 128,536 at 299 companies by September 10, 2026. Challenger's broader technology category recorded 154,445 cuts in 2025 and 171,501 in January to October 2026.

Are layoffs increasing or decreasing in 2026?

Announced layoffs are down sharply from 2025's federal-driven peak: October 2026 cuts were 71% below October 2025, and year-to-date cuts are the lowest since 2022. The BLS layoff rate has been flat at about 1.0% a month, and hiring plans are up 34%.

How many federal employees were laid off?

The civilian federal workforce fell by 237,652 (10.3%) during 2025, from about 2.31 million to 2.07 million, and by 264,228 between September 2024 and January 2026. More than 150,000 left through deferred resignation; reductions in force were a smaller share.

What is the difference between Challenger and BLS layoff numbers?

Challenger counts job cuts that employers publicly announce, which may be carried out over months or years. BLS's JOLTS survey counts actual layoffs and discharges at a sample of employers, including small, unannounced dismissals, so its totals are 25 to 30 times larger.

How many workers are worried about layoffs?

About a third: 32% feared losing their job in 2026 and 42% expected layoffs at their company (MyPerfectResume, February 2026); 51% worried about losing their job to AI (Resume Now). The New York Fed's survey put the perceived probability of job loss within a year at about 15%.
