Can My Employer Track My Laptop Location?

A Admin
August 21, 2026 · 8 min read

On a laptop your employer owns, the short answer is yes. Not always precisely, not always continuously, and not always deliberately, but the capability is there in software most companies already run. On a laptop you own, the answer changes sharply, and so does the law.

This page explains what a work laptop can actually report about where it is, which of those methods are accurate and which are not, what US law allows, and what you can reasonably ask or do about it. It is written for the person on the other end of the question as much as for the employer asking it.

How a work laptop reports its location

Four mechanisms account for nearly all of it, and they differ enormously in precision.

IP address geolocation. Every connection reveals an IP address, and an IP maps to an approximate area. This needs no software at all: a VPN login, an email header, or a login record already carries it. Accuracy is typically city level and frequently wrong, because the address may belong to an internet provider's hub some distance from you.

Device management enrolment. Most company laptops are enrolled in a device management system such as Microsoft Intune or Jamf. These platforms exist to push software, enforce disk encryption, and wipe a lost machine, and locating the device is part of that remit. Location reporting is usually available to an administrator and can often be scheduled or disabled outside working hours.

Operating system find my device features. Windows and macOS both ship a locate function tied to the account that owns the machine. On a corporate device, that account is the company. This is the same feature that recovers a stolen laptop, and it does not distinguish between a thief and an employee.

Wi-Fi network positioning. A laptop with location services enabled works out where it is by looking at nearby wireless networks and comparing them against a database. This is far more precise than IP geolocation, often to within a building, and it is what people usually mean when they worry about being tracked.

What US law allows

There is no federal statute that specifically governs tracking the location of an employee's work device. The framework is assembled from ownership, notice duties in a few states, and limits on what happens outside working hours.

Ownership does most of the work. Courts have generally found a reduced expectation of privacy on employer owned equipment. If the company bought the laptop and said so in a policy, it is on strong ground.

Three states require notice of electronic monitoring. Connecticut, Delaware, and New York each have a statute requiring employees to be told. Our guide to employee monitoring laws by state sets out exactly what each requires and what the penalties are. Notice duties follow the employee's location rather than the employer's head office.

Off duty tracking is where employers get into trouble. Several states restrict monitoring outside working hours, and continuous location history on a device that goes home with someone collects far more than any work question needs. The distinction that matters is not whether the company may look, but whether the tracking ever stops.

Personal devices are a different question entirely. Once the hardware is yours, the ownership argument disappears and off duty conduct statutes, state privacy rights, and consent requirements all apply with more force.

What your employer probably sees, and what they probably do not

The gap between capability and practice is wide. Location reporting is available to most IT departments and reviewed by almost none of them, because nobody has time to watch a map.

Continuous location trails are common on phones issued to field and shift workers, where the job genuinely happens in different places. On a desk based laptop they are unusual, and an employer running one should expect to explain why.

What you can actually do about it

Four things, in the order worth trying.

  1. Ask, in writing. In Connecticut, Delaware, and New York you are entitled to a notice describing what is monitored. Everywhere else, asking still produces a written answer that either reassures you or tells you something useful.
  2. Read the policy you signed. Most acceptable use policies describe location and device management in general terms. It is dull and it is usually where the answer already is.
  3. Keep personal life off the company laptop. This is the single most effective step, and it costs nothing. Personal accounts, personal browsing, and personal messages on employer hardware are the part you cannot argue about later.
  4. Do not disable device management. Removing an enrolment profile or blocking a management agent on a company machine is usually a policy violation and is visible to IT when the device stops checking in. If tracking is the problem, raise it rather than route around it.

If you are on the employer side of this question, the practical guidance is in monitoring people who work from home, and the things that should never be collected are in the privacy line.

If you are the employer

Location is the capability employees react to most strongly, and the reaction is not irrational. Pew Research Center found in 2023 that 61% of US adults oppose employers using AI to track workers' movements while they work, the highest opposition of any use tested.

Three rules keep this proportionate. Collect location only where the job is genuinely location dependent. Stop collecting when the shift ends, and be able to show that it stops. Say in the policy what precision you collect and who can query it, because vagueness reads as something worse than the truth usually is.

Key takeaways

  • On an employer owned laptop the capability to locate the device is almost always present, through device management, find my device, IP geolocation, or Wi-Fi positioning.
  • Precision varies enormously. IP geolocation is city level and frequently wrong. Wi-Fi positioning can be accurate to a building.
  • No federal law specifically governs work device location tracking. Ownership, three state notice statutes, and off duty limits do the work.
  • The question that matters legally is not whether the employer may look, but whether the tracking ever stops.
  • On a personal device the ownership argument disappears and the legal position changes sharply.
  • Capability is not practice. Most IT departments can locate a laptop and almost none routinely do.
  • Keeping personal accounts and browsing off company hardware is the single most effective protection, and it is free.

Frequently asked questions

Can my employer see my laptop's exact location?

On a company owned laptop, usually yes if they choose to look. Device management platforms such as Intune and Jamf report device location to administrators, and both Windows and macOS include a find my device function tied to the account that owns the machine. Precision ranges from city level for IP geolocation to building level for Wi-Fi positioning.

Can my employer track my laptop when I am at home?

Technically yes, if the laptop is theirs and connected. Whether they may lawfully do so outside working hours is a different question, and several states restrict off duty monitoring. Working from home does not by itself create a privacy right on employer owned equipment.

Is it legal for an employer to track a work laptop?

There is no federal statute specifically regulating it, and monitoring employer owned equipment is broadly permitted. Connecticut, Delaware, and New York require notice of electronic monitoring. Some states restrict tracking outside working hours or on personal devices. Notice duties follow where the employee is, not where the company is.

Can my employer track my personal laptop?

Only with far more constraint, and generally only to the extent you agreed to. Without ownership of the hardware, the employer loses its strongest argument, and off duty conduct statutes, state privacy rights, and consent rules apply with more force. Installing employer software on a personal machine is what usually creates the access.

How can I tell if my work laptop is being tracked?

Look for a device management profile: on Windows, Access work or school in Settings, and on macOS, Device Management under Privacy and Security. An enrolled device can generally be located. That is a separate question from whether anyone is actually looking.

Can I turn off location tracking on my work laptop?

You can usually turn off operating system location services, which stops Wi-Fi positioning. You cannot remove IP based location, because it comes with the connection itself. Removing a device management profile is normally a policy violation and is visible to IT when the machine stops checking in.

Does using a VPN hide my location from my employer?

It changes the IP address a website sees, but it does not hide anything from software running on the laptop itself, and connecting to the company VPN reports your real address to the company. On a managed device a VPN is not a privacy measure against the device owner.

Do employees object to location tracking?

Strongly. Pew Research Center found in 2023 that 61% of US adults oppose employers using AI to track workers' movements while they work, the highest level of opposition among the six uses it tested.
