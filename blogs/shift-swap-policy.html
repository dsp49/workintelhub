Shift Swap Policy: Rules and a Free Template

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 9 min read

A shift swap policy says who may trade a scheduled shift with a colleague, how the trade is requested and approved, and what happens to pay when it changes someone's hours. The core rule fits in one sentence: a swap stands if both employees are qualified for the shift, neither goes into unapproved overtime or below the minimum rest between shifts, and both confirm it in writing.

The details matter because a swap is where two pay rules meet. Overtime under federal law is counted by the workweek, so a trade can push one person over 40 hours. And in cities with fair workweek laws, an employee-initiated swap is usually exempt from the premium for schedule changes, while one a manager arranges may not be. The policy wording below is written to keep both straight. Copy it into your handbook and adapt the bracketed parts.

The short answer

Approve swaps by rule, not by mood. Both qualified, neither over their hours limit or inside a rest gap, both confirming in writing.

Overtime follows the hours, not the request. If a swap takes someone past 40 hours in the workweek, the overtime is owed even though the employee asked for it.

Keep swaps employee-initiated and recorded. That is what keeps them outside the change premiums in fair workweek cities.

What a shift swap policy needs to cover

  1. Eligibility. Who can swap: usually employees past a probation period, within the same role or with the qualifications the shift needs.
  2. How to request. One channel, in writing: the scheduling app, a form or an email to the scheduler. Verbal agreements on the shop floor are where disputes start.
  3. Approval criteria. The conditions that make a swap automatic, and the ones that need a manager.
  4. Deadlines. How far ahead a swap must be submitted, so it can be checked and the schedule reposted.
  5. Hours and overtime. Whether a swap may create overtime, and who must approve it if so.
  6. Rest between shifts. The minimum gap a swap may not cut into.
  7. Responsibility. Once approved, the person who picked up the shift owns it, including the attendance rules.
  8. Records. Who asked, who agreed and when, kept with the schedule.

How swaps interact with overtime

Under 29 U.S.C. 207, non-exempt employees are owed time and a half for hours over 40 in a workweek. Under 29 CFR 778.105 the workweek is a fixed, recurring 168-hour period the employer sets, and overtime is counted week by week, not averaged. Three consequences for swaps:

  • A swap inside one workweek changes nothing if each person gives up and takes on the same hours. A swap of an 8-hour shift for a 6-hour one moves two hours between them.
  • A swap across two workweeks moves hours from one week to the other. Taking a colleague's Saturday and giving back a shift the following Tuesday can leave one person at 48 hours in the first week and 32 in the next. That is 8 hours of overtime, not zero.
  • A swap into a differential shift raises the regular rate. A shift differential for nights or weekends is part of the regular rate on which overtime is calculated, so an employee who picks up a night shift and goes over 40 is owed overtime on the higher rate.

Overtime is owed whether or not the employer approved it, once the hours are worked: under 29 CFR 785.11, work the employer did not request but suffered or permitted is work time. A policy can require approval for swaps that create overtime and discipline people who ignore it, but it cannot withhold the pay. States with a daily threshold add another check: per the California Labor Commissioner, hours over 8 in a workday are overtime there, so a swap that turns an 8-hour day into a double shift is overtime even in a short week. The overtime calculator works out the regular rate.

How swaps interact with predictive scheduling laws

Oregon and a group of cities require covered employers to post schedules in advance and pay a premium when they change a posted shift. Our table of predictive scheduling laws by state and city has the details. For swaps, three points from the official guidance matter.

  • Employee swaps are usually exempt. Seattle's fact sheet lists "the employee swapped shifts with another employee" among the changes that owe no premium. Philadelphia exempts shift trades employees mutually agree to, "subject to an employer policy about exchanging Shifts", which is a reason to have one. New York City fast food, San Francisco's formula retail ordinance and Berkeley also exclude trades between employees.
  • Who initiated it decides. New York City's fast food FAQ treats a manager asking someone to fill in for, or trade with, a colleague as a schedule change that can owe a premium, and says an employee picking up an open shift the employer posted is not a voluntary trade. For an offline trade, the manager must record in writing that it was voluntary, which shifts moved and who traded.
  • Rest rules still apply. The swap exemption covers the change premium, not the rest gap. Seattle requires 1.5 times pay for hours worked inside its 10-hour close-to-open gap even when the employee asked for or agreed to them, and New York City fast food requires written consent and $100 for each clopening worked. A swap that creates a short turnaround can still cost a premium.

The practical rule is that a swap should start with an employee, be agreed by the employee taking the shift, and be recorded with both names. If managers need cover, they post it as an open shift and treat it as the employer change it is.

Shift swap policy template

Replace the bracketed text. If you have employees in a predictive scheduling jurisdiction, keep section 6; otherwise you can drop it.

[Company name] Shift Swap Policy
Effective [date]. Applies to all non-exempt employees on the posted schedule at [locations].

1. Purpose. This policy lets employees trade scheduled shifts with each other when they cannot work a shift, while keeping every shift covered by a qualified person and keeping pay accurate.

2. Who may swap. Employees who have completed [30] days of employment may swap a shift with another employee who holds the same role, or who holds the skills, certifications or keyholder status the shift requires. Employees on a final written warning for attendance may swap only with manager approval.

3. How to request a swap. The employee giving up the shift posts it in [scheduling app / the swap form / an email to the scheduler]. The employee taking the shift accepts it through the same channel. A swap agreed verbally is not approved until both employees have confirmed it in writing. Swaps must be submitted at least [24] hours before the start of the shift, except in an emergency.

4. When a swap is approved. A swap is approved automatically when all of these are true:

  • the employee taking the shift is qualified for it;
  • the swap does not take either employee over [40] hours in the workweek, or over [8] hours in a day where daily overtime applies;
  • the swap does not leave either employee with less than [10] hours between the end of one shift and the start of the next;
  • the employee taking the shift is not a minor scheduled outside the hours the law permits; and
  • the shift is not on a date listed as blackout in the posted schedule.

A swap that fails any of these conditions needs approval from [the store manager]. The manager will respond within [24] hours or before the shift starts, whichever is sooner. Swaps are not refused for any reason that is unlawful or unrelated to coverage, cost or safety.

5. Overtime. A swap that would cause overtime needs manager approval before it is worked. If an employee works overtime, it will be paid at the correct rate whether or not it was approved; working unapproved overtime may be handled under the attendance policy.

6. Predictive scheduling. At locations covered by a fair workweek or predictive scheduling law, a swap under this policy is a voluntary, employee-initiated change. Managers will not ask, pressure or assign employees to swap. When a manager needs a shift covered, it will be offered as an open shift and any premium the law requires will be paid. Where a swap leaves an employee with less rest than the local law requires, the employee's written consent will be recorded and any rest premium paid.

7. Responsibility after approval. Once a swap is approved, the employee who took the shift is responsible for working it, and the attendance policy applies to them for that shift. The employee who gave it up is no longer scheduled.

8. Limits. An employee may give away no more than [4] shifts in a [4-week] period without manager approval. Swaps may not be used to transfer a shift for payment between employees.

9. Records. The scheduler keeps a record of every swap: the shifts, the employees, the date of each confirmation and who approved it. Records are kept for [3] years.

10. Questions. Questions about this policy go to [name or role].

This template is a starting point, not legal advice. Check the rules in each state and city where you have employees before adopting it.

Running swaps without a manager in the loop

Most swaps meet the automatic conditions in section 4, and the point of writing them down is that nobody has to look. Scheduling apps do this well: an employee offers a shift, qualified colleagues see it, one claims it, and the app checks hours and qualifications before the manager taps approve. In our review of the best shift scheduling software, When I Work, Sling, 7shifts, Homebase, Deputy, Connecteam and Humanity all run that loop, and Deputy also encodes fair workweek rules by jurisdiction.

Two numbers tell you whether the policy is working. The share of swaps approved automatically should be high; if managers are approving most of them by hand, the conditions are too tight or the qualifications data is out of date. And the same few people giving away the same shifts every week is a scheduling problem, not a swap problem: the shift was unwanted when it was published. Rebuild that part of the rota using the steps in how to make a shift schedule, and see the no call no show policy for the absences a swap policy does not prevent.

Key takeaways

  • A shift swap policy sets eligibility, how swaps are requested, the conditions for automatic approval, deadlines, overtime, rest, responsibility and records.
  • Approve automatically when both employees are qualified, neither goes over their hours or inside a rest gap, and both confirm in writing.
  • Overtime is counted per workweek, so a swap across two workweeks can create overtime, and a swap into a differential shift raises the regular rate.
  • Overtime that is worked must be paid even if the swap was not approved; the policy can only require approval and enforce it.
  • Fair workweek laws in Seattle, Philadelphia, New York City, San Francisco and Berkeley generally exempt employee-initiated swaps from change premiums.
  • A swap a manager arranges can count as an employer change, and rest-between-shift premiums can still apply to a voluntary swap.

Frequently asked questions

What is a shift swap policy?

A written rule that says who may trade a scheduled shift with a colleague, how to request the trade, when it is approved automatically or needs a manager, how overtime and rest rules apply, and who is responsible for the shift once the trade is approved.

Can an employer deny a shift swap?

Generally yes, for reasons such as qualifications, overtime cost or coverage, provided the reason is not discriminatory or retaliatory. A written policy with clear approval criteria makes denials consistent and easier to explain.

Does a shift swap cause overtime?

It can. Overtime is owed for hours over 40 in a workweek, so a swap that moves hours from one workweek into another, or adds a longer shift, can push one employee over 40. In California and other states with daily overtime, a swap that makes a long day can also owe overtime.

Do predictive scheduling laws apply to shift swaps?

Most exempt swaps that employees arrange between themselves from the premium for schedule changes, including Seattle, Philadelphia, New York City fast food, San Francisco and Berkeley. A trade a manager asks for, or an open shift the employer posts, can count as an employer change, and rest-between-shift rules still apply.

Who is responsible for a swapped shift?

Once the swap is approved, the employee who took the shift is responsible for working it and the attendance policy applies to them. The employee who gave it up is no longer scheduled for that shift.

Should shift swaps be approved by a manager?

Only when they fail a written condition, such as creating overtime, cutting a rest gap or involving an unqualified employee. Swaps that meet every condition can be approved automatically, which most scheduling apps support.
