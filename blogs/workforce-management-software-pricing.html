Workforce Management Software Pricing (2026): What Teams Pay

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 10 min read

Specialist workforce management tools publish their prices: from $2.50 per user a month for When I Work, $3.90 to $13.90 for Time Champ, $5 to $9 for Deputy with a $30 monthly minimum, $29 to $99 a month for Connecteam's first 30 users and $30 to $120 per location for Homebase, all as read in October 2026. HCM suites with a workforce management module mostly quote, and the independent estimates our review reports put full suites at roughly $20 to $45 per employee a month, with implementation on top.

The two halves of the market are priced on different units, which is why a quick comparison usually goes wrong. A specialist charges for the people who use the tool, or for each location. A suite charges for every employee on the payroll, often on a multi-year term. This page lists the prices recorded in our review of twelve workforce management platforms, explains the pricing models, shows where the bill grows, and ends with a calculator that turns your headcount into a monthly and first-year figure.

The short answer

Under 50 people: $0 to about $250 a month for scheduling and time, according to the size bands in our review. Connecteam and Homebase both have free tiers.

50 to 500 people: about $500 to $12,000 a month, depending on whether you buy a specialist at a published price or a suite with HR and payroll included.

Above 500 people: mostly quote-only suites. The estimates run $20 to $45 per employee a month, and implementation can add 20 to 60 percent of the first year's software cost.

Check the unit first. Per user, per location and per employee on payroll can differ tenfold for the same workforce.

The pricing models, and what each does as you grow

  • Per user, published (When I Work, Deputy, Time Champ). You pay for people who use the tool. Managers who log in to approve timesheets usually count, so add them to the headcount.
  • Per hub, first 30 users (Connecteam). A flat fee covers up to 30 people, then a small per-user charge. Cheap per head at 25 users; at 300, the per-user rate is most of the bill.
  • Per location (Homebase). Good value at two or three sites, expensive at thirty. Our review calls it the right size for a restaurant group of three and the wrong one for a chain of thirty.
  • Per employee plus base, published entry (Rippling). The base fee matters at ten people and fades by a hundred; each module adds a per-employee charge.
  • Base fee plus per employee, quoted (Paycor, Paylocity). A monthly platform fee plus a rate per employee that depends on the tier.
  • Per employee on payroll, multi-year (Workday, and most suites). Every employee counts whether or not they log in. It is linear, and it never gets cheaper per head as you grow.

The unit matters more than the rate. Our review gives the example of a workforce of 2,000 with 200 managers: if only the managers use the scheduling tool, a per-user price bills 200 seats, while a per-employee module bills 2,000. That is a tenfold difference before anyone compares rates.

Where the bill grows

  1. Minimums and base fees. Deputy's $30 monthly minimum, Rippling's $35 base and the Paylocity platform fee weigh most on small teams.
  2. Payroll and HR add-ons. Deputy's payroll add-on ($8 per user plus $49) more than doubles a Core seat. Homebase payroll is $49 plus $6 per employee. Price the add-ons before you choose the tier.
  3. Modules that carry the feature you were shown. Time and attendance is a tier up on ADP. Forecasting is a module on several suites. Ask which line of the quote each demo screen sits on.
  4. Implementation. Our review puts UKG, Dayforce and Workday implementations at 20 to 60 percent of first-year software cost. Specialist tools deploy in days to weeks without a project fee of that size.
  5. Discount expiry and renewal uplifts. Introductory discounts end. Get the year-two and year-three price in writing, along with what happens when headcount crosses a band boundary.
  6. Exit. Historical hours, leave balances and schedules are what a migration loses. Ask for the export format before signing.

Three budgets, worked through

Using the published October 2026 prices, before tax and add-ons unless stated:

  • A cafe group with 3 locations and 40 hourly staff. Homebase Plus is $70 times 3, or $210 a month. Adding Homebase payroll brings it to $210 + $49 + $240 = $499. Deputy Core for the same 40 staff plus 3 managers is 43 × $6.50, or $279.50 a month, without payroll.
  • A field services firm with 120 deskless staff. Connecteam Operations Advanced is $49 for the first 30 users plus 90 × $2.50, or $274 a month, before the HR or Communications hubs.
  • A remote support team of 80 desk-based staff. Time Champ Professional is 80 × $6.90, or $552 a month on annual billing. It records attendance and shift adherence from a desktop agent, but it does not run payroll or shop-floor scheduling.

The same 120 people on a suite estimated at $20 to $33 per employee would cost $2,400 to $3,960 a month, but that figure includes HR and payroll. The fair comparison is the suite against the specialist plus your current HRIS and payroll. For a contact center, the call center workforce management guide explains the staffing math that decides how many seats you need, and WFM vs HRIS sets out which system should own which data when you buy them separately.

How to get a real number from a quote

  • Ask whether the price is per employee or per user, and who counts: seasonal staff, terminated employees still in the year, managers.
  • Ask what the implementation fee includes: data migration, rules configuration, clock hardware, integrations, and who does each.
  • Ask which module carries each feature you saw: forecasting, auto-scheduling, time clocks, leave and labor analytics.
  • Get the renewal terms: the uplift cap, when introductory pricing ends, and the price at the next headcount band.
  • Get the exit terms: the data export format and the notice period.

Then test the rules engine on your own state's overtime, break and scheduling law during the trial; our workforce management software comparison explains why compliance, more than features, separates these products.   • Most HCM suites quote; the independent estimates in our review run about $20 to $45 per employee a month, and those figures usually include HR and payroll.
  • Check the pricing unit before the rate: per user, per location and per employee on payroll can differ tenfold for one workforce.

Frequently asked questions

How much does workforce management software cost?

Specialist tools publish prices: When I Work from $2.50 per user a month, Time Champ $3.90 to $13.90, Deputy $5 to $9 with a $30 minimum, Connecteam $29 to $99 a month for the first 30 users and Homebase $30 to $120 per location, as read in October 2026. HCM suites mostly quote; independent estimates run about $20 to $45 per employee a month plus implementation.

Is there free workforce management software?

Yes, with limits. Connecteam is free for up to 10 users, and Homebase Basic is free for one location and 10 employees. Beyond those limits, both charge.

Why do WFM suites not publish prices?

Suite pricing depends on headcount, modules, contract length and implementation scope, and it is negotiated. UKG, ADP, Dayforce, Paylocity, Paycor, Workday and Legion are all quote-only. Rippling publishes an entry price of $8 per employee plus a $35 base fee, with modules on top.

What does WFM implementation cost?

For UKG, Dayforce and Workday, our review reports implementations at 20 to 60 percent of first-year software cost. Specialist tools such as Deputy, Connecteam and Homebase typically deploy in days to weeks.

Is per-user or per-employee pricing cheaper?

It depends on who uses the tool. If everyone clocks in, the two units bill the same number of people. If only managers use a scheduling module, a per-user price bills far fewer seats than a per-employee price that counts everyone on payroll.
