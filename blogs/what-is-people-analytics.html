What Is People Analytics? Definition, Examples and Maturity Levels

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 8 min read

People analytics is the practice of joining the data HR already holds, from the HRIS, applicant tracking, payroll, performance and survey systems, to explain and predict outcomes for groups of employees: why turnover rose, who is at risk of leaving, whether pay is equitable, which hiring sources produce people who stay. It differs from HR reporting in asking why, not just what happened.

This guide covers the definition, how people analytics differs from HR reporting and workforce analytics, six examples with the data each needs, the four maturity levels most organizations move through, and a short self-check to place your own team. People analytics, defined

People analytics is the analysis of joined employee data from HR systems, using agreed definitions, to explain and predict outcomes for employee populations and to inform decisions about hiring, pay, development, retention and organization design.

Three parts of that definition do the work. Joined: a single system describes; the insight usually comes from combining two, such as exits from the HRIS with source of hire from the ATS. Agreed definitions: if finance and HR count headcount differently, every downstream number is disputed. Populations: people analytics describes groups, with minimum group sizes, and its predictions are probabilities for populations rather than verdicts on individuals.

You will also see it called HR analytics, talent analytics or workforce analytics. HR analytics is usually narrower, measuring HR's own processes such as time to hire. Workforce analytics is usually broader, adding activity, collaboration, scheduling and planning data; our comparison of workforce analytics and people analytics sets out the boundary.

People analytics vs HR reporting

HR reportingPeople analytics
QuestionWhat happened?Why did it happen, and what is likely next?
SourcesUsually one systemSeveral, joined on employee ID
Typical outputHeadcount, hires and exits by month and departmentTurnover drivers, attrition risk, adjusted pay gaps, quality of hire by source
MethodCounts and ratesSegmentation, comparison, statistical controls, predictive models
AudienceHR operations, complianceLeaders making a decision

Reporting is not the lesser activity. Every analysis rests on it, and an organization whose monthly headcount report is disputed is not ready for a predictive model. The HR metrics guide has the standard formulas that reporting should get right first.

Six people analytics examples

QuestionData neededWhat the analysis shows
Why is turnover rising, and where?Exits, tenure, pay, promotions, manager changes, engagementWhere voluntary and regretted exits concentrate, by manager, tenure band and job family
Who is at risk of leaving?The same, over several yearsA probability for groups; a prompt for stay conversations, not a decision
Is pay equitable?Pay, grade, role, location, tenure, performance, protected attributesThe gap that remains after controlling for legitimate factors
How diverse are the pipeline and leadership?Applicants, hires, promotions and exits by attributeWhere representation drops between stages
Where does the hiring funnel leak?ATS stages, time to fill, source, first-year turnoverSlow stages and the sources whose hires stay
Does engagement predict exits?Survey results by team, joined to later turnoverWhich survey items move before turnover does

The last example shows why joining matters. Survey results alone say how people feel; joined to the turnover rate by team six months later, they say which feelings are worth acting on. The four maturity levels

Published maturity models differ in how many levels they name and what they call them. The sequence underneath is consistent: describe, explain, predict, act. Each level depends on the one before, and most organizations that buy a predictive platform before the definitions are agreed end up using it for level one reporting.

LevelQuestionWhat it needsTypical output
1. ReportingWhat happened?One trusted HRIS, agreed definitions of headcount, hire and exitMonthly headcount, turnover, hires and absence
2. DiagnosisWhy, and where?Joined sources, clean manager and job data, an analystTurnover by manager and tenure; quality of hire by source; adjusted pay gap
3. PredictionWhat is likely next?Several years of clean history, statistical skills, governance for modelsAttrition risk by population; hiring and headcount forecasts
4. ActionWhat should we do, and did it work?Leaders who commit to decisions in advance; follow-up measurementAnalysis built into planning, pay and retention decisions, with results tracked

Level four is less about technique than habit. The organizations that reach it decide what they will do before the analysis runs, then measure whether it worked. That is also the protection against the commonest failure: data that arrives with no question attached and gets used for whatever it happens to reveal.

Four common mistakes

  • Buying before defining. A platform loaded with disputed headcount and stale manager fields produces disputed dashboards faster. Agree the definitions first.
  • Analysis with no decision attached. A dashboard built because the tool could produce one gets used for whatever it happens to reveal. Start each project by naming the decision and the person who will take it.
  • Reading the company average. A company-wide turnover rate or engagement score hides the team with the problem. Cut by manager, tenure and job family.
  • Turning a diagnostic into a target. Once a manager's bonus depends on an engagement score or eNPS, the score measures the campaign for it. Our eNPS entry explains why it should stay a diagnostic.

Who does people analytics

In a company of a few hundred, people analytics is usually one HR analyst with Power BI or Tableau on HRIS exports, and that covers levels one and two for the cost of the licenses. In larger organizations it becomes a team, typically reporting into the CHRO, that combines HR knowledge, data engineering and statistics. Our guide on how to build a people analytics function covers who to hire first, which data sources to connect, how to govern privacy, and the first three projects worth running.

Platforms change the economics once data lives in several systems that disagree. Visier is the reference platform for organizations of a thousand or more; One Model suits analytics teams that want an open data layer; Crunchr targets European organizations; ChartHop is priced for mid-sized companies from $2 per employee for its Basic plan, as read in October 2026. Privacy and the limits

People analytics runs on personal data, and the rules follow it: GDPR and works council consultation for European employees, the California Consumer Privacy Act's employee provisions, New York City's automated employment decision tool rules and state AI-in-hiring laws for models that affect employment decisions, and pay transparency laws that make pay analysis an obligation in several states.

Three limits apply whatever the law says. Report groups, not individuals, with minimum group sizes, because a breakdown of a team of four identifies people. Treat attrition risk scores as a prompt for a conversation, never as a decision about a person. And remember what the data cannot tell you: why one particular person is leaving. That still comes from asking them.

Key takeaways

  • People analytics joins HR data from several systems to explain and predict outcomes for employee populations.
  • It differs from HR reporting by asking why and what next, and from workforce analytics by staying with the HR record rather than activity and planning data.
  • Common examples: turnover drivers, attrition risk, pay equity, pipeline diversity, hiring funnel leaks and engagement-to-turnover links.
  • Maturity runs from reporting to diagnosis, prediction and action; each level depends on the one before.
  • Agreed definitions and clean manager and job data matter more than software at the first two levels.
  • Report groups with minimum sizes and never let a risk score make a decision about a person.

Frequently asked questions

What is people analytics in simple terms?

Using the data HR already holds, joined across systems, to understand why things happen to groups of employees and what is likely to happen next: why turnover rose, who is at risk of leaving, whether pay is fair, which hiring sources work.

What is an example of people analytics?

Joining exit data from the HRIS with source of hire from the applicant tracking system to find which recruiting channels produce employees who stay past their first year, then moving recruiting budget accordingly.

What is the difference between people analytics and HR analytics?

HR analytics usually measures HR's own processes, such as time to hire and cost per hire. People analytics is broader and studies employee populations and outcomes, such as retention, pay equity and engagement, across several joined systems.

What are the levels of people analytics maturity?

This guide uses four: reporting what happened, diagnosing why, predicting what is likely next, and acting on the analysis with decisions committed in advance and results re-measured. Labels vary between models; the sequence does not.

Do small companies need people analytics?

They need the first two levels, which an HR analyst can deliver from clean HRIS exports and a spreadsheet or Power BI. Platforms usually pay only when data sits in several systems and headcount runs into the high hundreds or more.

Is people analytics legal?

Yes, within privacy law, pay transparency rules and AI-in-employment rules for any model that affects decisions. Aggregate reporting with minimum group sizes, a published purpose and human decisions keep most programs on safe ground.
