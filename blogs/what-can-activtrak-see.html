What Can ActivTrak See? Apps, Idle Time and Screenshots Explained

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

ActivTrak sees which applications and websites are active on your work computer, how long each one is in use, and when you are idle. It sorts each app and site into productive, unproductive or undefined for your role, and builds reports on that. It does not log keystrokes on any plan, and it does not take screenshots unless your employer has bought a separate add-on, which is only available on its two lower plans. There is no stealth mode: the agent is visible.

The rest of this page explains each item, what your manager actually sees, what ActivTrak cannot see, and how to find out what your own account records. If you are an employer deciding what to switch on, the same detail tells you what your team will be living with.

Apps, websites and the time in each

The core of ActivTrak is a record of what is in the foreground. The agent on a Windows or Mac computer, or on a Chromebook, notes which application or website is active and for how long. That becomes a timeline of your day: an hour in a spreadsheet, twenty minutes in email, ten on a news site. A manager sees totals per person and per team, and the share of time spent in each category.

It records the application and the site, not what you did inside them. Without the Screen Details add-on, ActivTrak does not capture what was on the screen. Full URLs are listed as part of that add-on, so how much page-level detail your employer sees depends on whether they bought it. Our guide to employee internet monitoring covers what web tracking in general can and cannot reveal.

Active time, idle time and the productivity label

ActivTrak counts you as active when there is keyboard or mouse input, and idle when there is none for a while. It does not record which keys you pressed. Productive time is then active time spent in an application or site your employer has classified as productive for your role. A code editor can be productive for an engineer and undefined for a salesperson.

That is why the number can be unfair. Reading a long document, thinking through a problem or sitting in a meeting with no input can look like idling. ActivTrak's calendar integrations and offline meeting time are designed to stop meetings counting as idle, but only if your employer has set them up. If your work happens in a tool that has been left undefined or marked unproductive, ask for it to be reclassified rather than working around it; a mouse jiggler tends to end in a dishonesty finding, which is far worse than a low score. Our piece on why activity metrics fail knowledge teams is a useful thing to share with a manager who reads the label as a grade.

Screenshots, live view and full URLs

On its standard plans ActivTrak takes no screenshots. The images come with the Screen Details add-on, which also adds full URLs, a live view of your screen and website blocking. It is priced separately, sold only on Work Activity Tracking and Workforce Management, and not offered on the higher plans. So if your employer is on Productivity Optimization or Workflow, screenshots are not available at all.

If the add-on is on, anything on your screen at the moment of capture is in the image: a chat window, a document, a browser tab. Live view means a manager can watch the screen as you work. Because it is an add-on, it is a specific purchasing decision, and a fair question to ask about directly.

When ActivTrak is recording

ActivTrak is not a timer you start and stop. It is an agent on the device, and its reports include after-hours work and workload patterns, so assume activity on a monitored work device can be recorded whenever you use it. Ask your employer whether recording is limited to your scheduled hours. The simplest protection is to keep personal browsing, banking and messages off work devices.

ActivTrak has no stealth mode, so the agent is not designed to be hidden. If you are not sure what else may be installed on a work device, our guide to detecting employee monitoring explains how to check.

How long the data is kept, and where it goes

Data history depends on your employer's plan: 30 days on the free plan, six months on Work Activity Tracking and Workforce Management, and a year on Productivity Optimization. Employers can pay to extend it to three years. Data can also leave ActivTrak: the ActivConnect add-on exports it into Google BigQuery for reporting in Tableau or Power BI, and reports can be downloaded as files. Once exported, the retention period inside ActivTrak no longer limits how long it is kept, so ask about exports as well as the plan.

How to check what your account records

  1. Read the monitoring policy. In Connecticut, Delaware and New York, employers must give written notice of electronic monitoring. Elsewhere it is good practice. Our monitoring laws by state guide lists the rules.
  2. Ask whether Screen Details is on. It decides whether screenshots, full URLs and live view exist at all. The answer should be a plain yes or no.
  3. Ask how your role is classified. Which of the apps and sites you use are marked productive, unproductive or undefined, and who can change that.
  4. Ask about hours and retention. Whether recording is limited to scheduled hours, how long data is kept, whether it is exported, and who can see named reports.
  5. Ask whether you can see your own data. Whether you can see your own numbers depends on how your employer has configured the account.

For employers: what to switch on

ActivTrak's appeal is that it can answer workload and focus questions without images or keystrokes, and that is easy to explain to a team. Keep it that way: decide before rollout whether you will ever buy Screen Details, classify applications per role before the first report, turn on calendar integrations so meetings are not idle, and write it all down before the agent is installed. Frequently asked questions

Can ActivTrak see my screen?

Only if your employer has bought the Screen Details add-on, which adds screenshots, full URLs, live screen view and website blocking. It is sold only on the Work Activity Tracking and Workforce Management plans and is not offered on the higher plans. Without it, ActivTrak records which apps and sites are active, not what is on screen.

Does ActivTrak record keystrokes?

No. ActivTrak does not log keystrokes on any plan. It notes whether there is keyboard or mouse input to tell active time from idle time, but not which keys you pressed.

Can ActivTrak see my browsing history?

It records which websites are active on a monitored work device and for how long. Full URLs are part of the Screen Details add-on. It does not see browsing on a personal device without the agent.

Is ActivTrak hidden from employees?

No. ActivTrak has no stealth mode; its agent is visible. Some competitors, including Insightful, offer a stealth mode on company-owned devices.

Can ActivTrak see my messages?

Not as text. It records that a chat or email application is in use and for how long. If the Screen Details add-on is on, a screenshot or live view taken while a message is open will show it.

Does ActivTrak track location?

No. ActivTrak has no GPS and its agents run on Windows, macOS and Chromebook, not phones.
