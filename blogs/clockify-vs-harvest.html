Clockify vs Harvest (2026): Price, Invoicing and Who Each Fits

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Clockify and Harvest both track time against clients and projects, and buyers who bill by the hour usually shortlist both. They are built around different jobs. Clockify is a broad tracker that adds modules by tier, with invoicing arriving on Standard alongside approvals, time off and attendance. Harvest was built around the invoice: time and expenses become a bill with online payment on every plan, including the free one, and sync to the accounts.

The rest follows from that. Clockify is cheaper at almost every plan and covers hourly staff with a kiosk, attendance and GPS. Harvest costs more, especially for approvals, but closes the loop from hours to payment. This comparison uses October 2026 pricing read from both vendors' sites for our alternatives research.

The short answer

Choose Clockify if you want approvals, time off, invoicing and attendance in one tool for the least money, or you have hourly staff who need a kiosk or GPS. The realistic plan is Standard at $5.49 or Pro at $7.99.

Choose Harvest if your tracking exists to produce a bill: invoices with online payment on every plan, expenses, budgets that surface in the invoice, and sync to QuickBooks Online and Xero. The realistic plan is Teams at $9, or Enterprise at $14 if you need approvals.

Choose neither if what you need is a free plan for a growing team. Clockify's free plan stops at five users and Harvest's at one seat; TimeCamp is free for unlimited users.

Invoicing: a module or the point

This is the difference that decides most evaluations. In Clockify, invoicing is one of several modules that arrive on Standard, alongside approvals, time off and attendance. It turns billable time into an invoice, and the QuickBooks integration on the same tier passes the data on. For a team that bills some clients and runs payroll for others, that is a lot of tool for $5.49.

Harvest was built the other way round. Time and expenses are tracked against clients and projects, turned into invoices, paid online through Stripe, and synced to QuickBooks Online or Xero without re-keying. Even the free plan invoices. Budgets are visible to whoever runs the project and surface in the invoice, so the person sending the bill sees the overrun coming. For a consultancy whose time record is the invoice, that closed loop is what the higher price buys.

Price at the plan you will actually use

Headline prices understate the gap. For hours with billable rates and budgets, compare Clockify Pro at $7.99 with Harvest Teams at $9. Hourly staff and attendance

Clockify covers people who clock in as well as people who bill projects. Basic adds a shared-device kiosk and breaks, Standard adds attendance with overtime and time off, and Pro adds scheduling and GPS. That makes it a plausible single tool for a business with an office team and an hourly crew.

Harvest has no attendance, kiosk, GPS or payroll features. It is a project and billing tracker, and a business with shift staff will need a separate time clock alongside it. If that is your situation, the clock in clock out apps guide covers the options.

Switching between them

Moving from Harvest to Clockify is well trodden: Clockify publishes a guide for importing from Toggl, Harvest and ClickUp, and time entry import needs a paid Clockify plan or its trial. Going the other way, Harvest imports projects and people from CSV and time entries through its CSV template, with assisted migration on Enterprise Plus. If you are leaving Clockify's free plan, export on a paid trial first, because Free exports PDF only, 31 days at a time.

If neither fits, our alternatives to Clockify, sorted by why you are leaving covers TimeCamp, Everhour, My Hours and eight more, and our Clockify review sets out where it is strong and where it falls short. For the other common head-to-head, read Toggl vs Clockify.

Key takeaways

  • Clockify puts invoicing, approvals, time off and attendance on Standard at $5.49; Harvest invoices on every plan but keeps approvals and profitability on Enterprise at $14.
  • Per seat on annual billing, Clockify runs $3.99 to $11.99 and Harvest $9 to $14, as read in October 2026.
  • Harvest closes the loop from time to invoice to online payment, with QuickBooks Online and Xero sync; Clockify integrates with QuickBooks on Standard.
  • Clockify covers hourly staff with a kiosk, attendance, overtime and GPS; Harvest has none of these.
  • Clockify's free plan is 5 users with PDF-only export; Harvest's is one seat and two projects with invoicing.

Frequently asked questions

Is Clockify better than Harvest?

For most teams that want approvals, time off and attendance cheaply, yes: Clockify Standard costs $5.49 per seat on annual billing. For a business whose time tracking exists to produce invoices and take payment, Harvest's billing flow is the stronger product.

Which is cheaper, Clockify or Harvest?

Clockify, at every comparable plan. Its paid plans run $3.99 to $11.99 per seat per month on annual billing against Harvest's $9 and $14, and timesheet approvals cost $5.49 on Clockify against $14 on Harvest, as read in October 2026.

Does Clockify have invoicing like Harvest?

Clockify includes invoicing from its Standard plan. Harvest includes invoicing with online payment on every plan, including the free one, and syncs to QuickBooks Online and Xero.

Can I import Harvest data into Clockify?

Yes. Clockify publishes a guide for importing from Toggl, Harvest and ClickUp. Importing time entries requires a paid Clockify plan or its trial.

Does Harvest have a free plan?

Yes, for one seat and two projects, with invoicing and expenses. Clockify's free plan covers five users but, since April 2026, exports PDF only and has no billable rates.
