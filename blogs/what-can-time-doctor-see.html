What Can Time Doctor See? Screenshots, Apps and Activity Explained

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Time Doctor sees more than most time trackers, and how much depends on the plan your employer bought. On every plan it takes screenshots of your screen while it is tracking. On the Standard plan and above it also records the websites and applications you use, rates them as productive or not, and reports your attendance, breaks and idle time. On Premium it can record your screen as video and flag mouse jigglers, irregular keyboard patterns and unusual activity.

This page explains each item, what your manager actually sees, what Time Doctor's published features do not include, and how to find out what your own account records. It is written for employees; if you are an employer deciding what to switch on, the same detail tells you what your team will be living with. It is based on Time Doctor's plan features as published in October 2026, so where your employer's settings matter, we say so.

Screenshots on every plan

Screenshots are the defining feature. There is no Time Doctor plan without them, so if your employer uses Time Doctor at all, assume your screen is captured at intervals while tracking is on. Your employer can adjust how often they are taken and who can see them, but cannot buy the product without them.

Anything on your screen at the moment of capture is in the image: a chat window, a document, a browser tab, a personal message you glanced at. On Premium, video screen recording goes further and shows what happened between those moments. The simplest protection is habit: keep personal browsing, banking and private messages off work devices and out of tracked time. Our guide to computer screen monitoring covers how screen capture works across tools.

Apps, websites and productivity ratings

On Standard and Premium, Time Doctor records the applications and websites you use and how long you spend in each. Your company classifies them, per team, as productive or unproductive, and the reports roll that up into a productivity rating. The rating is only as good as that list, so if a tool you need for your job shows up as unproductive, ask how your team's list is set up.

Site and app names show where you were, not what you wrote there. The text of what you typed on a page is not part of these reports, though a screenshot taken while the page was open will show whatever was on screen.

Activity, idle time and attendance

Time Doctor measures activity from keyboard and mouse input during tracked time, and marks stretches without input as idle. Long calls, reading and thinking produce little input, so a meeting-heavy day can look idle on paper. Standard adds attendance and schedules, break and leave tracking, and time approvals, so your manager can see when you started, when you took breaks and whether that matched your schedule. Attendance handles time zones explicitly, which matters if your team is spread out.

Some people respond to an idle flag with a mouse jiggler. On Time Doctor that is a particularly bad idea: Premium includes mouse-jiggler and irregular-keyboard detection and an unusual-activity report built to catch exactly that, and a dishonesty finding is far worse than a low score. If idle time is the worry, how idle detection works in Teams explains a related signal your employer may also see.

Reports that can work in your favor

Not everything Time Doctor reports is about catching underwork. Its work-life balance report flags late hours and long days, and our employee monitoring software review calls it the most employee-protective feature in the category. If you are regularly working into the evening, that report is evidence you can point to. The Office versus Remote report compares the two groups as a whole, which can help if your team is arguing about whether home working is working.

When tracking starts, and whether you will know

Time Doctor gives the people it tracks their own dashboard showing what is collected about them. Two caveats matter. First, a silent mode exists, so on some deployments the agent may not be obvious. Second, public reviews describe the desktop app recording activity without the timer being started on some configurations. One Trustpilot reviewer wrote, Automatically tracked my activities without me turning it on! (one star, August 5, 2025). If you are unsure whether tracking is running, ask, and see our guide to detecting employee monitoring.

Time Doctor also tracks offline and syncs when the connection returns, so being off the internet does not mean you are off the record.

How to check what your account records

  1. Open your own dashboard. Time Doctor gives tracked employees a view of their own hours, activity and screenshots. What you see there is a good guide to what your manager sees.
  2. Find out the plan. Basic means screenshots and time; Standard adds app and website reports, ratings and attendance; Premium adds video and jiggler detection. The plan tells you most of what is possible.
  3. Read the monitoring policy. In Connecticut, Delaware and New York, employers must give written notice of electronic monitoring. Ask for the policy if you have not seen one; our monitoring laws by state guide lists the rules.
  4. Ask about retention and access. Time Doctor keeps three months of data on Basic, six on Standard and two years on Premium. Who can open your screenshots matters as much as how often they are taken.

For employers: what you are asking staff to accept

Every item above is something your team will notice, and screenshots are not optional on any plan. Decide who can see them, set the interval to what you will actually review, classify apps per team before anyone is rated, and write it down before the agent is installed. The monitoring policy template covers the wording. If mandatory screenshots are why your team dislikes the tool, our list of tools that replace Time Doctor is sorted by exactly that reason. For comparison, see what Hubstaff can see and what ActivTrak can see, and for the employer's view of the product, our Time Doctor review.

Key takeaways

  • Time Doctor takes screenshots on every plan, including Basic; your employer sets how often and who can see them.
  • Standard and Premium record the apps and websites you use, rate them as productive or not per team, and report attendance, breaks and idle time.
  • Premium adds video screen recording, mouse-jiggler and irregular-keyboard detection and an unusual-activity report.
  • Chats and email are not read as text, but anything on screen during a screenshot or recording is captured.
  • A silent mode exists, and some reviewers report tracking that started without the timer; ask if you are unsure.
  • Data is kept three months on Basic, six on Standard and two years on Premium, as published in October 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Can Time Doctor see my screen?

Yes. Time Doctor takes screenshots on every plan while it is tracking, and on Premium it can record your screen as video. Your employer can change how often screenshots are taken and who can see them.

Does Time Doctor record keystrokes?

Recording what you type is not listed among Time Doctor's published plan features. It measures activity from keyboard and mouse input, and Premium adds irregular-keyboard detection to catch automated input. Ask your employer what else is installed on the device.

Can Time Doctor see my browsing history?

On Standard and Premium it records the websites and applications you use during tracked time and rates them as productive or not. It shows where you were, not what you typed there, though screenshots show whatever was on screen.

Can Time Doctor detect a mouse jiggler?

Yes, on Premium. It includes mouse-jiggler and irregular-keyboard detection and an unusual-activity report.

Can Time Doctor see my messages?

Not as text. It does not read chat or email content as such, but a screenshot or video taken while a message is open on screen will show it.

Does Time Doctor track you when you are offline?

Yes. It keeps tracking without a connection and syncs when you reconnect.
