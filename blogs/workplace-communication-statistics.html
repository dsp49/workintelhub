Workplace Communication Statistics (2026)

P Pavan
October 16, 2026 · 12 min read

The average Microsoft 365 user received 117 emails and 153 Teams messages per weekday in early 2025, and 60% of their time in Microsoft's applications went to email, chat and meetings rather than to creating anything. Fifty-three percent of desk workers feel pressure to respond quickly to messages even after hours. Only 46% of US employees strongly agree they know what is expected of them at work, down from 56% in March 2020.

The pages that rank for this question are built on a $1.2 trillion cost figure from a 2022 vendor survey, an 86% figure from 2011 and a 20% to 25% productivity uplift from a 2012 McKinsey model, all presented as current and attributed to the wrong organizations. This page separates telemetry from surveys, uses Microsoft's Work Trend Index series, Slack's three indexes, Atlassian, Gallup, Pew, Buffer, Owl Labs and the PMI project study, gives the sample and date for each, and explains how the cost estimates were built.

Message volume: what the telemetry shows

Microsoft's Work Trend Index reports aggregated activity from its commercial customers outside the EU, which is the largest telemetry on workplace communication and describes one vendor's users. The June 2025 report, on a 28-day window to mid-February 2025, found:

  • 117 emails received per worker per day, most skimmed in under a minute; 153 Teams messages per weekday, up 6% on the year.
  • More than 50 messages sent or received outside core hours per worker, with after-hours chats up 15%; 29% of active workers back in their inbox by 10pm; 40% of those online at 6am reading email.
  • Mass emails to 20 or more recipients up 7% while one-to-one threads fell 5%.
  • An interruption by email, meeting or notification every two minutes during the working day, for the most-messaged fifth of users.

The 2024 report found 60% of time in Microsoft's applications spent on email, chat and meetings against 40% in creation tools, up from 57% against 43% in 2023, when the split was 23% Teams meetings, 19% Teams chat and 15% email. Eighty-five percent of emails were read in under 15 seconds, and users read about four emails for every one they sent. The heaviest quarter of email users spent 8.8 hours a week on it. Between February 2020 and February 2022, chats per person rose 32% and after-hours work 28%. The one US probability survey on email volume is Adobe's 2019 study of 1,002 adults, which found about five hours a day on email, three of them on work email. Overload and what employees say about it

Microsoft's 2025 survey of 31,000 knowledge workers in 31 markets found 48% of employees and 52% of leaders describing work as chaotic and fragmented, and one in three saying the pace makes it impossible to keep up. Its 2024 survey found 68% struggling with the pace and volume of work and 46% feeling burned out; its 2023 survey found 68% without enough uninterrupted focus time and 62% spending too much time searching for information. Atlassian's 2024 State of Teams survey of 5,000 knowledge workers found 65% saying responding quickly to messages matters more than making progress on top priorities, 55% finding it hard to track down information, 50% having duplicated another team's work and 56% saying teams plan and track work differently. Its 2025 survey of 12,000 workers put time wasted searching for answers at 25%. Slack's 2024 index of 10,045 desk workers found about a third of the day going to low-value tasks, with unnecessary meetings, low-value emails and paperwork the top three, and 75% using more applications than five years earlier. Our workplace distraction statistics page covers the interruption data.

After-hours communication and its cost

Slack's State of Work 2023, from 18,149 desk workers and executives in nine countries including 3,115 in the US, found 53% feeling pressure to respond quickly to messages even after hours and 63% keeping their online status active when not working. Its late-2023 Workforce Index of 10,333 found 37% logging on outside standard hours at least weekly, 54% of them because they felt pressured rather than by choice, and that those who work after hours out of obligation score 20% lower on productivity, with 2.1 times the stress and twice the burnout of those who log off. Those who log on after hours by choice show no penalty. Pew's 2023 survey of 5,188 US workers found 55% responding to work emails or messages outside normal hours at least sometimes and 28% often or extremely often. Buffer's 2022 survey of 3,000 remote workers found 81% checking work email outside working hours, 63% on weekends and 34% on vacation. Our working hours statistics page covers the after-hours trend.

Manager communication and clarity

The communication that matters most in the engagement data is the one between a manager and each employee. Gallup's 2024 data from 79,087 US employees found 46% strongly agreeing they know what is expected of them, down from 56% in March 2020; 39% that someone cares about them as a person, down from 47%; and 30% that someone encourages their development, down from 36%. Gallup's 2015 analysis found employees who have regular meetings with their manager almost three times as likely to be engaged, engagement highest among those with some daily communication, and one in two US adults having left a job to get away from a manager. Its 2022 recommendation, from a study of 15,091 employees, is one meaningful conversation of 15 to 30 minutes per employee per week. Gallup's 2014 regression across 11,781 teams estimated the manager accounts for at least 70% of the variance in team engagement.

Pew's 2023 data show what frequency does: among workers who get feedback from their manager extremely often or often, 84% are highly satisfied with that relationship, against 36% of those who rarely or never do. Only 34% receive feedback often. Our one-on-one meeting template and employee engagement statistics page cover this in depth.

Remote and hybrid communication

Microsoft's 2022 survey of 31,102 workers found 43% of remote and 44% of hybrid employees did not feel included in meetings. Owl Labs' 2024 survey of 2,000 US workers found 86% attending online or hybrid meetings, 72% having lost time to technical problems, and 47% of employers having trained staff on mixing real-time and asynchronous communication. Gallup's 2023 panel of 18,871 US workers found hybrid employees in the office 2.6 days a week, 80% of them with no formal training for hybrid work, 73% of hybrid managers feeling unprepared, and only 20% to 30% of the hybrid workforce naming collaboration, communication or culture as a top challenge. Buffer's remote workers named collaboration and communication difficulties as a struggle at 15%, down from 17%, while 75% felt connected to colleagues and 62% worked across time zones. Pew found 65% of US workers have a close friend at work. The data say remote communication is a training gap more than a technology gap. Our return to office statistics page covers the arrangement split.

Communication and project outcomes

The one study that quantifies communication against delivery is the Project Management Institute's 2013 Pulse of the Profession report on communication, from 742 project practitioners, 148 executive sponsors and 203 business owners. It found 55.7% of unsuccessful projects cited ineffective communication as a contributing factor, that about one in five projects was unsuccessful because of it, and that $75 million of every $135 million at risk per $1 billion of project spend was attributable to communication. Highly effective communicators delivered on time 71% of the time against 37% for minimally effective ones, within budget 76% against 48%, and met goals 80% against 52%. Only one in four organizations qualified as highly effective communicators. It is a 2013 practitioner survey and the figures are derived, but it remains the only primary source for the "communication causes project failure" claim. Our project management statistics page covers the wider delivery data.

The cost figures and how they were built

Every national cost of poor communication is a self-reported hours figure multiplied by salary and headcount. The $1.2 trillion that leads most pages is from Grammarly and the Harris Poll's 2022 State of Business Communication, which asked about 1,000 knowledge workers and 250 leaders how many hours a week they lost to communication problems, got 7.47, valued it at $12,506 per worker and multiplied across the US knowledge workforce. Grammarly's 2024 edition, on a landing page without sample or dates, reports 88% of the workweek spent communicating and impacts of poor communication of 51% increased stress, 41% lower productivity and 26% missed deadlines; its 2025 edition reports 13 hours a week and $9,284 per employee. The $37 billion or $62.4 million per company figure is from a 2011 Holmes Report on 400 large companies. The 20% to 25% productivity uplift is McKinsey Global Institute's 2012 modeled potential for "interaction workers" adopting social technologies, not an observed result. None of these is a measurement of anything, and the Grammarly surveys' samples and fieldwork dates could not be verified for this page. If you need a cost, use Slack's 20% productivity penalty for obligated after-hours work or Atlassian's 25% of time searching for answers, apply your own loaded hourly rate and label it an estimate. Our employee cost calculator does that arithmetic.

What employees say would help

In Slack's 2023 survey, 67% said predictable blocks when everyone is disconnected would improve their productivity and 71% said the same working hours as teammates would. In its late-2023 index, two hours a day of meetings was the overload point, about four hours of focus time the ideal, and 20% said they lacked time to connect with colleagues at all. Microsoft's 2023 survey found 80% comfortable using AI to summarize meetings and action items. Gallup's prescriptions are one weekly conversation per employee and consistent communication, which its 2025 analysis found can raise remote employees' felt trust by nearly 30 points. The practices with evidence behind them are fewer, more specific goals, protected disconnected time, shared hours for collaboration, and a manager who talks to each person weekly. Figures to stop using

  • "Poor communication costs US businesses $1.2 trillion a year." Grammarly and Harris Poll, 2022, a self-report extrapolation from about 1,250 people. Grammarly's own later editions give different numbers.
  • "86% of employees blame communication for workplace failures." A 2011 Fierce Inc. survey of about 1,400 people, attributed online to Salesforce, HBR and others.
  • "$37 billion lost" or "$62.4 million per company." The Holmes Report, 2011, on 400 companies; sometimes attributed to a 2022 Texas A&M study that does not exist.
  • "Better communication raises productivity 20% to 25%." McKinsey's 2012 modeled upper bound for social technology adoption.
  • "28% of the workweek is spent on email." Also McKinsey 2012. Microsoft's 2023 telemetry put email at 15% of application time.
  • "Companies with effective communication are 3.5 times more likely to outperform." Towers Watson 2009 to 2011 variants, with no linked primary.
  • "Managers control 70% of engagement." Gallup's finding is that managers account for at least 70% of the variance in engagement across teams.

Key takeaways

  • The average Microsoft 365 user received 117 emails and 153 Teams messages a day in early 2025, and 60% of application time went to email, chat and meetings against 40% creating.
  • 48% of employees and 52% of leaders say work feels chaotic and fragmented; 65% say responding quickly to messages matters more than progress on priorities.
  • 53% of desk workers feel pressure to respond after hours and 63% keep their status active when not working. Those who work after hours out of obligation score 20% lower on productivity with twice the burnout.
  • 46% of US employees strongly agree they know what is expected of them, down from 56% in 2020. Employees with regular manager meetings are almost three times as likely to be engaged.
  • 84% of workers who get frequent manager feedback are highly satisfied with that relationship against 36% of those who rarely do. Only 34% get feedback often.
  • 43% of remote and 44% of hybrid employees do not feel included in meetings, and 80% of hybrid workers have had no training for hybrid work.
  • Highly effective communicators delivered projects on time 71% of the time against 37%, in PMI's 2013 study of 1,093 practitioners and sponsors.
  • The $1.2 trillion, 86% and 20% to 25% figures are from 2022, 2011 and 2012 respectively, and none is a measurement.

Frequently asked questions

How much time do employees spend communicating at work?

About 60% of Microsoft 365 users' application time went to email, chat and meetings against 40% in creation tools, per Microsoft's telemetry to March 2024. In 2023 the split was 57% against 43%, made up of 23% Teams meetings, 19% Teams chat and 15% email.

How many emails and messages does the average worker get a day?

117 emails and 153 Teams messages per weekday for the average Microsoft 365 user in the 28 days to mid-February 2025, with more than 50 messages outside core hours. Most emails were skimmed in under a minute and 85% read in under 15 seconds.

Does after-hours messaging hurt productivity?

When it is forced. Desk workers who log on after hours out of obligation scored 20% lower on productivity, with 2.1 times the stress and twice the burnout of those who log off, in Slack's survey of 10,333. Those who work after hours by choice showed no penalty. 37% log on after hours at least weekly, 54% of them under pressure.

How often should managers talk to their employees?

Weekly. Gallup's recommendation from a 2022 study of 15,091 US employees is one meaningful conversation of 15 to 30 minutes per employee per week. Employees who have regular meetings with their manager are almost three times as likely to be engaged, and 84% of workers who get frequent feedback are highly satisfied with their manager relationship.

How much does poor communication cost projects?

PMI's 2013 study of 742 practitioners, 148 sponsors and 203 business owners found $75 million of every $1 billion in project spend at risk because of ineffective communication, and about one in five projects unsuccessful for that reason. Highly effective communicators met goals 80% of the time against 52%.

Do remote and hybrid workers feel left out?

Many do in meetings. 43% of remote and 44% of hybrid employees said they did not feel included in meetings in Microsoft's 2022 survey of 31,102 workers. Only 20% to 30% of hybrid workers name communication or collaboration as a top challenge, and 80% have had no training for hybrid work.

How clear are expectations at work?

Less clear than before the pandemic. 46% of US employees strongly agreed they know what is expected of them in Gallup's 2024 data from 79,087 workers, down from 56% in March 2020. By May 2026 it had recovered to 49%.

What would improve workplace communication?

Employees in Slack's 2023 survey of 18,149 said predictable disconnected blocks at 67% and shared working hours with teammates at 71% would improve productivity. The research also supports fewer and more specific goals, a cap of two hours of meetings a day and a weekly manager conversation.
