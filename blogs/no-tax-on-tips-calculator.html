No Tax on Tips Calculator: The $25,000 Deduction Explained

P Pavan
November 13, 2026 · 11 min read

"No tax on tips" is a federal income tax deduction, not an exemption, and it has edges that matter. For tax years 2025 through 2028, a worker in one of 71 Treasury-listed tipped occupations may deduct up to $25,000 of qualified tips from income, with the cap shrinking by $100 for every $1,000 of modified adjusted gross income above $150,000 ($300,000 on a joint return). Social Security and Medicare taxes still come out of every tipped dollar, service charges and automatic gratuities do not count, and about half the states with an income tax have declined to follow the federal rule, so the state return may look very different from the federal one.

The calculator below estimates the deduction and the federal tax saving for your tips, income and filing status, and flags the state treatment. Below it: the statute in plain language (new Internal Revenue Code section 224), which tips and which jobs qualify, how to claim it on Schedule 1-A, what changed on the 2026 W-2, how it interacts with the overtime deduction and with FICA, the state conformity map, and what employers must report. For the parallel overtime rule, see the no tax on overtime calculator.

No tax on tips calculator

Internal Revenue Code section 224: deduction equals qualified tips up to $25,000, reduced by $100 for each $1,000 of modified adjusted gross income over $150,000 ($300,000 joint). Tax years 2025 to 2028. Married taxpayers must file jointly. Self-employed workers cannot deduct more than the net income of the tipped business. The tax saving shown is deduction x marginal rate and is an estimate; the deduction does not reduce Social Security or Medicare tax. Not tax advice.

The rules in plain language

Section 70201 of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (Public Law 119-21, July 4, 2025) added section 224 to the Internal Revenue Code. It allows an individual to deduct qualified tips received during the tax year, subject to five limits:

  1. A $25,000 cap per return, not per person; a married couple who both earn tips share one cap.
  2. A phase-out of $100 for each $1,000 (or fraction of $1,000) by which modified adjusted gross income exceeds $150,000, or $300,000 on a joint return. The deduction reaches zero at $400,000 single and $550,000 joint for someone with the full $25,000; it reaches zero sooner for smaller tip amounts.
  3. Four tax years: 2025, 2026, 2027 and 2028. No deduction for tax years beginning after December 31, 2028 unless Congress extends it.
  4. Occupation: the tips must be received in an occupation that customarily and regularly received tips on or before December 31, 2024, as listed by the Treasury.
  5. Business type: tips received in a specified service trade or business under section 199A(d)(2) (health, law, accounting, actuarial science, performing arts, consulting, athletics, financial services, brokerage, and businesses whose principal asset is the reputation or skill of employees) do not qualify, and neither do tips received by an employee of such a business.

The deduction is available whether or not the taxpayer itemizes: it is a below-the-line deduction taken on the new Schedule 1-A and carried to Form 1040. A Social Security number is required for the taxpayer, and married taxpayers must file jointly. Self-employed workers (a hairdresser renting a chair, a rideshare driver) may claim it but not beyond the net income of the business in which the tips were earned.

Which tips qualify

Qualified tips are cash tips, which the statute defines to include tips paid by card or through an app and tips received under a tip-sharing or pooling arrangement, that are reported to the employer or on a return. Four conditions, drawn from the statute and the final regulations (Treasury Decision 10044, April 10, 2026, effective June 12, 2026):

  • The payment is voluntary: made without negotiation and with no consequence for not paying.
  • The amount is set by the payer, not the business.
  • It is not a service charge: an automatic 18% gratuity added to a large party's bill, a mandatory delivery fee or a banquet service charge is wages, not a tip, even if the restaurant passes it to staff.
  • It is reported: on Form W-2, Form 1099-NEC or 1099-K, another statement furnished to the worker, or on Form 4137 for unreported tips. Tips never reported anywhere cannot be deducted.

Tips paid in digital assets are not "cash" for this purpose under the final regulations. Tips a manager takes a share of unlawfully are still the server's tips for tax purposes, but the employer has a wage and hour problem; see the wage theft statistics page.

The 71 qualifying occupations

Treasury published a proposed list of 68 occupations on September 22, 2025 and a final list of 71 with the April 2026 regulations, adding floral designers, visual artists and gas pump attendants. How to claim it: Schedule 1-A and the 2026 W-2

For 2025 returns filed in early 2026, the IRS introduced Schedule 1-A, Additional Deductions, with Part II for qualified tips, Part III for qualified overtime, Part IV for car loan interest and Part V for the senior deduction; the total flows to Form 1040 line 13b. Because the 2025 Forms W-2 and 1099 were not changed, workers computed their 2025 qualified tips from their own records and employer statements using the Schedule 1-A instructions, and Notice 2025-62 gave employers penalty relief for 2025 information reporting.

For 2026 the W-2 changed. Box 12 has three new codes: TP (total cash tips reported to the employer), TT (total qualified overtime compensation) and TA (employer Trump account contributions). Box 14 is split into 14a (other) and 14b, which carries the Treasury Tipped Occupation Code. Employers report all cash tips in code TP; the worker applies the cap and phase-out on Schedule 1-A. The 2026 W-2 is due to employees and the Social Security Administration by February 1, 2027. Workers with tips on a 1099-NEC or 1099-K (rideshare, app-based delivery) use those forms plus the occupation code.

Withholding was not changed by the law: employers withhold income tax on tips as before, so the deduction shows up as a larger refund rather than a bigger paycheck, unless the worker adjusts Form W-4 (the IRS said it would update withholding tables for 2026 to reflect the deductions, and tipped workers can also use the W-4 deductions line).

What does not change: FICA, reporting, tip credits

  • Social Security and Medicare tax (7.65% employee, 7.65% employer) applies to every tipped dollar. The deduction is income tax only. A server with $16,000 of tips still pays $1,224 in FICA on them.
  • Tip reporting rules are unchanged: employees must report cash tips of $20 or more a month to the employer (Form 4070 or the employer's system), and employers must withhold and pay FICA on them. Under-reporting now costs the worker the deduction as well as creating the usual exposure.
  • The federal tip credit for employers (section 45B) and the FLSA tip credit that allows a $2.13 cash wage for tipped employees are unaffected. State tipped minimum wages are also unaffected; see minimum wage by state 2027.
  • Earned income for the earned income tax credit and the child tax credit still includes tips; the deduction does not reduce earned income, so low-income tipped workers do not lose credits by claiming it.
  • Service charges remain wages subject to full withholding and do not qualify.

The companion no tax on overtime deduction (section 225) allows up to $12,500 ($25,000 joint) for the premium half of time-and-a-half pay, with the same $150,000/$300,000 phase-out and the same 2025 to 2028 window. A worker can claim both; each has its own cap. Treasury reported in June 2026 that more than 29 million 2025 returns claimed the overtime deduction, averaging more than $3,100.

State conformity

States with an income tax start from federal adjusted gross income or federal taxable income and then decide whether to follow each federal change. Because section 224 is a below-the-line deduction, states that start from federal AGI do not pick it up automatically; they must legislate. As of mid-2026, Ballotpedia counted 19 of the 41 states that tax wage income as conforming to the tip deduction and 21 as declining, with Georgia partial. The known positions:

What employers must do

  1. Report tips in box 12 code TP on 2026 W-2s and the occupation code in box 14b; payroll vendors have added both fields. Report total cash tips; do not apply the cap.
  2. Classify service charges correctly. Automatic gratuities are wages (Revenue Ruling 2012-18) and must not be reported as tips. Restaurants that have blurred the line now have employees with a reason to care.
  3. Assign occupation codes to tipped roles in the HRIS. An employee with two roles (bartender and manager) gets the code for the role in which tips are received.
  4. Furnish statements to non-employee workers (1099 contractors) showing qualified tips and the occupation code.
  5. Do not change FICA or withholding on tips unless the employee submits a new W-4.
  6. Explain it to staff. A one-page note that the deduction appears at filing, that FICA still applies, and that reporting all tips is now in the employee's financial interest tends to improve tip reporting compliance, which reduces the employer's own exposure under section 3121(q).

Worked examples

Example 1: single server. Wages $38,000, W-2 tips $16,000, MAGI $54,000, no phase-out. Deduction $16,000 on Schedule 1-A Part II. At a 12% marginal rate the federal saving is $1,920. FICA on tips, $1,224, is unchanged. In Arizona the state return also deducts $16,000; in California the state taxes it in full.

Example 2: married bartender, high household income. Tips $31,000; household MAGI $322,000 filing jointly. Cap first: $25,000. Phase-out: $22,000 over the $300,000 threshold is 22 steps of $1,000, so $2,200 is removed. Deduction $22,800; the remaining $8,200 of tips is taxed normally. At 24% the saving is $5,472.

Example 3: salon owner renting chairs. A self-employed stylist with $9,000 of card and cash tips and $7,500 of net profit from the business after expenses can deduct only $7,500, the net income limit. Tips must appear on the 1099-K or the stylist's own return.

Example 4: the service charge trap. A banquet server receives $14,000 from mandatory 20% service charges distributed by the hotel and $3,000 in voluntary tips. Only the $3,000 qualifies; the $14,000 is wages and was always reported in box 1. Employees in this position have asked employers to convert mandatory charges to suggested gratuities, which is lawful but changes the employer's revenue accounting and the FLSA tip credit math.

Key takeaways

  • Section 224 lets workers in 71 Treasury-listed tipped occupations deduct up to $25,000 of qualified tips a year for 2025 through 2028, reduced by $100 for every $1,000 of modified AGI over $150,000 ($300,000 joint); married taxpayers must file jointly.
  • Qualified tips are voluntary, set by the customer, not service charges or automatic gratuities, and reported on a W-2, 1099 or Form 4137; tips in digital assets and tips from specified service businesses (law, health, consulting, finance, performing arts) do not count.
  • The deduction is income tax only: Social Security and Medicare tax of 7.65% still applies to every tipped dollar, tip reporting rules are unchanged, and earned income for credits still includes tips.
  • Claim it on Schedule 1-A Part II (flows to Form 1040 line 13b); 2026 W-2s show total cash tips in box 12 code TP and the occupation code in box 14b, due February 1, 2027.
  • The final regulations (T.D. 10044, April 10, 2026) set the list at 71 occupations in eight categories, from servers and bartenders to electricians, rideshare drivers, tattoo artists and digital content creators.
  • States split: Arizona, Idaho, Oregon and North Dakota conform; Colorado, Georgia and New York partly; California, Illinois, Connecticut, Maine and DC tax the tips in full; nine states have no wage income tax.
  • Employers report, not compute: total tips in code TP, occupation codes assigned per role, service charges kept out of tips, no change to FICA or withholding.

Frequently asked questions

How does the no tax on tips deduction work?

Workers in a Treasury-listed tipped occupation deduct their qualified tips, up to $25,000 a year, from income on Schedule 1-A for tax years 2025 through 2028. The cap falls by $100 for every $1,000 of modified adjusted gross income above $150,000 ($300,000 joint). It is an income tax deduction only; Social Security and Medicare taxes still apply.

Is no tax on tips really no tax?

No. It is a deduction from federal taxable income, capped at $25,000 and phased out at higher incomes. Payroll taxes of 7.65% still come out of tips, state income tax may still apply (California, Illinois, New York and others tax the tips in full), and tips above the cap remain taxable.

Which jobs qualify for the tip deduction?

The IRS list has 71 occupations in eight categories: food and beverage (servers, bartenders, cooks, dishwashers), entertainment (dealers, musicians, DJs, content creators), hospitality (bellhops, concierges, housekeepers), home services (electricians, plumbers, landscapers), personal services (nannies, tutors, photographers), appearance and wellness (barbers, manicurists, massage therapists, tattoo artists), recreation (caddies, instructors, guides) and transportation (rideshare and taxi drivers, delivery workers, parking attendants).

Do service charges count as tips for the deduction?

No. Mandatory service charges and automatic gratuities, such as an 18% charge added to a large party's bill, are wages, not tips, under Revenue Ruling 2012-18 and the final regulations. Only voluntary payments whose amount the customer sets qualify.

How do I claim the tip deduction on my tax return?

File Schedule 1-A with Form 1040 and enter qualified tips in Part II; the total carries to line 13b. For 2026 your W-2 shows total cash tips in box 12 code TP and your occupation code in box 14b. For 2025, W-2s were unchanged, so you used your own records and employer statements. Married taxpayers must file jointly.

Does the tip deduction apply to state taxes?

Only in states that have conformed: Arizona, Idaho, Oregon and North Dakota, with partial treatment in Colorado, Georgia and New York. California, Illinois, Connecticut, Maine, the District of Columbia and most other income-tax states tax the tips in full and require the federal deduction to be added back. Nine states have no wage income tax.

What is the income limit for no tax on tips?

The deduction starts shrinking at $150,000 of modified adjusted gross income ($300,000 joint), by $100 for every $1,000 over. With the full $25,000 of tips it disappears at $400,000 single or $550,000 joint; with smaller tip amounts it disappears sooner.

When does the tip deduction expire?

After tax year 2028. It applies to 2025, 2026, 2027 and 2028 returns unless Congress extends it. The overtime deduction has the same four-year window.
