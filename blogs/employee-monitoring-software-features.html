Employee Monitoring Software Features: A Buyer's Checklist

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

Employee monitoring software features fall into three groups: what the agent records (activity, applications, websites, screens, keystrokes, files, location), what it does with the record (scores, alerts, blocking), and the controls that limit it (visibility, self-access, blurring, retention). Most buyers compare the first group and ignore the third, which is backwards. Every feature you switch on is something you have to justify, secure and eventually delete.

This checklist takes each feature in turn: what it collects, what it costs in privacy and legal exposure, and who actually needs it. Where we have a deeper guide to a feature, it is linked. To see which tools offer what, use our comparison of the best employee monitoring software.

The short answer

Most teams need three features: application and website use, active and idle time, and reports at team level. That answers where time goes and whether workload is sane.

Screenshots, recording and keystroke content belong to billing proof, regulated work and defined investigations, not everyday management.

Judge a tool by what it lets you switch off, and whether employees can see that it is off.

Activity, applications and websites

Active and idle time is the base layer. The agent checks every few seconds whether the keyboard or mouse produced an event. It measures presence at the machine, not effort: reading a contract and doing nothing look identical. Idle timers that penalize thinking are one of the harms the Center for Democracy and Technology's research on bossware identifies.

Application tracking records which programs ran and for how long. It is low exposure and answers real questions about tool use and license waste. Website tracking is where exposure jumps: full URLs reveal health, finance and legal searches, and window titles carry client names and the subject lines of open messages. New York's notice law names internet usage directly. Our guide to employee internet monitoring explains the difference between network capture and an endpoint agent. If you need it, collect domains, not full paths.

Productivity scores classify each app and site as productive or not, per team. They help at team level and mislead at individual level, and ActivTrak's reviewers describe hours spent on the classification itself. The broader category is covered in our guide to user activity monitoring, which separates security monitoring from productivity tracking.

Screenshots, recording and live view

Screen capture is the most invasive common feature because it records content, not events: personal messages, banking, health portals and customers' data all land in the image. Blurring helps, but it is a mitigation, not a permission. Our guide to computer screen monitoring covers the narrow cases where it is defensible: regulated session recording, privileged access, third-party contractors, and a specific investigation with an end date.

Tools differ sharply here. Time Doctor includes screenshots on every plan, and they cannot be turned off. Hubstaff makes them optional per project, one to three per ten minutes, and blurrable. Insightful sells screen recording and on-demand screenshots as $4 add-ons, and ActivTrak offers screenshots only through its Screen Details add-on. Kickidler's live screen viewing of many employees at once, with remote control in its full tier, is intrusive by design and unlawful without notice in several states.

Keystroke counts and keystroke logging

The distinction to insist on is counting versus capturing. A keystroke count records how much was typed and feeds an activity score; it reveals no content and is easy to game. Keystroke content logging records what was typed, which sooner or later includes a password, a medical detail or a private message. Illinois treats typing rhythm used to identify a user as a biometric.

Teramind, Controlio, Kickidler and Veriato log keystrokes; Time Champ sells a keylogger as an add-on; ActivTrak and Insightful do not log keystrokes at all. Our keystroke logging guide has a notice checker by state. Outside a documented security investigation with legal sign-off, leave content capture off.

Files, DLP, blocking and communications

These are security features, and they answer a different question from productivity tracking. File and USB events show copies, uploads, prints and removable media writes, which is what data exfiltration looks like. Data loss prevention goes further: it classifies content and can block an upload before it completes. It is the only feature on this page that prevents anything rather than recording it.

Teramind's DLP plan ($32 per seat) adds content classification and exfiltration controls, and its rules engine can alert, block or lock a session. Veriato captures email, chat and files and adds risk scoring. CurrentWare blocks websites and applications and controls USB devices. If your real worry is a departing employee taking client data, a productivity dashboard will not help.

GPS, attendance and time

GPS location on a phone or vehicle draws the strongest public objection of any tracking Pew Research Center tested in April 2023: 61% of US adults oppose employers using AI to track workers' movements. Consent runs with the vehicle, and a tracked company phone goes home with the person, so the feature to look for is one that stops reporting at the end of the shift. Our guide to employee GPS tracking covers the state rules. Hubstaff and Time Champ offer mobile GPS; Insightful has no mobile app.

Attendance and time tracking are the least controversial features in the category. Clock times, breaks and hours against projects feed payroll and billing. If hours are the whole requirement, you may not need monitoring at all; see our guide to employee attendance tracking.

The controls that matter more than the features

  1. Visible agent. Insightful, Teramind, Controlio, Monitask and Veriato offer stealth modes, and Time Doctor and Time Champ have silent options; ActivTrak, Hubstaff and DeskTime run visibly. Our guide to visible vs stealth monitoring explains why the visible option is the one to choose.
  2. Employee self-access. People should see their own record. Insightful's stealth mode removes the employee dashboard.
  3. Private time and exclusions. DeskTime has a private time switch; Hubstaff records only while the timer runs.
  4. Blurring and frequency. If you capture screens at all, choose blurred images at the lowest frequency that serves the purpose.
  5. Retention you can shorten. History ranges from one to two months on Monitask to 24 months on Insightful. Short retention protects employees and limits breach exposure.
  6. Permanent off switches. Ask which collection categories can be disabled and stay disabled, and whether employees can see that they are off.

Features drive price as well as exposure: recording, extra screenshots and DLP are the add-ons that inflate a bill. Key takeaways

  • Most teams need only application and website use, active and idle time, and team-level reports.
  • Exposure rises from activity data (low) through full URLs and screenshots (high) to keystroke content, webcam and microphone (severe).
  • Count keystrokes if you must; do not capture their content outside a documented investigation.
  • DLP is the only feature that prevents a loss rather than recording it, and it belongs to security teams.
  • GPS draws the strongest public objection; choose tools that stop reporting at the end of the shift.
  • Judge a tool by its controls: a visible agent, employee self-access, blurring, private time and retention you can shorten.

Frequently asked questions

What features should employee monitoring software have?

For most teams: application and website tracking, active and idle time, team-level reports, a visible agent, employee access to their own data, and retention you can shorten. Screenshots, recording, keystroke logging and DLP are for specific billing, regulatory or security needs.

Which monitoring feature is most invasive?

Keystroke content logging, webcam and microphone capture, and continuous screen recording. They record content rather than events, so they capture passwords, personal messages and other people's data along with work.

Do all monitoring tools take screenshots?

No. ActivTrak takes screenshots only with a paid add-on, DeskTime only on its Premium plan, and Hubstaff makes them optional per project. Time Doctor includes them on every plan and they cannot be turned off.

What is the difference between keystroke counting and keystroke logging?

Counting records how many keys were pressed in an interval and reveals no content. Logging records the characters typed. Counting is an activity signal; logging is a store of passwords and personal data.

Does employee monitoring software include data loss prevention?

Only the security tools. Teramind's DLP plan classifies content and blocks exfiltration, Time Champ sells DLP as an add-on, and CurrentWare controls USB devices. Productivity analytics tools such as ActivTrak and Insightful do not prevent data loss.

Can employee monitoring software track location?

Some can, through a mobile app. Hubstaff and Time Champ offer GPS for mobile teams, and Time Doctor has an Android app. Insightful has no mobile app or GPS.
