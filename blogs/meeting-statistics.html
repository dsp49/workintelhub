Meeting Statistics (2026)

P Pavan
October 16, 2026 · 12 min read

Seventy-two percent of meetings are rated ineffective by the people in them, according to Atlassian's 2024 survey of 5,000 knowledge workers. The heaviest quarter of Microsoft 365 users spend 7.5 hours a week in meetings, and the time the average Teams user spends in them rose 252% between February 2020 and February 2022. Meetings that start after 8pm rose 16% in the year to February 2025. Two hours a day is the point at which most desk workers say they have too many.

The pages that rank for this question are built on three untraceable numbers: 11 million meetings a day from a 1998 phone survey, $37 billion wasted with no source, and 23 hours a week for executives from a 1960s comparison. This page separates telemetry from surveys, uses Microsoft's Work Trend Index series with its caveats, the NBER and Nature studies of three million users, Slack's 10,333-worker index, Atlassian's two surveys and the MIT Sloan meeting-free day study, and explains how every dollar figure was derived.

How much time goes to meetings

Two kinds of data measure meeting time, and they disagree for good reasons. Telemetry counts calendar and call activity in one vendor's software; surveys ask people to estimate.

The figure most pages lead with, 23 hours a week, is for executives and comes from a 2007 MIT Sloan review that estimated about 6 hours for the average employee. The Reclaim figure is for people who installed a calendar-management tool because their calendars were full. The honest range for a typical knowledge worker is five to ten hours a week, with the heavy quartile at double that. Our time management statistics page covers how that fits into the rest of the day.

The trend since 2020

Microsoft's Work Trend Index tracked the pandemic shift in its own telemetry, and the figures are often stacked as though they were one series. They are not.

  • February 2020 to February 2021: time in Teams meetings more than doubled, average meeting length rose from 35 to 45 minutes, and after-hours chats per person rose 42%.
  • February 2020 to February 2022: weekly time in Teams meetings per user up 252%, number of weekly meetings up 153%, overlapping meetings per person up 46% in the year to 2022, and 60% of meetings under 15 minutes.
  • To March 2023: Teams meetings and calls per week up 192% on February 2020; the same report also says "3x," which does not match.
  • To February 2025: meetings starting after 8pm up 16% on the year, 57% of meetings ad hoc with no invitation, 30% spanning multiple time zones, and a 122% spike in PowerPoint edits in the ten minutes before a meeting.

The independent check is DeFilippis, Impink, Singell, Polzer and Sadun's 2020 NBER paper on 3.1 million users in 16 metros. After lockdowns, meetings per person rose 12.9% and attendees per meeting 13.5%, but average length fell 20.1%, so total daily meeting time fell 11.5% while the workday lengthened by 48.5 minutes. Yang and colleagues' 2021 Nature Human Behavior study of 61,182 Microsoft employees found remote work cut total meeting and call hours by about 5% while unscheduled calls rose and cross-group collaboration fell by about a quarter. More meetings, shorter meetings, and a longer day is the pattern the data support.

How many meetings are considered unproductive

Atlassian's 2024 survey of 5,000 knowledge workers on four continents found 72% of meetings rated ineffective, 78% of respondents expected to attend so many meetings it was hard to get work done, 76% feeling drained on heavy-meeting days, 77% frequently attending meetings that end by scheduling another, 54% leaving without clear next steps, and 80% saying they would be more productive with less meeting time. By purpose, brainstorming and decision-making meetings were rated ineffective 75% of the time and status updates 70%.

Slack's Workforce Index of 10,333 desk workers in six countries, fielded in August and September 2023, found 27% saying they spend too much time in meetings, rising to 55% of executives, and identified two hours a day as the point at which most workers feel overburdened, with about four hours of focus time the stated ideal. Microsoft's 2023 survey of 31,000 workers ranked inefficient meetings the top productivity disruptor, found 55% unclear on next steps at meeting end, and 35% believing they would not be missed in most of their meetings. In Microsoft's 2022 telemetry, 42% of participants sent an email or chat during a meeting in an average week, and declined invitations rose 84% in two years. Doodle's 2018 survey of 5,528 professionals, the source of the "two hours a week in pointless meetings" line, found 71% losing time weekly to unnecessary or canceled meetings.

Cost estimates, and how they are built

Every dollar figure on this topic is hours in bad meetings multiplied by compensation and headcount. None is measured. Doodle's 2019 report put the US cost of poorly organized meetings at $399 billion a year, from two self-reported hours a week lost per professional; the live page now shows "$399.01" with no unit, so the billion wording rests on the original press release. Atlassian's 2024 State of Teams found workers in poor meeting cultures spent 50% more time in unnecessary meetings than on high-priority work, and Asana's 2023 survey of 9,615 workers put the loss at 3.6 hours a week. Reclaim's calendar data found 42% of one-on-ones rescheduled and 30% canceled each week, each reschedule costing about ten minutes. If you want a cost, take one of those hour figures, your own loaded hourly rate and your headcount, and label the result an estimate. Our employee cost calculator does the arithmetic.

Remote, hybrid and in-person

Owl Labs' 2024 survey of 2,000 US workers found 86% attending online or hybrid meetings and 83% in-person ones, 72% had lost time or started late to technical problems, and 58% block calendar time to protect it from meetings. Its 2025 survey found about six minutes lost per hybrid meeting to setup, 27% losing ten minutes or more, and in-office workers the most likely to feel left out of hybrid meetings, at 64% against 29% of hybrid and 7% of remote workers. Microsoft's 2022 survey of 31,102 workers found 43% of remote and 44% of hybrid employees did not feel included in meetings and only 27% of organizations had hybrid meeting etiquette. Buffer's 2022 remote workers preferred camera on by 62%. Our return to office statistics page covers how much of the workforce is in each arrangement.

After-hours meetings and fragmentation

Microsoft's June 2025 telemetry found half of meetings fall between 9 and 11am and 1 and 3pm, Tuesdays carry 23% of meetings and Fridays 16%, one in ten is booked last-minute, 29% of active workers are back in email by 10pm and 40% of those online at 6am are reading it. The interruption figure that gets repeated, every two minutes or 275 a day, is computed over 24 hours for the top 20% of users by message volume, not for the average worker in an eight-hour day. Slack found 37% of desk workers log on outside standard hours at least weekly, 54% of them because they feel pressured, and those who work after hours out of obligation score 20% lower on productivity with twice the burnout. Microsoft's 2021 Human Factors Lab study of 14 people found stress markers accumulating across four back-to-back meetings and staying flat when ten-minute breaks were inserted, a result worth knowing and too small to lean on. Our workplace distraction statistics page covers the interruption data in full.

Meeting-free days

Laker, Pereira, Budhwar and Malik's 2022 study in MIT Sloan Management Review followed 76 companies with more than 1,000 employees each that introduced one to five meeting-free days a week. Forty-seven percent chose two days, 35% three, 11% four and 7% eliminated meetings. With one free day, autonomy, communication, engagement and satisfaction improved and stress fell; the authors judged three days produced the best results, with productivity declining when meetings were removed entirely. Buffer found 37% of remote workers' companies offered no-meeting times and 36% of those without wished they did. Atlassian's internal experiment replaced meetings with recorded video for 43% of employees over two weeks, freeing about 5,000 hours. Our guide to focus blocks covers how to protect the time.

What makes meetings effective

In Atlassian's data, 79% said agendas make meetings more productive, 62% often attend meetings whose invitation states no goal, 80% said most meetings could be done in half the time and 43% said a few people dominate. Owl Labs found 70% consider a first meeting at 8am or earlier too early and 82% want meetings to end by 4pm. Slack found productive workers 1.6 times as likely to block time for tasks, and 71% calling late afternoon the worst time for work. Microsoft found 80% comfortable using AI to summarize meetings and action items, and 57% finding it hard to catch up after joining late. The practices with evidence are an agenda with a stated goal, a hard stop at two hours of meetings a day, breaks between back-to-back calls, and at least one protected day. Figures to stop using

  • "11 million meetings a day in the US." From a 1998 Verizon Conferencing white paper based on a 1,300-person phone survey, which itself cites unnamed "other data." The 55 million a week and 220 million a year figures are multiplications of it.
  • "$37 billion wasted on meetings." No source. Often attributed to BLS, which has never published it. The 1998 paper's "37% of employee time in meetings" may be the origin.
  • "$399 billion" is Doodle's 2018 self-report extrapolation, not a measured cost.
  • "71% of meetings are unproductive." From HBR's 2017 survey of 182 senior managers, about their experience, not meetings generally.
  • "Executives spend 23 hours a week in meetings." A literature estimate for executives, applied to employees.
  • "Meetings are up 252% / 192% / 3x." Different Microsoft metrics at different dates. Do not chain them.
  • "Interrupted every two minutes." Top 20% of users, over 24 hours.
  • "31 hours a month in unproductive meetings." From an undated Atlassian infographic that no longer exists.

Key takeaways

  • The heaviest quarter of Microsoft 365 users spend 7.5 hours a week in meetings, and 57% of their software time goes to communicating rather than creating.
  • Weekly Teams meeting time per user rose 252% and meeting count 153% between February 2020 and February 2022. Independent data on 3.1 million users found more, shorter meetings and a workday 48.5 minutes longer.
  • 72% of meetings are rated ineffective by 5,000 knowledge workers, 54% end without clear next steps and 80% say most could be done in half the time.
  • Two hours a day of meetings is the point at which most desk workers feel overburdened; the stated ideal is four hours of focus time.
  • Every dollar cost figure is hours multiplied by pay. Doodle's $399 billion is a 2018 self-report extrapolation; the $37 billion figure has no source at all.
  • Meetings starting after 8pm rose 16% in the year to February 2025, and 29% of workers are back in email by 10pm.
  • In 76 large companies, one meeting-free day a week improved autonomy, engagement and satisfaction and cut stress; three days produced the best results.
  • 43% of remote and 44% of hybrid employees do not feel included in meetings, and 64% of in-office workers feel left out of hybrid ones.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours a week does the average employee spend in meetings?

Five to ten hours for a typical knowledge worker. The heaviest 25% of Microsoft 365 users log 7.5 hours a week in Microsoft's telemetry, and 75% of remote workers self-report one to ten hours in Buffer's survey of 3,000. The 23-hour figure is a literature estimate for executives.

Have meetings increased since 2020?

Yes. Weekly Teams meeting time per user rose 252% and the number of meetings 153% between February 2020 and February 2022, per Microsoft. Independent data on 3.1 million users found meetings per person up 12.9% but 20.1% shorter after lockdowns, with the workday 48.5 minutes longer.

What percentage of meetings are unproductive?

72% of meetings were rated ineffective by 5,000 knowledge workers in Atlassian's 2024 survey, and 54% ended without clear next steps. 71% of professionals lose time every week to unnecessary or canceled meetings, per Doodle's 2018 survey of 5,528.

How much do wasted meetings cost US businesses?

No measured figure exists. Doodle estimated about $399 billion a year from two self-reported hours a week lost per professional in 2018. The widely quoted $37 billion has no traceable source. Any cost figure is hours multiplied by compensation and headcount.

Do meeting-free days work?

In a study of 76 companies with more than 1,000 employees each, one meeting-free day a week improved autonomy, communication, engagement and satisfaction and reduced stress. The researchers found the best results at three meeting-free days and declining productivity when meetings were removed entirely.

How many meetings is too many?

Slack's Workforce Index of 10,333 desk workers found more than two hours of meetings a day is the point at which most say they have too many. Workers want about four hours of focus time a day, and those with too many meetings are twice as likely to lack it.

Did remote work increase meetings?

It changed their shape. Meetings per person rose 12.9% and attendees per meeting 13.5% after lockdowns, but average length fell 20.1% and total daily meeting time fell 11.5%, per an NBER study of 3.1 million users. At Microsoft, firm-wide remote work cut meeting and call hours by about 5% while unscheduled calls rose.

Are after-hours meetings increasing?

Yes. Meetings starting after 8pm rose 16% year on year in Microsoft's telemetry through February 2025, after-hours chats rose 15%, and 29% of active workers were back in their inbox by 10pm. 37% of desk workers log on outside standard hours at least weekly, 54% of them under pressure.
