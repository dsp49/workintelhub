Time Tracking Software Features: What to Look For

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 7 min read

The features that matter most in time tracking software are the ones that keep people tracking (low-friction capture), the ones that make the record usable (approvals, an audit trail and clean exports), and the ones your use case needs on top: invoicing for client billing, overtime and break rules for hourly payroll, budgets for projects. Everything else is secondary.

This checklist sets out what to look for in each area, why it matters, and the tier where the feature usually sits, using the October 2026 plan ladders from our review of twelve time tracking tools. Use it to write your shortlist before you open a single pricing page.

The short answer

Decide which kind of tool you are buying first. A billing tracker, a time clock, an automatic tracker and a tracker with monitoring have different feature sets; comparing across kinds is the most common buying mistake.

Then check five things on every shortlist: how entries get made, approvals, an edit history, export format, and which tier carries the features you need.

Approvals are the usual upgrade trigger. They sit above the entry plan on most tools.

Step one: pick the kind of tool

Capture: how entries get made

Time tracking fails in one way: people stop doing it. Capture features decide whether they keep going.

  • One-click timer and manual entry. The baseline. Check that the timer runs on every device your team uses: web, desktop, mobile and a browser extension.
  • Tracking inside the tools people already use. Everhour adds timers to tasks in Asana, Jira, ClickUp and monday.com, so nobody opens a separate app.
  • Automatic capture. Timely's Memory app and Toggl's desktop timeline record activity privately for the user, who approves the draft. DeskTime and Time Champ classify activity for the employer, which is a form of monitoring and needs notice.
  • Idle detection and offline tracking. Useful where laptops sleep or connections drop; Time Champ lists offline tracking and time zone support.
  • Kiosk. A shared tablet or PC where staff clock in with a PIN, photo or face scan. Jibble, Clockify, TimeCamp and QuickBooks Time offer one. Facial recognition is a biometric system with notice and consent duties in Illinois, Texas and Washington; see the biometric time clock entry.
  • GPS and geofencing. GPS records where someone clocked in; geofencing only allows clock-in inside an area. Both suit field and site work, not office staff. The GPS tracking guide covers the obligations.

Records: features that make the hours usable

For non-exempt employees the time record is a legal document: the Fair Labor Standards Act requires hours worked each day and each workweek. That makes the following features requirements, not extras, for anyone running hourly payroll.

  1. Timesheet approvals. A manager signs off before payroll or billing uses the hours. They are on Clockify Standard, Toggl Premium, Harvest Enterprise, TimeCamp Ultimate, Everhour Team, Hubstaff, Time Doctor, QuickBooks Time and Jibble's free plan.
  2. An audit trail. If employees can edit their own entries, you need a record of who changed what and when. My Hours puts approvals with an audit log on Pro; QuickBooks Time Elite adds timesheet signatures.
  3. Overtime by workweek. Federal overtime is owed per workweek, never averaged over a two-week pay period. The tool should calculate it that way, and handle daily overtime where state law requires it.
  4. Neutral rounding. Rounding is allowed only where it is neutral over time. If the tool rounds clock times, check the rule; the time card calculator explains the arithmetic.
  5. Break rules you can check. A tool that auto-deducts breaks needs checking against state meal and rest break rules. QuickBooks Time alerts managers to approaching overtime but does not enforce break rules by state; Jibble calculates overtime and paid breaks.
  6. A payroll export or integration. Check whether the sync carries payroll items or only invoices, because they are different. QuickBooks Time is built for QuickBooks payroll; Hubstaff pays through PayPal, Wise, Gusto and Deel.

Whether salaried staff need any of this depends on their exemption status; see do you have to track hours for salaried employees.

Billing and project features

  • Billable rates and amounts. Toggl puts billable rates on Starter; Clockify removed them from its free plan in April 2026.
  • Invoicing. Harvest turns time and expenses into invoices with online payment on every plan, free included. TimeCamp adds invoicing on Starter at $3.99, Clockify on Standard at $5.49. Toggl Track has none.
  • Budgets and estimates. A budget is only useful if the person doing the work sees it. Everhour puts the estimate on the task; Harvest, Toggl and Clockify show it in dashboards.
  • Profitability and labor cost. Toggl Premium and Harvest Enterprise report cost against revenue.

The billable hours calculator covers the utilization and realization math these features feed.

Trust: how the tool treats the person tracking

Adoption depends on whether tracking benefits the person doing it. Look for reports people can see for their own time, a private timeline that only they see until they submit, and a private-time switch (DeskTime has one). If you need screenshots or activity levels, check that they are optional and configurable by team: Hubstaff makes screenshots optional at one to three per ten minutes, DeskTime keeps them to Premium, and Toggl Track and Timely refuse them by design. Activity scores reward keyboard and mouse input rather than output, which is a known weakness.

If monitoring is the point, compare tools on the employee monitoring software shortlist instead, and read the rollout guide before launch.

Admin, data and exit

  • Export format. CSV or Excel export is what lets you leave. Clockify's free plan has exported PDF only since April 2026, 31 days at a time.
  • Report history. Some plans cap how far back reports reach; Clockify's free plan shows 31 days at a time, and Time Champ keeps a shorter detailed history below Enterprise.
  • Platforms. Linux, Chromebook and mobile coverage vary. Hubstaff and Time Doctor run on Chromebook; Harvest and Timely have no Linux app in our review.
  • SSO and permissions. SSO is on Toggl Premium, Harvest Enterprise, Clockify Enterprise and Everhour's standard paid plan.
  • Self-hosting. Kimai is open source and self-hosted; TimeCamp offers self-hosting on Enterprise from 50 users.

Key takeaways

  • Decide the kind of tool first: billing tracker, time clock, automatic tracker or tracker with monitoring.
  • Low-friction capture is the feature that decides whether tracking survives: one-click timers, tracking inside project tools, or private automatic capture.
  • For hourly payroll, approvals, an audit trail, overtime by workweek, neutral rounding and break checks are requirements.
  • Approvals usually sit above the entry plan, on Clockify Standard, Toggl Premium, Harvest Enterprise or TimeCamp Ultimate.
  • Check export format before you buy: CSV or Excel export is what lets you leave.
  • Price the tier that carries every must-have, not the entry plan.

Frequently asked questions

What features should time tracking software have?

At minimum: a timer and manual entry on every device your team uses, reports, and CSV or Excel export. Add timesheet approvals and an edit history if payroll or billing depends on the hours, invoicing if you bill clients, and overtime, break rules, kiosk or GPS if you pay hourly staff.

Do I need timesheet approvals?

If someone other than the person tracking depends on the record, such as payroll, client billing or a manager sign-off, usually yes. Solo users and trust-based teams can skip them. Approvals typically sit on the second or third tier.

What is the difference between a time tracker and a time clock?

A time tracker records hours against clients, projects or tasks, usually for billing or budgets. A time clock records shifts, breaks and overtime for payroll, often with GPS and a kiosk. Some tools, such as Clockify and TimeCamp, do both on different tiers.

Is automatic time tracking a form of monitoring?

It depends on who sees the data. Tools such as Timely and Toggl's timeline keep the record private to the user until they approve a timesheet. Tools that classify activity by productivity for the employer, such as DeskTime and Time Champ, are a form of monitoring and need notice.

Why does export format matter in time tracking software?

Export is how you get your history out when you switch tools or need records for payroll or an audit. Since April 2026 Clockify's free plan exports PDF only, 31 days at a time, so check the export on the plan you will actually use.
