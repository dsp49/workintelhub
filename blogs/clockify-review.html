Clockify Review (2026): Strengths, Limits and Who It Fits

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Clockify is a timer and timesheet with modules added by tier: billable rates and a kiosk, then approvals, time off, invoicing and attendance, then budgets, scheduling, expenses and GPS, then SSO. It is the cheapest paid ladder of any full time tracker, and the one most teams that want everything in one tool for the least money end up on. It is weakest where its free plan used to be its selling point, because in April 2026 that plan was cut to five users.

This review is built from Clockify's published pricing and documentation, read in October 2026, and from recurring themes in public reviews on Trustpilot and Clockify's help forum. We have not run Clockify through a pay cycle ourselves, so the verdicts below are about what the product does and costs, not a scored hands-on test. Where Clockify documents its own behavior, that is what we cite.

Verdict

Buy it if you want approvals, time off, invoicing and attendance in one tracker for the least money, and your team can live with a dense interface.

Skip it if you need a free plan for more than five people, want one plan with every feature, or need a tracker people will adopt with no training.

Expect to pay $5.49 to $7.99 per seat a month on annual billing for the plans most employers need, with every invited user billed.

What Clockify is

Clockify runs on the web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS and Android, with a shared-device kiosk for clocking in. People start a timer or fill a timesheet against projects and clients, and an auto tracker on desktop helps them reconstruct what they worked on. It is owned by CAKE.com, which also sells Pumble for chat and Plaky for project management, and offers the three as a bundle.

The modules arrive by tier. Basic adds billable rates, the kiosk, breaks, a time audit and imports. Standard adds approvals, time off, invoicing, attendance with overtime, targets and the QuickBooks integration. Pro adds budgets, scheduling, expenses, GPS, labor cost, custom fields and an opt-in activity monitoring feature that Clockify labeled new in October 2026. Enterprise adds SSO, SCIM and an audit log. Clockify does not list screenshots on any tier.

Where Clockify is strong

  • Price. Approvals and invoicing at $5.49 per seat on annual billing is the cheapest ladder of any full tracker we cover. Toggl Track charges $16 for approvals.
  • Breadth. Attendance, overtime, the kiosk and GPS sit alongside project time, so one tool can serve office and hourly staff.
  • Platform coverage. A Linux app, both mobile platforms and a kiosk, which several competitors lack.
  • Easy to arrive. Clockify publishes a guide for importing from Toggl, Harvest and ClickUp, with an official Toggl importer for the Detailed Report CSV. Time entry import needs a paid plan or the trial.
  • Data is kept. Canceling a paid plan drops the workspace to Free rather than deleting it, so old records remain reachable.

Where it falls short

  • The free plan cut. On April 21, 2026 the free plan was capped at five active users, reports at 31 days, and export at PDF only, and lost billable rates, the kiosk and time off. Many users say they saw no warning, though Clockify says an in-app notice went out a month earlier.
  • Tier-shopping. Approvals are on Standard, budgets and scheduling on Pro, SSO on Enterprise. A team that needs approvals and budgets pays $7.99, not $5.49.
  • Every user is a seat. Managers who only approve are billed like the people tracking.
  • A dense interface. Clockify has more settings than most teams use, and reviewers describe the member and project screens as slow and occasionally hanging as workspaces grow.
  • Support on lower tiers. Our time tracking review found support slower on the cheaper plans.

What recent reviews say

Clockify's scores on the large software review sites are high, but they mostly predate April 2026. On Trustpilot, where most recent complaints sit, it stood at 2.9 of 5 over 85 reviews when checked in October 2026, and most of those reviews came after the free plan change. The themes repeat: terms changed overnight, years of history out of reach on the 31-day report window, surprise at the price of a paid plan for a team used to free, and a workspace that hangs when editing members.

Two of those complaints are partly about the plan rather than the product. The data is kept; it is the report window and PDF-only export that put it out of reach. A paid trial restores full exports. Our guide to the tools people move to from Clockify quotes the reviews with dates and gives the export steps.

How employees experience it

For staff, Clockify is a timer or a weekly timesheet, a kiosk PIN for hourly workers, and their own reports. It does not take screenshots, and its activity monitoring on Pro is opt-in, so most teams run it as a plain tracker. The friction is the interface: a workspace with every module switched on shows people more than they need, which is how timesheets end up rebuilt on Friday afternoons. Switch off what you do not use, and our rollout guide covers the conversation before launch.

Alternatives worth comparing

If the free plan cut is the issue, TimeCamp is free for unlimited users with CSV export, Jibble is a free time clock for unlimited users, and Kimai is free because you host it. If tier-shopping is the issue, Everhour ($8.50) and My Hours ($8) put approvals and budgets in one plan. If people stopped tracking, Toggl Track is the one teams keep using. Our ranked list of Clockify alternatives sorts eleven by the reason you are leaving, the Toggl alternatives page covers the reverse move, and the time tracking software review places Clockify second for value among twelve tools.

Key takeaways

  • Clockify is a timer and timesheet with modules by tier: rates and kiosk, then approvals, time off, invoicing and attendance, then budgets, scheduling and GPS, then SSO.
  • Its strengths are price, breadth across office and hourly staff, Linux and mobile apps with a kiosk, and published import paths from Toggl, Harvest and ClickUp.
  • Its weaknesses are the April 2026 free plan cut, features spread across four tiers, every user billed, and a dense interface.
  • Most employers need Standard ($5.49) or Pro ($7.99) per seat per month on annual billing.
  • Trustpilot stood at 2.9 of 5 over 85 reviews in October 2026, most after the free plan change.

Frequently asked questions

Is Clockify worth it?

For teams that want approvals, time off, invoicing and attendance in one tool for the least money, yes: Standard costs $5.49 per seat on annual billing. For a free team of more than five, no longer, since the April 2026 cap.

Is Clockify safe to use?

Clockify is a mainstream tracker that does not list screenshots on any plan; its activity monitoring on Pro is opt-in. If you enable monitoring, tell staff what is recorded, and in Connecticut, Delaware and New York give written notice first.

What are the main complaints about Clockify?

The April 2026 free plan cut, report history out of reach on the 31-day free window, features spread across four tiers, every invited user billed, and a dense interface that reviewers say can hang on large workspaces.

Does Clockify take screenshots?

No. Clockify's pricing page lists an opt-in activity monitoring feature on Pro and does not list screenshots on any tier.

What is the best alternative to Clockify?

It depends on why you are leaving: TimeCamp if the free plan is the problem, Everhour or My Hours if you want one plan with everything, Harvest if invoicing is the job, and Toggl Track if people stopped tracking.
