Time Doctor vs ActivTrak (2026): Screenshots, Analytics and Price

P Pavan
November 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Time Doctor and ActivTrak both record which applications and websites people use, and buyers often shortlist them together. They are built for different jobs. Time Doctor is a time tracker built around proof of work: hours against projects, screenshots on every plan, payroll, and video recording on Premium. ActivTrak is productivity analytics: it classifies activity and reports utilization, focus, schedule adherence and workload without screenshots on its standard plans, and it does not track time against projects.

So the first question is not which is better but which job you have. If you bill or pay by the hour and need evidence, Time Doctor is the closer fit. If you want to know where the team's time goes and whether the workload is sane, with the least collection needed to answer that, ActivTrak is. This comparison uses October 2026 pricing and plan facts from our research on both tools.

The short answer

Choose Time Doctor if you need hours against projects, payroll, a Linux agent, or screen evidence for clients. The realistic plan is Standard at $11.70, with monthly billing available.

Choose ActivTrak if you want productivity and workload analytics without screenshots or keystroke logging, a free plan to start, and Chromebook coverage, and you can commit to an annual contract. Plans run $10 to $21.

Choose neither if you need security-grade recording and data loss prevention, or simple timesheets without monitoring.

Screenshots: on every plan, or an add-on

This is the difference most employees will notice. Time Doctor takes screenshots on every plan, including Basic, and Premium adds continuous video. An admin can decide who sees them, but you cannot buy Time Doctor without them. For an outsourcing firm whose client contract requires screen evidence, that is the point.

ActivTrak takes no screenshots on its standard plans. Screen Details, which adds screenshots, full URLs, live screen view and website blocking, is a separately priced add-on on Work Activity Tracking and Workforce Management, and is not offered on the higher plans. ActivTrak does not log keystrokes at all. That makes it simpler to explain to a team, and the wrong tool for an investigation. Our screen monitoring guide covers what either approach means for retention and access.

Time and evidence, or workload and focus

Time Doctor's productivity ratings classify applications and websites as productive or not per team, alongside attendance, breaks and idle time. Its two distinctive reports look at overwork and location: work-life balance flags late hours and long days, and Office versus Remote compares the two groups directly.

ActivTrak goes further on analytics: utilization, focus time, schedule adherence and workload balance, with burnout signals, capacity planning and AI coaching on the higher plans. The cost is setup. Its value depends on classifying every application and website as productive or not for each team, and reviewers describe the hours that takes. What it lacks is the time record: no hours against projects, no payroll and no GPS, so a business that bills or pays by the hour needs a second tool.

Both reward presence at a keyboard more than output, so read the numbers as context rather than a grade. Our guide to workplace analytics without surveillance sets out what to measure instead.

Price at the plan you will actually use

Time Doctor's realistic plan is Standard at $11.70, because attendance, approvals, app reports and payroll start there. ActivTrak's useful features sit on the $15 and $17 plans; the $10 plan is thin.

Contracts, cancellation and reputation

Both have exit risks of different kinds. Time Doctor's Trustpilot score stood at 1.2 of 5 across 235 reviews in October 2026, and most complaints concern billing after cancellation. Cancel inside the account, keep the confirmation and watch the card for two cycles. ActivTrak's review-site scores are solid, around 4.3 to 4.5, but its complaints are structural: annual contracts only, a hard cancellation process and refunds that took weeks. Find your renewal date and notice period in the order form, and export through ActivConnect before removing agents, because remote uninstall deletes that computer's data.

If neither fits, our Time Doctor alternatives and ActivTrak alternatives guides sort the replacements by the reason people leave. For each tool on its own, read the Time Doctor review and the ActivTrak review.

Key takeaways

  • Time Doctor is a time tracker with screenshots on every plan; ActivTrak is productivity analytics with no screenshots on its standard plans and no keystroke logging.
  • At the realistic plan, Time Doctor Standard costs $11.70 per user per month on annual billing; ActivTrak runs $10 to $21, with the useful features on the $15 and $17 plans.
  • Time Doctor offers monthly billing; ActivTrak is annual contracts only but has a free plan for 3 users.
  • Time Doctor has project time, payroll and a Linux agent; ActivTrak has deeper workload, focus and burnout analytics and Chromebook support but no Linux agent.
  • Data history is 3 months to 2 years on Time Doctor and 6 months to 1 year on ActivTrak, extendable to 3 years.

Frequently asked questions

Is Time Doctor better than ActivTrak?

They do different jobs. Time Doctor is better if you need hours against projects, payroll or screen evidence. ActivTrak is better if you want productivity and workload analytics without screenshots or keystroke logging.

Which is cheaper, Time Doctor or ActivTrak?

At the entry plans, Time Doctor Basic at $6.70 is cheaper than ActivTrak's $10. At the plans most teams use, Time Doctor Standard is $11.70 and ActivTrak's useful plans are $15 and $17, per user per month on annual billing, as read in October 2026.

Does ActivTrak take screenshots like Time Doctor?

Only with the Screen Details add-on, priced on request and offered on the two lower plans. Time Doctor takes screenshots on every plan.

Does ActivTrak track time like Time Doctor?

No. ActivTrak records application and website activity but not hours against projects, and has no payroll, so teams that bill or pay by the hour need a separate time tracker.

Do Time Doctor and ActivTrak work on Linux?

Time Doctor has a Linux agent. ActivTrak has no native Linux agent; its support material suggests a Chrome-based agent for other operating systems.
