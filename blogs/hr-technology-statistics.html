HR Technology Statistics (2026)

P Pavan
October 16, 2026 · 12 min read

Worldwide revenue for human capital management software reached $36.3 billion in 2024, up 13%, and Gartner expects it to pass $68 billion by 2029. Thirty-nine percent of organizations have AI in at least one HR function as of December 2025 and 46% expect to by the end of 2026. Yet only 30% of organizations plan to increase HR technology investment in 2026, down from a five-year high of 47%, and 88% of HR leaders say their organization has not realized significant business value from AI tools.

The HR technology statistics that rank are market-size projections from research vendors with different scopes, forecasts presented as adoption rates, and a 2022 survey figure presented as current. This page uses Gartner's revenue analysis and HR leader surveys, the Sapient Insights annual systems survey, SHRM's 2026 State of AI in HR, the Census Bureau's business survey, Pew, LinkedIn, BLS and the audit of New York's AI hiring law, with sample and date on each, and labels every vendor-commissioned figure.

Market size, deals and spending intentions

Gartner's market share analysis put worldwide HCM software revenue at $36.3 billion in 2024, up 13%, with the Americas growing 14.3%, and its November 2025 forecast expects spending above $68 billion by 2029. Drake Star counted more than 650 HR technology transactions and $22.3 billion in disclosed capital in 2025 to September, including the $4.1 billion Paychex acquisition of Paycor. The $47 billion and $59 billion figures that circulate for 2026 are projections from research vendors using different definitions, and none is a measurement.

Spending intentions have cooled. Sapient Insights Group's 28th annual HR Systems Survey, from 9,886 HR professionals at 4,670 organizations in 71 countries, found 30% planning to increase HR technology investment in 2026, down from a five-year high of 47%, while spending on AI tools rose about 50% year on year. The prior edition found 41% planning increases. Paycom-commissioned research on more than 1,000 US HR decision-makers found 43% prioritizing HR technology upgrades in 2026 and four in five expecting to buy new HCM software within a year; it is a vendor survey and reads that way. Our guide to AI agents for HR covers what the spend goes on.

How many systems HR runs

Sapient's 27th survey, from 3,318 organizations in 2024, found the average organization running 22 HR-related systems, up from 10 in 2020 and 16 in 2021. Forty-three percent had a defined HR technology function, 58% of HR technology staff had been in the role under three years, and IT owned the HR technology budget at 33% of enterprises. User experience scores rose 1% and vendor satisfaction 3% in the 2025-26 edition. A smaller HR.com survey of 121 mostly US HR professionals in 2026, sponsored by Eightfold, found payroll the most common component at 76%, followed by onboarding at 72%, recruiting at 67% and benefits at 66%, with workforce management at 36%; 41% said their systems integrate well and 26% said poorly, and 39% called their analytics accurate. Forrester research for Paycom found employers averaging 6.17 HR and payroll providers and 77% running multiple HCM databases. Our payroll error statistics page covers what fragmentation costs.

Replacement and migration

Sapient's 2024 data found 10% of organizations had decided to replace their core HR management system and 8% had active requests for proposals, with 28% of enterprises of 5,000 or more staff planning a replacement or in an RFP within twelve months, up from 10% the year before. SHRM's coverage called the HRMS the top target for replacement. In the HR.com sample, 57% said they were likely to substantially reconfigure their HR technology, with more AI at 53%, user experience at 51%, self-service at 47% and integration at 46% the planned changes.

SHRM's 2025 Talent Trends survey had found 43% of HR professionals saying their organization used AI for HR, up from 26% in 2024, with recruiting at 51% of users; its February 2022 survey found nearly one in four. Gartner's surveys of HR leaders track the generative AI wave: 19% piloting or planning in June 2023, 38% in January 2024, 61% in January 2025, and by late 2025 95% of 110 heads of HR had implemented AI in some capacity, with one in five realizing significant value and 22% saying a leader had stopped entry-level hiring because of it. In recruiting specifically, LinkedIn's Talent 2026 survey of 6,554 HR professionals in 13 markets found 93% planning to increase AI use in 2026 and 66% expanding AI pre-screening, while 81% of job seekers used or planned to use AI and US applications per role had doubled since 2022. The Census Bureau's probability sample of all businesses found 19.8% using AI in any function in May 2026, which is the figure to set against any vendor claim. What candidates think of AI hiring

Pew's December 2022 survey of 11,004 US adults remains the only probability-sample measure. Sixty-six percent would not apply to an employer that uses AI to help make hiring decisions and 32% would; 71% opposed AI making a final hiring decision and 7% favored it; 41% opposed AI reviewing applications; and 47% thought AI would treat applicants more consistently than humans. It is four years old and nothing newer with a comparable sample exists. Our California AI workplace laws page covers the disclosure candidates say they want.

Regulation and compliance

New York City's Local Law 144 has required bias audits of automated hiring tools since July 2023. A Cornell and Consumer Reports audit of 391 employers in late 2023 found 18, or 5%, had posted a bias audit and 13, or 3%, a transparency notice; the researchers titled the paper "Null Compliance." The New York State Comptroller's December 2025 audit found the city agency had reviewed 32 audits and found one non-compliance while auditors identified at least 17 potential issues. SHRM's 2026 survey found 19 of the most populous states now have AI employment laws, 57% of HR professionals in those states unaware of them, and 12% of those aware having taken steps. Our California AI workplace laws page tracks them.

Payroll, core HR and the HR workforce

PayrollOrg's 2025 Getting Paid in America survey of more than 25,900 respondents found 92.7% paid by direct deposit, 3.3% by paper check and under 2% by payment apps or payroll cards. Payroll is the most common component of the HR stack at 76% in the HR.com sample. BLS's Occupational Outlook Handbook counts 939,700 human resources specialist jobs in 2025 at a median $75,940, projected to grow 6% to 2035 with about 73,700 openings a year, and 224,900 HR managers at a median $149,280, also growing 6%. Our HR metrics guide covers how those numbers scale.

Outcomes and ROI

The outcome data are early and mixed. Gartner's October 2025 survey found 88% of HR leaders saying their organization had not realized significant business value from AI tools, while 65% of 2,986 employees were excited about AI, 62% had saved time and 7% had lost time. Workday's 2026 research on 3,200 AI-using employees, run by Hanover Research, found about 40% of AI time savings lost to rework and 14% of employees consistently net positive. Among SHRM's AI-using organizations, 87% reported efficiency gains, 75% better work quality, 39% shifted responsibilities and 7% displacement; 56% did not measure success at all. Sapient reports AI-adopting organizations outperforming by 8% over two years without defining the metric. Deloitte's 2026 Human Capital Trends, from more than 9,000 leaders in 89 countries, found 41% of workers had used AI to automate part of their job, often without their employer's knowledge, and 27% saying their organization manages change well. Mercer's 2026 trends, from about 12,000 respondents, found worker concern about AI job loss up from 28% in 2024 to 40%. The HR technology market is growing faster than the evidence that it works. Figures to stop using

  • "SHRM: 51% of organizations use AI for recruiting." 51% of the 43% that used AI in HR in 2025. Across all organizations, SHRM's 2026 figure is 27%.
  • "Gartner: half of organizations have integrated AI into HR." Gartner's February 2024 figure was 38% of 179 HR leaders piloting, planning or implementing generative AI.
  • "Gartner: 50% of HR tasks will be automated by 2030." A prediction, not survey data.
  • "$47.5 billion HR tech market in 2026" and "$58.9 billion HR software market in 2026." Projections from two research vendors with different scopes. Gartner measured $36.3 billion in HCM software revenue for 2024.
  • "Nearly 1 in 4 organizations use AI in HR (SHRM)." SHRM's February 2022 figure. Current readings are 43% for 2025 and 39% to 46% for 2026.
  • "7 in 10 companies will use AI in hiring in 2025." A forecast from an October 2024 ResumeBuilder poll.
  • "AI will handle 95% of candidate screening in 2026." No source. iCIMS found screening the top AI use at 58% and 18% using AI broadly.
  • Pew's 66% presented as 2024 or 2025 data. Fieldwork was December 2022.

Key takeaways

  • Worldwide HCM software revenue was $36.3 billion in 2024, up 13%, and Gartner expects more than $68 billion by 2029. The $47 billion to $59 billion '2026 market' figures are vendor projections.
  • Only 30% of organizations plan to increase HR technology investment in 2026, down from a five-year high of 47%, while AI tool spending rose about 50%, across 4,670 organizations.
  • The average organization runs 22 HR-related systems, up from 10 in 2020. 28% of enterprises with 5,000 or more staff plan to replace their core HR system within a year.
  • 39% of organizations have AI in at least one HR function and 46% expect to by the end of 2026, per SHRM's 1,908-person survey. Recruiting leads at 27%.
  • 93% of recruiters plan to increase AI use in 2026 and 66% are expanding AI pre-screening. 66% of US adults would not apply to an employer using AI in hiring decisions.
  • 5% of 391 New York City employers posted the bias audits the city's AI hiring law requires. 57% of HR professionals in states with AI employment laws are unaware of them.
  • 88% of HR leaders say their organization has not realized significant business value from AI tools, and about 40% of AI time savings are lost to rework.
  • HR specialist employment is 939,700 at a median $75,940, growing 6% to 2035. 92.7% of US workers are paid by direct deposit.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the HR technology market?

Gartner measured worldwide human capital management software revenue at $36.3 billion in 2024, up 13%, and forecasts spending above $68 billion by 2029. Figures of $47 billion to $59 billion for 2026 are projections from other research vendors using different definitions, not measurements.

What share of companies use AI in HR?

39% of organizations had AI in at least one HR function as of December 2025 and 46% expected to by the end of 2026, per SHRM's survey of 1,908 HR professionals. Among the largest organizations the figure was 60%; among small ones, 33%.

Where is AI used most in HR?

Recruiting, at 27% of organizations, followed by HR technology at 21%, learning and development at 17% and employee experience at 14%, per SHRM's 2026 survey. Among talent acquisition teams, screening is the top use at 58%.

Are companies spending more on HR technology in 2026?

Fewer plan to. 30% of organizations intend to increase HR technology investment in 2026, down from a five-year high of 47%, per Sapient Insights Group's survey of 4,670 organizations, though spending on AI tools rose about 50% year on year.

How many HR systems does a typical company run?

An average of 22 HR-related systems in 2024, per Sapient's survey of 3,318 organizations, up from 10 in 2020 and 16 in 2021. Only 43% of organizations have a defined HR technology function.

Do recruiters plan to use more AI?

Yes. 93% of 6,554 HR professionals in 13 markets planned to increase AI use in 2026 and 66% were expanding AI pre-screening interviews, per LinkedIn's November 2025 survey. 81% of job seekers were using or planning to use AI in their search.

Do job seekers trust AI in hiring?

Mostly not. 66% of US adults said they would not apply to an employer that uses AI to help make hiring decisions and 71% opposed AI making the final decision, per Pew's December 2022 survey of 11,004 adults. 47% thought AI would be more consistent than human reviewers.

Is AI in HR paying off?

Not yet for most. 88% of HR leaders said their organization had not realized significant business value from AI tools in Gartner's October 2025 survey, and Workday's 2026 research found about 40% of AI time savings lost to rework. 56% of organizations do not measure the success of their AI investment at all.
