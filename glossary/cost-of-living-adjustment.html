Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA)

WH The WorkIntel Hub editorial team
November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

A cost-of-living adjustment, or COLA, is an increase in pay or benefits intended to offset inflation, usually set as a percentage tied to a price index such as the Consumer Price Index, and given to everyone covered regardless of individual performance.

The best-known COLA is the annual increase in Social Security and Supplemental Security Income benefits: the Social Security Administration announced a 3.6% COLA for 2027 on October 14, 2026, based on the rise in the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W) from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026. Federal civilian and military retirement benefits, many state pensions and many union contracts use the same mechanism.

In private-sector pay, COLAs are less common than merit increases. Most employers set an annual salary budget and distribute it by performance and position in range, sometimes with a general increase component that functions like a COLA.

How a COLA is calculated

COLA % = (index in current period − index in base period) / index in base period × 100. The Social Security formula compares the average CPI-W for July, August and September of the current year with the same quarter of the last year a COLA was paid, rounded to the nearest tenth of a percent. Contracts and plans may use the CPI-U (all urban consumers), a regional CPI, or a cap and floor (for example, CPI with a 1% minimum and 4% maximum).

Example: a union contract with a CPI-U COLA capped at 4%, CPI-U rising 3.1% over the reference period, and an hourly rate of $28.00: the new rate is $28.00 × 1.031 = $28.87. Applying a COLA to a salary band means moving the minimum, midpoint and maximum by the same percentage (see salary band).

COLA vs merit increase vs market adjustment

TypeBasisWho receives itTypical use
Cost-of-living adjustmentPrice inflation (CPI)Everyone covered, same percentagePublic pensions, Social Security, union contracts, some public employers
Merit increaseIndividual performance and position in rangeVaries by person, may be zeroMost private-sector salary budgets
Market adjustmentMovement in market pay for the jobSpecific jobs or people below marketFixing pay compression and retention risk
General increaseCompany decision, often influenced by inflationEveryone, same percentage or amountUnion and hourly workforces, years of high inflation

Inflation and market pay do not move together: in some years wages for a job rise faster than prices, in others slower. A pure COLA protects purchasing power but does not keep pay competitive; a pure merit budget can leave average employees falling behind inflation. Many employers communicate both numbers so employees can see the real change in pay. The pay raise calculator shows a raise net of inflation.

Do employers have to give a COLA?

No. No federal or state law requires private employers to give cost-of-living increases. Employers must pay at least the applicable minimum wage, and several states and cities index their minimum wages to inflation each year (including Washington, Oregon, Colorado, Arizona, California and others), which works like a mandatory COLA at the bottom of the pay scale; see minimum wage by state 2027. Some state exempt salary thresholds are also indexed. COLAs become mandatory only where a collective bargaining agreement, employment contract or plan document requires them.

The Social Security COLA and payroll

The Social Security COLA affects beneficiaries, not paychecks, but the same October announcement sets payroll figures that do change: the Social Security wage base for 2027 (reported at $192,300, up from $184,500) and the earnings limits for working beneficiaries. See 2027 payroll tax rates and limits.

Key points

  • A COLA is an across-the-board increase in pay or benefits tied to inflation, usually a percentage based on the Consumer Price Index.
  • The 2027 Social Security COLA is 3.6%, announced October 14, 2026, based on the third-quarter CPI-W.
  • COLA % = (current index − base index) / base index; contracts often use CPI-U and add caps and floors.
  • A COLA differs from a merit increase (by performance), a market adjustment (by job market pay) and a general increase (by company decision).
  • Private employers are not required to give COLAs, though inflation-indexed state minimum wages work like one at the bottom of the scale; union contracts and plans may require them.

Frequently asked questions

What is a cost-of-living adjustment?

An increase in wages or benefits meant to keep pace with inflation, usually a percentage tied to a price index such as the Consumer Price Index and given equally to everyone covered, regardless of performance.

What is the COLA for 2027?

The Social Security and SSI COLA for 2027 is 3.6%, announced by the Social Security Administration on October 14, 2026. Private employers set their own raises; there is no general private-sector COLA.

How is a COLA calculated?

By the percentage change in a price index between two periods. Social Security compares the average CPI-W for July to September with the same quarter of the previous COLA year. A contract might apply the 12-month change in CPI-U, often with a cap and floor.

Is a COLA the same as a raise?

It is a type of raise, but different from a merit increase. A COLA gives everyone the same inflation-based percentage; a merit increase varies with individual performance and position in the salary range. Many employers give only merit increases.

Are employers required to give cost-of-living raises?

No. Private employers are not legally required to give COLAs. They must pay the applicable minimum wage, which some states and cities index to inflation each year, and must honor any COLA written into a union contract, employment agreement or benefit plan.

Should salary bands move with COLA?

Salary bands should move with market pay for the jobs, which is related to but not the same as inflation. Many employers move bands by the market movement shown in salary surveys and set the merit budget separately.
