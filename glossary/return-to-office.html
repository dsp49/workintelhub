Return to Office (RTO)

WH The WorkIntel Hub editorial team
September 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Return to office, usually shortened to RTO, is a requirement that employees who have been working remotely resume working from a company location, either every day or for a set number of days each week or month.

The term came into general use after the pandemic, when remote work stopped being an emergency arrangement and became something employers had to decide about. Since 2023 it has mostly meant the tightening of hybrid arrangements: fewer remote days, tracked attendance, and consequences for non-compliance.

The five models

ModelWhat it requiresWhere it is used
Full returnIn the office every working daySome large employers, government, front-line and regulated roles
Fixed hybridNamed days, the same for everyone or each teamThe most common corporate model
Flexible hybridA minimum number of days, employee chooses whichKnowledge work where collaboration days are not fixed
Role basedRequirement depends on the jobOrganisations mixing site-bound and desk roles
VoluntaryOffice available, attendance not requiredRemote-first companies with a physical space

The difference that matters operationally is between the second and third rows. Fixed days make the office full on those days and empty on the others, which is the point if the goal is collaboration and a waste if the goal is desk utilisation. Flexible minimums fill the office evenly and put nobody in the same room on purpose.

What employees say they will do

The most cited figures are from Pew Research Center, which surveyed 2,315 US workers whose jobs can be done from home, in October 2024. Seventy-five per cent said their employer required them in the office a certain number of days, up from 63 per cent in early 2023. Forty-six per cent said they would be unlikely to stay in their job if they could no longer work from home at all.

Two cautions on reading that. Stated intention to leave is not the same as leaving, and the figure was collected in a labour market where moving was getting harder. And the question was about losing remote work entirely, not about an extra day. The realistic reading is that full-return mandates carry a retention cost, that partial tightening carries a smaller one, and that both fall hardest on the people with the most options elsewhere.

How attendance is enforced

A policy that is not measured is a suggestion, and most organisations that tightened RTO discovered this within a quarter. Enforcement has settled on three sources of evidence.

  • Badge data. Building access records show who entered and when. This is the most common source, and the least invasive, because it records presence rather than activity. Our RFID employee tracking entry covers what badge systems do and do not capture.
  • Network and device location. Which office network a laptop connected to. More granular than badges and harder to explain to employees, because it looks like monitoring rather than attendance.
  • Manager attestation. Cheapest, least accurate, and the one that produces uneven enforcement between teams.

Whichever source is used, the attendance figure ends up in performance conversations, and in some organisations in pay and promotion decisions. That is where RTO meets proximity bias: once presence is scored, the people who are present more get rated better, whether or not the work is.

Where the data comes from device monitoring rather than the door, the notice rules in four states apply, and the policy should say what is collected and how it is used. The remote work policy is the usual place for that.

Deciding the number of days

The honest version of the decision starts with what the office is for. If it is for collaboration, fixed shared days and a reason to be there matter more than the count. If it is for oversight, the office is being used to do a job that output measurement does better and cheaper. If it is for the lease, say so; employees can tell.

Then apply the rule the same way to everyone with the same kind of role, write down the exceptions process, and measure attendance in a way you would be comfortable describing to the people it measures. Most of the resentment attached to RTO comes not from the number of days but from a number announced without a reason and enforced unevenly.

Key points

  • RTO is a requirement to resume working from a company location, full time or for a set number of days.
  • Five models are in use. Fixed hybrid days put people in the room together; flexible minimums fill the office evenly and put nobody together on purpose.
  • Pew found 75 per cent of remote-capable workers had in-office requirements in late 2024, and 46 per cent said they would be unlikely to stay if remote work ended entirely.
  • Stated intention to leave is not leaving, but full-return mandates carry a retention cost that falls hardest on the people with options.
  • Enforcement runs on badge data, device location or manager attestation. Badge data records presence without activity and is the least intrusive.
  • Once presence is scored it feeds proximity bias. Decide what the office is for before deciding the number of days.

Frequently asked questions

What does RTO mean at work?

Return to office: an employer requirement that staff who have been working remotely resume working from a company location, either every day or for a set number of days per week or month.

What is the difference between RTO and hybrid work?

Hybrid work is the arrangement; RTO is the act of moving toward more office time. A company can have a hybrid policy and still issue an RTO mandate that increases the required days, which is what most mandates since 2023 have done.

Can an employer force a return to office?

In most cases yes, subject to the employment contract and any written remote-work agreement. An employer that agreed remote work in writing may need to vary the contract; one that allowed it informally can usually change the requirement with notice. Accommodation requests for disability or caring responsibilities still have to be considered individually.

How do companies track RTO compliance?

Mostly through building badge data, which records who entered and when. Some use network or device location, which is more granular and reads as monitoring. Manager attestation is the least reliable and produces uneven enforcement.

What percentage of workers would leave over an RTO mandate?

Pew Research Center found in late 2024 that 46 per cent of workers with remote-capable jobs would be unlikely to stay if they could no longer work from home at all. That is stated intention rather than actual turnover, and it concerns losing remote work entirely rather than adding a day.

Does return to office improve productivity?

The research does not support a simple answer. Some studies find collaboration and mentoring benefits from shared days; others find no output difference or a loss from commuting time. The effect depends on the kind of work and on whether the office days are used for something remote work does badly.
