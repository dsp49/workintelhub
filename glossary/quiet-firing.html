Quiet Firing

WH The WorkIntel Hub editorial team
September 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Quiet firing is making a job unpleasant enough that the employee resigns, instead of addressing performance or ending the employment directly. No decision is announced and nothing is documented, which is the point.

It is usually described as a tactic. In practice it is more often avoidance: a manager who does not want a difficult conversation, or an organisation that wants to avoid a severance cost, allowing the situation to become unbearable instead.

What it looks like

The behaviours are recognisable and they accumulate. Individually each is deniable, which is why the pattern matters more than any single instance.

  • Interesting work quietly reassigned, leaving only routine tasks.
  • Exclusion from meetings the person used to attend.
  • One-to-ones repeatedly cancelled or never scheduled.
  • No feedback, no development conversation, no path described.
  • Passed over for a rise or promotion with no explanation given.
  • Schedule, location or duties changed in ways that are technically permitted and personally difficult.
  • Goals set that are unreachable, or moved after being agreed.

The distinguishing feature is the absence of a conversation. Genuine performance management is uncomfortable but explicit: the person knows there is a problem, what it is, and what would resolve it. Quiet firing is the same outcome pursued without any of that.

How it differs from what it resembles

Who withdrawsNobodyThe managerThe employee
Is it stated?Yes, explicitlyNo, deliberately notRarely

The second row is the whole distinction. A performance improvement plan is not quiet firing, even when both parties suspect how it ends, because the person has been told and given terms. The same intention pursued silently is.

Why it costs more than the conversation would have

It is slower and more expensive. Months of reduced output from the person, plus the time of everyone working around them, usually exceeds whatever severance was being avoided.

The whole team learns from it. Colleagues can see what is happening. What they learn is that this organisation does not deal with problems directly, which affects people who were not the target.

It selects the wrong leavers. A deteriorating environment is noticed first by the people with the most options, so the ones who leave are frequently not the ones intended.

It removes the chance of improvement. Some proportion of underperformance is an unclear brief, a mismatch of role, or something happening outside work. None of that can be addressed by a conversation nobody has.

Where it becomes a legal problem

Two doctrines are worth knowing about, and both reward the employer who handled things directly.

Constructive dismissal. Where conditions are made intolerable enough that a reasonable person would resign, a resignation can in some circumstances be treated as a dismissal. The threshold is high and varies by jurisdiction, but the pattern quiet firing creates is precisely the pattern the doctrine addresses.

Discrimination and retaliation. The greater exposure is usually here. If the treatment began after protected activity, a complaint, a leave request, a disclosure, or if those affected share a protected characteristic, the absence of documentation works against the employer rather than for it. An organisation that cannot show why someone's role deteriorated is in a weak position to explain that it was unrelated.

The irony worth stating plainly: quiet firing is usually chosen to avoid a documented process, and the lack of documentation is the thing that makes it legally dangerous.

What to do instead

  1. Say the thing. Specifically, once, with examples. Most quiet firing begins with a conversation that was postponed.
  2. Establish whether it is capability, clarity or circumstance. These have different remedies and only one of them is a performance problem.
  3. Agree what changes and by when, in writing, with a review date.
  4. If it does not work, end it properly. A clean exit with terms is cheaper and kinder than six months of attrition by discomfort.

If you are on the receiving end, ask directly and in writing whether there is a concern about your performance and what would resolve it. A written question is difficult to answer with silence, and it converts an ambiguous situation into a documented one.

Key points

  • Quiet firing is making a job worse until the person resigns, instead of managing or dismissing them.
  • The distinguishing feature is the absence of a conversation, not the behaviours themselves.
  • It is usually avoidance rather than strategy: a postponed conversation or an avoided severance cost.
  • Performance management is not quiet firing, because the person has been told and given terms.
  • The team can see it, and what they learn is that problems here are not dealt with directly.
  • It selects the wrong leavers: people with the most options notice a deteriorating environment first.
  • The lack of documentation chosen to avoid a process is exactly what creates the legal exposure.

Frequently asked questions

What is quiet firing?

Making someone's job progressively worse so that they resign, rather than addressing performance directly or ending the employment. Work is reassigned, meetings stop including them, feedback dries up and no path is described, all without anyone stating that there is a problem.

What are the signs of quiet firing?

Interesting work reassigned, exclusion from meetings the person used to attend, one-to-ones repeatedly cancelled, no feedback or development conversation, being passed over without explanation, and goals that are unreachable or moved after being agreed. The pattern matters more than any single instance.

Is quiet firing illegal?

The behaviour itself is not generally unlawful, but it creates exposure. Where conditions become intolerable enough that a reasonable person resigns, constructive dismissal may apply. The larger risk is discrimination or retaliation: if the treatment began after protected activity, the absence of documentation works against the employer.

How is quiet firing different from performance management?

Performance management is explicit. The person is told what the problem is, what would resolve it, and by when, and it is documented. Quiet firing pursues the same outcome without any of that, and the missing conversation is the whole distinction.

How is quiet firing different from quiet quitting?

They are mirror images. In quiet quitting the employee withdraws discretionary effort; in quiet firing the manager withdraws work, feedback and opportunity. Both are unstated, but the party doing the withdrawing is different.

What should I do if I think I am being quiet fired?

Ask in writing whether there is a concern about your performance and what would resolve it. A written question is hard to answer with silence, and it converts an ambiguous situation into a documented one. Keep your own record of the changes you have observed and their dates.

Why do managers quiet fire instead of having the conversation?

Usually avoidance rather than calculation: the conversation is uncomfortable, the manager lacks confidence in handling it, or the organisation wants to avoid a severance cost. It is almost always slower and more expensive than the alternative, because months of reduced output exceeds what was being avoided.
