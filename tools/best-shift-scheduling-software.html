Best Shift Scheduling Software: 8 Tools Reviewed (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 14 min read

Shift scheduling is the retail, hospitality, healthcare and site version of workforce scheduling: shifts rather than hours, a rota rather than a plan, and a team that needs to see it on their phones, swap what they cannot work and pick up what is open. The tools that do it well make the swap-and-cover loop painless, because that loop is where no-shows come from, and the best of them know the sector: a restaurant scheduler reads the POS, a healthcare scheduler reads credentials.

I reviewed eight tools for shift work, with pricing checked in October 2026. The first three are the ones I would trial for a restaurant, a small hourly team and a shop respectively; the rest fit particular sectors and sizes. The workforce scheduling review covers the wider category including demand forecasting, and the rotating shift guide covers the patterns.

The tools in detail

7shifts

Shift schedules against sales, with tips and compliance
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, POS integrations Pricing (October 2026) Comp free for one location and 30 employees; Entrée $29.99 and The Works $69.99 per location per month on annual billing ($34.99 and $76.99 monthly); Gourmet by quote

What it is

Restaurant scheduling against POS sales forecasts with tip pooling and compliance; free for one location.

Why it is on this list

7shifts leads shift scheduling because restaurants are the hardest shift environment and 7shifts was built for them: schedules drafted against POS sales forecasts and a labour target, break and overtime compliance by state, tip pooling, a manager log, and task lists for opening and closing, with the clock and payroll integrations behind. The free Comp plan covers one location with 30 staff.

Where it is strong

  • Schedules against sales forecasts from the POS
  • Tip pooling and manager log
  • Break and overtime compliance
  • Free for one location with 30 employees

Where it falls short

  • Restaurant-specific; little use elsewhere
  • Per-location pricing
  • Payroll only in some states

Sling

Free shift scheduling for 30 people
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 30 users; Premium $1.70 and Business $3.40 per user per month on annual billing ($2 and $4 monthly)

What it is

The simplest and cheapest scheduler: free for 30 users, time clock and labour cost from $1.70.

Why it is on this list

Sling is second because a small hourly team needs a shift schedule everyone can see, swap and request time off against, and Sling gives that to 30 people for nothing, with a clock and labour cost from $1.70. It has no auto-scheduling, which a team of fifteen does not miss.

Where it is strong

  • Free for 30 users
  • The cheapest paid scheduler here
  • Bills only active users
  • Simple enough for any team

Where it falls short

  • No auto-scheduling or forecasting
  • No payroll
  • Reporting is basic

Homebase

Shifts, clock and payroll for one or two sites
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, POS kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Basic free for one location and 10 employees; Essentials $30, Plus $70, All-in-One $120 per location per month; payroll $49 plus $6 per employee

What it is

Scheduling, time clock, hiring and payroll for small hourly businesses, priced per location, free for one shop.

Why it is on this list

Homebase is third for shops, cafes and service businesses: shifts, an AI scheduling assistant on Plus, a clock on the POS, hiring and payroll in one app, priced per location and free for one. It stops making sense at many sites.

Where it is strong

  • Free for a single shop with ten employees
  • Per-location pricing suits one or two sites
  • Scheduling, time clock, hiring and payroll in one app
  • Runs on the POS

Where it falls short

  • Per-location pricing gets expensive with many sites
  • Payroll is $49 plus $6 a head
  • Not for salaried or office teams

The swap-and-cover loop

Most no-shows are predictable: the shift was unwanted when it was published and nobody made it easy to trade. The loop that prevents them is in-app swaps with manager approval, open shifts offered to qualified staff first, availability collected in the tool and respected, and reminders before the shift. When I Work, Sling, 7shifts, Homebase, Deputy, Connecteam and Humanity all run the loop; Clockify runs part of it; Time Champ, Buddy Punch and uAttend do not, which is why they are on the attendance pages and not this one. The no call no show policy handles the cases the loop does not prevent.

Patterns and fairness

Fixed, rotating, split and on-call patterns each carry pay and law consequences: the rotating shift guide covers the health evidence on forward rotation, the split shift page the premiums some states require, and the shift differential calculator the night and weekend pay that must be in the overtime rate. Fair workweek laws in Oregon and several large cities add advance notice and change premiums for retail and food service; Deputy encodes them, 7shifts and Homebase flag some. The fairness question that the law does not settle, who gets the good shifts, is answered by rotation and by publishing the rule; the work schedule template shows one way to do it.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Disclosure. I have done work for Time Champ. It appears here because it belongs in this category, it is held to the same criteria as every other product, its drawbacks are listed like everyone else's, and it bought no placement. There are no affiliate or vendor links on this page.

Key takeaways

  • Shift scheduling lives or dies on the swap-and-cover loop: in-app swaps, open shifts, availability respected, reminders.
  • 7shifts for restaurants, Sling free for 30 people, Homebase for shops; Deputy for multi-site compliance, Humanity for hospitals, When I Work on a budget.
  • Rotating, split and on-call patterns carry pay and law consequences; fair workweek laws add notice and change premiums in several cities.
  • Publish the rule for who gets the good shifts.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best shift scheduling software?

7shifts for restaurants, because it schedules against POS sales forecasts with tip pooling and compliance; Sling for a small hourly team that wants a free rota with swaps for up to 30 people; Homebase for shops and cafes with one or two sites. Deputy for multi-site hourly operations, Humanity for hospitals and large chains, When I Work for any hourly team on a budget, Connecteam for deskless teams.

Is there free shift scheduling software?

Sling is free for up to 30 users with shifts, availability, swaps, open shifts and messaging. 7shifts is free for one location with 30 staff, Homebase for one location with ten, and Connecteam for ten users. The free tiers lack forecasting and, mostly, a time clock.

How do shift swaps work in scheduling apps?

An employee offers a shift, qualified colleagues see it as an open shift in the app, one claims it, and a manager approves the trade, with hours caps and overtime rules checked automatically. When I Work, Sling, 7shifts, Homebase, Deputy, Connecteam and Humanity all support it; the approval step is what keeps overtime and skills coverage intact.

What is the difference between shift scheduling and workforce scheduling software?

Shift scheduling is the rota for hourly staff: shifts, swaps, open shifts, availability and a clock. Workforce scheduling adds demand forecasting, auto-scheduling against labour targets and compliance rules, and at the enterprise end credential and census-driven coverage. Deputy, 7shifts and Humanity do both; Sling and When I Work are primarily shift schedulers.

How far in advance should shifts be published?

Two weeks is the common standard and the legal minimum under most fair workweek laws (Oregon, New York City, Chicago, Seattle, San Francisco, Philadelphia, Los Angeles) for covered retail, food service and hospitality employers, which also require premium pay for changes after publication. Elsewhere it is policy, and a week is the practical minimum for a team that plans its life around the rota.

How much does shift scheduling software cost?

Free on Sling (30 users), 7shifts and Homebase (one location) and Connecteam (ten users); $1.70 to $5 per user on Sling, When I Work and Deputy Lite; $29.99 to $69.99 per location on 7shifts and $30 to $120 on Homebase; $7.99 on Clockify Pro; Humanity by quote. When I Work's clock is an extra $1.50 to $2 per user.
