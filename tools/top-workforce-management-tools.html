Top Workforce Management Tools by Company Size (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 15 min read

The workforce management tool that fits a company of twenty is wrong at two hundred and absurd at two thousand, and most buying mistakes in this category are size mistakes: a small firm signing a three-year HCM quote, or a growing one still running a chain of restaurants on a free plan built for one. This page organises ten tools by the headcount and workforce type they fit, says what each costs at that size, and says when to move up.

Pricing was checked in October 2026; published figures are stated as published and quote-only vendors carry labelled estimates. The list below is in size order rather than ranked, and the calculator models the tools with published prices at your headcount. The full workforce management review ranks the same field by situation.

The tools in detail

Connecteam

Free for ten, everything in one app
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 10 users; Operations hub Basic $29, Advanced $49, Expert $99 per month for the first 30 users then $0.80, $2.50 and $4.20 per extra user; HR and Communications hubs priced separately

What it is

Deskless workforce management in one app; free for ten, priced for the first 30 users.

Why it is on this list

Under 50 people, deskless: Connecteam. Scheduling, GPS clock, forms, tasks and chat on everyone's phone, free for ten users and $29 a month for 30. Payroll through integrations.

Where it is strong

  • Free for ten users
  • Everything a deskless team needs in one app
  • Priced for the first 30 users
  • Fast adoption on phones

Where it falls short

  • Hubs priced separately
  • No payroll
  • Light compliance depth

Homebase

Scheduling to payroll per location
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, POS Pricing (October 2026) Basic free for one location and 10 employees; Essentials $30, Plus $70, All-in-One $120 per location per month; payroll $49 plus $6 per employee

What it is

Scheduling, clock, hiring and payroll per location; free for one shop.

Why it is on this list

Under 50 people, one to five hourly locations: Homebase. Scheduling, clock on the POS, hiring, HR and payroll per location, free for one.

Where it is strong

  • Free for one location
  • Clock on the POS
  • Hiring and payroll in the same app
  • Per-location pricing suits small groups

Where it falls short

  • Per-location pricing fails at scale
  • Payroll add-on cost
  • Hourly businesses only

When I Work

Cheapest scheduling at any headcount
Runs on Web, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Essentials $2.50, Pro $5, Premium $8 per user per month; time and attendance add-on $1.50 or $2 per user

What it is

Scheduling and attendance for unlimited users from $2.50 plus a clock add-on; no payroll or HR.

Why it is on this list

Under 50 to 1,000, hourly, payroll elsewhere: When I Work. Auto-scheduling, swaps and forecasting from $2.50 a user, clock for $1.50 to $2 more.

Where it is strong

  • Cheapest scheduling at $2.50
  • Auto-scheduling and swaps on every plan
  • Unlimited users
  • Payroll integrations

Where it falls short

  • Clock is an add-on
  • No payroll or HR
  • Thinner compliance rules

Deputy

Compliance and demand scheduling at published prices
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Lite $5, Core $6.50, Pro $9 per user per month; $30 monthly minimum; payroll add-on $8 per user plus $49 and HR add-on $2

What it is

Scheduling, time and compliance for hourly operations at published prices from $5; payroll and HR as add-ons.

Why it is on this list

50 to 5,000, hourly, compliance matters: Deputy. Demand-driven auto-scheduling, rules by jurisdiction, a compliant clock, from $5 a user, with payroll and HR add-ons for the firms that grow into them.

Where it is strong

  • Published pricing from $5
  • Demand-driven auto-scheduling
  • Compliance by jurisdiction
  • Integrates with existing payroll and HR

Where it falls short

  • $30 minimum
  • Payroll and HR are add-ons
  • Not an HCM suite

Rippling

HR, payroll, time and IT together
Runs on Web, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Platform from $8 per employee per month plus a $35 monthly base fee, with payroll, benefits, time, HR and IT sold as modules; fully configured HR and payroll typically lands in the low-to-mid $20s per employee

What it is

HR, payroll, time and IT on one employee record from $8 plus a $35 base; modular.

Why it is on this list

10 to 2,000, mixed or salaried, want one system: Rippling. HR, payroll, benefits, time, scheduling and IT on one record from $8 plus $35, modules added as needed.

Where it is strong

  • HR, payroll, time and IT on one record
  • Published entry pricing
  • Fast to deploy
  • Strong automation and app provisioning

Where it falls short

  • Modular bill grows quickly
  • WFM is a module, not the centre
  • Base fee on small teams

Paycor

Payroll strength with hourly scheduling
Runs on Web, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Quote only since Paycor withdrew public pricing in 2025. Its last published ladder ran from $99 a month plus $6 per employee (Basic) to $299 plus $16 (Complete), which remains a reasonable guide to the shape of a quote

What it is

HR, payroll, time and scheduling for small and mid-sized employers; pricing withdrawn in 2025, last ladder $99 plus $6 to $299 plus $16.

Why it is on this list

20 to 1,000, payroll first: Paycor. Payroll and compliance with an hourly scheduler and talent tools on the upper tiers; quoted, with a known ladder.

Where it is strong

  • Payroll and compliance for mid-sized employers
  • Scheduling built for hourly teams
  • Talent and analytics included on upper tiers
  • Quote shape is known

Where it falls short

  • Public pricing withdrawn
  • Introductory discounts expire
  • ATS limited on lower tiers

Paylocity

Time and labour with a strong employee app
Runs on Web, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Quote only. Independent estimates describe a monthly base fee of around $200 to $250 plus a per-employee charge that rises from a few dollars for payroll to the high $20s for the full HCM suite; implementation is charged separately

What it is

Mid-market HR and payroll with time and labour and a strong mobile app; base fee plus per employee, quoted.

Why it is on this list

50 to 2,000, mobile-first workforce: Paylocity. Time and labour with cost allocation, benefits, talent and learning, and an employee app people open; quoted on a base fee plus per employee.

Where it is strong

  • Employee-facing mobile experience
  • Time and labour with cost allocation
  • Benefits, talent and learning in the suite
  • Mid-market focus

Where it falls short

  • Quote only
  • Base fee hurts small firms
  • Reporting can need configuration

UKG (Ready and Pro WFM)

The rules engine
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, UKG clocks Pricing (October 2026) Quote only. Independent buyer estimates for UKG Ready cluster around $20 to $33 per employee per month for the full suite, with implementation at 20 to 40 percent of first-year fees; UKG Pro Workforce Management (formerly Dimensions) is enterprise-priced

What it is

Mid-market (Ready) and enterprise (Pro WFM) workforce management with the deepest compliance and scheduling rules; quote only.

Why it is on this list

50 to enterprise, complex compliance: UKG Ready, then UKG Pro WFM at the top. The deepest rules engine, clocks and forecasting; quoted; budget for implementation.

Where it is strong

  • Deepest compliance and scheduling rules engine
  • Two products spanning mid-market to enterprise
  • Clocks, forecasting and labour analytics
  • Strong in healthcare, manufacturing and retail

Where it falls short

  • Quote only; implementation heavy
  • Two products can confuse buyers
  • Expensive below fifty people

Dayforce

One engine for HR, pay and schedules
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, clocks Pricing (October 2026) Quote only. Independent estimates run $22 to $31 per employee per month for core HCM and payroll and $28 to $34 with the workforce management suite; implementation is a significant one-time cost

What it is

HR, payroll, time and scheduling on one continuously calculating engine for mid-to-large employers; quote only.

Why it is on this list

500 to enterprise, especially multi-country: Dayforce. HR, payroll, time and schedules on one continuously calculating engine; quoted; a serious implementation.

Where it is strong

  • Single continuous-calculation engine
  • Schedule changes show payroll cost immediately
  • Multi-country payroll
  • Strong scheduling for hourly workforces

Where it falls short

  • Quote only; large implementation
  • Overkill below a few hundred staff
  • Long contracts

Workday HCM

System of record
Runs on Web, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Quote only, priced per employee on the payroll rather than per user. Independent estimates for HCM at scale run roughly $34 to $45 per employee per month; multi-year contracts and six-figure implementations are the norm

What it is

The enterprise HR system of record, with time, absence, frontline scheduling, planning and analytics; quote only.

Why it is on this list

1,000-plus: Workday. The system of record, with time, absence, frontline scheduling, planning and analytics as modules; per employee on payroll, multi-year.

Where it is strong

  • Enterprise system of record
  • Planning and analytics modules in the same data
  • Frontline scheduling added
  • Global scale

Where it falls short

  • Priced per employee on payroll, multi-year
  • Months to implement
  • Wrong size below a thousand people

When to move up

  • From a free plan when you open a second location (Homebase, 7shifts), pass ten users (Connecteam) or thirty (Sling), or need a clock or compliance rules the free tier lacks.
  • From a scheduler to Deputy or a suite when a manager is spending a day a week on the rota, when you employ in a state with daily overtime or break penalties, or when a fair workweek ordinance applies.
  • From specialist tools to an HCM suite when payroll, benefits and HR records live in three systems that disagree, when you cross about 300 to 500 employees, or when auditors ask for a single record.
  • From a mid-market suite to UKG Pro, Dayforce or Workday when headcount passes a few thousand, when you run payroll in several countries, or when planning and analytics need the same data as the schedule. The HR metrics guide covers what that data should produce.

Reading a quote

Most of the platforms on this page are quote-priced. Where I give a figure for a quote-only vendor it is the range independent buyer surveys and procurement advisers report, labelled as an estimate, not a price list; your quote will depend on headcount, modules, contract length and how late in the quarter you sign. Published prices are stated as published.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Disclosure. I have done work for Time Champ. It appears here because it belongs in this category, it is held to the same criteria as every other product, its drawbacks are listed like everyone else's, and it bought no placement. There are no affiliate or vendor links on this page.

Key takeaways

  • Most WFM buying mistakes are size mistakes; match the tool to headcount and workforce type first.
  • Under 50: Connecteam, Homebase, When I Work, Time Champ. 50 to 500: Deputy, Rippling, Paycor. 500 to 5,000: UKG Ready, Dayforce, ADP, Paylocity. Over 5,000: UKG Pro, Workday, Dayforce.
  • Move up when a free tier's limits bite, when compliance rules exceed the scheduler, or when three systems disagree about payroll.
  • Quote-only figures here are labelled estimates; model your own headcount with the calculator for published-price tools.

Frequently asked questions

What are the top workforce management tools?

By company size: Connecteam, Homebase and When I Work under 50 people; Deputy, Rippling and Paycor from 50 to 500; UKG Ready, Dayforce, ADP Workforce Now and Paylocity from 500 to 5,000; UKG Pro Workforce Management, Workday and Dayforce above that; Legion for large retail and hospitality; Time Champ for desk-based teams at any size.

What is the best workforce management tool for a small business?

Connecteam for deskless teams (free for ten users), Homebase for one to five hourly locations (free for one), When I Work for cheap hourly scheduling (from $2.50), Deputy when state labour rules matter (from $5), Time Champ for a desk-based team ($3.90), and Rippling if a small company wants HR, payroll and IT together at a published entry price.

When should a company move from a scheduling app to an HCM suite?

When payroll, benefits and HR records live in several systems that disagree, when headcount crosses roughly 300 to 500, when auditors or multi-state compliance demand a single record, or when the firm runs payroll in more than one country. Below those points a specialist tool integrated with payroll is usually cheaper and better at scheduling.

How much does workforce management cost per employee?

Published tools run $2.50 to $9 per user per month (When I Work, Time Champ, Deputy), $29 for 30 users (Connecteam) or $30 to $120 per location (Homebase). Rippling publishes $8 plus a $35 base as an entry point. Independent estimates for full HCM suites run roughly $20 to $35 per employee per month for UKG Ready, ADP and Dayforce and more for Workday, all before implementation.

Is enterprise workforce management software worth it for mid-sized companies?

Rarely below a thousand employees. The enterprise systems (UKG Pro WFM, Workday) are priced per employee on payroll on multi-year terms with long implementations, and mid-sized firms get most of the capability from UKG Ready, Dayforce, Deputy or Rippling at a fraction of the cost and effort.

Which workforce management tools publish their pricing?

Deputy, When I Work, Connecteam, Homebase, Sling, 7shifts, Time Champ and Rippling (entry price) publish prices. UKG, ADP Workforce Now, Dayforce, Workday, Paylocity, Legion and, since 2025, Paycor are quote-only.
