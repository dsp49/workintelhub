Best Employee Monitoring Software: 12 Tools Reviewed (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 22 min read

Employee monitoring software records what happens on work devices and accounts: which applications and websites are used, for how long, when the person is active or idle, and, depending on the product and the settings, screenshots, screen video, keystrokes, files, email and location. The twelve tools below are the ones a US buyer keeps meeting, and they are not twelve versions of the same thing. Some are productivity analytics tools that refuse to log a keystroke; some are security products that record everything; most are time trackers that grew a monitoring layer. Buying the wrong kind is the most common mistake in this category.

I reviewed each against the same checklist: what it captures and how much of that is optional, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee, what the pricing published in October 2026 actually costs once minimums and add-ons are counted, how long it keeps data, and what users say about living with it. The first three are starting points for the three situations most buyers are in, the rest follow alphabetically, and every card ends with where the product falls short, because every one of them does somewhere.

The tools in detail

ActivTrak

The most useful data with the least intrusion
Runs on Windows, macOS, Chromebook Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 3 people (30-day retention); Work Activity Tracking $10, Workforce Management $15, Productivity Optimization $17, Workflow $21 per user per month, billed annually

What it is

ActivTrak is productivity analytics first and monitoring second. The agent records which applications and websites are in use, how long they are active, and when the person is idle, and the platform turns that into productivity classifications, utilisation and focus reports, schedule adherence and, on the higher plans, capacity and headcount planning and AI coaching suggestions. Screenshots are available but optional, and there is no keystroke logging at all, which is a design choice the company is open about. The free plan covers three people with 30 days of history and is the easiest way in this category to see what the data looks like.

Why it is on this list

ActivTrak leads this list because it answers the question most buyers actually have, which is where the team's time goes and whether the workload is sane, with the least collection needed to answer it. The free tier lets you see your own team's data for three people before spending anything, and the paid plans add the schedule adherence, capacity and coaching views that turn the data into a management conversation. It is the wrong tool for an investigation and says so; for everyday management it is the one I would start with.

Where it is strong

  • A free plan for three people, which is the best way to see the data before buying
  • Productivity, utilisation and focus analytics that managers can act on without reading screenshots
  • No keystroke logging and optional screenshots: the least invasive serious tool here
  • Workforce planning, capacity and coaching features on the upper plans

Where it falls short

  • No Linux agent and no keystroke logging, so it cannot serve investigations
  • The useful features sit on the $15 and $17 plans; the $10 plan is thin
  • Data retention of 6 to 12 months unless you pay for more

Hubstaff

Time, screenshots, GPS and payroll in one product
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10, Enterprise $25 per seat per month billed annually ($7, $9 and $12 monthly); 2-seat minimum; 14-day trial

What it is

Hubstaff started as a time tracker for remote and distributed teams and still behaves like one: time is tracked against projects and tasks, activity levels are measured from keyboard and mouse input, optional screenshots are taken at a configurable interval, and the result flows into timesheets, payroll, invoices and client reports. It has GPS tracking and job sites for field teams, Chromebook and Linux agents alongside Windows and Mac, and the largest body of user reviews of any product here. Monitoring depth is deliberately moderate: activity percentages and screenshots, not keystrokes or screen video.

Why it is on this list

Hubstaff is here for the team that is paying people by the hour across locations and needs the time, the proof and the payment in one place. The screenshots and activity levels are the proof; the timesheets, payroll and invoicing are the payment; GPS covers the people who are not at a desk. Its breadth of platforms and its review volume make it the safe choice in that situation, with the caveat that activity percentages measure input, and teams that are judged on them learn to produce input.

Where it is strong

  • Time tracking, screenshots, GPS, payroll and invoicing in one product
  • Runs everywhere, including Linux and Chromebook, with mobile apps
  • Over 2,300 verified reviews and a long track record with distributed teams
  • Clear per-seat pricing with a cheap entry plan

Where it falls short

  • Activity percentages reward mouse movement, not work, and people know it
  • Two-seat minimum and add-ons on the lower plans
  • Monitoring depth is modest by design; not a security tool

Teramind

The deepest capture and the only real DLP here
Runs on Windows (full); macOS and Linux with reduced features Pricing (October 2026) Starter $14, UAM $28, DLP $32 per seat per month billed annually ($15, $30, $35 monthly); 5-seat minimum; Enterprise by quote

What it is

Teramind is the security end of this market. The Windows agent records screens as video, logs keystrokes, reads text on screen with OCR, tracks files, printing, email and chat, and runs a behaviour rules engine that can alert, block or lock a session when a rule is tripped. The DLP plan adds content classification and data exfiltration controls. It deploys to the cloud, a private cloud or on-premise, offers revealed, standard and stealth agents, and is the product compliance and security teams reach for when the question is insider risk rather than productivity. Mac and Linux agents exist with reduced features.

Why it is on this list

Teramind is on the list for the buyer whose problem is risk, not productivity: regulated data, insider threats, a compliance team that needs to show what happened on a screen on a given afternoon. Nothing else here records, searches and blocks the way it does, and nothing else should be deployed without a written policy, notice and a retention limit, because it can collect everything. If the question is "are people working", it is the wrong tool and an expensive one.

Where it is strong

  • The deepest capture in the category: screen video, keystrokes, OCR, files, email, chat
  • Behaviour rules that can block or lock, not just report
  • DLP, private cloud and on-premise for regulated environments
  • Revealed, standard and stealth agents for different policies

Where it falls short

  • Five-seat minimum and the highest entry price here
  • Mac and Linux agents lack many Windows features
  • Capable of collecting far more than most employers should; needs policy discipline

Controlio

Recording and keystrokes at time-tracker prices
Runs on Windows, macOS Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.67 and Advanced $9.99 per user per month billed annually ($7.99 and $11.99 monthly); Enterprise by quote; 14-day trial

What it is

Controlio, from the company that made Work Examiner, is a cloud or on-premise monitoring tool with continuous screen recording, keystroke logging, application and website tracking, productivity scoring and a stealth mode, at a price closer to the time trackers than to Teramind. It is a straightforward product: a dashboard of what each person did, searchable recordings, and reports by user and department, without the workforce planning layer that ActivTrak and Insightful sell on top.

Why it is on this list

Controlio earns a place as the budget route to screen recording and keystroke capture for a Windows or Mac estate. It does the investigative job that ActivTrak declines to do, at a third of Teramind's price, without the rules engine, DLP or Linux coverage. Small companies with a specific concern, such as a department handling card data, choose it over the security suites they cannot justify.

Where it is strong

  • Continuous screen recording and keystrokes at a mid-market price
  • Cloud or on-premise
  • Simple to deploy and read; little training needed
  • Stealth mode where policy and law allow it

Where it falls short

  • No Linux agent
  • Continuous recording and keystrokes raise notice and consent obligations in several states
  • Analytics are basic next to ActivTrak or Insightful

CurrentWare

Control and reporting in one suite
Runs on Windows, macOS Pricing (October 2026) BrowseReporter $12, BrowseControl $6, AccessPatrol $12, enPowerManager $6 per user per month; full suite $20; billed annually with a minimum of about $1,440 a year; 14-day trial

What it is

CurrentWare is a modular suite rather than a single monitoring product. BrowseReporter tracks web and application use with productivity reporting and optional screenshots, BrowseControl filters and blocks websites and applications, AccessPatrol controls USB and other peripherals with data loss alerts, and enPowerManager handles power and logon schedules. It runs on Windows and macOS, can be hosted on your own server or in CurrentWare's cloud, and is bought annually. The strength is control, blocking and USB policy alongside the reporting; the productivity analytics are simpler than the specialists'.

Why it is on this list

CurrentWare is the IT department's pick: it blocks as well as reports. Web filtering, USB lockdown and power management sit alongside activity reporting, and the data can stay on a server you own. It belongs on this list for the organisation whose monitoring is a policy enforcement question rather than a management one.

Where it is strong

  • Web and application blocking, USB control and reporting in one suite
  • Your own server or their cloud; data stays where you put it
  • Modules can be bought separately
  • Strong fit for IT-led policy enforcement

Where it falls short

  • Annual billing with a minimum around $1,440 a year
  • Productivity analytics are simpler than the specialists'
  • Suite pricing adds up if you only need reporting

DeskTime

The cheapest tool with scheduling built in
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Pro $7 and Premium $10 per user per month ($6.42 and $9.17 billed annually); Enterprise by quote; free Lite plan for one user; 14-day trial

What it is

DeskTime is an automatic time tracker from Latvia that has been monitoring desktops since 2011. It tracks applications and URLs, classifies them as productive, unproductive or neutral per role, computes a productivity score, and adds project time tracking, an absence calendar, shift scheduling and, on the Premium plan, screenshots. A private time toggle lets people stop tracking for personal use. It is cheaper than most of the field, runs on Windows, Mac and Linux with mobile apps, and has a free single-user plan.

Why it is on this list

DeskTime makes the list as the tool with the most respect for the person being tracked at the lowest price: automatic tracking, a private time switch, no screenshots unless you pay for them and switch them on, plus shift scheduling and an absence calendar that small teams would otherwise buy separately. It is the sensible choice for a company that wants productivity data without a monitoring culture.

Where it is strong

  • Automatic tracking with a private time toggle employees can use
  • Shift scheduling and absence calendar built in
  • Low price with a free single-user plan
  • Windows, Mac, Linux and mobile apps

Where it falls short

  • Screenshots only on Premium
  • Productivity scoring is simple and needs tuning per role
  • Reporting is lighter for large organisations

Insightful

Location-aware productivity analytics
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Workforce Analytics $8, Workflow Optimization $12 (beta), both together $16 per seat per month billed annually; Enterprise by quote; screen recording and on-demand screenshots $4 add-ons; 7-day trial

What it is

Insightful, formerly Workpuls, sits between the productivity analysts and the security tools. It tracks applications and websites, takes screenshots, measures active and idle time, classifies productivity by team, and reports utilisation, capacity and work location (office, remote, hybrid). Screen recording, on-demand screenshots and a workspace security module are paid add-ons rather than defaults. It runs on Windows, Mac and Linux, offers on-premise deployment from 250 employees, keeps data for 24 months, and carries one of the highest review ratings in the category.

Why it is on this list

Insightful is here for the distributed knowledge team that wants ActivTrak-style analytics with better coverage of where people worked and the option to add recording when a case requires it. The work location insights separate office, remote and hybrid days without extra setup, the retention is long, and the Linux agent covers engineering teams the others miss.

Where it is strong

  • Work location insights that separate office, remote and hybrid days
  • Capacity and utilisation reporting, with screen recording as an add-on rather than a default
  • 24 months of retention and on-premise at scale
  • Among the highest user ratings in the category

Where it falls short

  • Screen recording and on-demand screenshots are $4 add-ons each
  • Workflow Optimization is still in beta
  • On-premise needs 250 employees

Kickidler

Live screen viewing at the lowest price
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Time Tracking from about $2.75 and Employee Monitoring from about $5.50 per user per month depending on term, with cheaper long-term and lifetime licences; free trial

What it is

Kickidler is a low-cost monitoring tool whose distinguishing feature is live screen viewing: a manager can watch employee screens in real time in a grid, with video recording, keystroke logging, violation detection and remote control available in the full Employee Monitoring tier. A lighter Time Tracking tier drops the video and keystrokes for activity and productivity reports and an employee self-monitoring view. It runs on Windows, Mac and Linux, can be installed on-premise, and sells long-term and lifetime licences that make it the cheapest serious option on a multi-year view.

Why it is on this list

Kickidler is on the list because of its price and its live view, which some operations environments, call centres and back offices among them, genuinely want. On a lifetime or long-term licence it is the cheapest full monitoring suite here. It is also the one I would be most careful deploying: live viewing and remote control are intrusive, and the notice and consent requirements apply with full force.

Where it is strong

  • The lowest cost here on a multi-year or lifetime licence
  • Live screen viewing of many employees at once
  • Video recording, keystrokes and remote control in the full tier
  • Windows, Mac and Linux, cloud or on-premise

Where it falls short

  • Live viewing and remote control are intrusive and unlawful without notice in several states
  • Interface and documentation lag the premium products
  • Pricing varies by term and region; confirm in writing

Monitask

Quick, cheap and adjustable screenshots
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Pro $6.49, Business $8.99, Business Premium $12.99 per user per month billed annually ($7.99, $10.99, $15.99 monthly); Enterprise for 100-plus; 10-day trial

What it is

Monitask is a time tracker with monitoring attached, aimed at small teams and agencies that pay by the hour. It tracks time to projects and tasks, takes screenshots at a configurable rate (30 an hour by default, adjustable or off), measures activity from input, tracks applications and websites on the Business plan and full URLs and search queries on Business Premium, and produces timesheets and payroll exports. The agent can run visibly or in stealth mode. Data retention is short on the lower plans, one to two months, which matters for anyone who needs history.

Why it is on this list

Monitask is here for the agency or small team that bills by the hour and needs screenshots as proof of work without a long contract or a complicated product. It is cheap, quick, and lets each team set or disable screenshots. The short data retention on the main plans is the thing to check before choosing it for anything beyond billing.

Where it is strong

  • Inexpensive and quick to set up for a small team or agency
  • Screenshot frequency adjustable per team, or off
  • Project time, timesheets and payroll exports
  • Full URL and search query tracking on the top plan

Where it falls short

  • Data kept for only one or two months on the main plans
  • Website and app tracking only from the Business plan
  • Not built for large organisations

Attendance, shifts and monitoring from $3.90 a seat

Time Champ combines attendance, time tracking and monitoring in one agent at a low per-seat price. Time Champ is on the list for the employer who needs attendance, shift management and monitoring in one agent and does not want to pay enterprise prices for it. The Starter plan is the cheapest route here to automatic attendance plus productivity classification, and the configurable screenshots, blurring and silent-or-visible choice on Professional give a policy team real options. Time Doctor

Remote-team time tracking with work-life balance reporting

Where it is strong

Veriato

Behaviour-based risk scoring

Veriato is an insider risk product. Three kinds of tool sold under one name

Productivity analytics (ActivTrak, Insightful, DeskTime) answers "where does the time go": application and website use, active and idle time, utilisation, focus and workload, with screenshots optional or absent and no keystrokes. It is the right kind for a manager who wants to run a team better and the wrong kind for an investigation. Time trackers with monitoring (Hubstaff, Time Doctor, Monitask, Time Champ) start from hours worked against projects, add screenshots and activity as proof of work, and flow into timesheets and payroll; they suit hourly, remote and outsourced teams. Security and insider risk tools (Teramind, Veriato, and in part Controlio, CurrentWare and Kickidler) record screens, keystrokes, files and communications, run rules, and block; they suit regulated data and security teams, and they are the tools the word bossware was coined for. Decide which kind you need before comparing features within it; the how monitoring works guide explains what each layer actually captures.

The law in one paragraph

Monitoring company devices and accounts is lawful across the United States, with notice laws in New York, Connecticut and Delaware, all-party consent rules for audio in about a dozen states, limits under the National Labor Relations Act on monitoring that chills discussion of working conditions, and biometric and privacy statutes in Illinois and California. Every tool on this page can be configured lawfully and most can be configured unlawfully. The monitoring laws by state page has the rules, the policy template and consent form are the documents, and the privacy line page lists what should not be collected whatever the law allows.

How to run your own evaluation

  1. Write the decision first. What will you do differently with the data? If the honest answer is "see whether people are working", read what to measure first; output measures answer that question better and cheaper.
  2. Trial on your own team, visibly. Two tools, two weeks, with the people tracked told what is collected and able to see their own data. A trial run secretly on a few people tells you nothing about how the tool will land.
  3. Turn off everything you do not need. Screenshots off, or blurred; keystrokes off; recording off. Then ask whether the remaining data answers the decision. Usually it does.
  4. Price it at your headcount with minimums and add-ons. A $4.99 plan with a two-seat minimum and a $4 add-on is not $4.99. The small-business page in this series has a calculator for that.
  5. Check retention and export. How long is data kept, can you shorten it, and can you get it out. Short retention protects employees; it also limits investigations.
  6. Write the policy before go-live. The policy template and how to write one employees will sign cover it. In New York, Connecticut and Delaware the notice is a legal requirement.

When not to monitor

Monitoring is the wrong answer when the problem is a manager who cannot describe what good output looks like, when the team is salaried knowledge workers whose output is visible in the work itself, when trust is already low and the tool will be read as an accusation, and when the data would feed automatic decisions about pay or discipline. The monitoring statistics page has the survey data on how employees respond, the ethical monitoring playbook sets out a design that holds up, and the mouse jiggler page shows what happens to activity metrics once people are judged on them.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Key takeaways

  • Decide which kind of tool you need first: productivity analytics, a time tracker with monitoring, or a security tool. Features only compare within a kind.
  • ActivTrak for the most useful data with the least intrusion; Hubstaff for hourly and distributed teams; Teramind when the problem is security.
  • Entry prices in October 2026 run from about $2.75 (Kickidler) and $3.90 (Time Champ) to $18 (Veriato); minimums and add-ons move the real cost more than the tier.
  • Every tool can be configured lawfully and most can be configured unlawfully; notice laws apply in New York, Connecticut and Delaware.
  • Trial visibly on your own team, switch off what you do not need, and write the policy before go-live.

Time Champ and DeskTime are the value choices; Insightful and Time Doctor suit hybrid and remote teams. The chooser on this page shortlists three from those rules.

How much does employee monitoring software cost?

Published entry prices in October 2026 range from about $2.75 to $5 per user per month (Kickidler, Time Champ, Hubstaff) through $6 to $10 (DeskTime, Monitask, Controlio, Time Doctor, Insightful, ActivTrak) to $14 to $18 (Teramind, Veriato). Minimum seat counts, annual billing and add-ons for screenshots, recording or longer retention change the total, often by more than the tier difference.

Is employee monitoring software legal?

Monitoring company-owned devices and accounts is lawful across the United States. New York, Connecticut and Delaware require written notice; about a dozen states require all-party consent to record audio; the National Labor Relations Act limits monitoring that chills discussion of working conditions; Illinois and California add biometric and privacy rules. Monitoring personal devices or accounts without consent is not lawful.

Can employee monitoring software see everything on my computer?

On a company-managed device, the security tools (Teramind, Veriato, Controlio, Kickidler) can record the screen, keystrokes, files and communications. The productivity tools (ActivTrak, Insightful, DeskTime) record application and website use, activity and optionally screenshots, and ActivTrak logs no keystrokes at all. On a personal device with nothing installed, none of them see anything.

What is the least invasive employee monitoring software?

ActivTrak and DeskTime: both measure application and website use and activity, neither logs keystrokes, screenshots are optional (DeskTime only on Premium), and DeskTime gives employees a private-time switch. Insightful and Time Champ can be configured similarly, with screenshots off or blurred.

Does employee monitoring improve productivity?

The evidence is mixed. Visibility into workload and time allocation helps managers rebalance work and cut meetings; activity scoring tends to produce the appearance of activity rather than output, and surveys show it damages trust. Tools used for workload analytics with transparent policies do better than tools used to score individuals. The monitoring statistics page on this site has the survey data.

Which employee monitoring software works on Mac and Linux?

Hubstaff, DeskTime, Insightful, Kickidler, Monitask, Time Champ and Time Doctor run on Windows, macOS and Linux. ActivTrak covers Windows, Mac and Chromebook but not Linux. Controlio and CurrentWare cover Windows and Mac. Teramind's Mac and Linux agents exist but with fewer features than Windows. Veriato covers Windows, Mac, Chromebook and Android.
