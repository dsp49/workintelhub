Best People Analytics Software: 8 Platforms Reviewed (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 14 min read

People analytics software answers questions about employees from the data HR already holds: why turnover rose, who is at risk of leaving, whether pay is equitable, where the hiring funnel leaks, how diverse the pipeline and the leadership are, and what a restructure would cost. The eight platforms here differ less in features than in who they are built for: the enterprise with a thousand employees and a data team, the European organisation with GDPR obligations, the mid-sized company that needs a price on a web page, or the customer of Workday, SAP or Microsoft 365 for whom a module beats a platform.

I reviewed eight with pricing checked in October 2026 and labelled estimates where it is quoted. The first three are starting points for the three most common buyers; the rest follow alphabetically, and the use-case table below says which platform handles which question. The workforce analytics review covers the activity-side and planning tools that sit beside these.

Governed model, hundreds of metrics, predictive attrition

What it is

The reference people analytics platform: governed data model, hundreds of metrics, predictive attrition and planning; quote only, 1,000-plus employees.

Why it is on this list

Visier leads people analytics as the platform that defined the category: a governed data model across HR systems, several hundred pre-built metrics and questions, predictive attrition, pay equity and diversity analytics, and a natural-language assistant. Maturity before tooling

People analytics fails more often on data and definitions than on software. Before a quote: one agreed definition of headcount, turnover, hire and promotion across the organisation (the HR metrics guide has the standard ones); clean job architecture and location data in the HRIS; a known owner for each source system; and two or three business questions that leaders will act on. With those in place, a mid-sized organisation can get most of the value from ChartHop or an analyst with Power BI, and an enterprise will get far more from Visier or One Model than one that buys the platform first and the definitions later.

Privacy and AI rules

People analytics runs on personal data and the rules follow it: GDPR and the UK and EU works council regimes for European employees, the California Consumer Privacy Act's employee provisions, Illinois's biometric and AI-in-hiring laws, New York City's automated employment decision tool audit requirement, and the California automated decision rules for any model that affects employment decisions. Activity-side tools (ActivTrak, Time Champ, Viva Insights) are monitoring and need the monitoring policy and the notice several states require; the privacy line guide says what stays out of scope. Aggregate first, with minimum group sizes, and treat predictive attrition scores as a prompt for a conversation, never as a decision.

Reading a quote

Most of this category is quote-priced. Where I give a figure for a quote-only vendor it is the range independent buyer reports and procurement advisers describe, labelled as an estimate, not a price list; your quote will depend on headcount, data sources, modules and contract length. Published prices are stated as published.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Key takeaways

  • People analytics platforms differ in who they are built for more than in features: enterprise, European, mid-sized, or a customer of Workday, SAP or Microsoft 365.
  • Visier as the reference, One Model for data teams, Crunchr in Europe, ChartHop for mid-sized at a published price; Orgvue for design; Viva for collaboration; Workday and SAP modules for their customers.
  • Agree definitions and clean the HRIS before buying; the platform cannot fix either.
  • Pay equity analysis is now a legal obligation in several states; attrition scores are probabilities for population-level use.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best people analytics software?

Visier for organisations of a thousand or more, as the platform that defined the category with a governed data model, hundreds of metrics and predictive attrition; One Model for analytics teams that want the data layer and explainable models; Crunchr for European organisations wanting fast deployment with GDPR governance. ChartHop for mid-sized companies at a published price, Orgvue for organisation design, Viva Insights for collaboration analytics in Microsoft 365, Workday People Analytics and SAP SuccessFactors Workforce Analytics for their own customers.

What does people analytics software do?

It connects HRIS, applicant tracking, payroll, performance and engagement data into one model and answers questions about turnover and attrition risk, pay equity, diversity, hiring funnel performance, mobility and manager effectiveness, with dashboards, pre-built metrics, natural-language queries and in the stronger platforms predictive models and planning.

How much does people analytics software cost?

ChartHop publishes $2 per employee for Basic with analytics modules per employee above a $9,000 annual minimum; Viva Insights is $6 per user as a Microsoft 365 add-on. Visier, One Model, Crunchr and Orgvue are quoted; independent reports place One Model between roughly $30,000 and $150,000 a year, Visier above $50,000 for mid-market and well above for enterprise, Orgvue between $40,000 and $200,000. Workday and SAP analytics are priced within their contracts.

Is people analytics software worth it for a company under 500 employees?

Usually not the enterprise platforms. ChartHop is priced for that size, and an HR analyst with Power BI or Tableau on clean HRIS exports covers headcount, turnover, hiring and pay reporting for the cost of the licences. The enterprise platforms pay when data lives in several systems, definitions must be governed across a large organisation, and predictive models are wanted.

Can people analytics software do pay equity analysis?

Yes: Visier, ChartHop, One Model, Crunchr, SAP and Workday all offer pay equity analysis controlling for role, grade, location, tenure and performance, with gaps by gender and race surfaced. Several states' pay transparency laws now require employers to analyse and in some cases report pay data, which makes this the most common first use case.

What is the difference between people analytics and HR reporting?

HR reporting describes what happened: headcount, hires, exits, by month and department, from one system. People analytics joins several sources, applies agreed definitions, segments and compares, explains why, and in the stronger platforms predicts: who is at risk, which hiring sources produce retained employees, where pay gaps exist after controlling for legitimate factors.
