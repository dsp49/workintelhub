Top Employee Productivity Tracking Tools: How Each Scores

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 15 min read

Employee productivity tracking tools produce a number per person, and the number is only as good as the method behind it. This page reviews eight tools by that method: what the score is built from, what it rewards, whether it accounts for attendance and meetings, who can see it, and what it costs. It is a narrower cut than the productivity monitoring review, which covers the whole category including the agentless and security tools; here the question is how each tracker scores an individual and whether that score is one you can stand behind.

Pricing was checked in October 2026. The first three represent three defensible scoring philosophies: coaching-oriented (ActivTrak), attendance-aware (Time Champ) and screenshot-free (WorkTime). The rest follow with the method each uses stated plainly. Read the productivity formula page first if you want the measure the tools are approximating.

The tools in detail

ActivTrak

Utilisation, focus and coaching prompts
Runs on Windows, macOS, Chromebook Pricing (October 2026) Free for 3 users; Work Activity Tracking $10, Workforce Management $15, Productivity Optimization $17, Workflow $21 per user per month, annual

What it is

Productivity analytics from app and website use with utilisation, focus and coaching reports; optional screenshots, no keystrokes.

Why it is on this list

ActivTrak leads the tracking list because of what it does with the score: rather than ranking people it turns the data into focus time, utilisation and workload balance per person and surfaces coaching opportunities, so the conversation that follows is about load and interruptions rather than about a number. Its classification engine is the most mature here and its free tier shows the method on three people.

Where it is strong

  • Utilisation, focus and workload reports managers act on
  • No keystrokes; screenshots optional
  • Free for three people
  • Coaching prompts and capacity planning on the upper plans

Where it falls short

  • No Linux
  • The reports that justify it are on the $15 and $17 plans
  • Retention 6 to 12 months unless extended

Time Champ

Attendance-aware scoring from $3.90
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux; iOS and Android for GPS Pricing (October 2026) Starter $3.90, Professional $6.90, Enterprise $13.90 per user per month, annual; recording, keylogger and DLP as add-ons

What it is

Attendance and a productivity score from one agent at $3.90, with configurable screenshots.

Why it is on this list

Time Champ is second because it scores productivity in the context of attendance: when the person arrived, which shift they were on, how much of the shift was active in productive applications, with intensity graphs that show the shape of the day rather than a single figure. At $3.90 for Starter and $6.90 for the plan with reports it is the cheapest tracking tool with a real scoring model, and the employee can be shown their own data in visible mode.

Where it is strong

  • Attendance and productivity from one agent at $3.90
  • Configurable, blurrable or visible screenshots
  • Intensity graphs and app reports
  • Linux and mobile GPS

Where it falls short

  • Short detailed history below Enterprise
  • Add-ons for recording and DLP
  • Smaller review base

WorkTime

Productivity and attendance without images
Runs on Windows, macOS, terminal and Citrix servers Pricing (October 2026) Free for 3 employees with two weeks of history; Basic $5.83, Premium $7.49, Enterprise $9.16 per employee per month, annual ($6.99, $8.99, $10.99 monthly)

What it is

Non-invasive productivity and attendance monitoring with no screenshots and content-free keystroke counts.

Why it is on this list

WorkTime is third for its refusal to take screenshots while still producing a productivity and attendance record: application and website classification, active and idle time, logins, and a keystroke counter that records how much was typed and never what. For an employer that wants a score it can defend to a works council or a regulator, that design is the point.

Where it is strong

  • No screenshots at any price
  • Keystroke counter without content
  • Cloud or on-premise with compliance modes
  • Free for three employees

Where it falls short

  • Windows and Mac only
  • No screenshots means no evidence for investigations
  • Reporting is plainer than ActivTrak's

DeskTime

Automatic productive, neutral, unproductive scoring
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Lite free for one user; Pro $6.42 and Premium $9.17 per user per month, annual ($7 and $10 monthly); Enterprise by quote

What it is

A daily productivity score from automatic app and URL classification, with a private time switch and screenshots only on Premium.

Why it is on this list

DeskTime is here for the simplest version of the method: every application and URL is productive, neutral or unproductive for the role, the active time is totalled, and the person gets a daily percentage. It works well once the lists are tuned and poorly before, and the private time switch keeps it fair.

Where it is strong

  • Cheapest daily productivity score here
  • Private time switch
  • Shifts and absence built in
  • Free for one user

Where it falls short

  • Simple scoring that needs tuning per role
  • Screenshots only on Premium
  • Light reporting at scale

Hubstaff

Activity percentage against tracked time
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10, Enterprise $25 per seat per month, annual; 2-seat minimum

What it is

Activity-based productivity attached to tracked time, projects and pay, on every platform.

Why it is on this list

Hubstaff is on the list because many employers mean activity when they say productivity: the share of tracked time with keyboard and mouse input, shown per person and per project with screenshots as evidence. It is honest about what it measures and ties it to pay, which is the right use of an input metric and the wrong one for knowledge work.

Where it is strong

  • Productivity attached to projects and pay
  • Every platform
  • Insights add-on for utilisation and focus
  • Cheap entry plan

Where it falls short

  • Activity measures input, not output
  • Two-seat minimum
  • Not a knowledge-work productivity measure

Insightful

Utilisation and capacity with two years of history
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Workforce Analytics $8, Workflow Optimization $12, both $16 per seat per month, annual; Enterprise by quote; recording add-ons $4

What it is

Utilisation, capacity and work-location analytics with 24 months of history and recording as an add-on.

Why it is on this list

Insightful is here for employers who want to track the team's utilisation over time rather than score individuals day by day: capacity, utilisation and productivity trends with two years of history and work-location context, which is what a quarterly review of how a team is working needs.

Where it is strong

  • Location-aware utilisation and capacity
  • 24 months of history
  • Linux agent
  • Recording is an add-on, not a default

Where it falls short

  • Recording and on-demand screenshots cost extra
  • Workflow Optimization still in beta
  • On-premise needs 250 seats

Monitask

Cheap classification and activity
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Pro $6.49, Business $8.99, Business Premium $12.99 per user per month, annual; 1 to 2 months of history

What it is

Simple productivity reports with adjustable screenshots for small teams, short retention.

Why it is on this list

Monitask earns its place as the budget tracking tool for a small team: activity, screenshots at an adjustable rate and application classification from the Business plan, with a short retention window that suits billing and not much else.

Where it is strong

  • Cheap and fast to deploy
  • Adjustable screenshots
  • Project and task tracking
  • Payroll exports

Where it falls short

  • One to two months of history
  • Classification only from Business
  • Small-team tool

Time Doctor

Productivity plus work-life balance
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, Android Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.70, Standard $11.70, Premium $16.70 per user per month, annual; Enterprise by quote

What it is

Productivity from classified web and app use with work-life balance and Office versus Remote reports.

Why it is on this list

Time Doctor closes the list with a scoring model that includes the other direction: its work-life balance report flags people working late or too long alongside the usual productivity classification, and the Office versus Remote report compares settings. Remote and outsourced employers are its home.

Where it is strong

  • Work-life balance report
  • Office versus Remote comparison
  • Payroll and 60-plus integrations
  • Chromebook and Android agents

Where it falls short

  • Real price is $11.70
  • Screenshots draw objections
  • Interface shows its age

The four scoring methods

MethodHow the number is builtRewardsTools
ClassificationActive time in apps and sites classed productive, neutral or unproductive per roleTime in approved toolsDeskTime, Time Champ, WorkTime, Insightful, ActivTrak, Time Doctor, Monitask
Activity percentageShare of tracked time with keyboard or mouse inputInput volumeHubstaff, Time Doctor, Monitask
Utilisation and focusProductive active time against available hours, with focus blocks and meeting time separatedSustained work and sane loadActivTrak, Insightful
Attendance-weightedClassification within the scheduled shift, with late starts and idle timeBeing present and active in the shiftTime Champ, Time Doctor

Every method is gameable in proportion to how directly it is rewarded. Activity percentages produce mouse jigglers; classification produces work done in the approved application; attendance weighting produces presence. The methods that resist gaming best are the ones used for team patterns rather than individual consequences, which is why ActivTrak and Insightful score best here.

A score you can defend

  • Role-specific classification, reviewed with the team. A browser is not unproductive for a researcher.
  • Meetings and offline work counted. ActivTrak's offline meeting time and calendar integrations do this; most tools treat a meeting as idle.
  • Trend, not snapshot. A week against the person's own baseline, not a day against a colleague.
  • Visible to the person. Every tool here except WorkTime offers a self-view; Time Champ's visible mode and RescueTime's design go furthest.
  • Written into policy, including what the score will and will not be used for. The performance tracking guide and the policy template cover the wording.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Disclosure. I have done work for Time Champ. It appears here because it belongs in this category, it is held to the same criteria as every other product, its drawbacks are listed like everyone else's, and it bought no placement. There are no affiliate or vendor links on this page.

Key takeaways

  • A productivity score is built one of four ways: classification, activity percentage, utilisation and focus, or attendance-weighted; each rewards something different and is gamed accordingly.
  • ActivTrak for coaching-oriented scoring, Time Champ for attendance-aware scoring at the lowest price, WorkTime for scoring without screenshots.
  • Scores used for team patterns resist gaming; scores used for individual consequences do not.
  • Defensible scoring is role-specific, counts meetings, compares to the person's own trend, is visible to the person and is written into policy.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best employee productivity tracking tool?

ActivTrak if the score should feed coaching and workload conversations, because it turns activity data into focus time, utilisation and coaching prompts rather than rankings. Time Champ if attendance belongs in the score and price matters, from $3.90 a seat. WorkTime if screenshots are unacceptable. Hubstaff and Monitask suit hourly teams where activity is tied to pay; Insightful suits team trends over time.

How do productivity tracking tools calculate a productivity score?

Most classify every application and website as productive, neutral or unproductive for the role and total the active time in each, where active means keyboard or mouse input within a timeout; the score is the productive share. Some report an activity percentage (share of time with input) instead, and the better tools separate meetings and focus blocks and express the result as utilisation against available hours.

Can employees game productivity tracking?

Yes, in proportion to how directly the score is rewarded. Activity percentages are defeated by mouse jigglers and busywork; classification is defeated by doing low-value work in approved applications; attendance weighting rewards presence. Scores used for team-level patterns and coaching are gamed far less than scores tied to individual pay or discipline.

Should employees see their own productivity score?

Yes. People who can see their own pattern tend to adjust it, and a score the person cannot see is a surveillance measure rather than a productivity one. Every tool on this page except WorkTime offers a self-view; Time Champ's visible mode and RescueTime's employee-first design go furthest.

Which productivity tracking tools work without screenshots?

WorkTime takes none at any price. ActivTrak and DeskTime have screenshots off by default (DeskTime only offers them on Premium). Time Champ can run with screenshots off or blurred in visible mode. Insightful sells recording and on-demand screenshots as add-ons. Hubstaff, Time Doctor and Monitask are built around optional screenshots.

How much do productivity tracking tools cost?

In October 2026 on annual billing: Time Champ $3.90, Hubstaff $4.99, WorkTime $5.83, DeskTime $6.42, Monitask $6.49, Time Doctor $6.70, Insightful $8, ActivTrak $10 per user per month at entry, with the fuller reporting on ActivTrak at $15 to $17 and on Time Doctor at $11.70. ActivTrak, DeskTime and WorkTime have free tiers.
