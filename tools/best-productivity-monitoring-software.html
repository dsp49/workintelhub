Best Productivity Monitoring Software: 12 Tools (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 20 min read

Productivity monitoring software tries to turn what happens on a work computer into a measure of productivity. Every tool on this page does it the same basic way: it classifies the applications and websites a person uses as productive or not for their role, totals the active time in each, and reports the result as a score, a utilisation figure or a trend. The differences are in what else is captured (screenshots, keystrokes, nothing), how the result is presented (a coaching prompt, a capacity chart, a ranking), who gets to see it, and whether an agent is installed at all.

I reviewed twelve tools on those differences, with pricing checked in October 2026. The first three are starting points for the most common buyer, a manager who wants to understand workload and focus without a surveillance programme; the rest follow alphabetically and include the agentless, the employee-first and the security-grade ends of the market. A caution runs through all of it: the productivity these tools measure is activity, not output, and the page says where that matters.

The tools in detail

ActivTrak

The most actionable productivity analytics
Runs on Windows, macOS, Chromebook Pricing (October 2026) Free for 3 users; Work Activity Tracking $10, Workforce Management $15, Productivity Optimization $17, Workflow $21 per user per month, annual

What it is

ActivTrak measures productivity from the applications and websites in use and the time spent active in them, classified by role as productive, unproductive or undefined. On top of that sit the reports managers actually use: utilisation and focus time per person and team, schedule adherence, workload balance, and on the higher plans capacity planning and AI-generated coaching prompts that suggest what to raise in a one-on-one. Screenshots are optional and keystrokes are never captured. The free tier covers three people.

Why it is on this list

ActivTrak leads because it answers the productivity question a manager actually has, where the team's time goes, who is overloaded and who is under-used, with reports built for a one-on-one rather than a disciplinary file. Its coaching prompts and capacity views are the most developed in the category, it never logs a keystroke, and the free tier lets you see your own team's data before paying. The reports that justify the purchase sit on the $15 and $17 plans.

Where it is strong

  • Utilisation, focus and workload reports managers act on
  • No keystrokes; screenshots optional
  • Free for three people
  • Coaching prompts and capacity planning on the upper plans

Where it falls short

  • No Linux
  • The reports that justify it are on the $15 and $17 plans
  • Retention 6 to 12 months unless extended

Insightful

Capacity and location analytics with long history
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Workforce Analytics $8, Workflow Optimization $12, both $16 per seat per month, annual; Enterprise by quote; recording add-ons $4

What it is

Insightful builds its productivity picture from application and website use, active and idle time and optional screenshots, and reports it as utilisation, capacity and productivity trends by team, with work-location insight that separates office, remote and hybrid days. It keeps 24 months of history, which makes before-and-after comparisons possible, runs on Linux as well as Windows and Mac, and sells screen recording as an add-on rather than a default.

Why it is on this list

Insightful is second for teams that want ActivTrak-style analytics with better coverage of where people worked and two years of history to compare against. Its utilisation and capacity reports, Linux agent and add-on approach to recording suit hybrid knowledge teams that want measurement without a monitoring culture.

Where it is strong

  • Location-aware utilisation and capacity
  • 24 months of history
  • Linux agent
  • Recording is an add-on, not a default

Where it falls short

  • Recording and on-demand screenshots cost extra
  • Workflow Optimization still in beta
  • On-premise needs 250 seats

DeskTime

The cheapest productivity score with employee controls
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Lite free for one user; Pro $6.42 and Premium $9.17 per user per month, annual ($7 and $10 monthly); Enterprise by quote

What it is

DeskTime assigns a productivity score to each person by classifying applications and URLs as productive, neutral or unproductive for their role and totalling the active time in each. It adds project tracking, an absence calendar, shift scheduling and a private time switch, and keeps screenshots behind the Premium plan. It is one of the cheapest ways to get a daily productivity number and one of the least intrusive.

Why it is on this list

DeskTime is third because a daily productivity score, automatically computed, with a private time switch and no screenshots unless you pay for Premium, is what most small teams actually want from this category, and nothing delivers it cheaper. The scoring is simple and needs the application lists tuned per role.

Where it is strong

  • Cheapest daily productivity score here
  • Private time switch
  • Shifts and absence built in
  • Free for one user

Where it falls short

  • Simple scoring that needs tuning per role
  • Screenshots only on Premium
  • Light reporting at scale

Controlio

Recording and keystrokes at a low price
Runs on Windows, macOS Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.67 and Advanced $9.99 per user per month, annual ($7.99 and $11.99 monthly); Enterprise by quote

What it is

Controlio adds productivity scoring to a monitoring product whose main features are continuous screen recording and keystroke capture. It is inexpensive and simple, but its productivity reports are basic and its capture is heavy, which places it at the surveillance end of this list.

Why it is on this list

Controlio is on the list for buyers who want a productivity score but whose real requirement is recording and keystrokes, at a price far below Teramind. Its analytics are basic; its capture is heavy; it is the surveillance end of this page and is listed as such.

Where it is strong

  • Low price for recording and keystrokes
  • Cloud or on-premise
  • Simple to read

Where it falls short

  • Basic productivity reports
  • Heavy capture for a light question
  • No Linux

Hubstaff

Productivity tied to time and pay
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10, Enterprise $25 per seat per month, annual; 2-seat minimum

What it is

Hubstaff measures productivity as activity, the share of tracked time with keyboard and mouse input, alongside application and URL use and optional screenshots, and attaches it to time tracked against projects. Its Insights add-on turns that into utilisation, focus and meeting-time reports. It is a time tracker first, which means its productivity data is strongest for hourly and project work and weakest as a measure of knowledge-work output.

Why it is on this list

Hubstaff is here for teams whose productivity question is really a time question: hours against projects with activity and screenshots as evidence, and payroll at the end. Its activity percentage is a measure of input, which it is honest about, and the Insights add-on gives utilisation and focus views.

Where it is strong

  • Productivity attached to projects and pay
  • Every platform
  • Insights add-on for utilisation and focus
  • Cheap entry plan

Where it falls short

  • Activity measures input, not output
  • Two-seat minimum
  • Not a knowledge-work productivity measure

Monitask

Cheap and quick with adjustable screenshots
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Pro $6.49, Business $8.99, Business Premium $12.99 per user per month, annual; 1 to 2 months of history

What it is

Monitask tracks activity and screenshots against projects and classifies applications and websites on the Business plan, producing simple productivity reports for small teams and agencies. Retention is short (one to two months on the main plans), the price is low, and it is quick to deploy.

Why it is on this list

Monitask earns its place as the cheapest way for a small agency to get screenshots, activity and app classification against projects. Its one-to-two-month retention is the trade.

Where it is strong

  • Cheap and fast to deploy
  • Adjustable screenshots
  • Project and task tracking
  • Payroll exports

Where it falls short

  • One to two months of history
  • Classification only from Business
  • Small-team tool

Prodoscore

Agentless scoring with nothing installed
Runs on Agentless: Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, CRM and phone connectors; optional desktop agent Pricing (October 2026) Basic $19.99 per user per month with a 15-seat minimum ($300 a month); Premium and Enterprise by quote; trial for up to 25 users

What it is

Prodoscore takes a different route to the same question: no agent, no screenshots, nothing installed. It connects to Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, CRMs and phone systems and derives a daily productivity score from the volume and pattern of work done in those tools, with an optional desktop connector for application activity. It suits sales and service organisations that live in cloud tools and distrust agents, and its 15-seat minimum and $19.99 price put it in the mid-market.

Why it is on this list

Prodoscore is on the list because it proves a productivity score does not need an agent: it reads Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, CRM and phone activity and produces a daily number per person. For sales and service teams that live in those tools it is the least invasive option here, and its 15-seat minimum and $19.99 price make it a mid-market purchase.

Where it is strong

  • No agent and no screenshots
  • Works from Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 and CRM data
  • A single daily score leaders understand
  • Strong fit for sales and service teams

Where it falls short

  • $19.99 with a 15-seat minimum
  • Needs the team to live in cloud tools
  • A score is a blunt instrument

RescueTime

Personal focus tracking scaled to a team
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, browser extensions Pricing (October 2026) Team $10 and Team+ $16 per user per month, annual ($12 and $18 monthly); individual plans $7 and $12; 14-day trial

What it is

RescueTime is the personal productivity tracker scaled to teams: automatic categorisation of applications and sites, a focus score, focus sessions that block distractions, daily goals and weekly reports, with the team plan adding aggregate reports and the Team+ plan adding timesheets. The individual sees everything; the manager sees summaries. It is the tool for a team that wants each person to manage their own attention.

Why it is on this list

RescueTime is here for the team that believes productivity is the individual's to manage: each person sees their own categorised time, focus score and distraction patterns, runs focus sessions, and the team sees summaries. It is the only tool here designed from the employee's side first.

Where it is strong

  • The individual owns the data
  • Focus sessions and distraction blocking
  • Automatic categorisation that needs little setup
  • Team summaries without surveillance

Where it falls short

  • Individual-first; limited manager controls
  • Timesheets need Team+ at $16
  • No attendance or payroll

Teramind

The most detailed reports, at a price
Runs on Windows (full); macOS and Linux reduced Pricing (October 2026) Starter $14, UAM $28, DLP $32 per seat per month, annual; 5-seat minimum

What it is

Teramind includes productivity scoring and classification, but its design centre is security: screen video, keystrokes, OCR and behaviour rules that can block. As a productivity tool it is expensive and heavy; it belongs on this list because some buyers need productivity and insider-risk capability in one agent, and because its reports are genuinely detailed.

Why it is on this list

Teramind is on the list for the organisation that needs productivity reporting and insider-risk capability from the same agent and has the budget and the policy discipline for it. As a productivity tool alone it is overkill.

Where it is strong

  • The most detailed reports in the category
  • Productivity plus insider-risk capability
  • On-premise and private cloud
  • Behaviour rules that act

Where it falls short

  • Expensive and heavy for a productivity question
  • Five-seat minimum
  • Mac and Linux limited

Time Champ

Attendance plus a productivity score from $3.90
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux; iOS and Android for GPS Pricing (October 2026) Starter $3.90, Professional $6.90, Enterprise $13.90 per user per month, annual; recording, keylogger and DLP as add-ons

What it is

Time Champ computes a productivity score from application and website classification, active and idle time and attendance, with intensity graphs and app and URL reports on Professional and suspicious-activity detection and custom reports on Enterprise. It is the lowest-priced tool here that combines attendance and productivity in one agent, and screenshots can be blurred, reduced or run in a visible mode the employee can see.

Why it is on this list

Time Champ is here for the employer who wants attendance and a productivity score from one agent at the lowest price on the page, with screenshots that can be reduced, blurred or made visible to the employee. Its intensity graphs and app reports on Professional cover most managers' needs; history below Enterprise is short.

Where it is strong

  • Attendance and productivity from one agent at $3.90
  • Configurable, blurrable or visible screenshots
  • Intensity graphs and app reports
  • Linux and mobile GPS

Where it falls short

  • Short detailed history below Enterprise
  • Add-ons for recording and DLP
  • Smaller review base

Time Doctor

Productivity with overwork and location reports
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, Android Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.70, Standard $11.70, Premium $16.70 per user per month, annual; Enterprise by quote

What it is

Time Doctor rates productivity from web and application use classified per company, activity levels and attendance, and adds two reports the field lacks: work-life balance, which flags late nights and overlong days, and Office versus Remote, which compares the two settings. Premium adds benchmark-style AI reporting and executive dashboards. It is built for remote and outsourced teams and shows it.

Why it is on this list

Time Doctor is on the list for remote and outsourced teams, where its work-life balance and Office versus Remote reports add something nobody else has: a view of overwork alongside underwork. The plan with those features is $11.70.

Where it is strong

  • Work-life balance report
  • Office versus Remote comparison
  • Payroll and 60-plus integrations
  • Chromebook and Android agents

Where it falls short

  • Screenshots draw objections
  • Interface shows its age

WorkTime

Productivity monitoring with no screenshots at all
Runs on Windows, macOS, terminal and Citrix servers Pricing (October 2026) Free for 3 employees with two weeks of history; Basic $5.83, Premium $7.49, Enterprise $9.16 per employee per month, annual ($6.99, $8.99, $10.99 monthly)

What it is

WorkTime calls its approach socially responsible monitoring: productivity from application and website use, attendance from logins, active and idle time, an optional keystroke counter that records counts and never content, and no screenshots at any price. It runs on Windows, Mac and terminal servers, deploys to the cloud or on-premise with HIPAA-safe and GDPR-safe modes, and offers a free plan for three employees.

Why it is on this list

WorkTime closes the list as the tool for employers who want productivity data and refuse screenshots on principle or by regulation: it has none at any price, counts keystrokes without content, runs on-premise with HIPAA-safe and GDPR-safe modes, and is free for three employees.

Where it is strong

  • Keystroke counter without content
  • Cloud or on-premise with compliance modes
  • Free for three employees

Where it falls short

  • Windows and Mac only
  • No screenshots means no evidence for investigations

What these tools actually measure

A productivity score from any tool on this page is built from three inputs: which application or site was in the foreground, whether the person was active (keyboard or mouse input within a timeout) and how that application is classified for the role. Writing code in an IDE is productive for an engineer and undefined for a salesperson; a browser is anything. The score is therefore a measure of time spent in approved tools while touching the keyboard, which is a reasonable proxy for a data-entry role and a poor one for anyone whose best hour of the week was spent thinking, reading or in a meeting the agent cannot see. ActivTrak and Insightful try to correct for meetings and offline time; Prodoscore and Viva Insights avoid the agent and measure output signals in cloud tools; RescueTime hands the measure to the individual. The output metrics page explains why counting activity goes wrong for knowledge teams, and the productivity guide what to measure instead.

Using the data well

  • Compare people to themselves, not to each other. Roles differ; a ranking of scores across roles is noise with a leaderboard attached.
  • Look at the team, then the person. A team at 95 per cent utilisation has a workload problem, not a motivation problem; the burnout signals page shows what that looks like.
  • Never automate a decision from it. Pay, discipline and layoffs based on activity scores are the use the research links to harm and the regulators are moving on.
  • Give people their own data. Every tool here except Teramind, Controlio and WorkTime has an employee self-view; use it. People who can see their own pattern fix it.
  • Tell them. Notice is law in New York, Connecticut and Delaware and good practice everywhere; the policy template is the document.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Key takeaways

  • Every tool measures the same thing: active time in applications classified by role. The differences are capture, presentation, who sees it, and whether an agent is installed.
  • ActivTrak for coaching and utilisation data, Insightful for hybrid capacity analytics, DeskTime for a cheap daily score; Prodoscore and Viva are agentless; RescueTime is employee-first; WorkTime has no screenshots at any price.
  • Activity is not output; the score is a proxy that suits data-entry roles and fails knowledge work.
  • Choose your position on the analytics-to-surveillance spectrum before choosing a tool.
  • Compare people to themselves, look at teams first, never automate decisions, give people their data, and tell them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best productivity monitoring software?

ActivTrak for managers who want utilisation, focus and coaching data without surveillance; Insightful for hybrid knowledge teams that want capacity analytics with two years of history; DeskTime for the cheapest daily productivity score. Prodoscore is the agentless choice for teams in Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, RescueTime for teams that want individuals to own their data, WorkTime for employers that refuse screenshots, and Time Champ for attendance and productivity together at the lowest price.

How does productivity monitoring software measure productivity?

By classifying each application and website as productive, unproductive or neutral for the person's role and totalling the time spent actively using each, where active means keyboard or mouse input within a timeout. The result is reported as a score, a utilisation percentage or a trend. It measures time in approved tools, which is a proxy for productivity, not productivity itself.

Is there productivity monitoring software without screenshots?

Yes. WorkTime has none at any price; Prodoscore and Microsoft Viva Insights are agentless and capture nothing on the device; RescueTime and Timely capture categories privately for the individual; ActivTrak and DeskTime have screenshots off by default (DeskTime only on Premium). Time Champ can run in a visible mode with screenshots off or blurred.

Does productivity monitoring software work on Mac and Linux?

Hubstaff, DeskTime, Insightful, Monitask, Time Champ, Time Doctor and RescueTime have Linux agents. ActivTrak covers Windows, Mac and Chromebook. WorkTime and Controlio cover Windows and Mac. Teramind's Mac and Linux agents have fewer features. Prodoscore and Viva Insights are agentless and platform-independent.

How much does productivity monitoring software cost?

Entry plans in October 2026 run from $3.90 (Time Champ) and $4.99 (Hubstaff) through $5.83 to $10 (WorkTime, DeskTime, Monitask, Controlio, Time Doctor, Insightful, ActivTrak, RescueTime) to $14 (Teramind) and $19.99 (Prodoscore, 15-seat minimum), per user per month on annual billing. Viva Insights organisational is $6 on top of Microsoft 365. ActivTrak, DeskTime and WorkTime have free tiers.

Does monitoring productivity actually improve it?

The evidence is mixed. Visibility into workload and time allocation helps managers rebalance work and reduce meetings; individual activity scoring tends to increase the appearance of activity and reduce trust, and surveys show it damages engagement. Tools used for team-level analytics with transparent policies and employee self-view do better than tools used to rank individuals.

Is productivity monitoring legal?

Monitoring company devices is lawful across the United States, with written notice required in New York, Connecticut and Delaware, all-party consent for audio in about a dozen states, limits under the National Labor Relations Act on monitoring that chills discussion of working conditions, and privacy rules in Illinois and California. Automated decisions based on monitoring data are under increasing regulatory attention.
