Best Employee Time Tracking Software for Employers (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 16 min read

Employee time tracking is the employer's side of the problem: recording when hourly staff start, stop and break, applying overtime and rounding rules lawfully, getting a manager's approval, and delivering the hours to payroll with as little re-keying as possible. The tools that do this well are time clocks and attendance systems first, and project trackers second, which is the reverse of the general time tracking review. Kiosks, GPS, geofencing, overtime rules and payroll integrations matter here; billable rates and client invoices mostly do not.

I reviewed nine tools against the employer's list, with pricing checked in October 2026. The calculator below prices the relevant plan of each at your headcount, including QuickBooks Time's base fee and Hubstaff's seat minimum. The first three are the ones I would trial first; the rest fit particular workforces.

The tools in detail

QuickBooks Time

The time clock that feeds payroll directly
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Premium $20 base plus $10 per user per month; Elite $40 base plus $12 per user per month; introductory base fees of $10 and $20 for three months; sold with QuickBooks Online

What it is

The time clock for hourly teams that run QuickBooks payroll: mobile clock-in, GPS, kiosk, scheduling and approvals.

Why it is on this list

QuickBooks Time leads for employers because the employer's problem is payroll, and QuickBooks Time is the time clock whose hours arrive in payroll without a spreadsheet in between. Mobile clock-in with GPS, a kiosk for the shop floor, schedules, approvals, and on Elite geofencing and timesheet signatures that satisfy an auditor. The base fee makes it poor value under ten people and the QuickBooks dependency is real.

Where it is strong

  • The natural time clock for QuickBooks payroll
  • GPS, geofencing, kiosk and scheduling for hourly crews
  • Mileage and project budgets on Elite
  • Timesheet signatures for compliance

Where it falls short

  • Base fee plus per-user pricing is expensive for small teams
  • Tied to QuickBooks Online
  • Not a project time tracker for knowledge work

Jibble

Free for unlimited users with kiosk, GPS and overtime rules
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, kiosk, Slack and Teams Pricing (October 2026) Free with unlimited users including GPS and timesheets; Premium from about $4.49 and Ultimate from about $7.99 per user per month on annual billing, with Enterprise by quote; the vendor does not publish a fixed table, so confirm the quote

What it is

Free attendance tracking for unlimited users with GPS, a facial recognition kiosk and overtime rules; paid tiers add geofencing and payroll exports.

Why it is on this list

Jibble is second because an employer can run attendance for a whole workforce on it without paying anything: unlimited users, GPS, overtime rules, timesheets and a facial recognition kiosk. For retail, hospitality, clinics and sites it is the obvious first trial. The paid tiers add geofencing and payroll exports at prices the vendor does not publish, and the kiosk's face recognition triggers biometric notice and consent rules in Illinois and Texas.

Where it is strong

  • Genuinely free for unlimited users, including GPS and timesheets
  • Facial recognition kiosk for shared devices
  • Clock in from Slack or Teams
  • Overtime rules and approvals

Where it falls short

  • Paid pricing is not published as a fixed table
  • Reporting and project tracking are lighter than the project tools
  • Facial recognition raises Illinois and Texas biometric law obligations

Clockify

Attendance, overtime and approvals from $5.49
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 5 users; Basic $3.99, Standard $5.49, Pro $7.99, Enterprise $11.99 per seat per month billed annually ($4.99, $6.99, $9.99, $14.99 monthly)

What it is

The broadest free time tracker, five users at no cost, with the cheapest paid ladder adding time off, invoicing, approvals, scheduling and GPS.

Why it is on this list

Clockify is third for the employer that wants attendance, overtime, time off, approvals and invoicing in one place at the lowest paid price: all of that is on Standard at $5.49, with scheduling, GPS and a kiosk arriving on Pro. It is the budget alternative to a workforce management suite for a team of ten to a hundred.

Where it is strong

  • Free for five users with real features
  • The cheapest paid ladder here, from $3.99
  • Kiosk, time off, invoicing, approvals, scheduling and GPS as you climb
  • Part of a bundle with chat and project tools

Where it falls short

  • Approvals and time off start at Standard; GPS and scheduling at Pro
  • Interface is dense once every module is on
  • Support is slower on the lower tiers

What an employer's time tracking has to do

  • Record start, stop and breaks accurately. The FLSA requires daily and weekly hours for non-exempt employees; several states require meal and rest break records. A kiosk or mobile clock-in with a timestamp beats a timesheet filled in on Friday.
  • Apply overtime by workweek. Over 40 hours in the workweek federally; over 8 in a day in California and a few other states. The tool should compute it, and the overtime calculator is the check.
  • Round lawfully or not at all. Quarter-hour rounding is permitted only where neutral over time; most tools now default to exact minutes, which is safer.
  • Approve. A manager sign-off with an audit trail, so edits are visible.
  • Deliver to payroll without re-keying: a native link (QuickBooks Time), a payroll module (Hubstaff, Time Doctor) or an export in the payroll provider's format.
  • Know where, when it matters. GPS and geofencing for field crews; a kiosk for shared sites; nothing for office staff who do not need it.
  • Keep the records. Three years for payroll records under the FLSA, two for the time cards they were computed from.

The attendance tracking and attendance policy pages cover the policy that sits around the tool.

Kiosks, GPS and biometrics

A kiosk is a shared tablet or PC where staff clock in with a PIN, a photo or a face scan; Jibble, Clockify, TimeCamp and QuickBooks Time offer one. GPS clock-in records where the employee was when they started; geofencing (QuickBooks Time Elite, Jibble paid tiers, TimeCamp Enterprise) only allows clock-in inside a defined area. Both are proportionate for field and site work and intrusive for office staff. Facial recognition kiosks are biometric systems: Illinois's BIPA requires written notice, consent and a retention policy before capture, Texas and Washington have their own rules, and several cities add more. The biometric time clock entry and the GPS tracking guide cover the obligations.

If you only need timesheets

Some employers need none of this: a salaried team recording hours for project costing, or a small business whose payroll provider already has a time clock. For the first, Toggl Track or a timesheet template is enough; for the second, the payroll provider's clock is usually cheaper than a separate tool. The small business timesheet software page covers the minimal options and the timesheet reminder guide covers getting them in on time.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Key takeaways

  • Employer time tracking is a time clock problem: accurate starts, stops and breaks, overtime by workweek, approvals, and delivery to payroll.
  • QuickBooks Time if payroll is in QuickBooks, Jibble for free attendance with a kiosk, Clockify for the cheapest full attendance ladder; Time Champ for attendance plus productivity at $3.90.
  • GPS and geofencing suit field work, kiosks suit shared sites, and facial recognition triggers biometric law.
  • Keep payroll records three years and time cards two.
  • If you only need timesheets, a template or the payroll provider's clock may be enough.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best employee time tracking software?

QuickBooks Time for hourly crews whose payroll runs in QuickBooks, Jibble for free attendance with a kiosk and GPS for unlimited users, and Clockify for attendance, overtime, approvals and time off at the lowest paid price. Hubstaff and Time Doctor suit remote hourly staff, Time Champ suits employers who want attendance and productivity from one agent, and TimeCamp is the budget alternative.

What is the difference between employee time tracking and a time clock?

Little. A time clock records clock-in and clock-out, usually at a kiosk or from a mobile app; employee time tracking software is a time clock plus schedules, overtime rules, approvals, time off and a payroll export. Project time trackers such as Toggl or Harvest record hours against work rather than shifts and are not time clocks.

Is there free employee time tracking software?

Jibble is free for unlimited users with timesheets, GPS, overtime rules and a kiosk. TimeCamp is free for unlimited users for basic timesheets. Clockify is free for five users. The free tiers generally lack approvals and payroll exports, which are the features employers end up paying for.

Can employers use GPS to track employee time?

Yes, for work time on company devices or with consent on personal ones, and it is proportionate for field and site work. Several states require notice; tracking outside working hours or on personal devices without consent is unlawful in many. The GPS tracking guide on this site covers the rules.

Are facial recognition time clocks legal?

Generally yes, with obligations. Illinois's Biometric Information Privacy Act requires written notice, consent and a published retention policy before capturing a face scan, and has produced large settlements; Texas and Washington have biometric laws; several cities add rules. A PIN or photo kiosk avoids the issue.

How long must employers keep time records?

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act, payroll records for three years and the time cards and schedules they were computed from for two years. Several states require longer. Most cloud tools retain data for the life of the account; check what happens on cancellation.

Can employees edit their own time entries?

Yes if the tool keeps an audit trail and a manager approves the result. Edits without a trail are the common cause of disputes. Kiosk and GPS clock-ins reduce the need for edits in the first place.
