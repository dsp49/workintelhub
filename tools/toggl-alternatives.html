Toggl Track Alternatives (2026): 11 Tools by Price and Invoicing

P Pavan
October 21, 2026 · 15 min read

A Toggl Track alternative is a time tracker that does what Toggl does, record hours against projects and clients without monitoring anyone, and adds the thing Toggl will not. People leave Toggl for four reasons. Price: approvals, labor cost and profitability sit on Premium at $16 per user, and Clockify sells them for $5.49. Invoicing: Toggl reports billable amounts and stops there, and Harvest sends the bill. The free plan: since the Toggl 2.0 relaunch on September 15, 2026, new accounts get 3 free seats with capped report views, and TimeCamp has no cap. Attendance: Toggl has no time clock, GPS or payroll, and Clockify, Jibble and QuickBooks Time do.

The pages that rank for this search are written by Timely, ClickUp and Productive, each ranking itself first, none dating its prices and none explaining that Toggl's free plan now differs between old and new accounts. This guide gives the reasons, October 2026 pricing for eleven tools with free-plan limits, and a step-by-step export because Toggl, to its credit, makes leaving easy. No tool here monitors employees on its standard plans except Hubstaff, which is included for the teams that now need proof of work.

The short answer

If the price is the problem, Clockify does what Toggl Starter does for $3.99 and adds approvals and invoicing at $5.49, against $16 on Toggl Premium. TimeCamp is free for unlimited users and $3.99 for invoicing.

If invoicing is the problem, Harvest turns time into invoices and payments on every plan, including the free one; Paymo adds full project management.

If the free plan shrank on you, new Toggl accounts get 3 seats and limited report views since the September 15, 2026 relaunch. TimeCamp has no user cap on free, Clockify allows 5, Everhour and My Hours 5.

What Toggl Track costs and does in October 2026

Toggl Track

The incumbent
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, browser extensions Pricing (October 2026) Free for 3 users on new accounts (older workspaces keep 5); Starter $9 and Premium $16 per user per month billed annually ($12 and $24 monthly); Enterprise by quote; add-ons Time Off Pro $2 and Capacity Management $5 to $6; 30-day trial on paid plans Monitoring No monitoring

What it is

Toggl Track is a timer with projects, clients and reports, plus a private desktop timeline, calendar integration and a Pomodoro mode. Starter adds billable rates, team reports and project estimates; Premium adds timesheet approvals, labor cost and profitability, fixed-fee projects, Jira and Asana integrations and SSO. Toggl's FAQ states that it does not monitor employees, take screenshots or record keystrokes, browsing or location. On September 15, 2026 Toggl relaunched as Toggl 2.0 with an AI assistant, an MCP connector and new plans; existing Track workspaces are grandfathered, which is why free seat counts and Premium prices differ between accounts.

Better than Toggl Track

  • The tracker people keep using: one click, private timeline, nothing to resent
  • A written policy against monitoring
  • Imports and exports CSV cleanly, so leaving is easy

Where Toggl Track is still better

  • No invoicing, payroll, attendance, GPS or kiosk
  • Approvals, labor cost and profitability sit at $16 per user
  • Free plan now 3 seats with capped report views on new accounts

Who should switch to it

Individuals and teams that want time tracking without friction and bill or budget elsewhere. Our time tracking software review still places it first for adoption.

Why people leave Toggl Track

The price for what it does

Starter at $9 tracks time; approvals, costs and profitability require Premium at $16. For a 20-person team that is $3,840 a year for features Clockify sells for $1,318 on Standard.

Fixed by: Clockify ($5.49 with approvals and invoicing), TimeCamp ($3.99 to $9.99), My Hours ($8 with approvals).

No invoicing

Toggl reports billable amounts but does not issue invoices or take payments; agencies re-key hours into an accounting tool.

Fixed by: Harvest (invoicing on every plan, Stripe, QuickBooks and Xero), Paymo, Everhour, Clockify Standard.

The free plan changed under you

New accounts after the Toggl 2.0 relaunch get 3 free seats and limited report views; a community thread shows users with 3 and 5 seats side by side, and Toggl's own answer was that older plans are grandfathered.

Fixed by: TimeCamp (free, unlimited users), Clockify (5), Everhour and My Hours (5), Kimai (self-hosted, no cap).

No attendance, scheduling or payroll

Hourly teams discover that Toggl has no time clock, overtime rules, schedules, GPS or payroll export, because it was never built for them.

Fixed by: Clockify (attendance on Standard, GPS and scheduling on Pro), Jibble (free time clock), QuickBooks Time (payroll).

Timers that keep running and sync gaps

Reviews describe timers that will not stop and mobile and desktop entries that fail to sync, which produce inflated or duplicated entries the user must clean up.

even after I hit stop, the timer will not stop runningCapterra review, as surfaced in search results; wording and date not independently confirmed

Fixed by: automatic capture that drafts the timesheet from activity: Timely, TimeCamp's AI tracker.

Budgets live in the wrong place

Toggl's project estimates sit in Toggl's dashboards, which the people doing the work rarely open. Overruns surface at month end.

Fixed by: Everhour, which puts the estimate on the task inside Asana, Jira or ClickUp, and ClickUp's native tracking.

The alternatives in detail

Clockify

The same tracker for a third of the price
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for 5 users with 31-day reports and PDF export only; Basic $3.99, Standard $5.49, Pro $7.99, Enterprise $11.99 per seat per month billed annually ($4.99, $6.99, $9.99, $14.99 monthly); 7-day Pro trial

What it is

Clockify is the direct substitute: timer, timesheet, projects, clients, billable rates on Basic, approvals, time off, invoicing and attendance on Standard, budgets, scheduling and GPS on Pro. It has an official Toggl importer that takes the Detailed Report CSV. The free plan was reduced on April 21, 2026 to five users, one-month reports and PDF-only export, so compare paid tiers rather than free plans.

Better than Toggl Track

  • Approvals and invoicing at $5.49 against Toggl's $16
  • Attendance, overtime, kiosk and GPS for hourly staff
  • Imports Toggl's CSV directly

Where Toggl Track is still better

  • Denser interface with modules spread across four tiers
  • Free plan now capped harder than Toggl's grandfathered one
  • Activity monitoring exists on Pro, which Toggl refuses on principle

Who should switch to it

Teams that like Toggl and dislike the invoice.

Switching

Export Toggl's Detailed Report as CSV in one-year chunks (the report cap), invite users to Clockify so emails match, then Workspace settings, Import. Time entry import needs a paid plan or the trial.

Harvest

Time to invoice to payment
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, browser extensions Pricing (October 2026) Free for one seat and two projects, including invoicing; Teams $9 and Enterprise $14 per seat per month billed annually ($11 and $17.50 monthly); Enterprise Plus by quote with assisted migration; 30-day trial

What it is

Harvest tracks time and expenses against projects and turns them into invoices with online payment through Stripe, syncing to QuickBooks Online and Xero. Billable and cost rates are on Teams; approvals, profitability and SSO on Enterprise. It is the tool for a business whose time tracking exists to produce a bill.

Better than Toggl Track

  • Invoicing and payments on every plan, free included
  • Accounting integrations that close the loop
  • Project and people import from CSV

Where Toggl Track is still better

  • Approvals only at $14
  • No attendance, GPS, kiosk or payroll
  • Single-seat free plan

Who should switch to it

Agencies, consultancies and freelancers who re-key Toggl hours into invoices today.

Switching

Harvest imports projects and people from CSV; time entries import through its CSV template or, on Enterprise Plus, assisted migration.

Hubstaff

When you now need proof of work
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10 per seat per month billed annually ($7, $9, $12 monthly); 2-seat minimum; 14-day trial; 30-day money-back

What it is

Some teams leave Toggl in the other direction, because a client or payroll now requires evidence. Hubstaff tracks time with optional screenshots, activity levels, GPS job sites and payroll.   • GPS and job sites for field staff
  • Cheaper entry than Toggl Starter

Where Toggl Track is still better

  • Screenshots and activity scores change how a team feels about tracking
  • 2-seat minimum
  • Needs a monitoring notice in several states

Who should switch to it

Teams whose clients or payroll now require evidence of hours.

Import members and projects from CSV; issue the monitoring notice before installing the agent.

Kimai

Open source, self-hosted
Runs on Any browser; Docker or manual install; mobile apps Pricing (October 2026) Free under the AGPL 3 license, self-hosted with community support; a hosted cloud version is sold on kimai.org with pricing on that site; expenses is a paid plugin

What it is

Kimai is an open-source time tracker with customers, projects, budgets, roles and teams, reports, invoice templates in PDF and DOCX, exports, LDAP and SAML, two-factor authentication and a JSON API, translated into 30 or more languages. You own the data and there is no seat count.

Better than Toggl Track

  • Free with no user limit, on your own server
  • Invoicing templates and a full API
  • No vendor can change the free plan

Where Toggl Track is still better

  • You run the server, the backups and the upgrades
  • No automatic tracking or monitoring
  • Community support unless you pay for cloud

Who should switch to it

Technical and privacy-conscious teams that want to stop renting a timer.

Switching

Kimai imports CSV time entries through its importer plugin; test on a copy first.

How to move your Toggl data

  1. Export the Detailed Report as CSV, one year at a time, because reports are capped at a year. On free plans the detailed CSV export may require a trial of a paid plan; check before the free period ends.
  2. Export workspace data (projects, clients, tags) from Settings, Data Export, which produces JSON and PDF invoices.
  3. Invite users to the new tool first so email addresses match on import. Clockify's importer requires this.
  4. Import on a paid plan or trial. Clockify, Harvest and TimeCamp import time entries only on paid tiers or during a trial.
  5. Reconcile one month of totals by project between Toggl and the new tool before canceling. Toggl bills month to month, so there is no annual refund to chase if you were on monthly.

How these tools were chosen

Each tool was checked on October 21, 2026 against the same list: what it tracks, whether it monitors, platforms, published pricing with free-plan limits and minimums, invoicing and approvals, and whether Toggl's CSV can be imported. Toggl's own pricing page and community forum were read for the relaunch and free-plan changes. Complaints come from public review platforms; one quote is reproduced as surfaced in search results because the review page itself could not be read, and is labeled as such. No vendor paid for placement and there are no affiliate links.

Key takeaways

  • Toggl Track in October 2026: free for 3 users on new accounts (5 on grandfathered workspaces), Starter $9, Premium $16 per user annually; no invoicing, attendance or payroll; no monitoring by policy.
  • Cheaper for the same features: Clockify Standard $5.49 with approvals and invoicing, TimeCamp Starter $3.99 with invoicing, My Hours Pro $8 with approvals.
  • Invoicing: Harvest on every plan with Stripe, QuickBooks and Xero; Paymo with full project management; Clockify Standard; Everhour Team.
  • Free without a tight cap: TimeCamp (unlimited users), Jibble (unlimited, time clock), Kimai (self-hosted), Clockify, Everhour and My Hours (5 users).
  • Automatic capture for timer-forgetters: Timely (private by design) and TimeCamp's AI tracker.
  • Export the Detailed Report CSV a year at a time, invite users to the new tool first, and import on a paid plan or trial. Clockify has an official Toggl importer.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Toggl Track?

Clockify for most teams: the same tracker with approvals and invoicing at $5.49 and an official Toggl importer. Harvest if time tracking exists to produce invoices. TimeCamp if the free plan matters and you have more than five people. Everhour if budgets should live inside Asana, Jira or ClickUp. Timely if nobody remembers to start timers.

What is the Toggl Track free plan limit now?

3 users on accounts created since the Toggl 2.0 relaunch on September 15, 2026, with limited report views. Older Track workspaces keep 5 users; Toggl's community answers describe those plans as grandfathered. Check your own workspace settings rather than a third-party page.

Does Toggl take screenshots or monitor employees?

No. Toggl's pricing FAQ states that it does not monitor employees, take screenshots or record keystrokes, browsing history or location. Of the alternatives here, only Hubstaff monitors on its standard plans; Clockify and TimeCamp offer optional monitoring on their top tiers.

Does Toggl Track have invoicing?

Toggl reports billable amounts by client and project but does not generate invoices or take payments. Harvest, Paymo, Clockify (Standard plan), Everhour (Team plan), TimeCamp (Starter plan) and Kimai all issue invoices from tracked time.

What is the cheapest paid alternative to Toggl?

Clockify Basic and TimeCamp Starter at $3.99 per user per month on annual billing, and My Hours Basic at $4. The cheapest plan with timesheet approvals is Clockify Standard at $5.49. Toggl's approvals are on Premium at $16.

How do I move from Toggl to Clockify?

Export Toggl's Detailed Report as CSV, invite your team to Clockify so email addresses match, then go to Workspace settings, Import, and upload the CSV. Importing time entries requires a paid Clockify plan or its trial. Clockify publishes a guide for migrating from Toggl, Harvest and ClickUp.

Is there a free alternative with unlimited users?

TimeCamp's free plan has no user cap and includes timers, timesheets, a calendar and automatic tracking. Jibble is free for unlimited users as a time clock. Kimai is free and self-hosted with no limit. Clockify's free plan is capped at 5 users since April 2026.

Which alternative is best for an agency that bills clients?

Harvest, because invoicing and online payment are on every plan and it syncs to QuickBooks Online and Xero. Paymo if the agency also wants project management, Gantt charts and resource scheduling in the same tool. Everhour if the agency runs projects in Asana or ClickUp.
