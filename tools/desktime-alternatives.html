DeskTime Alternatives (2026): 11 Tools by What You Want Tracked

P Pavan
October 28, 2026 · 15 min read

DeskTime is automatic: it starts with the computer, classifies every application and website as productive or not, and reports a productivity percentage, arrival time and idle time with nothing for the employee to press. People leave it for three different reasons that point to three different kinds of tool. Some want the automation with screenshots, which DeskTime sells only on its $9.17 Premium plan. Some want the automation without the productivity score. And some have decided that automatic recording itself is the problem and want a timer the employee controls.

This guide starts from those reasons with dated quotes, gives October 2026 pricing for every tool with minimums and billing cycles, and marks what each one records. ActivTrak and WorkTime keep the automation and drop the images; Hubstaff, Time Champ and Insightful add screenshots for less than Premium; Toggl Track and Clockify hand control back to the employee; RescueTime and Timely record automatically but keep the record private to the person it concerns.

The short answer

If you want the same automatic tracking with no images at all, ActivTrak (free for three, $10 after) and WorkTime ($5.83) classify application and website use and never take screenshots.

If you need screenshots without paying for DeskTime Premium, Hubstaff Starter at $4.99 and Time Champ Starter at $3.90 include them, configurable per project or team, and Insightful at $8 adds 24 months of history.

If the automatic tracking itself is what the team objects to, Toggl Track and Clockify are timers the employee starts, with no capture of apps or sites unless they choose it.

What DeskTime costs and does in October 2026

DeskTime

The incumbent
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Pro $6.42 and Premium $9.17 per user per month billed annually ($7 and $10 monthly); Enterprise by quote for 200 or more users; 14-day trial without a card; the pricing page's seat slider starts at 10 and showed only Indian rupee prices to automated requests on October 28, 2026; a free Lite plan for one user has been offered but did not appear on the pricing page that day Monitoring Optional, Premium

What it is

DeskTime starts tracking when the computer does, classifies every application and website as productive, unproductive or neutral per role, and reports productivity percentages, idle time, arrival and departure, with shift scheduling, absence calendars and project time. Screenshots are on Premium and Enterprise only, taken at a random moment inside a window of 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, with optional blur; owners can turn them off account-wide or per user. A Private time button, on by default and employer-disableable, stops all collection while the employee runs personal errands. A June 2026 release added IP-based location.

Better than DeskTime

  • Automatic start and classification with almost nothing for the employee to do
  • Private time built in and on by default
  • Screenshots are opt-in at the plan level, not universal

Where DeskTime is still better

  • Screenshots require Premium at $9.17, and the standard window is 5 to 30 minutes rather than configurable to the second
  • Trustpilot reviews cite renewals charged without consent and refused refunds, from a small sample
  • No GPS, payroll or invoicing; prices shown in local currency confuse comparison

Who should switch to it

Office and hybrid teams that want attendance and productivity classification to happen automatically and are comfortable with a per-role productivity score. Our employee monitoring software review places it among its peers.

The US dollar prices above were read from desktime.com in October 2026 for our monitoring software review; on October 28, 2026 the page served only rupee figures to automated requests (Pro ₹366.67, Premium ₹513.33 per user per month annually), which correspond to the same tiers. Check the live page in your currency before you budget.

Why people leave DeskTime

DeskTime's public reviews are mostly positive (Trustpilot 4.5 of 5 across 44 reviews), and the negative ones are few and specific. The reasons below are the ones with evidence or with a clear product basis, each with a fix.

Screenshots are a Premium feature

A team that needs occasional proof of work pays $9.17 a user for Premium, 43% more than Pro, to get screenshots it may use rarely.

Fixed by: screenshots on the entry plan, configurable: Hubstaff Starter $4.99, Time Champ Starter $3.90, Insightful $8 with on-demand screenshots as a $4 add-on.

Renewal charged without consent

The most recent negative review describes an annual renewal charged without agreement and a refund refused.

DeskTime renewed our annual subscription without consent and charged Rs 1,23,199Trustpilot, one star, September 19, 2025

Fixed by: month-to-month billing or a published refund window: Hubstaff (30-day money-back), Clockify and Toggl Track (monthly, cancel to free).

Slow support

Two reviewers describe multi-hour response times and a five-day wait for a support call; DeskTime is a Latvian company and US-hours support is thinner.

support takes 5.5 hours to respond and booking a support call is 5 days waitTrustpilot, one star, November 29, 2023

Fixed by: vendors with US support hours and chat: ActivTrak, Hubstaff, Insightful.

The productivity percentage

DeskTime's headline metric is the share of tracked time in applications classified as productive. Classification is per role and editable, but the number is published to managers as if it measured output, and teams whose work happens in meetings, on paper or on the phone score badly.

Fixed by: tools that report activity without a single score, or that let you hide it: ActivTrak (focus and workload rather than a percentage), WorkTime, Toggl Track and Clockify (no scoring).

Automatic start is the point, and the problem

The agent starts with the computer and records until shutdown unless Private time is pressed. Employees who forget it at lunch generate records they did not intend; employers who disable Private time remove the safety valve.

Fixed by: timers the employee starts: Toggl Track, Clockify; or automatic tools that keep the timeline private to the user: RescueTime, Timely.

No payroll, invoicing or GPS

DeskTime stops at the report. Teams that need to pay contractors from tracked hours, invoice clients or track a field crew add a second tool.

Fixed by: Hubstaff (payroll, invoicing, GPS job sites), Clockify (invoicing, GPS on Pro), Time Doctor (payroll on Standard).

The alternatives in detail

ActivTrak

Analytics without images
Runs on Windows, macOS, Chromebook Pricing (October 2026) Free for 3 users with 30 days of history (the account goes inactive after 30 days without a login); Work Activity Tracking $10, Workforce Management $15, Productivity Optimization $17, Workflow $21 per user per month on annual contracts; AI Insights add-on $4; a paid support plan is required above 50 licenses

What it is

ActivTrak is the closest match to DeskTime's model: automatic classification of application and website use, utilization, focus time, schedule adherence and workload signals, with no screenshots on its standard plans and no keystroke logging. It is an analytics product rather than a time tracker, so there is no project billing, GPS or payroll.

Better than DeskTime

  • No screenshots on the plans most buyers choose, and the vendor states it does not log keystrokes
  • Workload balance and burnout signals DeskTime does not attempt
  • Free for three people

Where DeskTime is still better

  • Annual contracts only
  • Entry plan costs more than DeskTime Premium
  • No Linux agent, no shift scheduling, no project time

Who should switch to it

Office and hybrid teams that used DeskTime for visibility and want the analytics without any images.

Switching

No DeskTime importer; ActivTrak starts collecting on install and reports from the first week.

Hubstaff

Screenshots on every plan, optional and blurrable
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10, Enterprise $25 per seat per month billed annually ($7, $9, $12 monthly); 2-seat minimum; 14-day trial; 30-day money-back guarantee

What it is

Hubstaff is a timer rather than an automatic recorder: the employee starts it, and while it runs it records activity levels, apps and URLs, and optional screenshots at one to three per ten minutes, blurrable and switchable per project. Payroll, invoicing, GPS job sites and scheduling sit on the higher plans.

Better than DeskTime

  • Screenshots from $4.99 rather than $9.17
  • Payroll, invoicing and GPS that DeskTime lacks
  • Thirty-day money-back guarantee and in-app cancellation

Where DeskTime is still better

  • Nothing is recorded until the employee starts the timer, so attendance is not automatic
  • The activity level is a keyboard and mouse ratio
  • Two-seat minimum

Who should switch to it

Remote teams paid by the hour that want proof of work and payroll from the same tool and are happy to press start.

Switching

Hubstaff imports members and projects from CSV; DeskTime time exports are not imported. Our Hubstaff alternatives guide lists its own trade-offs.

Insightful

Analytics with two years of history
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Workforce Analytics $8, Workflow Optimization $12 (beta), Combo $16 per seat per month billed annually; add-ons per seat: on-demand screenshots $4, higher-frequency screenshots $3, screen recording $4; 7-day trial; monthly billing on select plans only; on-premises from 250 seats

What it is

Insightful records app and website use, activity and optional screenshots with per-team controls, in automatic or manual mode, with time and attendance, projects and payroll on Combo, and 24 months of history. The Workpuls name redirects here.

Better than DeskTime

  • Automatic or manual tracking per team
  • Twenty-four months of data
  • Screenshots are add-ons you choose, not a plan jump

Where DeskTime is still better

  • No mobile app or GPS
  • Add-ons raise the $8 entry quickly
  • Stealth mode and seat counting draw trust complaints

Who should switch to it

Office teams of 20 or more that want DeskTime's automation with longer history and finer control over who is screenshotted.

Switching

API and CSV reports; no DeskTime importer. Overlap one period and reconcile.

How to leave DeskTime cleanly

  1. Check the renewal date and turn off auto-renew in the billing settings; the public complaints are about renewals, not cancellations.
  2. Export reports for the periods you may need: productivity, attendance and project reports export to CSV and Excel from the web app.
  3. Uninstall the agent from every machine, including machines of people who have left. An agent still reporting after the account lapses is a data problem.
  4. Issue the monitoring notice for the new tool. If the replacement records anything, employees in Connecticut, Delaware and New York must receive written notice first; our monitoring policy template has the wording.
  5. Overlap one pay period and compare attendance totals before payroll relies on the new tool.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to DeskTime?

ActivTrak if you want DeskTime's automatic app and website analytics without screenshots and with workload signals; it is free for three users and $10 after on annual contracts. Hubstaff if you want screenshots on the entry plan with payroll and invoicing. Toggl Track or Clockify if the team objects to automatic recording and you want a timer they control.

Is there a free alternative to DeskTime?

ActivTrak is free for 3 users with 30 days of history, WorkTime for 3 with two weeks, Clockify for 5 users, Toggl Track for 3, and RescueTime offers a free Lite plan. DeskTime has offered a free Lite plan for one user, though it did not appear on the pricing page on October 28, 2026.

How much does DeskTime cost?

Pro is $6.42 and Premium $9.17 per user per month billed annually ($7 and $10 monthly), with Enterprise quoted for 200 or more users, based on US dollar prices read in October 2026. The pricing page shows local currency, so the figure you see may differ; screenshots require Premium.

Does DeskTime take screenshots?

Only on the Premium and Enterprise plans. It captures one screenshot at a random moment within a window the administrator sets at 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, with optional blurring. Owners can turn screenshots off for the whole account or for individual users. The Pro plan records application and website use, idle time and attendance without images.

Which DeskTime alternatives do not take screenshots?

ActivTrak (on its standard plans), WorkTime (never), RescueTime, Timely, Clockify and Toggl Track. Hubstaff, Insightful, Monitask and Time Champ take them but let you turn them off or configure them per project or team. Time Doctor takes them on every plan.

What is the cheapest DeskTime alternative with screenshots?

Time Champ Starter at $3.90 and Hubstaff Starter at $4.99 per user per month on annual billing, followed by Monitask Pro at $6.49. DeskTime's screenshots require Premium at $9.17.

Can employees see what DeskTime tracks?

Yes. DeskTime shows each employee their own productivity data, and the Private time button, on by default, lets them pause all tracking; employers can disable the button. Employees see the duration of private time but managers do not see what happened during it.

How do I cancel DeskTime?

Turn off auto-renewal in the billing settings before the renewal date, export the reports you need, and uninstall the desktop agent from every computer. The most recent negative public review concerns an annual renewal charged without consent, so confirm the renewal date in writing.
