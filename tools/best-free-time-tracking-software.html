Best Free Time Tracking Software: 12 Plans Compared (2026)

P Pavan
November 9, 2026 · 16 min read

Free time tracking changed in 2026. Clockify cut its free plan to five users and moved exports and the kiosk to a paid tier in April; Toggl cut new free accounts to three users in September; DeskTime removed its free plan; Hubstaff took its off the pricing page. Three tools went the other way: Jibble and TimeCamp still have no user cap, and TrackingTime removed its cap. Most lists of free time trackers online describe plans that no longer exist, which is why this one was rebuilt from each vendor's pricing and help pages on November 9, 2026.

This guide compares twelve free plans on what they actually include: user and project caps, exports, billable rates and invoicing, approvals, kiosk and GPS, platforms including Linux, API access and retention, plus the price of the first paid tier so you know what upgrading costs. A chooser scores them against your situation, each has a detailed card, and the closing sections cover where free plans fail and the numbers on manual timesheets. The free plans that shrank or vanished in 2025 and 2026

Free tiers in this category have been cut back hard, and most lists online describe plans that no longer exist. The changes that matter:

  • Clockify capped Free at 5 users in April 2026 and moved the kiosk, billable rates, CSV and Excel export and shared reports to the $3.99 Basic plan; reports on Free cover 31 days at a time.
  • Toggl Track cut new free organizations from 5 users to 3 with its September 2026 relaunch and added a cap of 30 report views a month; older workspaces keep 5.
  • DeskTime Lite, the free single-user plan, was removed between 2025 and early 2026; DeskTime now starts at about $6.42 a user.
  • Hubstaff no longer shows a free plan on its pricing page; a one-user free tier survives in its help center with 100 screenshots a month and limited reports.
  • ActivTrak stopped treating Free as a product in 2024; it remains as a three-worker tier with 30 days of data.
  • Vacation Tracker launched and then retired a free plan within a year, and Everhour and TrackingTime went the other way: Everhour is free for 5 and TrackingTime removed its 3-user cap entirely.

The pattern is that free plans for individuals and tiny teams survive while free plans that a 20-person company could run on have gone paid. The exceptions, Jibble, TimeCamp and TrackingTime, are the reason this list exists.

The free plans in detail

Jibble

Best free plan for a whole team
Runs on Web, Windows, Mac, iOS, Android, Chrome extension, tablet kiosk Pricing (November 2026) Free forever for unlimited users with no card. Paid tiers are no longer listed; the pricing page offers a demo for Enterprise. Older documents priced Premium at about $4.49 and Ultimate at $7.99 per user on annual billing

What it is

Jibble is the most generous free plan in time tracking and the one built for attendance rather than projects. The free tier includes clock-ins on web, mobile and a tablet kiosk with QR, NFC and facial recognition, GPS and geofencing, projects and activities, time off, basic timesheets and reports, and Slack, Teams and QuickBooks integrations. Jibble's own materials describe all of this as included for unlimited users and do not publish exclusions; earlier versions limited free accounts to two geofences and one work schedule. It appears in our clock in clock out apps guide for the same reasons.

What the free plan gives you

  • Unlimited users at no cost
  • Kiosk with face recognition, GPS and geofencing on the free plan
  • Time off and basic timesheets included
  • Slack, Teams and QuickBooks

What it holds back

  • Paid tier prices no longer published; limits of the free plan not spelled out
  • Attendance-first; project reporting is thinner than TimeCamp or Clockify
  • No Linux app
  • Facial recognition triggers biometric notice laws in Illinois, Texas and Washington

Who it suits

Hourly teams of any size that want a free clock with a kiosk, and small firms that want timesheets without a bill.

TimeCamp

Best free plan for project time
Runs on Web, Windows, Mac, Linux, iOS, Android, Chrome and Edge extensions Pricing (November 2026) Free for unlimited users and projects with no card; Starter $3.99 per user per month on annual billing ($5.49 monthly), Premium $6.99 ($9.99), Ultimate $9.99 ($13.99)

What it is

TimeCamp's free plan has no user or project cap and includes timers and manual entry, automatic tracking with idle detection, a calendar view, GPS location, keyword-based project assignment, ten report types with PDF export, CSV import, two-factor authentication, an API and a browser plugin that adds timers to 80 or more web apps. What it holds back is the money layer: billable rates, invoicing, budgets and Excel or Google Sheets export are paid, as are approvals, attendance, time off, screenshots and activity levels. The Starter tier at $3.99 is among the cheapest paid plans in the category.

What the free plan gives you

  • No user or project cap
  • Automatic tracking and idle detection free
  • Ten reports with PDF export and an API
  • Linux desktop app

What it holds back

  • Excel and Sheets export are paid
  • No billable rates or invoicing on free
  • Approvals, attendance and time off are paid
  • Kiosk only on Enterprise

Who it suits

Agencies, developers and consultancies that want project time for a whole team free and can live with PDF reports.

Toggl Track

Best free timer for up to three
Runs on Web, Windows, Mac, iOS, Android, Chrome, Edge and Firefox extensions Pricing (November 2026) Free for up to 3 members on new accounts (older workspaces keep 5), with a 30-day Premium trial; Starter $9 per user on annual billing ($12 monthly), Premium $16 ($24). Full tiers on our Toggl pricing page

What it is

Toggl's free plan is the cleanest timer in the category: one-click start, projects, tags and tasks, boards, focus mode and Pomodoro, Google and Outlook calendar integration and a private desktop timeline that reconstructs the day. New free organizations are limited to three members and 30 report views a month, and the plan has no billable rates, team reports, exports beyond a PDF summary, or integrations. Toggl has a written policy against monitoring features.

What the free plan gives you

  • Simplest timer and best adoption
  • Calendar integration and private timeline free
  • No monitoring by policy
  • 30-day full trial

What it holds back

  • 3 users on new accounts
  • 30 report views a month
  • No exports, billable rates or team reports
  • First paid tier is $9

Who it suits

Freelancers and teams of two or three who want a timer people will keep using.

Clockify

Free for five, with Linux
Runs on Web, Windows, Mac, Linux, iOS, Android, Chrome, Firefox and Edge extensions Pricing (November 2026) Free for up to 5 users; Basic $3.99 per seat on annual billing ($4.99 monthly), Standard $5.49, Pro $7.99, Enterprise $11.99; kiosk seats from $0.79. Full tiers on our Clockify pricing page

What it is

Clockify's free plan is still a working tracker for five people: unlimited entries, projects and clients, the timer and timesheet views, an auto tracker, calendar, Pomodoro, idle detection, every app including Linux and most integrations. Since April 2026 it lacks the kiosk, billable rates, CSV and Excel export, shared reports, approvals, time off and invoicing, reports are limited to a 31-day range with PDF export, and the API allows 30 requests an hour. Basic at $3.99 restores the kiosk and exports.

What the free plan gives you

  • Five users with unlimited projects
  • Linux, Windows and Mac desktop apps
  • Auto tracker and idle detection
  • Cheapest upgrade path at $3.99

What it holds back

  • Reports limited to 31 days, PDF only
  • Kiosk, billable rates and CSV export now paid
  • API nearly unusable on free
  • Five-user hard cap

Who it suits

Teams of up to five who want projects and desktop apps and do not need exports.

Harvest

Free invoicing for one person
Runs on Web, Mac, Windows, iOS, Android Pricing (November 2026) Free forever for 1 seat and 2 projects; Teams $9 per seat on annual billing ($11 monthly), Enterprise $14 ($17.50); 30-day trial

What it is

Harvest's free plan is for a single freelancer with up to two active projects, and it is the only free plan on this list that includes invoicing and expenses, plus basic reports, four custom reports and the integration library. Team reporting, rates, approvals, profitability and more seats need Teams at $9. Data carries over on upgrade.

What the free plan gives you

  • Invoicing and expenses on the free plan
  • Full integration library
  • Clean desktop and mobile apps
  • Data carries over on upgrade

What it holds back

  • One seat and two projects
  • No team features at all
  • Second seat costs $9 (or $11 monthly)
  • No Linux

Who it suits

A solo freelancer with one or two clients who needs to send invoices from tracked time.

Hubstaff

Free for one, with screenshots of yourself
Runs on Windows, Mac, Linux, Chrome OS, iOS, Android, Chrome and Firefox extensions Pricing (November 2026) Free for 1 user (the organization owner) through the help center; Starter $4.99 per seat on annual billing with a 2-seat minimum; Grow $7.50; Team $10. Full tiers on our Hubstaff pricing page

What it is

Hubstaff's free tier is not on its pricing page, which lists only a 14-day trial, but its help center describes a one-user plan with time tracking, 100 screenshots a month, three clients, 500 to-dos and limited reports with limited retention. It is a way for a freelancer to prove hours to a client with screenshots, and nothing more.

What the free plan gives you

  • Screenshots and activity for a freelancer proving hours
  • Linux and Chrome OS apps
  • Keeps the Hubstaff workflow if a team later pays
  • No card

What it holds back

  • One user, owner only
  • Not shown on the pricing page
  • Limited reports and retention
  • Paid plans have a 2-seat minimum

Who it suits

A contractor whose clients want screenshot evidence, before a team buys seats.

My Hours

Free for five, mobile-first
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, browser extension, desktop as a progressive web app Pricing (November 2026) Free for up to 5 active users with a 14-day Pro trial; Basic $4 per user on annual billing ($5 monthly), Pro $8 ($9)

What it is

My Hours is a Slovenian tracker with a clear free plan for five users covering time tracking, projects, clients and reports. Approvals with an audit log sit on Pro. Its feature matrix is not fully published, so the exact free exclusions are unclear; what is published is that the paid tiers are among the cheapest in the category.

What the free plan gives you

  • Five users free
  • Cheap paid plans at $4 and $8
  • Good mobile apps
  • Simple to set up

What it holds back

  • Free exclusions not fully published
  • Desktop app is a web app
  • Approvals need Pro
  • Smaller integration library

Who it suits

Small service teams of five who may pay $4 later.

Everhour

Free for five, if you do not need integrations
Runs on Web, Mac menu-bar app, iOS, Android, Apple Watch, browser extension Pricing (November 2026) Free for up to 5 seats with a 14-day full trial; Team $8.50 per seat on annual billing with a 5-seat minimum; custom from 50 seats

What it is

Everhour is known for embedding timers inside Asana, Jira, Trello, ClickUp and other project tools, and that is exactly what its free plan withholds: integrations are paid-only. Free covers basic tracking, projects and tasks, reports and data export for five people. Invoicing and approvals are on Team.

What the free plan gives you

  • Five seats free with data export
  • Apple Watch and menu-bar apps
  • Strong project reporting once paid
  • 14-day full trial

What it holds back

  • No integrations on free, which is Everhour's point
  • No Windows or Linux app
  • Team plan has a 5-seat minimum at $8.50
  • Invoicing and approvals paid

Who it suits

Teams trialing Everhour's reporting before paying for the integrations.

TrackingTime

Unlimited users, PDF reports
Runs on Web, Mac, Windows, iOS, Android, Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge extensions Pricing (November 2026) Free for unlimited users (the 3-user cap was removed in 2026) after a 14-day trial; Starter $4.50 per user on annual billing ($6 monthly) with a 2-user minimum, Pro $6.50, Business $10

What it is

TrackingTime dropped its free-plan user cap in 2026, making it one of three trackers here with no limit on headcount. Free includes timers, manual and batch entry, imports, unlimited projects, tasks and clients, day and week views, all apps and the browser extension, and PDF export. The company dashboard, project reports, saved and shared reports, CSV and Excel export, calendar sync, approvals and invoicing are paid.

What the free plan gives you

  • No user cap
  • Unlimited projects and clients
  • Every browser extension
  • Starter at $4.50 is cheap

What it holds back

  • PDF export only
  • No team dashboard or project reports on free
  • No Linux
  • 2-user minimum on paid plans

Who it suits

Teams of any size that need everyone tracking against projects and can report from PDFs.

Traqq

Free for three, no surveillance
Runs on Desktop, web, browser extensions Pricing (November 2026) Free for up to 3 users and 1 project; Plus $4 per user monthly (about $3.40 annual) for up to 10 users, Business $9 for up to 50, Scale $13 for up to 300; 14-day trial and money-back period

What it is

Traqq restructured its plans in 2026 and now offers a free tier for three users and one project. It is notable for what it refuses to do: no screenshots, no activity levels, no keystrokes and no idle tracking, by design. Paid plans are priced by team-size bands rather than per feature.

What the free plan gives you

  • Explicit no-monitoring design
  • Three users free
  • Cheap paid bands
  • Money-back guarantee

What it holds back

  • One project on free
  • No mobile apps
  • Small integration library
  • Desktop platforms not fully documented

Who it suits

Small teams that want a basic tracker and want to be able to say it does not watch anyone.

Kimai

Free forever if you host it
Runs on Web app on any device, installable to the home screen; your own Linux or Docker server Pricing (November 2026) Open source under AGPL 3 with no user cap when self-hosted; Kimai Cloud from about 2.99 euros per user on annual billing (3.99 monthly) with a 30-day trial and no free cloud tier; paid plugins in the store, including a kiosk at 199 euros

What it is

Kimai is the serious open-source option: customers, projects, activities, rates, invoicing, exports, a full API, roles and permissions, and a plugin store, with no limits because you run it on your own server with Docker or a manual install. The cost is hosting and upkeep. For a company with an IT function, it is the only way on this list to get unlimited users, unlimited exports and an API at no license cost.

What the free plan gives you

  • No caps of any kind
  • Invoicing, API and exports included
  • Your data on your server
  • Active plugin ecosystem

What it holds back

  • You host, back up, update and secure it
  • No native mobile apps
  • Cloud version has no free tier
  • Kiosk is a paid plugin

Who it suits

Companies with IT staff that want a free tracker with no vendor dependency.

Connecteam

Free for ten with everything
Runs on iOS, Android, web Pricing (November 2026) Small Business plan free for life for up to 10 users (active and archived) with every feature, limited to one time clock and one schedule; paid hubs from $29 a month on annual billing for 30 users. Compared in Connecteam alternatives

What it is

Connecteam's free plan is unusual in giving a small business every feature rather than a stripped tier: GPS time clock, shared kiosk, scheduling, timesheets, time off, chat, forms and training, for up to ten users. The limits are the user count (the eleventh forces a paid plan) and one time clock and one schedule. It is built for deskless, hourly teams and is the best free option for them; project-based knowledge work is not what it does.

What the free plan gives you

  • Every feature free for ten
  • GPS clock, kiosk, schedules and time off
  • Payroll exports to Gusto, QuickBooks and others
  • Mobile-first for hourly staff

What it holds back

  • Ten users, counting archived ones
  • One clock and one schedule
  • Not a project time tracker
  • Paid pricing is per 30 users, poor value at 11 to 20

Who it suits

Shops, restaurants, cleaning and care businesses with up to ten hourly staff.

When free stops being free

The free plans above fail at predictable points. The first is exports: Clockify, TimeCamp, TrackingTime and Toggl all hold back CSV and Excel export, so the moment you need to put time into a spreadsheet, payroll or an invoice you pay $3.99 to $9 a seat. The second is headcount: Toggl at 3, Clockify, My Hours and Everhour at 5, Connecteam at 10. The third is money features: billable rates, invoicing and approvals are paid everywhere except Harvest (one seat) and Kimai (self-hosted). The fourth is compliance: if you need auditable timesheets for hourly staff, meal-break records and overtime calculation, see our guides to rounding, meal deductions and retention; Jibble and Connecteam cover this free, the project trackers mostly do not.

The cheapest paid steps are TimeCamp and Clockify at $3.99, My Hours at $4, Traqq at about $3.40 annual and TrackingTime at $4.50. For a five-person team that means $20 to $25 a month for exports and billing, which is less than the time spent working around the free limits. Why it matters: the manual timesheet numbers

  • In a vendor survey of 3,247 small-business owners and HR and operations leads across 40 countries (DeskTrack, fieldwork late 2025 to early 2026), 67 percent relied on manual timesheets, spreadsheets or manager observation: 41 percent manual timesheets and 26 percent spreadsheets. HR teams on manual systems reported spending 40 percent of their hours on reconciliation and correction. A US-only survey of 731 owners (QuickBooks Time, 2018) found 28 percent on paper or spreadsheets and 44 percent naming timesheet errors their biggest struggle.
  • In a July 2026 survey of 831 US hourly workers (OnTheClock with Centiment), 43 percent said they had adjusted their reported hours and 25 percent had punched in for a colleague in the past year; one over-reported hour a week costs about $1,684 a year per employee at the Bureau of Labor Statistics average hourly wage of $32.40. Our time theft statistics page has the series and the caveats.
  • Connecteam's analysis of its own customer conversations (January 2024 to May 2026, not a representative survey) puts manager time correcting timesheets at one to two hours a week, or 52 to 104 hours a year.

How these free plans were evaluated

Each vendor's pricing page and, where it exists, the help-center article on free-plan limits were read on November 9, 2026 for the user cap, project and client caps, feature exclusions, export and API limits, retention, whether the plan is permanent or a trial, the first paid price and the platforms supported. Where the vendor does not publish a limit (Jibble's exclusions, My Hours' feature matrix, Hubstaff's retention) the card says so. DeskTime Lite was dropped because it no longer exists; RescueTime Lite and ActivTrak Free were left out because they are personal analytics and monitoring tools rather than timesheets, and Paymo's one-user free plan because Harvest covers the same case better. Key takeaways

  • Three free plans have no user cap in November 2026: Jibble (attendance, kiosk, GPS, time off), TimeCamp (unlimited projects, automatic tracking, API) and TrackingTime (unlimited projects, PDF reports). Kimai is unlimited if you host it yourself.
  • Clockify's free plan is 5 users with 31-day PDF reports since April 2026; Toggl's is 3 users with 30 report views a month since September 2026; DeskTime Lite is gone; Hubstaff's one-user free tier exists only in its help center.
  • CSV and Excel export is paid on Clockify, TimeCamp, TrackingTime and Toggl; billable rates and invoicing are paid everywhere except Harvest (one seat) and self-hosted Kimai.
  • Connecteam is free for 10 users with every feature, including a GPS clock, kiosk, schedules and time off, limited to one clock and one schedule; it suits hourly teams rather than project work.
  • Linux desktop apps on free plans: Clockify, TimeCamp and Hubstaff. Kiosks on free plans: Jibble and Connecteam.
  • The cheapest upgrades are TimeCamp and Clockify at $3.99, Traqq at about $3.40 on annual billing, My Hours at $4 and TrackingTime at $4.50 per user per month.
  • Surveys in 2025 and 2026 put 67 percent of small businesses on manual timesheets or spreadsheets globally and 43 percent of US hourly workers admitting to adjusting reported hours; one over-reported hour a week costs about $1,684 a year per employee.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best completely free time tracking software?

For a team of any size, Jibble (attendance, kiosk, GPS and time off for unlimited users) or TimeCamp (project time, automatic tracking and an API for unlimited users and projects). For a team of up to five that wants desktop apps including Linux, Clockify. For one to three people who want the simplest timer, Toggl Track. For a company with IT staff, self-hosted Kimai has no limits at all.

Is Clockify still free?

Yes, for up to five users, with unlimited entries, projects and clients, all apps and most integrations. Since April 2026 the free plan no longer includes the kiosk, billable rates, CSV and Excel export, shared reports, approvals, time off or invoicing, and reports are limited to a 31-day range with PDF export. Basic at $3.99 per seat restores exports and the kiosk.

Is Toggl Track free?

For up to three members on new accounts since September 2026 (older workspaces keep five), with timers, projects, tags, calendar integration and a private timeline, limited to 30 report views a month. Team reports, billable rates, exports and integrations need Starter at $9 per user on annual billing.

Which free time tracker has no user limit?

Jibble, TimeCamp and TrackingTime as of November 2026, plus Kimai if you host it yourself. Connecteam is free for ten users with every feature. Everhour, My Hours and Clockify cap free at five; Toggl and Traqq at three; Harvest and Hubstaff at one.

Which free time tracker works on Linux?

Clockify, TimeCamp and Hubstaff have Linux desktop apps on their free plans, and Kimai runs on a Linux server. Toggl's current site lists Windows and Mac desktop apps only; Jibble, Harvest, Everhour and TrackingTime have no Linux app.

Can I invoice clients with a free time tracker?

Only with Harvest, which includes invoicing and expenses on its one-seat, two-project free plan, or with self-hosted Kimai. Every other free plan on this list holds billable rates and invoicing for paid tiers; the cheapest routes are Clockify Standard at $5.49 or TimeCamp Premium at $6.99 per user on annual billing.

Which free time tracker has a kiosk for clocking in?

Jibble, with QR, NFC and facial recognition on a tablet, and Connecteam, with a shared device kiosk, both free. Clockify moved its kiosk to the $3.99 Basic plan in April 2026, and TimeCamp's kiosk is Enterprise only. Facial recognition clocks require notice and, in Illinois, written consent under biometric privacy laws.

Do free time tracking apps export to payroll?

Mostly not. Connecteam's free plan exports to Gusto, QuickBooks Online, Paychex and Xero; Jibble's free plan integrates with QuickBooks. Clockify, TimeCamp, Toggl and TrackingTime hold CSV and Excel export for paid plans, and Everhour's free plan includes data export but no integrations. If payroll export is the requirement, budget $4 to $6 a seat or use Connecteam or Jibble.
