Best Time Tracking Software for Remote Employees (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 15 min read

Tracking time for remote employees adds three problems to the ordinary one. The connection drops, so the tool has to track offline and sync later. The team spans time zones, so attendance and "late" have to be defined per person. And the employer has to decide, before choosing a tool, whether it wants proof of work or just hours, because the tools split cleanly on that line and the choice shapes the relationship more than any feature.

I reviewed eight tools for remote employees, with pricing checked in October 2026. The first three represent the three answers to the proof-of-work question: Hubstaff (proof, with payroll), Time Doctor (proof, with overwork reporting), Toggl Track (hours on trust). The rest fit particular remote teams. The remote monitoring tools page covers the heavier end of proof of work.

The tools in detail

Hubstaff

Time, proof and payroll across every platform
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10, Enterprise $25 per seat per month billed annually ($7, $9, $12 monthly); 2-seat minimum

What it is

A time tracker for distributed and field teams with screenshots, activity, GPS, payroll and invoicing, on every platform.

Why it is on this list

Hubstaff leads for remote employees paid by the hour because it was built for them: offline tracking that syncs later, screenshots and activity at an interval each team sets, payroll in the contractor's currency, and agents for Windows, Mac, Linux, Chromebook and mobile. The activity percentage is the thing to de-emphasise in how you manage with it.

Where it is strong

  • Time, proof of work, GPS and payment in one product
  • Every platform including Linux and Chromebook
  • Largest review base in the category
  • Cheap entry plan

Where it falls short

  • Activity scores reward input
  • Two-seat minimum
  • Monitoring is the point, which not every team wants

Time Doctor

Attendance, payroll and work-life balance reports
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, Android Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.70, Standard $11.70, Premium $16.70 per user per month billed annually ($8, $14, $20 monthly); Enterprise by quote

What it is

Remote-team time tracking with screenshots, web and app use, payroll, work-life balance reporting and an Office versus Remote comparison.

Why it is on this list

Time Doctor is second for remote and outsourced employees because its attendance, breaks, payroll and integrations are the standard kit for that situation and its work-life balance report catches the remote problem nobody else does, which is people working too long. The plan with those features is Standard at $11.70.

Where it is strong

  • Built for remote and outsourced teams
  • Work-life balance and Office versus Remote reports
  • Payroll and 60-plus integrations
  • Chromebook and Android agents

Where it falls short

  • Payroll and integrations start at $11.70
  • Screenshots and activity draw objections
  • Interface shows its age

Toggl Track

Tracking people keep doing, with no surveillance
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, browser extensions Pricing (October 2026) Free for very small teams; Starter $9 and Premium $16 per user per month billed annually ($12 and $24 monthly); Enterprise by quote

What it is

A timer-and-reports time tracker on every platform, with billable rates and budgets, approvals on Premium, and a principled refusal to add screenshots.

Why it is on this list

Toggl Track is third for remote knowledge teams managed on trust: every platform, a private timeline that fills gaps, project reports and approvals on Premium, and nothing that watches the employee. It records hours for costing and capacity, not attendance, and that is the right tool for a salaried remote team.

Where it is strong

  • The simplest tracker to adopt; people actually use it
  • No screenshots or activity scoring, by principle
  • Every platform plus a private desktop timeline
  • Approvals, labour cost and profitability on Premium

Where it falls short

  • Approvals and labour cost need Premium at $16
  • No invoicing, payroll or GPS
  • The free plan has become smaller over time

Free for five, GPS and approvals cheaply

The broadest free time tracker, five users at no cost, with the cheapest paid ladder adding time off, invoicing, approvals, scheduling and GPS.

Hours or proof of work: decide first

Every tool here records hours. Four of them (Hubstaff, Time Doctor, Clockify Pro, Time Champ) can also record screenshots and activity as evidence the hours were worked; four (Toggl, Timely, Jibble, DeskTime Pro) cannot or do not by default. Proof of work is reasonable when a client requires it for billable hours, when the employee is a contractor whose contract specifies it, or when the role is hourly and remote and the employer has no other way to verify attendance. It is corrosive for salaried knowledge workers whose output is visible in the work, and the mouse jiggler page shows what activity metrics produce once people are judged on them. Decide which case you are in, write it into the remote work policy, and then choose from the matching half of the list.

What remote time tracking needs

  • Offline tracking that syncs when the connection returns: Hubstaff, Time Doctor, Toggl, Clockify, Time Champ, DeskTime and Jibble all do it; Everhour does not, which is why it is on the team page and not this one.
  • Time zones per person, so a 9 a.m. start means the employee's 9 a.m.: Time Champ and Time Doctor handle this explicitly in attendance; the trackers that do not report attendance avoid the problem.
  • Every platform, including Linux and Chromebook for the developers and contractors who use them: Hubstaff, Time Doctor, Toggl, Clockify, DeskTime and Time Champ.
  • Payment attached to time for hourly staff: Hubstaff and Time Doctor have payroll; Clockify and Jibble export; Toggl and Timely do not.
  • Overwork signals. Remote staff work too long more often than too little; Time Doctor's work-life balance report and Timely's overtime view address it, and the burnout data explains why it matters.
  • A self-view. People working alone should see their own record; every tool here provides one, and Timely makes the record private until released.

Contractors are different

Remote teams are often mixed, and the tracking rules differ. A contractor controls how the work is done; requiring tracking software on their own machine, scoring their activity and setting their hours is evidence of employment under the tests on the 1099 versus W-2 page. For contractors, track what the contract specifies as proof of billable time, on their terms, and keep the heavier tools for employees on company devices. The monitoring remote employees guide covers the employee side.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Disclosure. I have done work for Time Champ. It appears here because it belongs in this category, it is held to the same criteria as every other product, its drawbacks are listed like everyone else's, and it bought no placement. There are no affiliate or vendor links on this page.

Key takeaways

  • Decide first whether you need hours or proof of work; the tools split on that line and the choice shapes the relationship.
  • Hubstaff for hourly remote staff with proof and payroll, Time Doctor where overwork is the worry, Toggl Track for salaried teams on trust.
  • Remote tracking needs offline sync, per-person time zones, every platform, payment attached to time, overwork signals and a self-view.
  • Contractors are not employees; tracking their own devices and scoring their activity is misclassification evidence.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time tracking software for remote employees?

Hubstaff for remote employees paid by the hour, with offline tracking, proof of work, payroll and every platform. Time Doctor for remote and outsourced teams where attendance, payroll and overwork reporting matter. Toggl Track for salaried remote knowledge teams managed on trust with no surveillance. Time Champ is the value choice for attendance across time zones; Jibble is free for remote clock-in.

Do remote employees need to be monitored to track time?

No. Toggl Track, Timely, Jibble and DeskTime record hours without screenshots or activity scores, and salaried knowledge teams are usually better managed on output. Proof of work is reasonable for hourly remote staff, for contractors whose contracts specify it, and where clients require it; Hubstaff and Time Doctor are built for those cases.

How does time tracking work when a remote employee is offline?

The desktop or mobile app keeps recording locally and uploads when the connection returns. Hubstaff, Time Doctor, Toggl, Clockify, Time Champ, DeskTime and Jibble all track offline. Browser-based tools and Everhour's extension generally do not, which is why they suit office teams better.

Which time tracking tools handle multiple time zones?

Time Champ and Time Doctor set time zones per user so attendance and late reports are correct for each person. Toggl, Clockify and Hubstaff display entries in each user's local time. Tools that do not report attendance avoid the problem but cannot tell you who started on time.

Can I require a remote contractor to use time tracking software?

You can require proof of billable time if the contract says so, but installing monitoring software on a contractor's own machine, scoring their activity and controlling their hours are indicators of employment under the IRS and Department of Labor tests. Keep contractor tracking to what the contract specifies, on the contractor's terms.

What does remote time tracking cost?

In October 2026, on annual billing: Jibble is free for unlimited users, Clockify is free for five then $3.99, Time Champ $3.90, Hubstaff $4.99 (two-seat minimum), DeskTime $6.42, Time Doctor $6.70 (Standard at $11.70 is the realistic plan), Toggl $9, Timely $9 to $22. The plan with payroll or approvals is usually the one you end up on.
