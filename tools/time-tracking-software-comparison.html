Time Tracking Software Comparison: 12 Tools (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 13 min read

This page is the data behind the time tracking review: twelve tools on fourteen criteria, pricing, free plans and minimums as published in October 2026, and a filter that removes anything that cannot run where you need it, costs more than you will pay or lacks a feature you need. It does not rank. The review pages in this series rank for particular situations; this one is here so you can check their reasoning.

Two things the matrix cannot show: which plan carries each feature, which the cards below list as a plan ladder, and how a tool feels to use every day, which is in the review pages and the verified user reviews the evaluation drew on.

The tools in detail

Clockify

Free for five, cheapest paid ladder
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 5 users; Basic $3.99, Standard $5.49, Pro $7.99, Enterprise $11.99 per seat per month billed annually ($4.99, $6.99, $9.99, $14.99 monthly)

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Free covers five users with timers, timesheets, auto tracker, calendar, apps and reports. Basic ($3.99) adds entering time for others, kiosk, bulk edit and project templates. Standard ($5.49) adds time off, invoicing, approvals, attendance and overtime and QuickBooks sync. Pro ($7.99) adds scheduling, forecasting, expenses, labour cost, budgets, GPS, activity monitoring and custom fields. Enterprise ($11.99) adds SSO, SCIM, audit logs and a custom subdomain. Monthly billing is $4.99, $6.99, $9.99 and $14.99.

In one line

Clockify is the value column: free for five, the cheapest ladder, and almost every feature somewhere on it.

Where it falls short

  • Approvals and time off start at Standard; GPS and scheduling at Pro
  • Interface is dense once every module is on
  • Support is slower on the lower tiers

DeskTime

Cheapest automatic tracker with scheduling
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Free Lite for one user; Pro $6.42 and Premium $9.17 per user per month billed annually ($7 and $10 monthly); Enterprise by quote

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Lite is free for one user. Pro ($6.42) adds URL and app tracking, productivity calculation, project tracking, offline time and calendar integrations. Premium ($9.17) adds screenshots, shift scheduling, the absence calendar, integrations and API. Enterprise is quoted for 200-plus users. Monthly billing is $7 and $10.

In one line

DeskTime is the automatic-and-cheap column: productivity scoring, private time, shifts, screenshots on Premium.

Where it falls short

  • Screenshots only on Premium
  • Productivity scoring needs tuning
  • No invoicing or approvals

Everhour

Tracking inside the tools you already use
Runs on Web and browser extension inside Asana, Jira, ClickUp, monday, Trello, GitHub, Notion and others; iOS Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 5 users without integrations; Team $8.50 per seat per month billed annually ($10 monthly) with a 5-seat minimum; Custom from 50 seats; 14-day trial

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Free covers five users with tracking, projects, reports and export but no integrations. Team ($8.50, five-seat minimum, $10 monthly) includes every integration, budgets and alerts, invoicing, time off and accruals, scheduling, SSO and permissions. Custom is quoted from 50 seats with volume pricing and an account manager.

In one line

Everhour is the integration column: time and budgets inside the project tool, five-seat minimum, no offline or GPS.

Where it falls short

  • Five-seat minimum on the paid plan
  • Useless without one of its integrations
  • No desktop automatic tracking or GPS

Harvest

Time to invoice to payment in one flow
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, browser extensions Pricing (October 2026) Free for one seat and two projects; Teams $9 and Enterprise $14 per seat per month billed annually ($11 and $17.50 monthly); Enterprise Plus by quote; 30-day trial

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Free covers one seat and two projects with time, invoicing and expenses. Teams ($9) adds unlimited seats and team reporting. Enterprise ($14) adds profitability reporting, timesheet approvals, unlimited custom reports and SAML SSO. Enterprise Plus is quoted. Monthly billing is $11 and $17.50, and usage above the plan's included invoices, projects and clients can be billed.

In one line

Harvest is the billing column: time to invoice to payment, approvals and profitability on Enterprise, no automatic tracking.

Where it falls short

  • Approvals and profitability need Enterprise at $14
  • Usage-based billing can grow with invoices and projects
  • No automatic tracking, GPS or attendance

Hubstaff

Time, proof and payment in one
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10, Enterprise $25 per seat per month billed annually ($7, $9, $12 monthly); 2-seat minimum

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Starter ($4.99) covers time, timesheets, limited screenshots and activity with a two-seat minimum. Grow ($7.50) adds projects, budgets, reports and integrations. Team ($10) adds unlimited screenshots, app and URL tracking, payroll, scheduling, idle timeouts and expenses. Enterprise ($25, annual) adds SSO and provisioning. Monthly billing is $7, $9 and $12.

In one line

Hubstaff is the proof-of-work column: screenshots, activity, GPS and payroll on every platform.

Where it falls short

  • Activity scores reward input
  • Two-seat minimum
  • Monitoring is the point, which not every team wants

Jibble

Free for unlimited users with GPS and a kiosk
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, kiosk, Slack and Teams Pricing (October 2026) Free with unlimited users including GPS and timesheets; Premium from about $4.49 and Ultimate from about $7.99 per user per month on annual billing, with Enterprise by quote; the vendor does not publish a fixed table, so confirm the quote

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Free covers unlimited users with timesheets, GPS, projects, overtime rules, the kiosk and integrations. Premium (roughly $4.49 per user on annual billing) adds geofencing, custom permissions, work schedules and payroll exports. Ultimate (roughly $7.99) adds live location, advanced reports and project costing. Enterprise is quoted. Jibble does not publish a fixed table and the figures vary by source; get the quote in writing.

In one line

Jibble is the free attendance column: unlimited users, GPS, facial recognition kiosk, unpublished paid prices.

Where it falls short

  • Paid pricing is not published as a fixed table
  • Reporting and project tracking are lighter than the project tools
  • Facial recognition raises Illinois and Texas biometric law obligations

QuickBooks Time

The time clock that feeds QuickBooks
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Premium $20 base plus $10 per user per month; Elite $40 base plus $12 per user per month; introductory base fees of $10 and $20 for three months; sold with QuickBooks Online

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Premium is a $20 monthly base fee plus $10 per user, with mobile clock-in, GPS, scheduling, time off, kiosk, photo attachments and alerts. Elite is $40 plus $12 per user and adds geofencing, mileage, project deadlines, budget-versus-actual and timesheet signatures. Introductory base fees of $10 and $20 apply for three months. Both are sold with QuickBooks Online and carry a 30-day trial.

In one line

QuickBooks Time is the payroll column: a time clock with GPS and kiosk that feeds QuickBooks, priced with a base fee.

Where it falls short

  • Base fee plus per-user pricing is expensive for small teams
  • Tied to QuickBooks Online
  • Not a project time tracker for knowledge work

Time Champ

Automatic attendance and time from $3.90
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux; iOS and Android for GPS Pricing (October 2026) Starter $3.90, Professional $6.90, Enterprise $13.90 per user per month billed annually; project management, HR and other modules as add-ons

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Starter ($3.90) covers automatic attendance, app and website tracking, productivity classification, timesheets, offline tracking and time zones. Professional ($6.90) adds silent tracking, screenshots, idle timeouts, shifts, hierarchies and more reports. Enterprise ($13.90) adds live views, alerts, custom reports and SSO. Project management and HR are add-ons.

In one line

Time Champ is the attendance column: automatic attendance with time zones and offline tracking at the lowest full-plan price.

Where it falls short

  • Short detailed history below Enterprise
  • Project management and HR are add-ons
  • Smaller review base than the leaders

Time Doctor

Remote tracking with payroll and balance reports
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, Android Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.70, Standard $11.70, Premium $16.70 per user per month billed annually ($8, $14, $20 monthly); Enterprise by quote

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Basic ($6.70) covers tracking, screenshots, timeline, offline sync and projects. Standard ($11.70) adds web and app use, attendance, breaks, payroll, the Office versus Remote report and 60-plus integrations. Premium ($16.70) adds screen video, AI reporting, two years of history and a success manager. Monthly billing is $8, $14 and $20.

In one line

Time Doctor is the remote-team column: attendance, payroll, integrations and overwork reporting from Standard.

Where it falls short

  • Payroll and integrations start at $11.70
  • Screenshots and activity draw objections
  • Interface shows its age

TimeCamp

Free for unlimited users, then a cheap ladder
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free with unlimited users; Starter $3.99, Premium $6.99, Ultimate $9.99 per user per month billed annually ($5.49, $9.99, $13.99 monthly); Enterprise by quote from 50 users with self-hosting

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Free covers unlimited users with timesheets, apps and the AI tracker. Starter ($3.99) adds the attendance calendar, invoicing, exports, time off and overtime. Premium ($6.99) adds the resource planner, billable time, budgets and estimates, app and website tracking and one integration. Ultimate ($9.99) adds custom fields, remote work detection, approvals, billing rates, expenses, screenshots and unlimited integrations. Enterprise is quoted from 50 users with self-hosting, geofencing, SLA and audit logs. Monthly billing is $5.49, $9.99 and $13.99.

In one line

TimeCamp is the free-for-unlimited column with attendance, budgets, screenshots and self-hosting up the ladder.

Where it falls short

  • Approvals and screenshots need Ultimate at $9.99
  • Geofencing only on Enterprise
  • Reporting is weaker than Harvest's for billing

Timely

Automatic, private capture with AI timesheets
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $9 (up to 5 users, 20 projects), Premium $16 (up to 50 users), Unlimited $22 per user per month billed annually ($11, $20, $28 monthly); Enterprise by quote; Tasks add-on from $5

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Starter ($9) covers up to five users and twenty projects with automatic Memory capture and AI timesheets. Premium ($16) lifts the cap to fifty users and unlimited projects and adds project management and accounting integrations and team administration. Unlimited ($22) removes the caps and adds capacity management and overtime and undertime monitoring. Enterprise is quoted. The Tasks module is a $5-plus add-on. Monthly billing is $11, $20 and $28.

In one line

Timely is the privacy column: automatic capture visible only to the user, AI timesheets, capacity, the highest price.

Where it falls short

  • The most expensive tracker here per seat
  • User caps on Starter (5) and Premium (50)
  • No approvals, invoicing or GPS

Toggl Track

The tracker people keep using
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, browser extensions Pricing (October 2026) Free for very small teams; Starter $9 and Premium $16 per user per month billed annually ($12 and $24 monthly); Enterprise by quote

Plans and add-ons

Plan ladder. Free covers very small teams with timers, the desktop timeline, reports and integrations. Starter ($9) adds billable rates, team reports, PTO and public holidays, timeline views, tags and milestones. Premium ($16) adds labour costs and profitability, fixed-fee projects, project dashboards, timesheet approvals, data accuracy features, Jira and Asana integrations and SSO. Enterprise is quoted with onboarding, custom roles and multiple workspaces. Monthly billing is $12 and $24.

In one line

Toggl Track is the adoption column: timers on every platform, a private timeline, no monitoring, approvals only on Premium.

Where it falls short

  • Approvals and labour cost need Premium at $16
  • No invoicing, payroll or GPS
  • The free plan has become smaller over time

How to read the matrix

Cross out first: a platform you need that is missing, a minimum above your team, a price above your ceiling. Then decide what must be absent as well as present; a team that wants no monitoring should prefer the tools that cannot take screenshots (Toggl, Harvest, Everhour, Timely, Jibble, QuickBooks Time) over those that can but are switched off. Then check the free plan column carefully, because "free" ranges from one seat and two projects (Harvest) to unlimited users (Jibble, TimeCamp). Only then compare the yeses.

Three ways to shortlist

  • By kind: billing trackers (Toggl, Harvest, Everhour, Timely), time clocks (QuickBooks Time, Jibble, Clockify and TimeCamp in attendance mode), automatic trackers (Timely, DeskTime, Time Champ), trackers with monitoring (Hubstaff, Time Doctor).
  • By situation: the employer page, the teams page and the remote employees page each rank a subset for one case.
  • By what the hours become: an invoice (Harvest, Everhour, Toggl), a payroll run (QuickBooks Time, Hubstaff, Time Doctor, Jibble), a capacity report (Timely, Toggl, Clockify Pro), or an attendance record (Time Champ, Jibble, TimeCamp, Clockify).

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Disclosure. I have done work for Time Champ. It appears here because it belongs in this category, it is held to the same criteria as every other product, its drawbacks are listed like everyone else's, and it bought no placement. There are no affiliate or vendor links on this page.

Key takeaways

  • Twelve tools, fourteen criteria, no ranking; filter by platform, price and features before reading anything else.
  • Free ranges from one seat and two projects to unlimited users; read the free plan column, not the word.
  • Entry prices run from free to $9 per user plus QuickBooks Time's base fee; the plan with approvals is usually a tier up.
  • Shortlist by kind, by situation, or by what the hours become.

Frequently asked questions

How do I compare time tracking software?

Cross out tools that miss a platform you need, have a seat minimum above your team or cost more than your ceiling; decide which features must be absent as well as present; read the free plan limits carefully; then compare features. The filter and matrix on this page follow that order.

Which time tracking software has the best free plan?

Jibble (unlimited users with GPS, timesheets, overtime rules and a kiosk) and TimeCamp (unlimited users for basic timesheets) are the most generous. Clockify is free for five users with a full tracker. Toggl Track is free for very small teams, Harvest for one seat and two projects, DeskTime for one user. Everhour's free plan has no integrations.

Which time tracking tools have no screenshots or monitoring?

Toggl Track, Harvest, Everhour, Timely, Jibble and QuickBooks Time do not take screenshots on any plan. Clockify, TimeCamp, DeskTime, Time Champ, Hubstaff and Time Doctor offer screenshots on some plan; on Clockify, TimeCamp and DeskTime they are upper-tier features that are off unless bought.

Which time trackers include payroll?

QuickBooks Time feeds QuickBooks payroll directly; Hubstaff and Time Doctor have payroll modules; Jibble and Clockify export to payroll providers. Toggl, Harvest, Everhour, Timely, DeskTime and Time Champ track hours but do not run payroll.

Which time tracking software works on Linux?

Toggl Track, Clockify, TimeCamp, Hubstaff, Time Doctor, DeskTime and Time Champ have Linux desktop apps. Harvest, Timely and Jibble do not (web access works); Everhour is a browser extension; QuickBooks Time is web and mobile.

What does the entry price leave out?

Base fees (QuickBooks Time's $20 or $40 a month), seat minimums (Hubstaff two, Everhour five), the plan that actually carries approvals, invoicing or payroll (often one or two tiers up), add-ons, and monthly versus annual billing, which differs by 15 to 30 per cent across these tools.
