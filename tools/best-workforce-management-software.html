Best Workforce Management Software: 12 Reviewed (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 19 min read

Workforce management (WFM) software plans and records the hours an organisation buys from its people: forecasting demand, building schedules, tracking time and absence, applying labour law, and feeding payroll. It is sold two ways. HCM suites (UKG, ADP, Dayforce, Workday, Rippling, Paycor, Paylocity) bundle it with HR, payroll and benefits and mostly price by quote. Specialist tools (Deputy, When I Work, Connecteam, Homebase, Legion, Time Champ) do scheduling and time on their own at published prices and connect to whatever runs payroll. The right answer depends far more on headcount, workforce type and compliance exposure than on features, which converge.

I reviewed twelve platforms with pricing checked in October 2026, published where it exists and estimated with a label where it does not. The first three are starting points for the three most common buyers; the rest follow alphabetically. The workforce management glossary entry defines the category and the HR software review covers the HR side of the suites in more depth.

The tools in detail

UKG (Ready and Pro WFM)

The deepest rules engine, from mid-market to enterprise
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, UKG clocks Pricing (October 2026) Quote only. Independent buyer estimates for UKG Ready cluster around $20 to $33 per employee per month for the full suite, with implementation at 20 to 40 percent of first-year fees; UKG Pro Workforce Management (formerly Dimensions) is enterprise-priced

What it is

UKG sells two workforce management products: UKG Ready, an all-in-one HR, payroll, time and scheduling suite for organisations from about fifty to several thousand people, and UKG Pro Workforce Management (the former Kronos Workforce Dimensions), the enterprise time, scheduling, forecasting and labour compliance system that runs hospitals, manufacturers and retailers. Both are quote-priced. The compliance engine, the clocks and the depth of scheduling rules are the reasons UKG wins complex hourly environments; the cost and the implementation are the reasons smaller firms look elsewhere.

Why it is on this list

UKG leads because workforce management at its hardest, hospitals, plants and retail chains with union rules, state break laws, differentials and predictive scheduling ordinances, is where its rules engine, clocks and forecasting are without peer, and UKG Ready brings a lighter version of the same discipline to organisations from about fifty people. Both are quote-priced; independent estimates put Ready in the $20 to $33 range per employee for the full suite. Implementation is the cost nobody budgets enough for.

Where it is strong

  • Deepest compliance and scheduling rules engine
  • Two products spanning mid-market to enterprise
  • Clocks, forecasting and labour analytics
  • Strong in healthcare, manufacturing and retail

Where it falls short

  • Quote only; implementation heavy
  • Two products can confuse buyers
  • Expensive below fifty people

Rippling

One record for everything, published entry price
Runs on Web, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Platform from $8 per employee per month plus a $35 monthly base fee, with payroll, benefits, time, HR and IT sold as modules; fully configured HR and payroll typically lands in the low-to-mid $20s per employee

What it is

Rippling unifies HR, payroll, benefits, time and attendance, scheduling, device management and app provisioning on one employee record, with published entry pricing ($8 per employee plus a $35 base) and modules that add to it. It suits companies from ten to a couple of thousand people that want HR and IT administered from one place and are comfortable with a modular bill. Workforce management is one of its modules, not its centre.

Why it is on this list

Rippling is second for the far more common buyer: a company of ten to two thousand people, mostly salaried or mixed, that wants HR, payroll, benefits, time, scheduling and IT provisioning on one employee record. It publishes an entry price ($8 per employee plus $35), deploys in weeks, and its workforce management module is competent rather than specialist. The modular bill is the thing to model before signing.

Where it is strong

  • HR, payroll, time and IT on one record
  • Published entry pricing
  • Fast to deploy
  • Strong automation and app provisioning

Where it falls short

  • Modular bill grows quickly
  • WFM is a module, not the centre
  • Base fee on small teams

Deputy

Demand scheduling and compliance at published prices
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Lite $5, Core $6.50, Pro $9 per user per month; $30 monthly minimum; payroll add-on $8 per user plus $49 and HR add-on $2

What it is

Deputy is the workforce management layer for hourly operations that do not want a full HCM suite: demand-driven auto-scheduling, a compliant time clock, labour-law rules by jurisdiction, and payroll and HR as add-ons, at published prices from $5 a user with a $30 minimum. It integrates with the payroll and HR systems most mid-sized firms already run.

Why it is on this list

Deputy is third because many hourly operations do not want an HCM suite; they want scheduling, time and compliance that plug into the payroll and HR they already have, at a price they can read on a web page. Deputy's demand-driven auto-scheduling and jurisdiction-aware rules do that from $5 a user with a $30 minimum, with payroll and HR add-ons if the business grows into them.

Where it is strong

  • Published pricing from $5
  • Demand-driven auto-scheduling
  • Compliance by jurisdiction
  • Integrates with existing payroll and HR

Where it falls short

  • $30 minimum
  • Payroll and HR are add-ons
  • Not an HCM suite

ADP Workforce Now

Payroll and compliance everywhere, time on Premium
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, ADP clocks Pricing (October 2026) Quote only. Select covers payroll and HR, Plus adds benefits, Premium adds time and attendance; independent estimates run $23 to $30 per employee per month for the platform, more with outsourced services, plus setup fees

What it is

ADP Workforce Now is the mid-market tier of the largest payroll provider: payroll, tax, HR records and onboarding on Select, benefits administration on Plus, and time and attendance with scheduling on Premium, with talent, analytics and outsourced HR services available. Its strength is payroll and compliance across every US jurisdiction and the breadth of its marketplace; its scheduling is adequate rather than specialist. Pricing is quoted and negotiated.

Why it is on this list

ADP Workforce Now is on the list for employers who start from payroll: tax and compliance in every jurisdiction, benefits on Plus, time and attendance on Premium, and the largest integration marketplace in HR. Scheduling is adequate rather than specialist, and pricing is quoted; estimates run $23 to $30 per employee for the platform.

Where it is strong

  • Payroll and tax compliance in every US jurisdiction
  • Tiered platform from payroll to time and attendance
  • Largest integration marketplace
  • Outsourced HR services available

Where it falls short

  • Quote only; negotiated pricing
  • Scheduling is adequate, not specialist
  • Service quality varies by account team

Connecteam

Everything in one app, free for ten
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 10 users; Operations hub Basic $29, Advanced $49, Expert $99 per month for the first 30 users then $0.80, $2.50 and $4.20 per extra user; HR and Communications hubs priced separately

What it is

Connecteam is workforce management for deskless teams: scheduling, GPS time clock, forms and checklists, tasks, chat, training and HR documents in one mobile app, free for ten users and priced per hub for the first 30 users after. It is strongest where the workforce has phones and no desks, and lightest on payroll and compliance depth.

Why it is on this list

Connecteam is here for deskless teams: scheduling, GPS clock, forms, tasks, chat and training in one phone app, free for ten users and priced for the first 30 after, with payroll through integrations.

Where it is strong

  • Free for ten users
  • Everything a deskless team needs in one app
  • Priced for the first 30 users
  • Fast adoption on phones

Where it falls short

  • Hubs priced separately
  • No payroll
  • Light compliance depth

Dayforce

One engine for HR, pay, time and schedules
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, clocks Pricing (October 2026) Quote only. Independent estimates run $22 to $31 per employee per month for core HCM and payroll and $28 to $34 with the workforce management suite; implementation is a significant one-time cost

What it is

Dayforce (formerly Ceridian) runs HR, payroll, benefits, time, scheduling and talent on a single continuously calculating engine, so a schedule change shows its payroll cost immediately and payroll is effectively always ready. It suits organisations from a few hundred to tens of thousands of employees, especially multi-country ones. Implementation is a serious project and pricing is quoted.

Why it is on this list

Dayforce earns its place for mid-to-large employers, particularly across countries, where its single continuously calculating engine means a schedule change shows its payroll cost at once. Quote only; estimates run $22 to $34 per employee depending on modules, with a significant implementation.

Where it is strong

  • Single continuous-calculation engine
  • Schedule changes show payroll cost immediately
  • Multi-country payroll
  • Strong scheduling for hourly workforces

Where it falls short

  • Quote only; large implementation
  • Overkill below a few hundred staff
  • Long contracts

What it is

Homebase is workforce management for a small number of hourly locations: scheduling, a time clock on the POS, hiring, team messaging, HR tools and payroll, priced per location and free for one. It is the right size for a restaurant group of three and the wrong one for a chain of thirty.

Why it is on this list

Homebase is on the list for a small number of hourly locations: scheduling, clock, hiring, HR and payroll in one app per location, free for one. It stops making sense at a chain.

Where it falls short

Why it is on this list

Legion is here for large retail and hospitality operators for whom the scheduling engine is the whole point: interval-level AI demand forecasting, automated schedules that balance cost, compliance and preferences, and instant pay. Quote only.

Workday HCM is the enterprise system of record for HR, with time tracking, absence, scheduling (including Workday Scheduling for frontline workers), payroll in some countries, talent, planning (Adaptive) and analytics (People Analytics) as modules. Reading a quote

Most of the platforms on this page are quote-priced. Where I give a figure for a quote-only vendor it is the range independent buyer surveys and procurement advisers report, labelled as an estimate, not a price list; your quote will depend on headcount, modules, contract length and how late in the quarter you sign. Published prices are stated as published.

  • Per employee or per user? Workday and most HCM suites charge for every employee on payroll; specialists charge for users of the tool. For a workforce of 2,000 with 200 managers, the difference is tenfold on a scheduling module.
  • What is in the implementation fee and who does the data migration and the rules configuration; UKG, Dayforce and Workday implementations run 20 to 60 percent of first-year software cost.
  • Which modules carry the features you were shown. Time and attendance is a tier up on ADP; forecasting is a module on several suites; payroll is an add-on on Deputy.
  • Introductory discounts and their expiry, auto-renewal uplifts, and the price per employee as headcount crosses band boundaries.
  • Exit terms: data export format and the notice period.

Compliance is the differentiator

Features converge; rules engines do not. The questions to test on a trial: daily overtime (California, Alaska, Nevada, Colorado), meal and rest break rules and penalties by state, predictive scheduling ordinances in Oregon and the large cities, minors' hours, union rules on seniority and shift bidding, differentials in the regular rate for overtime, and rounding set to exact minutes. UKG, Dayforce and Deputy encode most of this; the others apply what you configure. The overtime, shift differential and split shift pages describe the rules the engine has to get right, and the employee cost calculator the fully loaded cost the schedule is spending.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Disclosure. I have done work for Time Champ. It appears here because it belongs in this category, it is held to the same criteria as every other product, its drawbacks are listed like everyone else's, and it bought no placement. There are no affiliate or vendor links on this page.

Key takeaways

  • WFM is sold as HCM suites (quote-priced, HR and payroll included) or specialist tools (published prices, scheduling and time depth); choose by headcount, workforce type and compliance exposure.
  • UKG for complex hourly compliance, Rippling for 10 to 2,000 people wanting one record, Deputy for hourly WFM without a suite; Connecteam and Homebase for small deskless and hourly teams; Workday above a thousand.
  • Quote-only figures on this page are labelled estimates from buyer surveys; your quote depends on headcount, modules, term and timing.
  • Ask whether pricing is per employee or per user, what implementation includes, and which module carries each feature.
  • Test the rules engine on your own state's overtime, break and scheduling law before buying.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best workforce management software?

UKG for complex hourly operations where compliance rules and scheduling depth matter most; Rippling for companies of 10 to 2,000 that want HR, payroll, time and IT on one employee record with a published entry price; Deputy for hourly operations that want scheduling, time and compliance at published prices without an HCM suite. ADP and Paycor for payroll-first buyers, Dayforce and Workday for large and enterprise employers, Connecteam for deskless teams, Homebase for a few hourly locations, Legion for large retail, Time Champ for desk-based teams.

What does workforce management software do?

It forecasts demand, builds schedules, tracks time and attendance, manages absence and leave, applies labour law (overtime, breaks, predictive scheduling), reports labour cost and adherence, and feeds payroll. HCM suites add HR records, benefits and talent; specialist WFM tools do scheduling and time and integrate with payroll.

How much does workforce management software cost?

Specialist tools publish prices: When I Work from $2.50 per user, Time Champ $3.90, Deputy $5 with a $30 minimum, Connecteam $29 for 30 users, Homebase $30 per location. HCM suites quote: Rippling publishes $8 per employee plus $35 as an entry point; independent estimates for UKG Ready, ADP Workforce Now and Dayforce cluster in the $20 to $35 per employee range for full suites, and Workday higher, all plus implementation.

What is the difference between WFM and HCM software?

Human capital management (HCM) covers the whole employee lifecycle: HR records, payroll, benefits, talent, learning and analytics. Workforce management (WFM) is the part that plans and records hours: forecasting, scheduling, time and attendance, absence and labour compliance. HCM suites include a WFM module; specialist WFM tools do only that part and connect to an HCM or payroll system.

Which workforce management software is best for small business?

Connecteam (free for ten users) for deskless teams, Homebase (free for one location) for a shop or restaurant, When I Work (from $2.50) for hourly scheduling, Deputy (from $5) when compliance matters, Time Champ ($3.90) for a desk-based team, and Rippling when a small company wants HR, payroll and IT together with a published price. The workforce management for growing businesses review on this site covers the small-to-mid transition.

Do I need an HCM suite for workforce management?

Not unless payroll complexity, benefits administration or a mixed salaried workforce make a single record worth the quote. Hourly and deskless operations under about 500 people are usually better served by a specialist tool at a published price integrated with their existing payroll. Above that, and wherever compliance is complex, the suites and UKG in particular earn their cost.
