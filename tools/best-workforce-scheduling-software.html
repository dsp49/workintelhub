Best Workforce Scheduling Software: 10 Tools Reviewed (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 16 min read

Workforce scheduling software decides who works when. At the simple end it is a shared rota with availability and swaps; at the complex end it forecasts demand from sales or patient volume, builds the schedule automatically within labour-law and union rules, and measures the result against the clock. The ten tools here span that range, and the first question is which end you are at: a tool that forecasts demand is wasted on a team of twelve, and a free rota will not survive a hospital.

I reviewed ten with pricing checked in October 2026, which in this category means per user, per location or by quote. The first three represent the three ends of the range; the rest fit particular sectors and sizes. The work schedule template and rotating shift guides cover the patterns the tools implement.

The tools in detail

Deputy

Auto-scheduling, forecasting and compliance in one
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Lite $5, Core $6.50, Pro $9 per user per month (10% less on annual billing); $30 monthly minimum; payroll add-on $8 per user plus $49; HR $2

What it is

Deputy is the scheduling and attendance platform for hourly workforces that take labour law seriously. It builds schedules automatically from demand forecasts and availability, enforces break, overtime and predictive-scheduling rules by jurisdiction, runs a time clock with photo capture and geolocation on phones and kiosks, and exports or runs payroll. It is priced per user with a $30 monthly minimum and sells HR and payroll as add-ons.

Why it is on this list

Deputy leads workforce scheduling because it builds the schedule rather than hosting it: demand forecasts from sales or foot traffic set the required coverage, auto-scheduling fills it from availability, skills and cost, and compliance rules by jurisdiction stop the schedule breaking break, overtime or predictive-scheduling law before it is published. The time clock then measures the schedule against what happened. It is more than a single shop needs and priced accordingly.

Where it is strong

  • Auto-scheduling from demand forecasts
  • Break, overtime and predictive-scheduling compliance by jurisdiction
  • Time clock with photo and location on phones and kiosks
  • Payroll and HR add-ons

Where it falls short

  • $30 monthly minimum hurts very small teams
  • Payroll and HR are paid add-ons
  • More than a simple shop needs

When I Work

Auto-scheduling and swaps from $2.50
Runs on Web, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Essentials $2.50, Pro $5, Premium $8 per user per month; time and attendance add-on $1.50 (Essentials) or $2 (Pro and Premium) per user

What it is

When I Work is the low-cost scheduler: unlimited users at $2.50 a head on Essentials, with auto-scheduling, templates, open shifts, swaps, availability and team messaging, and labour forecasting and custom rules on Pro. Time and attendance is a separate add-on at $1.50 or $2 a user, which is how most buyers end up paying $4 to $10. It runs on web and mobile and integrates with payroll and point-of-sale systems.

Why it is on this list

When I Work is second because it does the scheduling job for unlimited users at $2.50: auto-scheduling, templates, open shifts, swaps and availability on every plan, with forecasting and custom rules on Pro. The time clock is a $1.50 to $2 add-on, which is the honest price to compare.

Where it is strong

  • Cheapest unlimited scheduling at $2.50
  • Auto-scheduling, swaps and open shifts on every plan
  • Forecasting and custom rules on Pro
  • Payroll and POS integrations

Where it falls short

  • Time clock is an add-on, so the real price is $4 to $10
  • Compliance rules thinner than Deputy's
  • No free plan

Humanity (TCP Software)

Enterprise demand-driven scheduling
Runs on Web, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Quote only; enterprise demand-driven scheduling sold by TCP Software, typically for organisations of several hundred staff and up

What it is

Humanity, now part of TCP Software, is enterprise shift scheduling for healthcare, retail and other large hourly operations: demand-driven schedules against patient or sales volume, credential and skill rules, compliance checks, a time clock and TCP's hardware clocks, and integrations with the enterprise HCM and payroll systems. It is quote-priced and sold to organisations with hundreds or thousands of shift workers.

Why it is on this list

Humanity, from TCP Software, is third for the enterprise end: hospitals, retail chains and multi-site operations that schedule hundreds or thousands of people against patient volume or sales, with credential rules, compliance checks and integrations into the HCM and payroll systems. It is quote-priced and sold through an enterprise process, and nothing on this list replaces it at that scale.

Where it is strong

  • Demand-driven scheduling at enterprise scale
  • Credential and skill rules for healthcare
  • TCP hardware clocks and HCM integrations
  • Compliance for complex labour rules

Where it falls short

  • Quote only; enterprise sales cycle
  • Overkill below a few hundred staff
  • Implementation effort

7shifts

Schedules against POS sales forecasts
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, POS integrations Pricing (October 2026) Comp free for one location and 30 employees; Entrée $29.99 and The Works $69.99 per location per month on annual billing ($34.99 and $76.99 monthly); Gourmet by quote

What it is

7shifts is scheduling built for restaurants: labour-cost-aware schedules against sales forecasts, a time clock with break and overtime compliance, tip pooling, a manager log book, task lists and payroll integrations, priced per location. The free Comp plan covers one location with up to 30 employees. It integrates with the major restaurant POS systems, which is where its forecasts come from.

Why it is on this list

7shifts is here for restaurants, where its forecasts come from the POS, its schedules show labour cost against projected sales, and tip pooling and the manager log sit alongside. Per location, free for one with 30 employees.

Where it is strong

  • Schedules against sales forecasts from the POS
  • Tip pooling and manager log
  • Break and overtime compliance
  • Free for one location with 30 employees

Where it falls short

  • Restaurant-specific; little use elsewhere
  • Per-location pricing
  • Payroll only in some states

Clockify

Scheduling and forecasting at $7.99
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for 5 users; Standard $5.49 (attendance, overtime, time off) and Pro $7.99 (scheduling, GPS) per seat per month on annual billing ($6.99 and $9.99 monthly)

What it is

Clockify's attendance and scheduling live on its Standard and Pro plans: attendance and overtime, time off and approvals at $5.49, scheduling, GPS and a kiosk at $7.99, on top of a time tracker that is free for five users. It is the budget route to attendance plus scheduling for a team that also tracks project time.

Why it is on this list

Clockify earns a place for teams that track project time and also run shifts: scheduling, forecasting and GPS arrive on Pro at $7.99, over the free-for-five tracker.

Where it is strong

  • Attendance and scheduling on a budget
  • Free for five users
  • Kiosk and GPS on Pro
  • Project time in the same tool

Where it falls short

  • Scheduling only on Pro
  • No payroll
  • Dense interface

Connecteam

Scheduling plus clock, forms and chat; free for ten
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Small Business plan free for up to 10 users; Operations hub Basic $29, Advanced $49, Expert $99 per month for the first 30 users, then $0.80, $2.50 and $4.20 per additional user

What it is

Connecteam is an all-in-one app for deskless teams: scheduling, a GPS time clock, forms and checklists, task management, chat, knowledge base, training and HR documents, sold as three hubs with plans priced for the first 30 users and a small per-user charge after. The Small Business plan is free for up to ten users with everything included, which makes it the most generous free tier in this category.

Why it is on this list

Connecteam is on the list for deskless teams that want the schedule inside the app that also holds the clock, forms, tasks and chat; free for ten users and priced for the first 30 after.

Where it is strong

  • Free for ten users with everything
  • Scheduling, GPS clock, forms, chat and training in one app
  • Priced for the first 30 users, cheap after
  • Built for deskless teams

Where it falls short

  • Hubs are priced separately; buying all three adds up
  • Per-user charges above 30 users on each hub
  • Breadth over depth in scheduling

Homebase

Scheduling with an AI assistant, per location
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, POS kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Basic free for one location and 10 employees; Essentials $30, Plus $70, All-in-One $120 per location per month; payroll $49 plus $6 per employee

What it is

Homebase is built for small hourly businesses, restaurants, retail and services, and prices per location rather than per person: scheduling, a time clock that runs on a tablet or the POS, team messaging, hiring and, on higher plans, an AI scheduling assistant, PTO controls, labour cost management and HR tools. Payroll is an add-on. The free plan covers one location with up to ten employees, which is a real product for a single shop.

Why it is on this list

Homebase is here for small shops and restaurants that want scheduling with an AI assistant, a time clock on the POS, and hiring and payroll in the same app, priced per location and free for one.

Where it is strong

  • Free for a single shop with ten employees
  • Per-location pricing suits one or two sites
  • Scheduling, time clock, hiring and payroll in one app
  • Runs on the POS

Where it falls short

  • Per-location pricing gets expensive with many sites
  • Payroll is $49 plus $6 a head
  • Not for salaried or office teams

QuickBooks Time

Schedules and swaps feeding payroll
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Premium $20 base plus $10 per user; Elite $40 base plus $12 per user, per month; sold with QuickBooks Online

What it is

QuickBooks Time is the attendance system for employers whose payroll runs in QuickBooks: mobile and kiosk clock-in with GPS, scheduling, shift swaps, approvals, break tracking and, on Elite, geofencing, mileage and timesheet signatures, with hours flowing into payroll directly. The base fee plus per-user pricing is expensive for small teams.

Why it is on this list

QuickBooks Time is on the list for QuickBooks payroll customers: schedules, swaps and notifications with the hours flowing into payroll, at a base fee plus per user.

Where it is strong

  • Hours straight into QuickBooks payroll
  • GPS, geofencing, kiosk and schedules
  • Timesheet signatures
  • Mature mobile app

Where it falls short

  • Base fee plus per user
  • Tied to QuickBooks
  • Not for project teams

Sling

Free scheduling for 30 users
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 30 users; Premium $1.70 and Business $3.40 per user per month on annual billing ($2 and $4 monthly)

What it is

Sling is the simplest scheduler here and the cheapest: free for up to 30 users with shift scheduling, availability, time off, open shifts and messaging, then $1.70 a user for a mobile time clock, labour cost and overtime tracking, and $3.40 for a kiosk, reporting, no-show tracking and PTO approvals. It bills only for active users and suits small hourly teams that want a schedule everyone can see.

Why it is on this list

Sling is here as the free scheduler: 30 users, shifts, availability, time off, open shifts and messaging at no cost, with a clock and labour cost from $1.70. It has no auto-scheduling or forecasting.

Where it is strong

  • Free for 30 users
  • The cheapest paid scheduler here
  • Bills only active users
  • Simple enough for any team

Where it falls short

  • No auto-scheduling or forecasting
  • No payroll
  • Reporting is basic

Time Champ

Shift patterns, time zones and automatic attendance
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux; iOS and Android for GPS Pricing (October 2026) Starter $3.90, Professional $6.90, Enterprise $13.90 per user per month on annual billing; HR module as an add-on

What it is

Time Champ records attendance automatically from the desktop agent, no clock-in required: login and logout, late starts against the shift, idle and break time, with multiple shift patterns, time zones and GPS check-ins from the mobile app, and productivity data from the same agent. It suits office and remote teams rather than shop floors, and is the cheapest full plan here at $3.90.

Why it is on this list

Time Champ closes the list for office and remote teams with shift patterns rather than rotas to fill: multiple shift configurations, per-user time zones, late reports and attendance recorded automatically, with productivity data alongside. It has no swap workflow or kiosk and does not suit a shop floor.

Where it is strong

  • Attendance with no clock-in action
  • Shifts, time zones and late reports
  • Productivity data from the same agent
  • Cheapest full plan here

Where it falls short

  • No kiosk or shift-swap workflow
  • Office and remote teams only
  • Short history below Enterprise

How schedules get built

Templates and hand-building (Sling, Connecteam, Clockify, Time Champ): a manager copies last week and adjusts. Fine for stable teams under about thirty. Auto-scheduling from availability and rules (When I Work, Deputy, Homebase's AI assistant): the tool fills required shifts from who is available, qualified and under their hours cap. Saves hours a week from about thirty people up. Demand-driven (Deputy, 7shifts, Humanity, When I Work Pro forecasting): required coverage is derived from forecast sales, footfall or patient volume, and the schedule is built to hit a labour percentage. This is where scheduling software pays for itself, and where the data quality of the POS or the census feed decides whether it works. The capacity planning guide covers the same logic for salaried teams.

Scheduling law

Predictive scheduling (fair workweek) laws in Oregon and in cities including New York, Chicago, Seattle, San Francisco, Philadelphia and Los Angeles require advance notice of schedules (typically 14 days), premium pay for changes, rest between shifts and offers of extra hours to existing staff before new hires, for covered retail, food service and hospitality employers. Deputy encodes these rules; Homebase and 7shifts flag some; the simpler tools leave them to the manager. Beyond that, meal and rest break rules and daily overtime in several states apply to every published schedule, and minors' hours rules apply to anyone under 18. The split shift and shift differential pages cover two pay rules that schedules trigger.

Getting the team to use it

  • Publish on a fixed day, in the app, with notifications, and stop the paper or group-chat copy that undermines it.
  • Let people trade. Swaps and open shifts with manager approval cut the no-shows that come from unwanted shifts; every tool here except Time Champ, Buddy Punch and uAttend supports them.
  • Collect availability in the tool, not by text, and respect it in the schedule or people stop updating it.
  • Measure adherence against the clock and talk about patterns, not incidents; the no call no show and attendance policy pages cover the policy side.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Disclosure. I have done work for Time Champ. It appears here because it belongs in this category, it is held to the same criteria as every other product, its drawbacks are listed like everyone else's, and it bought no placement. There are no affiliate or vendor links on this page.

Key takeaways

  • Scheduling tools range from a shared rota to demand-driven systems; decide which end you are at first.
  • Deputy for demand and compliance, When I Work for cheap scheduling at any headcount, Humanity for enterprise scale; Sling and Connecteam are free for small teams; 7shifts for restaurants.
  • Auto-scheduling pays from about thirty people; demand-driven scheduling pays when the POS or census data is good.
  • Fair workweek laws in Oregon and several cities require advance notice and premium pay for changes; only some tools encode them.
  • Publish in the app, allow swaps, collect availability in the tool, and measure adherence as patterns.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best workforce scheduling software?

Deputy for hourly operations that schedule to demand forecasts and need labour-law compliance built in; When I Work for auto-scheduling at the lowest price for unlimited users; Humanity (TCP) for enterprise demand-driven scheduling in healthcare and large retail. 7shifts for restaurants, Homebase for small shops, Connecteam for deskless teams, Sling for a free rota, and Time Champ for office and remote shift patterns.

Is there free workforce scheduling software?

Sling is free for up to 30 users with shifts, availability, time off, open shifts and messaging. Connecteam is free for up to ten users with its whole app. Homebase is free for one location with ten employees and 7shifts for one location with 30. The free tiers lack auto-scheduling and forecasting.

What is demand-driven scheduling?

Scheduling in which required staffing is derived from forecast demand (sales, footfall, patient volume) and the schedule is built to meet it within a labour cost or percentage target. Deputy, 7shifts, Humanity and When I Work Pro offer it; it needs reliable POS or census data to work.

What are predictive scheduling laws?

Fair workweek laws in Oregon and cities including New York, Chicago, Seattle, San Francisco, Philadelphia and Los Angeles that require covered retail, food service and hospitality employers to give advance notice of schedules (often 14 days), pay premiums for late changes, allow rest between shifts and offer extra hours to existing staff first. Deputy encodes the rules; most other tools leave compliance to the manager.

How much does scheduling software cost?

Free for small teams on Sling, Connecteam, Homebase and 7shifts; $1.70 to $5 per user on Sling, When I Work and Time Champ; $5 to $10 on Deputy and Clockify Pro; $30 to $120 per location on Homebase and $29.99 to $69.99 on 7shifts; QuickBooks Time adds a base fee; Humanity is quoted. When I Work's time clock is an extra $1.50 to $2.

Should scheduling and time tracking be in the same tool?

Usually, because adherence (what happened against what was scheduled) is the most useful report and it needs both. Deputy, Homebase, 7shifts, Connecteam, Clockify, QuickBooks Time and Sling include both; When I Work sells the clock as an add-on; Time Champ records attendance automatically against the shift.
