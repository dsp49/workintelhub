Clockify Alternatives (2026): 11 Tools After the Free Plan Cut

P Pavan
October 21, 2026 · 15 min read

A Clockify alternative is a time tracker that gives you back whatever Clockify took away or never had. For most people searching in 2026 that is the free plan: on April 21, 2026 Clockify's owner cut it to five users, 31-day reports and PDF-only export. TimeCamp is free for unlimited users with CSV export, Jibble is a free time clock for unlimited users, and Kimai is free because you host it. For paying teams the complaint is tier-shopping, approvals on Standard, budgets on Pro, SSO on Enterprise, and Everhour, My Hours and Harvest sell one plan with the lot. For teams that outgrew a tracker, Toggl Track, Paymo and QuickBooks Time are the exits.

The pages that rank for this search are written by Homebase, Time Champ, Time Doctor and My Hours, each ranking its own product first, and two of them predate the April change they do not mention. This guide gives the reasons with dated quotes, October 2026 pricing for eleven tools with free-plan limits and the tier that carries each feature, and an export plan that works around the PDF-only free plan. There are no vendor links and nothing paid for its place.

The short answer

If the April 2026 free-plan cut is the problem, TimeCamp is free for unlimited users with CSV export, Jibble is free for unlimited users as a time clock, and Kimai is free forever because you host it.

If you are paying for features spread across four tiers, Everhour and My Hours put approvals, budgets and invoicing in one paid plan, and Harvest puts invoicing on every plan including free.

If you have outgrown a tracker, Toggl Track gives you adoption and profitability reports, Paymo gives you project management with billing, and QuickBooks Time or Jibble give hourly crews a real time clock with payroll.

What Clockify costs and does in October 2026

Clockify

The incumbent
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for 5 users with 31-day reports and PDF-only export; Basic $3.99, Standard $5.49, Pro $7.99, Enterprise $11.99 per seat per month billed annually ($4.99, $6.99, $9.99, $14.99 monthly); CAKE.com bundle with Pumble and Plaky $12.99; kiosk-only seats from $0.79; 7-day Pro trial; canceling drops the workspace to Free with data kept Monitoring Opt-in activity monitoring on Pro; no screenshots

What it is

Clockify is a timer and timesheet with modules added by tier. Basic brings billable rates, kiosk, breaks, time audit and imports. Standard brings approvals, time off, invoicing, attendance and overtime, targets and the QuickBooks integration. Pro brings budgets, scheduling, expenses, GPS, labor cost, custom fields and an opt-in activity monitoring feature labeled new in October 2026. Enterprise brings SSO, SCIM and an audit log. On April 21, 2026 its owner CAKE.com cut the free plan to five active users, 31-day reports, PDF-only exports and no billable rates or kiosk; existing paid plans were untouched. Every invited user takes a paid seat.

Better than Clockify

  • The cheapest paid ladder of any full tracker: approvals and invoicing at $5.49
  • Attendance, overtime, kiosk and GPS alongside project time
  • Imports Toggl, Harvest and ClickUp CSVs with a published guide

Where Clockify is still better

  • The free plan is now a trial in all but name: 31-day reports and no CSV export
  • Features are spread across four tiers, so the plan you need is often one up
  • Trustpilot 2.9 of 5 over 85 reviews, most from after the April change

Who should switch to it

Teams that want the most features for the least money and can live with a dense interface. Our time tracking software review places it second for value.

Why people leave Clockify

Clockify's review-site scores are high and mostly predate April 2026; the recent complaints are on Trustpilot and in its own help forum. Each reason below points at a replacement.

The free plan was cut without warning

On April 21, 2026 the free plan lost CSV export, billable rates, the kiosk, time off and reports longer than a month, and was capped at five users. Clockify says an in-app notice went out a month earlier; many users say they saw nothing until features disappeared.

without warning or notice, the terms were changed overnight and nearly all the functions I had were blocked behind a paywallTrustpilot, three stars, August 14, 2026

Fixed by: TimeCamp (free, unlimited users, CSV export), Jibble (free time clock), Kimai (self-hosted).

Years of reports out of reach

Reports on the free plan now cover 31 days at a time and export only to PDF, so a freelancer's ten-year history is still stored but cannot be pulled out without a paid plan or trial.

Years and years of data lost - around 10 years.Trustpilot, one star, June 16, 2026 (the data is retained; the report window is the issue)

Fixed by: export on a paid trial first, then move to Toggl Track, Harvest or My Hours, which keep full history on paid plans.

Paying across four tiers

Approvals are on Standard, budgets and scheduling on Pro, SSO on Enterprise. A team that needs approvals and budgets pays $7.99, and every invited user is a seat, including managers who only approve.

Randomly changed the price of the offering from free to 300+ Euro a year.Trustpilot, one star, October 9, 2026

Fixed by: one-tier tools: Everhour ($8.50 with budgets, invoicing, approvals), My Hours ($8 with approvals), Harvest Teams ($9).

A dense interface

Clockify has more settings than most teams use, and reviewers describe the member and project screens as slow and occasionally hanging as workspaces grow.

the software is now so buggy, it's always hanging when I try to change member detailsTrustpilot, one star, October 17, 2026

Fixed by: Toggl Track and My Hours, which do less and are adopted faster.

Forgotten timers

Like every timer, Clockify records what you remember to start and stop. Its auto tracker helps on desktop, but the common failure is a timer left running overnight.

Fixed by: automatic capture: Timely drafts the timesheet privately; TimeCamp's AI tracker does the same.

You need a real time clock or project tool

Clockify's kiosk, GPS and scheduling are modules on a project tracker. Hourly crews outgrow them, and agencies outgrow its project features.

Fixed by: Jibble or QuickBooks Time for crews; Paymo for agencies.

The alternatives in detail

TimeCamp

Free for unlimited users

What it is

TimeCamp is the closest thing to what Clockify's free plan used to be: no user cap, automatic tracking, idle detection and a browser plugin for 80 or more tools. Starter adds invoicing and attendance, Premium adds budgets and app tracking, Ultimate adds approvals, screenshots and labor costs. Its marketplace lists a Clockify importer.

Toggl Track

The tracker people keep using

What it is

Toggl Track is simpler than Clockify and more expensive: a one-click timer, a private timeline, billable rates on Starter, approvals and profitability on Premium, no monitoring by policy. Who should switch to it

Teams that need people to track consistently more than they need modules.

Switching

How to get your data out of Clockify

  1. Start a paid trial before you export. The free plan exports PDF only and 31 days at a time. The 7-day Pro trial, or one month of Basic at $4.99, restores CSV and Excel export and full date ranges.
  2. Export the Detailed report with the longest date range and all columns, as CSV. Export projects, clients and users from Workspace settings.
  3. Check the import format of the new tool. TimeCamp lists a Clockify importer; Toggl, Harvest and My Hours take CSV; Everhour, Timely and Jibble start fresh.
  4. Downgrade rather than delete. Clockify keeps data when a workspace drops to Free, so an old report can still be reached later by starting another trial.
  5. Reconcile one month of totals by project before you tell the team to stop using Clockify.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Clockify?

TimeCamp if the free-plan cut is why you are leaving: free for unlimited users with CSV export and invoicing at $3.99. Toggl Track if people stopped tracking. Harvest if invoicing is the job. Everhour if you want one paid plan inside your project tool. Jibble if you need a time clock. The chooser above ranks them.

Is Clockify still free?

Yes, with limits set on April 21, 2026: five active users including the owner, reports covering 31 days at a time, PDF export only, no billable rates, kiosk or time off. Unlimited time entries and projects remain free. Paid plans start at $3.99 per seat per month billed annually.

What changed in Clockify's free plan in 2026?

CAKE.com announced the changes on April 21, 2026. The free plan dropped CSV and Excel export, billable rates and amounts earned, the kiosk, time off and shared reports, capped reports at a 31-day range, limited the API to 30 requests an hour and three webhooks, and capped users at five. Existing paid plans were unaffected.

Is there a free alternative to Clockify with unlimited users?

TimeCamp's free plan has no user cap and includes timers, timesheets, a calendar, automatic tracking and CSV export. Jibble gives unlimited users a time clock with GPS stamps and a kiosk at no charge. Kimai is free and self-hosted with no limit. Everhour and My Hours are free for five users; Toggl Track for three.

Is there an open-source alternative to Clockify?

Kimai, licensed under the AGPL 3, with customers, projects, budgets, invoice templates, SAML and LDAP, two-factor authentication and a JSON API. You host it yourself with Docker or buy the hosted cloud version from kimai.org.

How do I export my data from Clockify on the free plan?

The free plan exports PDF only, 31 days at a time. Start the 7-day Pro trial or one month of Basic, which restores CSV and Excel export and full date ranges, export the Detailed report with all columns, then downgrade. Data is kept when a workspace returns to Free.

Does Clockify take screenshots?

No. Clockify's pricing page lists an opt-in activity monitoring feature on the Pro plan and does not list screenshots on any tier. Of the alternatives here, Hubstaff takes optional screenshots and TimeCamp offers them on its Ultimate plan; the rest take none.

Which alternative imports Clockify data?

TimeCamp lists a Clockify importer on its marketplace. Toggl Track, Harvest, My Hours and Kimai import CSV time entries. Everhour, Timely, Paymo and Jibble start fresh, so keep Clockify's exports for closed periods.
