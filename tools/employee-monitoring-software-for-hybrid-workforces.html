Best Employee Monitoring Software for Hybrid Teams (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 16 min read

Hybrid work creates a monitoring question that neither the office nor the remote tools were built for: the same person is in two settings each week, and the manager wants to know whether the office days are doing what they are supposed to, whether the remote days produce focus or overwork, whether required office days are actually happening, and whether the two groups are being measured fairly. Most monitoring tools report on people. The hybrid question is about days and places, and only some tools can answer it.

I reviewed eight tools for that question, with pricing checked in October 2026. The three that lead can distinguish office from remote days and report on each; the other five fit particular hybrid situations, from field teams to regulated data. The main review covers the full field and the hybrid work schedule guide covers the policy the tool is measuring.

The tools in detail

ActivTrak

Schedule adherence and location-aware analytics without keystrokes
Runs on Windows, macOS, Chromebook Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 3 people (30-day retention); Work Activity Tracking $10, Workforce Management $15, Productivity Optimization $17, Workflow $21 per user per month, billed annually

What it is

Productivity analytics from application, website and activity data, with optional screenshots and no keystroke logging; free for three people.

Why it is on this list

ActivTrak leads the hybrid list because the hybrid manager's questions are about patterns rather than people: are office days actually collaborative, do remote days produce the focus they are supposed to, is anyone's week lopsided. Its schedule adherence, focus and workload balance reports answer those by day and by location without screenshots, and the location insights distinguish office from remote automatically. The hybrid-specific reports live on the Workforce Management plan at $15, not the $10 entry.

Where it is strong

  • A free plan for three people, which is the best way to see the data before buying
  • Productivity, utilisation and focus analytics that managers can act on without reading screenshots
  • No keystroke logging and optional screenshots: the least invasive serious tool here
  • Workforce planning, capacity and coaching features on the upper plans

Where it falls short

  • No Linux agent and no keystroke logging, so it cannot serve investigations
  • The useful features sit on the $15 and $17 plans; the $10 plan is thin
  • Data retention of 6 to 12 months unless you pay for more

Time Doctor

The only Office versus Remote report in the category
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, Android Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.70, Standard $11.70, Premium $16.70 per user per month billed annually ($8, $14, $20 monthly); Enterprise by quote; 14-day trial

What it is

Remote-team time tracking with screenshots, web and app use, payroll, work-life balance reporting and an Office versus Remote comparison.

Why it is on this list

Time Doctor is second for one feature nobody else has: an Office versus Remote report that compares the two settings directly, on hours, activity and application use. For a company deciding how many office days to require, that is the evidence, and it comes with the work-life balance reporting that catches the remote-day overwork hybrid arrangements tend to produce. The useful plan is Standard at $11.70.

Where it is strong

  • Built for remote and outsourced teams, with payroll and 60-plus integrations
  • Work-life balance and Office versus Remote reporting
  • Screen video and two years of history on Premium
  • Chromebook and Android agents alongside the desktop platforms

Where it falls short

  • Integrations and payroll start at the $11.70 Standard plan
  • Screenshots and activity scoring draw the usual objections from staff
  • Interface shows its age in places

Work location insights with long retention
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Workforce Analytics $8, Workflow Optimization $12 (beta), both together $16 per seat per month billed annually; Enterprise by quote; screen recording and on-demand screenshots $4 add-ons; 7-day trial

What it is

Workforce analytics with utilisation, capacity and work location insights, 24 months of history, and screen recording as an optional add-on.

Why it is on this list

Insightful is third for hybrid knowledge teams because its work location insights were built for exactly this question and because its utilisation and capacity reporting runs on 24 months of history, long enough to see whether a hybrid policy changed anything. Screen recording is an add-on you can leave off; the Linux agent covers engineering teams that split their week.

Where it is strong

  • Capacity and utilisation reporting, with screen recording as an add-on rather than a default

CurrentWare

Web and device control on and off the network
Runs on Windows, macOS Pricing (October 2026) BrowseReporter $12, BrowseControl $6, AccessPatrol $12, enPowerManager $6 per user per month; full suite $20; billed annually with a minimum of about $1,440 a year; 14-day trial

What it is

A modular suite that reports on web and application use and also blocks sites, applications and USB devices, on your server or theirs.

Why it is on this list

CurrentWare is here for the hybrid office whose monitoring is an IT policy question: the same web filtering, application blocking and USB control should apply at home as at the desk, and CurrentWare enforces it on the device wherever the device is. The reporting is adequate; the control is the point.

Where it is strong

  • Web and application blocking, USB control and reporting in one suite
  • Your own server or their cloud; data stays where you put it
  • Modules can be bought separately
  • Strong fit for IT-led policy enforcement

Where it falls short

  • Annual billing with a minimum around $1,440 a year
  • Productivity analytics are simpler than the specialists'
  • Suite pricing adds up if you only need reporting

Shift scheduling, absence calendar and tracking in one

What it is

An automatic time tracker with productivity scoring, a private time switch, shift scheduling and an absence calendar; screenshots only on Premium.

Why it is on this list

DeskTime fits small hybrid teams that need to know who is in the office on which days, which it handles with shift scheduling and an absence calendar alongside automatic tracking, at the lowest price here. Teams that want screenshots pay for Premium; most hybrid teams should not want them.

Where it is strong

  • Automatic tracking with a private time toggle employees can use
  • Shift scheduling and absence calendar built in
  • Low price with a free single-user plan
  • Windows, Mac, Linux and mobile apps

Where it falls short

  • Screenshots only on Premium
  • Productivity scoring is simple and needs tuning per role
  • Reporting is lighter for large organisations

Hubstaff

Time and payment wherever the day is worked
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10, Enterprise $25 per seat per month billed annually ($7, $9 and $12 monthly); 2-seat minimum; 14-day trial

What it is

A time tracker for distributed teams with screenshots, activity levels, GPS, payroll and invoicing, on every desktop and mobile platform.

Why it is on this list

Hubstaff is on the hybrid list for teams paid by the hour whose days split between locations: the time record follows the person, GPS and job sites cover the days in the field, and payroll runs from the result. It does not analyse office versus remote patterns; it records the hours wherever they happen.

Where it is strong

  • Time tracking, screenshots, GPS, payroll and invoicing in one product
  • Runs everywhere, including Linux and Chromebook, with mobile apps
  • Over 2,300 verified reviews and a long track record with distributed teams
  • Clear per-seat pricing with a cheap entry plan

Where it falls short

  • Activity percentages reward mouse movement, not work, and people know it
  • Two-seat minimum and add-ons on the lower plans
  • Monitoring depth is modest by design; not a security tool

Teramind

Security capture that follows the device home
Runs on Windows (full); macOS and Linux with reduced features Pricing (October 2026) Starter $14, UAM $28, DLP $32 per seat per month billed annually ($15, $30, $35 monthly); 5-seat minimum; Enterprise by quote

What it is

A security-grade monitoring and data loss prevention platform: screen video, keystrokes, OCR, behaviour rules that block, cloud or on-premise.

Why it is on this list

Teramind is here for hybrid teams handling regulated data, where the security controls that applied on the office network have to follow the laptop home. Its agent and DLP rules work off-network and its stealth and revealed modes let policy differ by role. It is a security purchase, not a productivity one.

Where it is strong

  • The deepest capture in the category: screen video, keystrokes, OCR, files, email, chat
  • Behaviour rules that can block or lock, not just report
  • DLP, private cloud and on-premise for regulated environments
  • Revealed, standard and stealth agents for different policies

Where it falls short

  • Five-seat minimum and the highest entry price here
  • Mac and Linux agents lack many Windows features
  • Capable of collecting far more than most employers should; needs policy discipline

Office versus remote: how the tools tell the difference

Three methods are in use. Network detection: the agent notes whether the device is on the office network or VPN and labels the day accordingly; Insightful's work location insights and ActivTrak's location reporting work this way. Attendance and GPS: the person is marked present by a desktop clock-in or a mobile check-in at a geofenced site; Time Champ and Hubstaff do this, and it works for field days as well. Declared schedule: the manager sets which days are office days and the tool reports adherence; DeskTime's shift scheduling and ActivTrak's schedule adherence use this. Time Doctor's Office versus Remote report combines detection with a comparison of the two groups' hours, activity and application use, which is the analysis a hybrid policy review actually needs. The tools that do none of these, including most of the security products, can monitor a hybrid team but cannot tell you anything about hybrid work.

Attendance, badges and required office days

Companies that require specific office days usually want to know whether they are happening, and badge data is the traditional answer. Monitoring tools offer a cheaper one: the device's location tells you where the work was done, and Time Champ's automatic attendance, DeskTime's shift calendar and ActivTrak's schedule adherence turn it into a report without hardware. Two cautions. Presence in the office is not work, and a report that rewards it will get it. And attendance data is personal data under the same rules as everything else here; the attendance tracking guide covers the policy side and the return to office glossary entry the context.

Fairness between the two groups

The hybrid-specific risk is that remote days are measured more closely than office days, because the agent sees everything at home and nothing in the meeting room, so the remote worker looks less active than the colleague who spent the afternoon in a conference room the tool could not see. ActivTrak's offline meeting time and Insightful's location-aware baselines partly correct for it; the rest is policy. Measure the same things in both settings, treat in-person time as work the tool cannot see rather than as idle, compare people to their own pattern rather than to each other, and never let a location-skewed activity score drive a decision on its own. The privacy line and the state law pages apply to both halves of the week equally.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Key takeaways

  • The hybrid question is about days and places, not people; only tools that distinguish office from remote days can answer it.
  • ActivTrak for workload and schedule adherence, Time Doctor for the Office versus Remote comparison, Insightful for location-aware utilisation.
  • Location is detected by network, by attendance or GPS check-in, or by a declared schedule; each has a cost and a blind spot.
  • Presence is not work; attendance reports that reward it will get it.
  • Remote days are seen more closely than office days; measure the same things in both and compare people to their own pattern.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best employee monitoring software for a hybrid workforce?

ActivTrak for hybrid teams managed on workload and schedule adherence, with location-aware reports and no keystroke logging; Time Doctor for the only direct Office versus Remote comparison in the category; Insightful for hybrid knowledge teams that want utilisation and location insights over two years of history. Time Champ is the value choice where attendance by location is the main need.

How does monitoring software know whether someone is in the office?

By network detection (the device is on the office network or VPN), by attendance or GPS check-in (desktop clock-in or a mobile check-in at a geofenced site), or by a declared schedule that the tool reports adherence against. Insightful and ActivTrak use detection, Time Champ and Hubstaff use attendance and GPS, DeskTime uses schedules, and Time Doctor combines detection with a comparison report.

Can monitoring software replace badge readers for office attendance?

For knowledge workers with company laptops, largely yes: device location gives a daily record without hardware, and Time Champ, DeskTime and ActivTrak report it. It does not cover visitors, people without devices or security access control, and it is personal data subject to the same notice rules as other monitoring.

Is it fair to monitor remote days more than office days?

No, and it happens by default: the agent sees everything at home and nothing in a meeting room. Measure the same things in both settings, treat offline in-person time as work the tool cannot see, compare people to their own baseline, and do not let location-skewed activity scores drive decisions alone. ActivTrak's offline meeting time and Insightful's location baselines partly correct the skew.

Which monitoring tools report office versus remote productivity?

Time Doctor has a dedicated Office versus Remote report. Insightful's work location insights and ActivTrak's location reporting break utilisation and focus down by where the day was worked. The other tools on this page record the work but do not compare the settings.

Do hybrid employees need to be told about monitoring at home?

Yes. The notice laws in New York, Connecticut and Delaware apply wherever the employee works, and monitoring that follows a company device home should be described in the policy as such. Personal devices need consent and a work profile. The monitoring policy template and the remote work policy on this site cover both halves of the week.
