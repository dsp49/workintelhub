Best Employee Attendance Tracking Software: 12 Tools (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 18 min read

Attendance tracking software records who was at work, when they arrived and left, and when they took breaks, and turns that into hours for payroll and a record for the file. The twelve tools here do it four different ways: a phone with GPS, a shared kiosk, a clock on the wall, or an agent on the desk that records attendance without any clock-in at all. They differ most in how much labour-law compliance they build in, whether payroll is native or exported, and whether the price is per user, per location, tiered by headcount or a base fee plus a per-user charge.

I reviewed twelve with pricing checked in October 2026. The first three are starting points for the three most common buyers; the rest follow alphabetically. The attendance tracking guide covers the policy and the records the law requires; this page covers the tools.

The tools in detail

Jibble

Free for unlimited users with kiosk, GPS and overtime rules
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, kiosk, Slack, Teams Pricing (October 2026) Free with unlimited users including GPS, timesheets and a facial recognition kiosk; Premium from about $4.49 and Ultimate from about $7.99 per user per month; the vendor publishes no fixed table

What it is

Jibble is free attendance for unlimited users: clock in from phone, web, a facial recognition kiosk, Slack or Teams, with GPS, timesheets, overtime rules and approvals, and paid tiers for geofencing, live location and payroll exports at prices the vendor does not publish as a fixed table. For a business whose whole requirement is who was here and when, it costs nothing.

Why it is on this list

Jibble leads attendance tracking because the free plan is the whole product for most small and mid-sized employers: unlimited users clocking in from phone, web, Slack, Teams or a tablet kiosk with face recognition, GPS on every punch, timesheets, overtime rules and approvals, at no cost. The paid tiers add geofencing and payroll exports at prices the vendor does not publish, and the face kiosk needs Illinois- and Texas-style biometric notices.

Where it is strong

  • Free for unlimited users
  • Facial recognition kiosk, GPS, Slack and Teams clock-in
  • Overtime rules and approvals
  • Fast to deploy

Where it falls short

  • Paid prices unpublished
  • Biometric kiosk triggers Illinois and Texas rules
  • Scheduling is lighter than the specialists'

Deputy

Attendance with compliance rules built in
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Lite $5, Core $6.50, Pro $9 per user per month (10% less on annual billing); $30 monthly minimum; payroll add-on $8 per user plus $49; HR $2

What it is

Deputy is the scheduling and attendance platform for hourly workforces that take labour law seriously. It builds schedules automatically from demand forecasts and availability, enforces break, overtime and predictive-scheduling rules by jurisdiction, runs a time clock with photo capture and geolocation on phones and kiosks, and exports or runs payroll. It is priced per user with a $30 monthly minimum and sells HR and payroll as add-ons.

Why it is on this list

Deputy is second because attendance for an hourly workforce is a compliance problem as much as a record-keeping one, and Deputy encodes break, overtime and predictive-scheduling rules by jurisdiction, flags violations as they happen, and keeps the audit trail. Its clock takes a photo and a location. The $30 monthly minimum and the add-ons make it a mid-sized employer's tool.

Where it is strong

  • Auto-scheduling from demand forecasts
  • Break, overtime and predictive-scheduling compliance by jurisdiction
  • Time clock with photo and location on phones and kiosks
  • Payroll and HR add-ons

Where it falls short

  • $30 monthly minimum hurts very small teams
  • Payroll and HR are paid add-ons
  • More than a simple shop needs

Buddy Punch

GPS, geofencing and photo punches, simply
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Pro $6.99, Advanced $12.99 per user per month on annual billing ($5.99, $7.99, $13.99 monthly), each plus a $19 monthly base fee; payroll $8 per user plus $49

What it is

Buddy Punch is a straightforward time clock: punches from phone, web or kiosk with GPS, geofencing, optional webcam photos and QR codes, PTO tracking, break compliance and payroll integrations, with scheduling included on Pro. Pricing is per user plus a $19 monthly base fee, and payroll is an add-on. It suits small and mid-sized employers who want attendance without a scheduling platform wrapped around it.

Why it is on this list

Buddy Punch is third for the employer who wants a time clock and nothing wrapped around it: GPS and geofenced punches, webcam photos, QR codes, PTO tracking, break compliance and payroll integrations, with scheduling available on Pro if wanted. The $19 base fee is the only awkward part of the pricing.

Where it is strong

  • GPS, geofencing and photo punches on the entry plan
  • Break compliance and PTO
  • Scheduling included on Pro
  • Simple to run

Where it falls short

  • $19 base fee on every plan
  • No forecasting or auto-scheduling
  • Scheduling only from Pro

What it is

Clockify's attendance and scheduling live on its Standard and Pro plans: attendance and overtime, time off and approvals at $5.49, scheduling, GPS and a kiosk at $7.99, on top of a time tracker that is free for five users. It is the budget route to attendance plus scheduling for a team that also tracks project time.

Why it is on this list

Clockify is here for teams that need attendance, overtime and time off alongside project time tracking: all on Standard at $5.49, with scheduling, GPS and a kiosk on Pro, over a tracker that is free for five users.

Time clock on the POS, free for one shop
Runs on Web, iOS, Android, POS kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Basic free for one location and 10 employees; Essentials $30, Plus $70, All-in-One $120 per location per month; payroll $49 plus $6 per employee

What it is

Homebase is built for small hourly businesses, restaurants, retail and services, and prices per location rather than per person: scheduling, a time clock that runs on a tablet or the POS, team messaging, hiring and, on higher plans, an AI scheduling assistant, PTO controls, labour cost management and HR tools. Payroll is an add-on. The free plan covers one location with up to ten employees, which is a real product for a single shop.

Why it is on this list

Homebase earns its place for single-site hourly businesses: the time clock runs on the POS or a tablet, schedules sit beside it, and the free plan covers one location with ten employees. Per-location pricing suits one or two sites and not twenty.

Where it is strong

The records the law requires

For non-exempt employees the Fair Labor Standards Act requires accurate daily and weekly hours, kept three years for payroll records and two for the time cards they came from; overtime is owed by workweek whatever the pay period, and rounding is lawful only where it is neutral over time. Several states require meal and rest break records, and California and a few others require daily overtime. Every tool here produces the hours; the compliance difference is whether it knows the rules (Deputy, Homebase, 7shifts, QuickBooks Time) or leaves them to the payroll system. The hours worked and overtime pages have the arithmetic, and the attendance policy template the policy the system enforces.

Biometrics and GPS

Facial recognition and fingerprint clocks are biometric systems. Illinois's Biometric Information Privacy Act requires written notice, consent and a published retention policy before capture and has produced large settlements; Texas and Washington have biometric laws; several cities add rules. A PIN, badge or photo clock avoids the obligation. The biometric time clock entry and the GPS tracking guide cover the rules for the two features that most often go wrong.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Key takeaways

  • Attendance is recorded by phone, kiosk, wall clock or automatically from the computer; choose the method before the tool.
  • Jibble is free for unlimited users; Deputy builds in compliance; Buddy Punch is the plain time clock; Time Champ records attendance with no clock-in.
  • Pricing models differ: per user, per location (Homebase, 7shifts), headcount tiers (uAttend), base fee plus per user (Buddy Punch, QuickBooks Time).
  • Keep payroll records three years and time cards two; overtime by workweek; neutral rounding only.
  • Face and fingerprint clocks are biometric systems with notice and consent obligations in several states.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best employee attendance tracking software?

Jibble for most small and mid-sized employers, because its free plan covers unlimited users with a kiosk, GPS, timesheets and overtime rules. Deputy for hourly workforces with labour-law exposure. Buddy Punch for a simple time clock with GPS and geofencing. Time Champ for office and remote teams that want attendance recorded automatically, uAttend for a physical clock, and QuickBooks Time if payroll runs in QuickBooks.

Is there free attendance tracking software?

Jibble is free for unlimited users with GPS, timesheets, overtime rules and a kiosk. TimeCamp is free for unlimited users for basic timesheets. Connecteam is free for up to ten users with its whole app. Homebase is free for one location with ten employees, Sling for 30 users on scheduling, and Clockify for five users.

How do employees clock in with attendance software?

From a phone app with GPS, from a shared tablet kiosk with a PIN, photo or face scan, from a physical wall clock with a fingerprint, badge or PIN, from Slack or Teams, or automatically when they log in to a work computer. Most tools offer several; uAttend is hardware-first and Time Champ is automatic.

Can attendance software track breaks and overtime automatically?

Yes. Deputy, Homebase, 7shifts, QuickBooks Time, Buddy Punch and Clockify apply break and overtime rules and flag violations; Jibble applies overtime rules. Deputy encodes state and city rules including predictive scheduling. Check that daily overtime is handled if you employ in California, and that break rules match your state.

Is a facial recognition time clock legal?

Generally yes with obligations: Illinois requires written notice, consent and a retention policy under its Biometric Information Privacy Act, Texas and Washington have biometric laws, and several cities add rules. A PIN, badge or photo clock avoids the issue. Jibble, uAttend and Deputy offer face recognition as an option.

How much does attendance tracking software cost?

From free (Jibble, TimeCamp, Connecteam for ten, Homebase for one shop) through $1.70 to $5 per user (Sling, Time Champ, TimeCamp Starter, When I Work with the clock add-on, Buddy Punch plus a $19 base fee) to $5 to $10 (Clockify, Deputy, QuickBooks Time plus a base fee). uAttend charges $29 to $195 a month by headcount tier plus hardware. Homebase and 7shifts charge per location.
