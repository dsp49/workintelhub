Hubstaff Alternatives (2026): 9 Tools by Why You Are Leaving

P Pavan
October 21, 2026 · 16 min read

A Hubstaff alternative is any time tracker that covers the job Hubstaff was doing for you, which is usually one of three: hours against projects, attendance for payroll, or proof of work for clients. People leave Hubstaff for four reasons that point at different replacements. If the activity score is the problem, Toggl Track, Everhour and Jibble do not score input at all. If screenshots are the problem, ActivTrak and DeskTime report applications without images on their standard plans. If the bill is the problem, Clockify and Time Champ cover attendance and overtime for less than Hubstaff's Grow plan, and Jibble is free. If you only ever needed a time clock, Jibble is one.

The pages that rank for this search are all written by vendors ranking themselves first, quoting prices without dates and never saying why anyone leaves. This guide starts with the reasons, cites Hubstaff's own documentation for how its activity level and screenshots work, gives October 2026 pricing for every tool with minimums and add-ons, and ends with a switch plan that keeps a pay period intact. There are no vendor links and nothing paid for its place.

The short answer

If the activity score is the problem, move to Toggl Track or Everhour, which record time without scoring keyboard and mouse input, or to Jibble if you need attendance and GPS without proof-of-work.

If screenshots are the problem, ActivTrak and DeskTime measure applications and websites without images on their standard plans, and Time Champ and Insightful let you switch screenshots off per team.

If the bill is the problem, Clockify Standard at $5.49 and Time Champ Starter at $3.90 cover timesheets, attendance and overtime for less than Hubstaff's $7.50 Grow plan, and Jibble is free for unlimited users.

What Hubstaff costs and does in October 2026

Hubstaff

The incumbent
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, iOS, Android, Chrome and Firefox extensions Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10, Enterprise $25 per seat per month billed annually ($7, $9 and $12 monthly); 2-seat minimum; 14-day trial without a card; 30-day money-back guarantee on paid plans Monitoring Optional screenshots

What it is

Hubstaff is a time tracker with proof of work. The desktop app records hours against projects, optional screenshots at one to three per ten minutes, an activity level calculated from the share of tracked seconds with keyboard or mouse input, application and URL use, idle time, and on the mobile app GPS location and job sites that clock people in and out. Payroll, invoicing, time off, overtime rules and scheduling sit on the higher plans, and every seat that logs in is billed except a free Project Viewer role. Screenshots can be turned off or blurred, and activity recording can be disabled for projects such as calls and meetings, which Hubstaff itself recommends.

Better than Hubstaff

  • Linux desktop agent, which most of the alternatives below lack
  • GPS job sites with automatic clock-in on the mobile app
  • Payroll runs through PayPal, Wise, Gusto and Deel from tracked hours
  • 35 or more integrations including Jira, Asana, QuickBooks and Slack

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • Every paid plan carries a 2-seat minimum and a per-seat charge for managers who only view
  • The activity level is a keyboard and mouse ratio, not a measure of work, and Hubstaff says so
  • GPS, extra screenshot frequency and Insights are sold as add-ons on lower tiers

Who should switch to it

Distributed and field teams paid by the hour that genuinely need proof of work for clients or payroll, and that have told employees what is recorded. Our employee monitoring software review places it among its peers.

The prices above were read from hubstaff.com in October 2026 for our time tracking review; on October 21, 2026 the pricing page served only a partial table to automated requests, with the Team plan at $10 confirmed in its FAQ. Check the live page before you budget.

Why people leave Hubstaff

Six reasons come up repeatedly in reviews and forums, and each points at a different replacement. Where a vendor documents the behavior itself, that document is cited rather than a review.

The activity score reads as a productivity grade

Hubstaff's activity level is the percentage of tracked time in which a key was pressed or the mouse moved. A person on a call, reading a contract or thinking scores near zero. Hubstaff's own support replies say it is not a perfect measure for people on calls and advise disabling activity recording for those projects, but managers read the number as a grade and employees know it.

Fixed by: Toggl Track, Everhour and Jibble, none of which score input.

Screenshots change how a team feels about work

Random screenshots up to three times every ten minutes are the feature that gets Hubstaff called bossware in employee forums. Blurring and per-project switches exist, but the default experience for a new hire is a timer that photographs their screen.

Fixed by: ActivTrak and DeskTime on plans without screenshots; Time Champ and Insightful with screenshots switched off per team.

Contractor pay disputes

Gig platforms that pay by Hubstaff-tracked hours have generated hundreds of forum posts from workers who lost pay to idle detection or a low activity score. If you pay contractors by tracked time, the dispute lands on you.

Fixed by: a tool with manual time entry and approvals instead of automatic deductions: Clockify, Toggl Track, Everhour.

The bill grows faster than the team

A two-seat minimum, a per-seat charge for anyone who logs in, and add-ons for GPS, extra screenshots and Insights mean the $4.99 headline is rarely the invoice. The plan with time off, overtime and scheduling is Grow at $7.50 or Team at $10.

Fixed by: Clockify Standard at $5.49, Time Champ Starter at $3.90, Jibble free for unlimited users.

Agent load and false idle time

Reviews describe the desktop agent using CPU on older machines and marking people idle during presentations or video calls, which then has to be corrected by hand.

Fixed by: lighter agents (DeskTime, Toggl Track) or tools where idle time is a prompt rather than a deduction (Clockify).

You only needed a time clock

Many Hubstaff accounts were opened for attendance and overtime and never used the proof-of-work features. For an hourly crew, a time clock with GPS and payroll export does the job without screenshots.

Fixed by: Jibble or the apps in our clock in clock out app review.

The alternatives in detail

Toggl Track

No monitoring, by policy
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, browser extensions Pricing (October 2026) Free for 3 users on new accounts (older workspaces keep 5); Starter $9 and Premium $16 per user per month billed annually ($12 and $24 monthly); Enterprise by quote; 30-day trial on paid plans

What it is

Toggl Track is a timer, a project and client list, and reports, with a private desktop timeline that fills in gaps only the user can see. Its pricing FAQ states that Toggl does not monitor employees, take screenshots or record keystrokes, browsing or location. Starter adds billable rates and team reports; Premium adds timesheet approvals, labor cost and profitability, Jira and Asana integrations and SSO. Toggl relaunched as Toggl 2.0 on September 15, 2026, and the free seat count and Premium price differ between new and grandfathered accounts.

Better than Hubstaff

  • The highest adoption of any tracker here, because it asks nothing of the person but a click
  • Approvals, budgets and profitability without any surveillance layer
  • Clockify, TimeCamp and others import Toggl's CSV export directly

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • No GPS, no kiosk, no payroll, no screenshots, by design
  • Costs more per seat than Hubstaff at every tier once you need approvals
  • Free plan is capped at 3 users on new accounts with limited report views

Who should switch to it

Agencies, consultancies and knowledge-work teams leaving Hubstaff because the activity score and screenshots were hurting trust, who bill or budget by project and do not pay hourly crews.

Switching

Export Hubstaff's time entries and projects to CSV, import into a Toggl workspace with the universal CSV importer, and keep Hubstaff read-only for a pay cycle to reconcile. Toggl keeps no screenshots, so archive Hubstaff's if a client contract requires them.

ActivTrak

Analytics without images
Runs on Windows, macOS, Chromebook; no Linux agent Pricing (October 2026) Free for 3 users with 30 days of history; Work Activity Tracking $10, Workforce Management $15, Productivity Optimization $17, Workflow $21 per user per month, annual contracts only; screenshots only through the Screen Details add-on

What it is

ActivTrak records which applications and websites are active and classifies them as productive or not, then reports utilization, focus time, schedule adherence and workload balance. It does not take screenshots unless you buy the Screen Details add-on, and its higher plans drop the option entirely. Data history is six months on the two lower paid plans and a year on Productivity Optimization, extendable at $1 per user per month per year. ActivTrak repackaged its plans in July 2026 and moved accounts in August.

Better than Hubstaff

  • No screenshots on the plans most buyers choose, which is easier to explain to a team
  • Analytics on workload, focus and burnout signals that Hubstaff does not attempt
  • A free plan for three people to test the method

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • No Linux agent, no time tracking against projects for billing, no payroll or GPS
  • Annual contracts only, and the entry plan costs twice Hubstaff's
  • Data retention caps mean last year's comparison may be gone

Who should switch to it

Office and hybrid teams that used Hubstaff for visibility rather than billing, and want to keep measuring application use while dropping screenshots. Our productivity monitoring review has the full comparison.

Switching

ActivTrak is not a time tracker, so you keep or replace Hubstaff's project time separately. Export Hubstaff reports before the switch; ActivTrak's own export runs through its ActivConnect add-on.

Clockify

Most features for the least money
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, kiosk Pricing (October 2026) Free for 5 users with 31-day reports and PDF export only; Basic $3.99, Standard $5.49, Pro $7.99, Enterprise $11.99 per seat per month billed annually ($4.99, $6.99, $9.99, $14.99 monthly); kiosk-only seats from $0.79

What it is

Clockify is a timer and timesheet with an attendance layer: kiosk and breaks on Basic, approvals, time off, overtime and invoicing on Standard, scheduling, GPS, expenses and activity monitoring on Pro. It does not screenshot, and its activity monitoring is an opt-in Pro feature, so the default is a tracker with no proof-of-work layer. The free plan was cut back on April 21, 2026: five users, one-month reports, no CSV export, which pushed many free users to paid tiers or elsewhere.

Better than Hubstaff

  • Attendance, overtime, time off, approvals and invoicing at $5.49, below Hubstaff's Grow plan
  • No minimum seats; kiosk seats for shared devices cost under a dollar
  • Imports Toggl and Harvest CSVs; QuickBooks sync on Standard

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • GPS only on Pro and no geofencing; no payroll runs
  • No Hubstaff-style proof of work unless you turn on Pro activity monitoring, and no screenshots at all
  • Free plan now too limited for a real team

Who should switch to it

Teams leaving because the Hubstaff invoice outgrew the need: you want timesheets, overtime and invoices and can live without screenshots.

Switching

Clockify imports CSV time entries with email, project, description, start date, start time and duration columns. Invite users first so the emails match, and run the import on a paid plan or trial.

DeskTime

Automatic tracking, screenshots optional
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Free Lite for one user; Pro $6.42 and Premium $9.17 per user per month billed annually ($7 and $10 monthly); Enterprise by quote for 200 or more users; 14-day trial

What it is

DeskTime starts when the computer does and classifies applications and websites as productive, unproductive or neutral, with a private time button that pauses tracking. Document title tracking is on Pro; screenshots, shift scheduling and the API are Premium only. It also handles absence and shifts, which makes it a light attendance tool for office teams.

Better than Hubstaff

  • Screenshots are a Premium add-on rather than the default, and private time is built in
  • Lighter desktop agent than Hubstaff's in user reports
  • Shift scheduling and absence calendar on the same plan

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • No GPS, no payroll, no invoicing
  • No project budgets or client billing to speak of
  • Pricing displayed in local currency on the vendor site; confirm the dollar rate

Who should switch to it

Office teams that liked Hubstaff's automatic capture but want the screenshots gone by default and a quieter agent.

Switching

DeskTime has no Hubstaff importer; start fresh at a pay-period boundary and archive Hubstaff's reports as PDFs.

Insightful

Analytics with screenshots you control
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Workforce Analytics $8 and Combo $16 per seat per month billed annually; Workflow Optimization $12 in beta; add-ons per seat: on-demand screenshots $4, higher-frequency screenshots $3, screen recording $4, workspace security $4; 7-day trial; on-premises from 250 seats

What it is

Insightful, formerly Workpuls, records application and website use, activity and screenshots, with time and attendance, projects and manual time on its analytics plan and payroll on Combo. Screenshots can be configured per team and switched off; stealth mode exists for company-owned devices. It keeps 24 months of data, the longest retention in this group, and offers a Linux agent.

Better than Hubstaff

  • Per-team screenshot settings let you keep proof of work for one client and none for the rest
  • Linux agent and 24-month retention
  • Analytics on capacity and utilization that Hubstaff lacks

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • No mobile app or GPS, so field teams cannot use it
  • Add-ons raise the $8 entry price quickly
  • Stealth mode and system accounts counted as seats appear in complaints

Who should switch to it

Office teams that want Hubstaff's level of visibility with finer control over who is screenshotted, and that do not need GPS.

Switching

Insightful has a developer platform and CSV reports but no Hubstaff importer; run both for one period and reconcile hours before canceling.

Time Doctor

If you want more monitoring, not less
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, Android Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.70, Standard $11.70, Premium $16.70 per user per month billed annually ($8, $14, $20 monthly); Enterprise by quote; 14-day trial; HRIS and payroll integrations $200 per integration per month as add-ons

What it is

Time Doctor is the one alternative on this list that monitors more than Hubstaff: screenshots on every plan, video screen recording and mouse-jiggler detection on Premium, web and app reports, attendance, payroll and a client login on Standard. Data history is three months on Basic and two years on Premium. It belongs here because some teams leave Hubstaff for the opposite reason, a client or regulator wants fuller records.

Better than Hubstaff

  • Video recording and unusual-activity detection for teams that must evidence work
  • Attendance, schedules, approvals and payroll on Standard
  • Two years of history on Premium

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • Screenshots are not optional on any plan
  • Standard at $11.70 is the realistic plan, above Hubstaff's Team
  • Trustpilot carries a 1.2 of 5 rating over 235 reviews, mostly about billing after cancellation and refunds

Who should switch to it

Outsourcing firms and BPOs whose contracts require screen records and who left Hubstaff for coverage gaps rather than ethics. Everyone else should read the Time Doctor alternatives page first.

Switching

Cancel in writing and keep the confirmation; Time Doctor's negative reviews cluster on continued billing. Export Hubstaff data before the overlap ends.

Everhour

Time inside the project tool
Runs on Web and browser extension inside Asana, Jira, ClickUp, monday, Trello, GitHub and Notion; iOS Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 5 users without integrations; Team $8.50 per seat per month billed annually with a 5-seat minimum; Custom from 50 seats; 14-day trial; no refunds on unused annual months

What it is

Everhour puts a timer and an estimate on the task inside the project management tool you already use, then reports budgets, billable time and invoices from there. There is no desktop agent, no screenshots and no activity score. Approvals and SSO are in the Team plan.

Better than Hubstaff

  • Estimates and budgets live where the work is planned, so overruns are visible to the people doing the work
  • No surveillance of any kind
  • Invoicing and budgeting in one tier

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • Needs a supported project tool; standalone it is thin
  • 5-seat minimum and no monthly price published
  • No GPS, kiosk, payroll or attendance

Who should switch to it

Project teams in Asana, Jira or ClickUp that used Hubstaff for project hours and were paying for monitoring they never wanted.

Switching

Map Hubstaff projects to tasks in your project tool first; Everhour has no CSV importer for historical time, so keep Hubstaff reports for closed projects.

Jibble

A free time clock with GPS
Runs on Web, Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, kiosk, Slack and Teams Pricing (October 2026) Free for unlimited users with mobile and web clock-in, desktop tracking, basic timesheets, GPS stamps and a facial recognition kiosk; paid tiers quoted rather than published, with third-party listings between about $3.49 and $5.99 for Premium and $6.99 to $10.99 for Ultimate depending on billing; confirm the quote

What it is

Jibble is an attendance tool: clock in from a phone, a browser or a shared kiosk with a selfie or face scan, with GPS stamps, geofencing, overtime and paid-break calculation, schedules and timesheet approvals. It exports to QuickBooks, Xero and Deel. Screenshot tracking exists as a paid option but is not the product's center of gravity.

Better than Hubstaff

  • Free for unlimited users, with the kiosk and GPS included
  • Facial recognition on the kiosk stops buddy punching
  • Overtime and paid-break rules built in

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • Paid pricing is not published, which makes budgeting awkward
  • No project budgets, invoicing depth or proof of work for clients
  • Facial recognition triggers Illinois BIPA consent duties

Who should switch to it

Hourly teams that used Hubstaff as a time clock and want attendance, GPS and overtime without monitoring or a per-seat invoice.

Switching

Jibble imports employees from CSV; historical time stays in Hubstaff's exports. Start at a pay-period boundary.

Time Champ

Attendance plus configurable monitoring
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $3.90, Professional $6.90, Enterprise $13.90 per user per month billed annually; screen recording, keylogger, project management, HR and DLP as add-ons; trial without a card

What it is

Time Champ records automatic attendance and classifies application and website use by productivity, with screenshots configurable per team and off by default on the entry plan, and shift and leave management on the same platform. It is the lowest-priced full plan in this group and covers the attendance and productivity use that most Hubstaff accounts actually serve.

Better than Hubstaff

  • Starter at $3.90 undercuts every paid plan here
  • Screenshots configurable per team rather than on by default
  • Attendance, shifts and leave in the base product

Where Hubstaff is still better

  • Shorter track record below enterprise accounts than Hubstaff
  • GPS is through the mobile app, without Hubstaff's job-site automation
  • Payroll and invoicing need add-ons

Who should switch to it

Small and mid-sized teams that want attendance and productivity classification for less than Hubstaff and want to decide screenshot policy themselves.

Switching

CSV import for users and projects; historical time is not imported. Set the screenshot policy before inviting anyone.

How to switch without losing a pay period

  1. Export everything first. Hubstaff exports time entries, projects and payments to CSV and exposes an API; screenshots are not exportable in bulk, so archive the reports a client might ask for.
  2. Decide the monitoring policy before you pick the tool. Write down what the new tool will record and who sees it. If you are in Connecticut, Delaware or New York, the written monitoring notice in our monitoring policy template must go out before the new agent is installed, even if the new tool records less.
  3. Run both for one pay period. Hubstaff's 30-day money-back guarantee does not cover trial accounts, and most alternatives offer 14 days, so overlap one cycle and reconcile hours.
  4. Cancel in the account, not by email. Remove seats before the renewal date; annual plans do not refund removed seats.
  5. Tell the team why. Teams that were monitored and are now not should hear it from a manager, not from the missing tray icon. The rollout guide covers the conversation.

How these tools were chosen

Every tool here was checked against the same list on October 21, 2026: what it records and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how the employee experiences it, the published pricing with minimums and add-ons, and whether Hubstaff's data can be moved into it. Pricing was read from each vendor's pricing page; where a vendor publishes no table (Jibble's paid tiers, Hubstaff's full grid on the day of checking) the page says so. Complaints are drawn from public review platforms and vendor documentation, not from a formal survey. No vendor paid for placement and there are no affiliate links.

Disclosure. I have done work for Time Champ. It appears here because it belongs in this category, it is held to the same criteria as every other product, and its drawbacks are listed like everyone else's.

Key takeaways

  • Decide why you are leaving first. The activity score, screenshots, the bill and idle-time disputes each point at different tools.
  • Hubstaff in October 2026: Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10 per seat annually with a 2-seat minimum, optional screenshots, and an activity level that is a keyboard-and-mouse ratio by Hubstaff's own description.
  • No monitoring at all: Toggl Track (from $9), Everhour ($8.50, 5-seat minimum). Both refuse screenshots and scoring by policy.
  • Analytics without images: ActivTrak ($10 to $21, annual only, free for 3) and DeskTime ($6.42 to $9.17).
  • Cheaper with attendance: Clockify Standard $5.49, Time Champ Starter $3.90, Jibble free for unlimited users.
  • Time Doctor monitors more than Hubstaff, not less, and carries a 1.2 Trustpilot score over billing complaints; choose it only if a contract requires screen records.
  • Export Hubstaff's CSVs first, issue a monitoring notice where state law requires one, overlap one pay period, and cancel inside the account before renewal.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to Hubstaff?

It depends on why you are leaving. Toggl Track if the activity score and screenshots damaged trust and you track project hours. ActivTrak if you want application analytics without images. Clockify if the bill is the problem and you need attendance and invoicing. Jibble if you only ever needed a time clock with GPS. The chooser above applies those rules.

Is there a free alternative to Hubstaff?

Yes. Jibble is free for unlimited users with GPS stamps, a kiosk and timesheets. Clockify is free for 5 users with one-month reports. Toggl Track is free for 3 users on new accounts and ActivTrak for 3 users with 30 days of history. None of the free plans includes screenshots.

Does Hubstaff have a free plan?

Not a permanent one in October 2026. Hubstaff offers a 14-day trial without a card and a 30-day money-back guarantee on paid plans, which excludes the trial. Paid plans start at $4.99 per seat per month billed annually with a 2-seat minimum.

How is Hubstaff's activity level calculated?

As the percentage of tracked time in which keyboard or mouse input was detected. Hubstaff's own support replies describe it this way and note it is not a reliable measure for people on calls, reading or in meetings, for which it recommends disabling activity recording on those projects.

Can Hubstaff screenshots be turned off?

Yes. Screenshots can be set to off, blurred, or one to three per ten minutes, per project or team. The default for a new workspace records them, which is why most teams that object to screenshots never see the setting.

Which Hubstaff alternatives do not take screenshots?

Toggl Track and Everhour take none by design. Clockify, ActivTrak and DeskTime take none on their standard plans (Clockify's Pro activity monitoring and ActivTrak's Screen Details add-on are opt-in, DeskTime's screenshots are Premium only). Jibble's free plan has none. Time Doctor takes them on every plan.

Which alternative is cheapest for a small team?

At five users on October 2026 prices: Jibble free, Clockify free or $19.95 on Basic, Time Champ Starter $19.50, Clockify Standard $27.45, DeskTime Pro $32.10, Hubstaff Starter $24.95. At fifty users the spread runs from free to about $500 a month; the plan with approvals is usually one tier up.

How do I move my data out of Hubstaff?

Export time entries, projects and payments to CSV from the reports and settings areas, or through the API. Screenshots are not bulk-exportable. Clockify, Toggl Track, TimeCamp and Jibble import CSV users or time entries; Everhour, DeskTime and Insightful start clean, so keep Hubstaff's exports for closed periods.
