Top Employee Monitoring Software for Small Businesses (2026)

P Pavan
October 6, 2026 · 16 min read

A small business buys monitoring software for one of three reasons: to track hours for people who are paid by them, to see how a small team's time is spent when the owner can no longer see it directly, or because of a specific worry about one person or one kind of data. The tools that suit a company of five to fifty are not the enterprise suites with five-seat minimums and quote-only pricing; they are the ones with a free tier or a cheap entry plan, no minimum or a small one, setup in an afternoon, and support that answers.

I reviewed eight against those tests, with pricing checked in October 2026. The calculator below prices each plan at your headcount, including the seat minimums that make a $4.99 plan cost more than it says. The first three are the ones I would trial first; the remaining five fit particular small-business situations. The full review covers the enterprise and security tools this page leaves out.

The tools in detail

Hubstaff

Time, screenshots and payroll from $4.99 a seat
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Starter $4.99, Grow $7.50, Team $10, Enterprise $25 per seat per month billed annually ($7, $9 and $12 monthly); 2-seat minimum; 14-day trial

What it is

A time tracker for distributed teams with screenshots, activity levels, GPS, payroll and invoicing, on every desktop and mobile platform.

Why it is on this list

Hubstaff leads for small businesses because the cheapest plan does the job most small teams actually have, which is tracking hours for people who are paid by them, and because the path from there to payroll and invoicing is inside the same product. At $4.99 a seat with a two-seat minimum, a team of eight costs about $40 a month on annual billing. It is forgiving to set up, well documented, and reviewed by thousands of small teams, which is the reassurance an owner without an IT department wants.

Where it is strong

  • Time tracking, screenshots, GPS, payroll and invoicing in one product
  • Runs everywhere, including Linux and Chromebook, with mobile apps
  • Over 2,300 verified reviews and a long track record with distributed teams
  • Clear per-seat pricing with a cheap entry plan

Where it falls short

  • Activity percentages reward mouse movement, not work, and people know it
  • Two-seat minimum and add-ons on the lower plans
  • Monitoring depth is modest by design; not a security tool

ActivTrak

Free for three people, then analytics that need no screenshots
Runs on Windows, macOS, Chromebook Pricing (October 2026) Free for up to 3 people (30-day retention); Work Activity Tracking $10, Workforce Management $15, Productivity Optimization $17, Workflow $21 per user per month, billed annually

What it is

Productivity analytics from application, website and activity data, with optional screenshots and no keystroke logging; free for three people.

Why it is on this list

ActivTrak is second because the free plan is a genuine product for a business of three people, and because the paid analytics answer the small-business owner's real question, how the team's time is spent and whether anyone is drowning, without screenshots or keystrokes that would change the culture of a ten-person office. The honest caveat is that the $10 plan is thin, and the reports that justify the purchase sit at $15.

Where it is strong

  • A free plan for three people, which is the best way to see the data before buying
  • Productivity, utilisation and focus analytics that managers can act on without reading screenshots
  • No keystroke logging and optional screenshots: the least invasive serious tool here
  • Workforce planning, capacity and coaching features on the upper plans

Where it falls short

  • No Linux agent and no keystroke logging, so it cannot serve investigations
  • The useful features sit on the $15 and $17 plans; the $10 plan is thin
  • Data retention of 6 to 12 months unless you pay for more

Time Champ

The cheapest full plan here, with attendance built in
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux; iOS and Android for GPS Pricing (October 2026) Starter $3.90, Professional $6.90, Enterprise $13.90 per user per month billed annually; add-ons for screen recording, keylogger, project management, HR and DLP; free trial without a card

What it is

Attendance, time tracking and monitoring in one agent from $3.90 a seat, with configurable screenshots, blurring and visible or silent tracking.

Why it is on this list

Time Champ is third on price and breadth: the Starter plan at $3.90 a seat is the cheapest route on this list to automatic attendance, application and website tracking, productivity classification and timesheets in one agent, with no seat minimum. For a small business that is also trying to replace a paper attendance register, that combination is the whole shopping list. Professional at $6.90 adds the screenshots and shift configuration a growing team wants. History is short until Enterprise, which is the main thing to check.

Where it is strong

  • Attendance, shifts and monitoring in one agent from $3.90 a seat
  • Screenshot frequency, blurring and silent or visible tracking are all configurable
  • Live screen view, real-time alerts and suspicious activity detection on Enterprise
  • Windows, Mac and Linux with GPS for mobile teams

Where it falls short

  • Detailed activity history is short on Professional; longer history means Enterprise
  • Screen recording, keylogging and DLP are paid add-ons
  • A younger vendor with a smaller review base than the leaders

Controlio

Recording and keystrokes without an enterprise contract
Runs on Windows, macOS Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.67 and Advanced $9.99 per user per month billed annually ($7.99 and $11.99 monthly); Enterprise by quote; 14-day trial

What it is

Cloud or on-premise monitoring with continuous screen recording, keystrokes and productivity scoring for Windows and Mac, at a mid-market price.

Why it is on this list

Controlio is on the small-business list for the owner with a specific concern, a department handling payment data or a suspected leak, who needs recording and keystrokes for a few machines without an enterprise contract. It is cheap, cloud or on-premise, and quick to read. Use it for the specific concern and the specific machines, with notice, not as the default for the whole office.

Where it is strong

  • Continuous screen recording and keystrokes at a mid-market price
  • Cloud or on-premise
  • Simple to deploy and read; little training needed
  • Stealth mode where policy and law allow it

Where it falls short

  • No Linux agent
  • Continuous recording and keystrokes raise notice and consent obligations in several states
  • Analytics are basic next to ActivTrak or Insightful

DeskTime

A free single-user plan and $6.42 after
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android Pricing (October 2026) Pro $7 and Premium $10 per user per month ($6.42 and $9.17 billed annually); Enterprise by quote; free Lite plan for one user; 14-day trial

What it is

An automatic time tracker with productivity scoring, a private time switch, shift scheduling and an absence calendar; screenshots only on Premium.

Why it is on this list

DeskTime is here for the very small team: free for one user, $6.42 a seat after, with automatic tracking, a private time toggle and the absence calendar and shift scheduling that a small business would otherwise run in a spreadsheet. It is the least threatening tool on the list to introduce to a team of six.

Where it is strong

  • Automatic tracking with a private time toggle employees can use
  • Shift scheduling and absence calendar built in
  • Low price with a free single-user plan
  • Windows, Mac, Linux and mobile apps

Where it falls short

  • Screenshots only on Premium
  • Productivity scoring is simple and needs tuning per role
  • Reporting is lighter for large organisations

Kickidler

Lifetime and long-term licences
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Time Tracking from about $2.75 and Employee Monitoring from about $5.50 per user per month depending on term, with cheaper long-term and lifetime licences; free trial

What it is

A low-cost monitoring tool with live screen viewing, video recording and keystrokes in its full tier, and long-term and lifetime licences.

Why it is on this list

Kickidler is here because small-business owners like paying once, and its lifetime and long-term licences make it the cheapest full monitoring suite over three years. Its defaults, live screen viewing and video recording, are more than most small businesses should deploy, and the notice and consent rules apply in full; buy the lighter Time Tracking tier unless there is a reason not to.

Where it is strong

  • The lowest cost here on a multi-year or lifetime licence
  • Live screen viewing of many employees at once
  • Video recording, keystrokes and remote control in the full tier
  • Windows, Mac and Linux, cloud or on-premise

Where it falls short

  • Live viewing and remote control are intrusive and unlawful without notice in several states
  • Interface and documentation lag the premium products
  • Pricing varies by term and region; confirm in writing

Monitask

Quick setup and adjustable screenshots
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux Pricing (October 2026) Pro $6.49, Business $8.99, Business Premium $12.99 per user per month billed annually ($7.99, $10.99, $15.99 monthly); Enterprise for 100-plus; 10-day trial

What it is

A time tracker with adjustable screenshots, activity and project tracking for small teams and agencies, with short data retention.

Why it is on this list

Monitask fits agencies and micro-teams that bill clients by the hour and need screenshots as the agreed proof. It is cheap, takes an afternoon to set up, and lets each team set or disable screenshots. The one-to-two-month retention on the main plans is enough for billing and not for an investigation, which for most small businesses is fine.

Where it is strong

  • Inexpensive and quick to set up for a small team or agency
  • Screenshot frequency adjustable per team, or off
  • Project time, timesheets and payroll exports
  • Full URL and search query tracking on the top plan

Where it falls short

  • Data kept for only one or two months on the main plans
  • Website and app tracking only from the Business plan
  • Not built for large organisations

Time Doctor

Mature remote tracking with payroll
Runs on Windows, macOS, Linux, Chromebook, Android Pricing (October 2026) Basic $6.70, Standard $11.70, Premium $16.70 per user per month billed annually ($8, $14, $20 monthly); Enterprise by quote; 14-day trial

What it is

Remote-team time tracking with screenshots, web and app use, payroll, work-life balance reporting and an Office versus Remote comparison.

Why it is on this list

Time Doctor closes the list for the small business with remote or outsourced staff. The Basic plan is competitive, but the payroll, attendance and integrations most such teams need are on Standard at $11.70, which makes it the most expensive realistic choice here. Its work-life balance reporting is the reason to pay it.

Where it is strong

  • Built for remote and outsourced teams, with payroll and 60-plus integrations
  • Work-life balance and Office versus Remote reporting
  • Screen video and two years of history on Premium
  • Chromebook and Android agents alongside the desktop platforms

Where it falls short

  • Integrations and payroll start at the $11.70 Standard plan
  • Screenshots and activity scoring draw the usual objections from staff
  • Interface shows its age in places

What a small business actually needs

Less than the vendors sell. A team of twelve needs to know hours worked if people are hourly, roughly how time divides between client work and everything else, and whether anyone is quietly overloaded. It does not need keystroke logs, screen video or an insider risk score, and buying them creates a culture problem in a company small enough for everyone to know who is being watched. Start with application and website tracking and activity, add screenshots only if there is a billing reason, and leave recording for a specific documented concern. The employee productivity guide covers what to measure instead, and the small business timesheet software page covers the case where hours are the whole requirement.

Minimums, add-ons and other traps

  • Seat minimums. Hubstaff bills two seats; Teramind and Veriato bill five and are not on this page for that reason. A sole owner with one employee pays for two.
  • Annual billing. Every annual price here is 15 to 30 per cent below the monthly one, and it is a year's commitment. Trial on monthly, then switch.
  • Add-ons. Screenshots (DeskTime Premium), recording and keylogging (Time Champ), longer history (ActivTrak) and website tracking (Monitask Business) are where the entry price grows.
  • Retention. Monitask keeps one to two months; Time Champ's detailed activity is a week on Professional; ActivTrak's free tier is 30 days. Fine for billing, not for a dispute six months later.
  • Lifetime licences. Kickidler's are cheap and real, and a lifetime licence is only as long as the vendor's. Weigh it accordingly.
  • The cost of the time. A tool that takes a day a month to administer costs more than its subscription for a business without an admin. Setup and support quality are in each card.

The law for small businesses

Size does not exempt an employer from the monitoring rules. New York's notice law applies to every private employer; Connecticut's and Delaware's likewise; the National Labor Relations Act covers almost every private business regardless of size; and California's privacy rules reach small companies that handle enough data. The practical minimum is a one-page notice employees sign, written from the policy template, and monitoring limited to company devices. The state rules are on one page.

How these tools were evaluated

I evaluated each tool against the same checklist: what it captures and how configurable that is, which platforms it runs on, how it treats the employee (visible or silent agent, access to their own data, screenshot blurring and private time), what the published pricing is and what the minimum commitment really costs, how long data is kept, where it can be deployed, and what the vendor's own documentation and verified user reviews say about reliability and support. Pricing was taken from each vendor's pricing page in October 2026; where a vendor publishes no price, the page says so rather than guessing. Free plans and trials were used where they exist. I did not run a formal benchmark, and the page does not claim one.

Disclosure. I have done work for Time Champ. It appears here because it belongs in this category, it is held to the same criteria as every other product, its drawbacks are listed like everyone else's, and it bought no placement. There are no affiliate or vendor links on this page.

Key takeaways

  • Small businesses need hours, a rough picture of where time goes, and overload signals. They do not need keystrokes or screen video.
  • Hubstaff for hourly teams from $4.99, ActivTrak free for three then $10, Time Champ for attendance and monitoring from $3.90.
  • Seat minimums, annual billing, add-ons and short retention are where the real cost hides; the calculator applies the minimums.
  • Trial on monthly billing, visibly, then switch to annual.
  • Size does not exempt you from notice laws or the NLRA; a signed one-page notice is the minimum.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best employee monitoring software for a small business?

Hubstaff for small teams paid by the hour, with time, screenshots and payroll from $4.99 a seat; ActivTrak for owners who want to understand how the team's time is spent without screenshots, free for three people; Time Champ for small businesses that need attendance and monitoring together, from $3.90 a seat with no minimum. DeskTime and Monitask are the other budget options.

Is there free employee monitoring software?

ActivTrak is free for up to three people with 30 days of history and is a complete product at that size. DeskTime has a free single-user plan. Everything else offers a trial of 7 to 14 days. Free tiers are a good way to see whether the data changes any decision before paying.

How much should a small business pay for employee monitoring?

In October 2026, $4 to $10 per seat per month on annual billing covers the tools a small business should consider: Time Champ from $3.90, Hubstaff from $4.99, DeskTime from $6.42, Monitask from $6.49, Controlio from $6.67, Time Doctor from $6.70, ActivTrak from $10. Twelve people therefore cost roughly $50 to $120 a month before add-ons.

Do I need to tell my employees I am monitoring them?

In New York, Connecticut and Delaware you must, in writing. Everywhere else it is strongly advisable: notice obtained through a signed policy supports the lawfulness of the monitoring, and undisclosed monitoring discovered later damages a small team far more than disclosed monitoring does. The policy template on this site produces the notice.

Can I monitor employees' personal phones or laptops in a small business?

Not without consent, and not beyond a work profile or work accounts even with it. For a small business the safer answer is company devices for anyone who will be monitored, or a work container on the personal device. Company email and chat accounts are visible to administrators from any device.

Which monitoring software is easiest to set up for a small team?

Hubstaff, Monitask, DeskTime and Time Champ can be running in an afternoon: create the account, send the agent link, set the screenshot policy. ActivTrak is similar on the free tier. Controlio and Kickidler take a little longer, especially on-premise.
